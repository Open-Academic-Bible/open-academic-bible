# How to give feedback

Thank you for helping improve The Open Academic Bible. The most useful feedback is specific and comes with evidence.

## What helps most

- **Translation:** a verse where the English does not say what the Hebrew or Greek says, or where an important
  alternative meaning is missing from the footnotes.
- **Text and manuscripts:** a place where our text or footnotes do not match the critical edition (for the New
  Testament, UBS6 / Nestle-Aland 28-29; for the Old Testament, the Masoretic text and the major ancient witnesses).
- **Footnotes:** a footnote that is wrong, unclear, or missing.
- **Typos and formatting:** spelling, punctuation, verse numbering, broken links.

## How to write it

1. **The verse:** book, chapter and verse (e.g. Romans 3:22).
2. **What it says now** and **what you suggest.**
3. **Your evidence:** the Hebrew or Greek, a standard lexicon (BDAG, HALOT), a critical commentary, a manuscript or a
   critical edition. Page numbers help.

Please open one issue per verse or passage, so each can be tracked and answered.

## Our standpoint

This project is academic and non-confessional. We follow the evidence of the languages and the manuscripts, and where
scholars disagree we print the main reading in the text and the main alternative in a footnote. Arguments that rest on a
particular theological position, rather than on the text, are better served by translations made for that purpose.

## What happens next

Every issue is read. Accepted changes are made in the next update and listed in the change log with their reason.
