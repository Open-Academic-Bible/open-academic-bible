# Ignatius to the Ephesians

Chapters: [1](#chapter-1) · [2](#chapter-2) · [3](#chapter-3) · [4](#chapter-4) · [5](#chapter-5) · [6](#chapter-6) · [7](#chapter-7) · [8](#chapter-8) · [9](#chapter-9) · [10](#chapter-10) · [11](#chapter-11) · [12](#chapter-12) · [13](#chapter-13) · [14](#chapter-14) · [15](#chapter-15) · [16](#chapter-16) · [17](#chapter-17) · [18](#chapter-18) · [19](#chapter-19) · [20](#chapter-20) · [21](#chapter-21)

<sup>1</sup> Ignatius, also called Theophorus[^0-1-1], writes to the church that is blessed through the greatness and fullness of God the Father, destined before the ages for a glory that will last and cannot change; united and chosen in true suffering, by the will of the Father and of Jesus Christ our God; the church most worthy of blessing, at Ephesus in Asia. Many greetings in Jesus Christ, and in joy without fault.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> In God I have welcomed your much-loved name, which is yours by a just nature, in keeping with faith and love in Christ Jesus our Savior. You are imitators of God: when you set your natural task ablaze again by the blood of God[^1-1-1], you brought it to perfect completion.

<sup>2</sup> When you heard that I was a prisoner from Syria, bound for the sake of the name and the hope we share, and that I hoped, with the help of your prayer, to reach Rome and fight with beasts there, so that through reaching it I could become a disciple, you were eager to see me.

<sup>3</sup> Since I have welcomed your large company in the name of God, in the person of Onesimus, a man of indescribable love who is your bishop, I ask that you love him, following Jesus Christ, and that all of you be like him. Praise to the one who gave you, as people worthy of it, such a bishop to have.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> About Burrhus, my fellow slave, your deacon by God’s appointment and blessed in all things: I pray that he will remain, to honor you and your bishop. And Crocus, who deserves God’s praise and yours, and whom I received as a model of the love you have, has comforted me in every respect. May the Father of Jesus Christ give him comfort too, along with Onesimus and Burrhus and Euplus and Fronto. Through these men I have seen every one of you in love.

<sup>2</sup> May I always have joy in you, if I am worthy. It is right, then, to glorify Jesus Christ in every way, since he has glorified you, so that you may be fitted together in a single obedience, submitting to the bishop and to the council of presbyters, and be made holy in all things.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> I am not laying down orders for you, as if I were someone important. Though I am in chains for the Name, I am not yet perfected in Jesus Christ. I have only just begun to be taught, and I address you as those who learn alongside me. In fact I was the one who needed to be rubbed with oil[^3-1-1] by you, with faith, warning, endurance, and long patience.

<sup>2</sup> Yet since love will not let me stay silent about you, I have hurried to urge you to move in step with God’s mind[^3-2-1]. Jesus Christ, our undivided life, is the Father’s mind, just as the bishops installed throughout the farthest regions share in the mind of Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> That is why you ought to run together with the bishop’s mind, which is just what you are doing. Your body of presbyters, which has earned its good name and is worthy of God, is joined to the bishop the way strings are joined to a lyre. And so Jesus Christ is sung in your unanimity and harmonious love.

<sup>2</sup> Each one of you, too, should become part of the choir, so that, being in tune with one another and receiving God’s pitch[^4-2-1] in your unity, you may sing with one voice through Jesus Christ to the Father. Then he will hear you, and by your good deeds he will recognize that you are members of his Son. It is therefore to your benefit to live in blameless unity, so that you may always share in God.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> In a short time I gained a bond with your bishop, not a human bond but a spiritual one. How much more, then, I call you happy, you who are so thoroughly mingled with him, as the church is mingled with Jesus Christ and Jesus Christ with the Father, so that all things may harmonize in oneness.

<sup>2</sup> No one should be fooled: unless a person is within the altar area[^5-2-1], that person misses out on God’s bread[^5-2-2]. If the prayer of just one or two carries so much force, how much greater is the force of the bishop’s prayer together with the whole church’s!

<sup>3</sup> A person who does not join the common gathering is already showing pride and has made a separation for themselves[^5-3-1]. As scripture says: “God sets himself against the proud[^5-3-2].” So let us take care not to set ourselves against the bishop, so that we will be put under God.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> The more anyone sees the bishop keeping silent, the more that person should hold him in awe. We must receive everyone whom the master of the house sends to manage his own household as we would receive the one who sent him. Clearly, then, we must look on the bishop as on the Lord himself.

<sup>2</sup> Onesimus himself speaks very highly of the good order you keep in God: that all of you are living in the truth, and that no sect[^6-2-1] makes its home among you. In fact you give a hearing to no one except someone who speaks about Jesus Christ in truth[^6-2-2].

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> Some people have a habit of carrying the Name around with malicious deceit while doing other things that are unworthy of God. You must shun them as wild animals. They are mad dogs that bite in secret, and you must be on guard against them, because they are hard to cure.

<sup>2</sup> There is one physician, of flesh and of spirit, begotten and unbegotten, God in a human being[^7-2-1], true life in death, both from Mary and from God, first subject to suffering and then beyond it: Jesus Christ our Lord.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Let no one lead you astray, just as you are not being led astray, since you belong wholly to God. For as long as no quarrel[^8-1-1] is planted among you that could torment you, you are indeed living according to God. I am a humble offering[^8-1-2] for you, and I dedicate myself for you Ephesians, a church famous through the ages.

<sup>2</sup> Fleshly people[^8-2-1] are unable to do spiritual things, and neither can spiritual people do fleshly things, just as faith cannot act as unbelief does, nor unbelief as faith does. And even your actions in the body are spiritual, because everything you do, you do in Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> Some people from elsewhere, I learned, had passed by, carrying a bad teaching. You refused to let them plant it in you, shutting your ears so that nothing they were sowing could be received. You are like stones in the Father’s temple, prepared for the construction of God the Father, being hoisted aloft by Jesus Christ’s machine, which is the cross, and using the Holy Spirit as the cable. Faith is what lifts you, and love is the way that carries you up to God.

<sup>2</sup> So all of you are fellow travelers on the road, God-bearers[^9-2-1], temple-bearers, Christ-bearers, bearers of holy things, decked out in every way with the commands of Jesus Christ. In writing, I rejoice, and I have been counted worthy to speak with you and celebrate with you, because, in the course of human life, you love only God and nothing else.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> Pray constantly for other people as well, for in them there is hope of repentance, that they may find God. Let them at least learn from you by what you do.

<sup>2</sup> Against their anger, be gentle; against their boasting, be humble; against their slander, offer your prayers; against their error, be firm in faith; against their savagery, be mild, and do not be eager to imitate them in return.

<sup>3</sup> Let us show ourselves their brothers and sisters[^10-3-1] by our fairness, and let us strive to be imitators of the Lord: who might be wronged more, who robbed, who rejected? In this way no weed of the devil will be found among you, but you will remain in complete purity and self-control in Jesus Christ, in body and in spirit.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> The times are the last[^11-1-1]. So let us now be ashamed and fear the long-suffering of God, so that it will not count against us at the judgment. Either we dread the wrath that is coming or we love the grace we have today: one or the other. Only let us be found in Christ Jesus, bound for true life.

<sup>2</sup> Apart from him, let nothing appeal to you. It is in him that I carry about my chains, my spiritual pearls, and in them may I rise, through your prayer. May I always have a part in it, so that I am found in the portion of the Ephesian Christians, who were always in agreement with the apostles through the power of Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> I know who I am, and to whom I write: I am a condemned man; you have been shown mercy. I stand in peril; you are firmly established.

<sup>2</sup> You are the passageway for those who are put to death for God, fellow initiates[^12-2-1] with Paul, who was made holy, who was approved, who is worthy of every blessing. May I be found at his feet when I reach God, for in every letter he mentions you in Christ Jesus.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> Make every effort, then, to gather more often to give thanks to God and to glorify him. For when you meet frequently, the powers of Satan are overthrown, and his destructive work is dissolved by the unity of your faith.

<sup>2</sup> Nothing is better than peace, by which all warfare of beings in heaven and on earth is brought to an end.

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> None of this escapes you, if your faith and love toward Jesus Christ are complete; these are the beginning of life and its goal. Faith is the beginning, and love is the goal. The two, joined in unity, are God, and everything else that is good and noble follows from them.

<sup>2</sup> Nobody who declares faith commits sin, and nobody who possesses love feels hatred. The tree shows itself by its fruit[^14-2-1]; likewise those who profess to be Christ’s will become visible through their conduct. Right now the work is not about profession; it is about being found in faith’s power right to the end.

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> Better to keep quiet and be genuine than to talk and be a fraud. Teaching is fine, provided the speaker acts. There is only one Teacher: he said it, and it came to be[^15-1-1], and even what he does in silence is worthy of the Father.

<sup>2</sup> Whoever truly possesses the word of Jesus is also able to hear his silence, in order to be complete: so that through what such a person says they act, and through what they keep silent about they are known.

<sup>3</sup> The Lord misses nothing; even our hidden things are near him. So let us act in every respect as though he were living in us, so that we are his temples[^15-3-1] and he is our God in us. That is how things are, and it will show itself before our faces, from the reasons we have to love him.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> Do not be led astray, my brothers and sisters[^16-1-1]: those who corrupt households will not inherit the kingdom of God[^16-1-2].

<sup>2</sup> If those who did these things in the flesh have died, how much more will it be so for anyone who corrupts, by bad teaching, the faith in God for which Jesus Christ was crucified? Such a person, having become filthy, will go into the unquenchable fire[^16-2-1], and so will anyone who listens to them.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> The Lord took perfumed oil[^17-1-1] on his head for this reason: to breathe imperishability over the church. Do not let yourselves be smeared with the foul smell of the teaching of this age’s ruler[^17-1-2], or he will carry you off as prisoners, away from the life that lies ahead.

<sup>2</sup> Why aren’t we all sensible, now that we have received knowledge of God, namely Jesus Christ? Why do we waste ourselves in folly, by not knowing the gift that the Lord has really sent?

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> My spirit is an offscouring[^18-1-1] for the cross, which is an offense[^18-1-2] to those who do not believe, but to us is salvation and life without end. Where is the wise one[^18-1-3]? Where is the debater? Where is the pride of those called discerning?

<sup>2</sup> Our God Jesus the Christ was borne in Mary’s womb, in line with God’s plan: of David’s seed[^18-2-1], yes, and of the Holy Spirit. He was born and baptized so that, by suffering, he might cleanse the water.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> Mary’s virginity and her labor, and likewise the death of the Lord, escaped the notice of this age’s ruler. These were three mysteries meant to be shouted aloud, yet worked out in the quiet of God.

<sup>2</sup> So how were they made known to the ages? In heaven a star[^19-2-1] blazed out beyond every other star. Its light could not be put into words, and its strangeness left people astonished. Every remaining star, along with the sun and moon, became a choir for that star, while it surpassed all of them with its light. There was confusion over the origin of this novelty, so unlike the rest.

<sup>3</sup> From that moment all magic was dissolved, and every bond of evil disappeared. Ignorance was destroyed and the old kingdom was ruined, as God appeared in human form for the newness of eternal life. What God had prepared began to take effect, and everything was thrown into commotion, because the destruction of death was being planned.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> If Jesus Christ grants me, through your prayer, and it is his will, in the second short letter that I intend to write to you I will explain more fully the plan I have begun to discuss, concerning the new human being, Jesus Christ, in his faith and his love, his suffering and his resurrection.

<sup>2</sup> This is especially so if the Lord reveals to me that all of you, one by one, by name, gather in common through grace, in one faith and in Jesus Christ, who by the flesh comes from David’s family, both a human son and Son of God[^20-2-1], so that with a mind free of distraction you obey the bishop and the presbyters, breaking a single loaf, the remedy that gives immortality, the antidote so as not to die but to live in Jesus Christ forever.

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> I am your ransom[^21-1-1], a life for yours, and for those you sent to Smyrna for God’s honor. From there I am writing to you, thanking the Lord and loving Polycarp as I love you. Keep me in mind, as Jesus Christ keeps you.

<sup>2</sup> Pray for the church in Syria. I am being led off in chains from there to Rome, though I am the last of the faithful there, as I was granted the honor of being found worthy for God. Farewell, in God the Father and in Jesus Christ, our shared hope.
