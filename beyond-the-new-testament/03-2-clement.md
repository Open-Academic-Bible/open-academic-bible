# 2 Clement

Despite its name, this is not a letter by Clement but the oldest surviving Christian sermon outside the New Testament, probably from the middle of the second century. It calls its hearers to repentance and self-control in view of the coming judgment. It follows 1 Clement in Codex Alexandrinus, where the text breaks off at 12:5. The full text survives in the same manuscript of 1056 that preserves the Didache, and in a Syriac translation.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Brothers and sisters, we must have the same thoughts about Jesus Christ as we have about God: that he is judge of those alive and those dead[^1-1-1]. And we must not have small thoughts about our salvation.

<sup>2</sup> For when we think lightly of him, we hope for only small things in return. Those who listen as though these were small matters sin, and so do we, because we do not know where we were called from, or by whom, or to what place, or how much Jesus Christ was willing to suffer on our behalf.

<sup>3</sup> What payment, then, can we give him in return? What fruit could match what he has given us? And how many holy acts do we owe him?

<sup>4</sup> He freely gave us the light; like a father he called us his children[^1-4-1]; he saved us when we were perishing.

<sup>5</sup> What praise, then, can we offer him, or what repayment for what we have received?

<sup>6</sup> Our minds were crippled. We bowed down to stones and wood, to gold, silver, and bronze, all of them the work of human hands, and our whole life was nothing but death. Wrapped in darkness and filled with such a haze in our sight, we looked up again, by his will, and shed that cloud which had wrapped us round.

<sup>7</sup> For he took pity on us and, moved with compassion, saved us. He saw in us great error and ruin, and saw that we had no hope of salvation except the one that comes from him.

<sup>8</sup> For he called us when we did not exist, and willed that we should exist out of nothing.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> “Rejoice, you childless woman who bears no children; break out and shout, you who feel no labor pains; for the children of the abandoned woman are more than those of the woman who has a husband.”[^2-1-1] When he said, “Rejoice, you childless woman who bears no children,” he meant us, for our church was childless before children were given to her.

<sup>2</sup> And when he said, “Shout, you who feel no labor pains,” he means this: that we should offer our prayers to God in simplicity, and that we should not lose heart like women in labor.

<sup>3</sup> He said this because our people used to look forsaken by God, but now that we have believed we outnumber those who seem to have God.

<sup>4</sup> Another scripture also says, “I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners.”[^2-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> This means that those who are perishing must be saved.

<sup>6</sup> For it is a great and marvelous thing to hold up not what is standing but what is falling.

<sup>7</sup> In the same way Christ wanted to save what was perishing, and he saved many by coming and calling us when we were already perishing.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Because he has shown such great mercy to us, first in that we who are alive no longer sacrifice to dead gods or bow before them, but have learned through him to know the Father of truth—what does knowing him amount to, if not declining to deny the one through whom we came to know him?

<sup>2</sup> He himself also says, “Whoever acknowledges me before people, I will acknowledge before my Father.”[^3-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Our reward, then, is that we acknowledge[^3-3-1] the one by whom we were saved.

<sup>4</sup> And how do we acknowledge him? By doing what he says, never ignoring his commands, and honoring him not only with our lips but from our whole heart and our whole mind[^3-4-1].

<sup>5</sup> And in Isaiah he says, “This people honors me with their lips, yet their heart stays far away from me.”[^3-5-1]

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> So let us not simply call him Lord, for that will not save us.

<sup>2</sup> He says, “Not everyone who calls me ‘Lord, Lord’ will be saved—only the one who does what is right.”[^4-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Therefore, my brothers and sisters, let us confess him through what we do: by loving one another, by staying away from adultery, slander[^4-3-1], and envy, and by being self-controlled, compassionate, and kind. We should also feel for one another in suffering, and not be lovers of money. It is by deeds like these that we are to confess him, and not by their opposites.

<sup>4</sup> And we should not fear people more than God.

<sup>5</sup> That is why the Lord said to those who do such things, “If you were with me, gathered close to my chest[^4-5-1], and yet did not keep my commands, I would throw you out and tell you, ‘Get away from me! I do not know where you come from, you who practice lawlessness.’”

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> For this reason, brothers and sisters, we should abandon our residence as foreigners[^5-1-1] in this world, do what the one who called us wants, and not be frightened to leave this world.

<sup>2</sup> For the Lord says, “You will be like lambs in the midst of wolves.”[^5-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Peter answered and said to him, “What if the wolves tear the lambs apart?”

<sup>4</sup> Jesus said to Peter, “Let the lambs have no fear of the wolves once they are dead; and you, have no fear of those who kill you and are then unable to do anything to you. Fear the one who, once you have died, has power over soul and body to hurl them into the fire of Gehenna.”[^5-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> Understand, brothers and sisters, that our time of living in the flesh here is brief and fleeting, whereas what Christ has promised is vast and wonderful: rest in the kingdom to come and in eternal life.

<sup>6</sup> What can we do to obtain these things, then, except live in a holy and righteous way, regard the things of this world as foreign to us, and not desire them?

<sup>7</sup> For in desiring to acquire them we fall away from the righteous way.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> The Lord says, “No household slave can serve two masters.”[^6-1-1] If we want to serve both God and money[^6-1-2], it will do us no good.

<sup>2</sup> “For what good is it if someone gains the whole world but forfeits their soul?”[^6-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> This age and the coming one are two enemies.

<sup>4</sup> This one speaks of adultery, corruption, love of money, and deceit; the other renounces these things.

<sup>5</sup> We cannot, then, be friends of both. We must renounce this one and use the other.

<sup>6</sup> We consider it better to hate what is here, since it is small, short-lived, and perishable, and to love what is there, the good things that cannot perish.

<sup>7</sup> If we do what Christ wants, we will find rest; if we do not, nothing can save us from eternal punishment, since we will have disregarded his commands.

<sup>8</sup> The scripture also says in Ezekiel that even if Noah, Job, and Daniel should rise up, they would not rescue their children in the captivity.[^6-8-1]

<sup>9</sup> If even people so righteous as these cannot save their children by their own righteousness, how can we, unless we keep our baptism[^6-9-1] clean and unstained, hope to walk into God’s kingdom with any confidence? Who will speak on our behalf[^6-9-2], if our deeds are found not to be holy and just?

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> So then, my brothers and sisters, let us compete, knowing that the contest is at hand, and that many sail in to the contests that perish, but not all are crowned, only those who have labored hard and competed well.

<sup>2</sup> Let us compete, then, so that we may all be crowned.

<sup>3</sup> Then let us take the straight track, the contest that does not perish, and let many of us sail into it and compete, so that we may be crowned as well; and if not everyone can be crowned, we should at least get near to the crown.

<sup>4</sup> We should know that anyone who enters the contest that perishes and is found cheating[^7-4-1] is flogged, carried off, and thrown out of the stadium.

<sup>5</sup> What do you think? What will happen to someone who has corrupted the contest of incorruption[^7-5-1]?

<sup>6</sup> It says that for those who have not preserved the seal[^7-6-1], “their worm will never die, their fire will never go out, and they will be on view to all flesh.”[^7-6-2]

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> While we are on earth, then, let us repent.

<sup>2</sup> We are clay in the craftsman’s hand[^8-2-1]. When a potter makes a vessel and it gets twisted or broken in his hands, he molds it again; but once he has gone so far as to throw it in the fiery kiln, he can do nothing more for it. In the same way, as long as we are in this world, we should repent with our whole heart of the wrongs we have done in the body, in order to be saved by the Lord while there is still time to repent.

<sup>3</sup> For after we depart from this world, we can no longer confess[^8-3-1] there or repent anymore.

<sup>4</sup> So, brothers and sisters, if we do the Father’s will, keep the flesh pure, and observe the Lord’s commandments, we will receive eternal life.

<sup>5</sup> For in the gospel the Lord says, “If you have not preserved what is little, who will entrust you with what is great? I tell you that anyone faithful in the least is faithful in much as well.”[^8-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> What he is saying, then, is this: preserve the flesh in purity and the seal without blemish, in order that we may obtain eternal life.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> And let none of you say that this flesh is not judged and does not rise.

<sup>2</sup> Consider: by what means were you saved, by what means did you regain your sight, if not while you were in this flesh?

<sup>3</sup> So we must guard the flesh as a temple of God[^9-3-1].

<sup>4</sup> For just as you were called in the flesh, you will also come in the flesh.

<sup>5</sup> If Christ the Lord, who saved us, though he was at first spirit, became flesh and so called us, then in this flesh we too will receive our reward.

<sup>6</sup> Let us love one another, then, so that we may all come into the kingdom of God.

<sup>7</sup> While we have time to be healed, let us put ourselves in the hands of God the healer, giving him what is due in return.

<sup>8</sup> What is that? To repent from a sincere heart.

<sup>9</sup> For he knows all things in advance and knows what is in our hearts.

<sup>10</sup> Let us offer him praise, then, not from the mouth alone but from the heart too, in order that he may take us in as his children[^9-10-1].

<sup>11</sup> For the Lord also said, “My brothers[^9-11-1] are the ones who carry out the will of my Father.”

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> So, my brothers and sisters, let us carry out the will of the Father who called us, in order to live. Let us rather chase after virtue, abandon wickedness as the scout that goes ahead of our sins, and run from ungodliness so that evils do not catch up with us.

<sup>2</sup> If we make a real effort to act well, peace will chase us.

<sup>3</sup> For this reason people cannot find peace[^10-3-1]; they bring in human fears, preferring enjoyment here and now to the promise of what is coming.

<sup>4</sup> They do not know how great the torment is that enjoyment here and now brings, or how great the delight is that the coming promise holds.

<sup>5</sup> If they alone were acting this way, it could be borne; but they persist in teaching evil to innocent souls, not knowing that they will receive double judgment[^10-5-1], both they and those who listen to them.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> We should serve God, then, with a clean heart and so be righteous; but if we refuse to serve because we do not trust God’s promise, we will be wretched.

<sup>2</sup> For the prophetic word says this as well: “Wretched are the double-minded[^11-2-1], those who waver in their hearts and say, ‘All this we heard long ago, in the days of our ancestors, and yet we have waited from one day to the next and seen none of it.’

<sup>3</sup> You fools! Think of a tree, for example a vine. First it loses its leaves, then a bud grows, after that an unripe grape, and finally a full bunch.

<sup>4</sup> So too my people have had disorder and trouble; afterward they will receive good things.”

<sup>5</sup> So, my brothers and sisters, let us not be double-minded, but endure with hope, so that we may also receive our reward.

<sup>6</sup> For the one who promised to pay back to each their due for their deeds is faithful.

<sup>7</sup> If we do what is right in God’s sight, we will enter his kingdom and receive the promises that no ear has heard[^11-7-1] and no eye has seen, and that have never risen in a human heart.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> We should wait hour by hour for God’s kingdom, then, with love and righteousness, because we do not know the day when God will appear.

<sup>2</sup> For someone once asked the Lord himself when his kingdom would come, and he answered, “When the two become one, the outer like the inner, and the male together with the female neither male nor female.”[^12-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> “The two are one” when we tell one another the truth and there is one soul in two bodies without pretense.

<sup>4</sup> By “the outer like the inner” he means this: the inner is the soul and the outer is the body. So just as your body can be seen, let your soul show clearly in good deeds.

<sup>5</sup> And what he means by “the male together with the female, neither male nor female” is that a brother seeing a sister[^12-5-1] should have no female thought about her, and she none that is male about him.

<sup>6</sup> “When you do these things,” he says, “my Father’s kingdom will come.”

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> Brothers and sisters, the time has come to repent and to wake up to what is good, for we are loaded with folly and evil. Let us erase the sins we committed before, and be saved by repenting from the depths of the soul. We must not become people-pleasers, nor want to satisfy only ourselves; we should also win the approval of outsiders by living rightly, so that the name[^13-1-1] is not slandered because of us.

<sup>2</sup> For the Lord says, “My name is slandered continually among all the gentiles,”[^13-2-1] and again, “Woe to the one through whom my name is slandered.”[^13-2-2] How is it slandered? By your not doing what I want.

<sup>3</sup> For when the gentiles hear the oracles of God from our mouths, they marvel at them as good and great. But then they learn that our deeds do not match the words we speak, and from there they turn to slander, saying that it is a myth and a delusion.

<sup>4</sup> For when they hear from us that God says, “It is nothing to your credit if you love those who love you; it is to your credit if you love your enemies and those who hate you,”[^13-4-1] they are amazed at such extraordinary goodness. But when they see that we not only fail to love those who hate us but do not even love those who love us, they ridicule us, and the name is slandered.

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> So, brothers and sisters, by doing the will of God our Father we will be part of the first church, the spiritual church, created before the sun and the moon. If we do not do the will of the Lord, we will fall under the scripture that says, “My house has become a robbers’ cave.”[^14-1-1] So let us choose to be members of the church of life, in order to be saved.

<sup>2</sup> I do not suppose you are ignorant that a living church is Christ’s body. For the scripture says, “God made the human being male and female.”[^14-2-1] The male is Christ; the female is the church. And further, the scriptures[^14-2-2] and the apostles teach that the church is not of today but from above, from the beginning. She was spiritual, just like our Jesus, and was made visible in the last days in order to save us.

<sup>3</sup> The church, being spiritual, appeared in the flesh of Christ, to show us that if any of us protect her in the flesh and do not ruin her, we will get her back in the Holy Spirit. This flesh is a counterpart of the Spirit, and no one who ruins the counterpart will have a part in the original. That is what he means, brothers and sisters: protect the flesh, so that you may have a part in the Spirit.

<sup>4</sup> But if we say the church is the flesh and Christ is the Spirit, then to mistreat the flesh is to mistreat the church. Anyone like that will have no share in the Spirit, who is Christ.

<sup>5</sup> So great a life and incorruption can this flesh share in, when the Holy Spirit is joined to it. No one can describe or speak of what the Lord has prepared for his chosen ones.[^14-5-1]

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> I do not think the advice I have given about self-control is a small matter. Anyone who follows it will not regret it, but will save themselves and me, the one who advised them. For it is no small reward to turn a wandering, perishing soul around so that it is saved.

<sup>2</sup> This is the payment we can offer to God who made us: when the one who speaks and the one who listens both speak and listen with faith and love.

<sup>3</sup> Let us then stay faithful, righteous, and holy in what we have believed, so that we may ask boldly of God, who says, “While you are still speaking, I will say, ‘Look, here I am.’”[^15-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> This statement marks a great promise, since the Lord says he is readier to give than the person asking is to ask.

<sup>5</sup> Since we share in such great kindness, let us not begrudge ourselves the receiving of so many good things. For as much pleasure as these words bring to those who act on them, so much condemnation they bring to those who disobey.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> So, brothers and sisters, since we have been given no small opportunity to repent, let us use the time we have to turn back to God who called us, while we still have one who welcomes us.

<sup>2</sup> For if we renounce these pleasures and master our soul by not carrying out its evil desires, we will share in the mercy of Jesus.

<sup>3</sup> You should know that the day of judgment is coming now, burning like a kiln[^16-3-2], and some of the heavens[^16-3-1] will dissolve, and all the earth will be like lead melting in a fire; then the secret and open works of human beings will be made plain.

<sup>4</sup> Giving to the poor[^16-4-2] is good, then, and so is repentance for sin. Fasting is better than prayer, but giving to the poor is better than either. Love covers a multitude of sins[^16-4-1], and prayer that arises from a good conscience delivers from death. Blessed is anyone found complete in these, for giving to the poor lifts the burden of sin.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> Let us repent with all our heart, then, so that none of us is lost along the way. If we have commands to do this as well, to draw people away from idols and teach them, how much more should a soul that already knows God not be lost!

<sup>2</sup> Let us join in helping the weak toward what is good, so that all of us are saved, and let us convert and warn one another.

<sup>3</sup> Let us not simply appear to believe and listen now, while the elders[^17-3-1] are warning us, but when we go back home too, let us remember the commands of the Lord, let us not be pulled away by worldly cravings, and let us come here more often and strive to advance in the Lord’s commands, so that we may all be gathered in one mind for life.

<sup>4</sup> For the Lord said, “I am coming to gather all the nations, tribes, and languages.”[^17-4-1] He means the day of his appearing, when he will come and redeem us, each one according to our works.

<sup>5</sup> Then the unbelievers will see his glory and power, and they will be shocked to see the sovereignty over the world in Jesus’s hands. They will say, “Woe to us! It was you, and we never knew it; we refused to believe, and we did not listen to the elders who told us of our salvation.” “Their worm will not die, and their fire will not go out, and they will be a spectacle for all flesh.”[^17-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> He is speaking of that day of judgment, when they will see those among us who have lived ungodly lives and twisted the commandments of Jesus Christ.

<sup>7</sup> The righteous, however, who did well, put up with the torments, and hated the pleasures of the soul, will see those who went astray and denied Jesus in word or in deed being punished in unquenchable fire[^17-7-1] with fearful torments. Then they will give their God the glory and say, “Hope is there for whoever has served God from the whole heart.”

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> Let us also be numbered with those who offer thanks, who have served God, and not with the godless who are condemned.

<sup>2</sup> For I myself am a complete sinner and have not yet escaped temptation, but am still in the midst of the devil’s instruments; yet I am eager to pursue righteousness, so that I may have the strength at least to come near it, since I fear the coming judgment.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> So, brothers and sisters, after the God of truth[^19-1-1] I am reading you an appeal[^19-1-2] to pay attention to what is written, so that you may save yourselves and the one who is reading among you. As my reward I ask this of you: that you repent with all your heart and give yourselves salvation and life. For if we do this, we will set a goal for all the young who want to work hard at devotion and the goodness of God.

<sup>2</sup> And let us not be displeased and angry, foolish as we are, when someone corrects us and turns us from injustice to righteousness. For sometimes while we are doing evil we do not realize it, because of the double-mindedness and unbelief in our hearts, and our understanding has been darkened[^19-2-1] by empty desires.

<sup>3</sup> Let us do what is right, then, so that in the end we are saved. Blessed are the people who heed these instructions; even if they are badly treated for a little while in this world, they will gather the immortal harvest of resurrection.

<sup>4</sup> So let the devout person not be distressed if they are miserable in the present time; a blessed time awaits them. They will live again above with the ancestors[^19-4-1] and rejoice in an age without sorrow.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> But do not let this trouble your mind either, that we see the unjust becoming rich while the slaves of God[^20-1-1] are in distress.

<sup>2</sup> Have faith, brothers and sisters. We are competing in the living God’s test, and training in the life we have now so that we may wear the crown in the life ahead.

<sup>3</sup> None of the righteous has received their fruit quickly; they wait for it.

<sup>4</sup> For if God paid the reward of the righteous at once, we would immediately be practicing commerce and not godliness; we would seem to be righteous while pursuing not what is devout but what is profitable. For this reason divine judgment harms a spirit[^20-4-1] that is not righteous, and weighs it down with chains.

<sup>5</sup> Glory forever and ever to the one God, who is unseen, the Father of truth, who sent out for us the Savior and originator[^20-5-1] of immortality, through whom he also made known to us the truth and the life of heaven. Amen.[^20-5-2]
