# Ignatius to the Trallians

Chapters: [1](#chapter-1) · [2](#chapter-2) · [3](#chapter-3) · [4](#chapter-4) · [5](#chapter-5) · [6](#chapter-6) · [7](#chapter-7) · [8](#chapter-8) · [9](#chapter-9) · [10](#chapter-10) · [11](#chapter-11) · [12](#chapter-12) · [13](#chapter-13)

<sup>1</sup> Ignatius, who is also called Theophorus[^0-1-1], writes to the holy church that is in Tralles of Asia, beloved of God, the Father of Jesus Christ; a church elect and worthy of God, at peace in flesh and in spirit by the passion of Jesus Christ, who is our hope in rising to be with him. I salute it in the divine fullness[^0-1-2], in the apostolic fashion, and wish it many joys.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> I have learned that your mind is blameless and steady in endurance, not through practice but by nature, as Polybius, your bishop, made plain to me. By the will of God and of Jesus Christ he came to Smyrna and shared my joy, though I am a prisoner in Christ Jesus, so that I saw your whole multitude in him.

<sup>2</sup> So I welcomed your goodwill toward God through him, and I glorified God, since I found you to be what I had been told: imitators of God.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> You appear to me to be living by Jesus Christ rather than by human ways when you obey the bishop as you would Jesus Christ, who died on our behalf, so that, trusting in his death, you may escape death.

<sup>2</sup> So you must, as in fact you do, take no action apart from the bishop. Also be obedient to the presbytery, who stand for the apostles of Jesus Christ, our hope; if we live in him, we will be found in him.

<sup>3</sup> Deacons also, who serve the mysteries of Jesus Christ, must please everyone in every way. They are not servants of food and drink, after all, but assistants of God’s church; so they must keep clear of charges against them as they would of fire.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> In the same way, let everyone treat the deacons with the respect due to Jesus Christ, and the bishop as a model of the Father, and the presbyters as God’s council and the bond of the apostles. Without these, no church can be called by the name.

<sup>2</sup> I am sure that you are like this. What I took from you was a specimen of your love, and I keep it beside me in your bishop. His very manner is a powerful lesson, and his mildness is strength; I consider that even people without God respect him.

<sup>3</sup> Because I love you, I hold back, though I could write more sharply about this. I did not think that I, a condemned man, should order you about like an apostle.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> I have many thoughts in God, but I keep myself within bounds so that I will not perish through boasting. Now I need to be more afraid, and not to pay attention to those who inflate me. Those who speak to me are whipping me.

<sup>2</sup> I do love the idea of suffering, but I cannot tell whether I deserve it. Many people do not see that jealousy[^4-2-1], yet to me it brings a harder fight. So I need mildness, which is what undoes the ruler of this age.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Can I not write you about heavenly matters? I am afraid that, since you are infants[^5-1-1], I would do you harm. Forgive me; otherwise, unable to take it in, you might be choked.

<sup>2</sup> I am in chains, and I can comprehend heavenly things, the locations of the angels, the arrangements of the rulers, things visible and invisible. But that does not make me a disciple yet. Much is lacking in us, so that we may not lack God.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> I urge you, then (it is not I but the love of Jesus Christ), to take only Christian nourishment and to keep away from foreign plants, which is to say heresy[^6-1-1].

<sup>2</sup> Such people weave Jesus Christ into their own teaching to win trust. They are like those who give a deadly drug mixed with honeyed wine; the person who does not know takes it gladly, in a wicked pleasure, and so dies.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> Be on guard against people like that. You will manage it if you are not puffed up and if you stay inseparable from God, Jesus Christ, the bishop, and the ordinances of the apostles.

<sup>2</sup> Those within the sanctuary[^7-2-1] are clean; those outside it are not. Put another way: a person who acts apart from the bishop, presbytery, and deacons does not have a clean conscience.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> I do not say this because I know of anything like it in you. I am keeping watch over you in advance, as people I love, since I can see the devil’s ambushes coming. So put on mildness, and rebuild yourselves in faith, which is the Lord’s flesh[^8-1-1], and in love, which is the blood of Jesus Christ.

<sup>2</sup> Let none of you hold anything against a neighbor. Give the gentiles no openings, so that the community in God is not slandered because of a few foolish people. For “Woe to the one through whom my name is slandered[^8-2-1] in vain among some.”

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> So stop up your ears if anyone talks to you without mentioning Jesus Christ, who descended from David and was Mary’s son, who was truly born, who ate and drank, who was truly hunted down in the time of Pontius Pilate and truly crucified, and who died as beings in heaven, on earth, and below the earth looked on;

<sup>2</sup> he was also truly raised from the dead, since his Father raised him. In the same likeness, his Father will raise us who believe in him, in Christ Jesus, apart from whom we have no true life.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> But suppose, as some say who are godless, that is, unbelieving, that he only seemed to suffer (it is they who only seem to exist). Then why am I in chains? Why do I even pray to fight with wild animals? In that case I am dying for nothing, and I am slandering the Lord.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> Run away from these bad shoots; they bear fruit that brings death, and whoever tastes it dies at once. They are not something the Father has planted[^11-1-1]. If they were, they would be seen to be branches of the cross[^11-1-2], and their fruit would never decay. By means of the cross, in his suffering, he invites you, who are his members. And a head cannot come into being by itself, apart from the members; God promises union, and that is himself.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> I greet you from Smyrna, along with the churches of God that are here with me. They have refreshed me in every way, in flesh and in spirit.

<sup>2</sup> My chains, which I wear for Jesus Christ’s sake while begging to attain God, call on you to keep up your unity and your prayers for each other. Each of you ought to put new life into the bishop, and the presbyters most of all, to the credit of the Father, of Jesus Christ, and of the apostles.

<sup>3</sup> I pray that you will listen to me in love, so that I will not be a witness against you through having written. Pray for me too. In God’s mercy I need your love, to be counted worthy of the lot that I am about to obtain, so that I may not be found unqualified.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> The love of the people of Smyrna and Ephesus sends you greetings. In your prayers keep in mind the church of Syria, a church whose name I do not deserve to bear, as the least of its members.

<sup>2</sup> Farewell in Jesus Christ. Submit to the bishop as to the commandment, and likewise to the presbytery. And each one of you, love one another with an undivided heart.

<sup>3</sup> Not only now but also when I attain God, my spirit is offered in purity for you. I am still at risk; but the Father is faithful in Jesus Christ to grant what I ask and what you ask, and in him may you be found blameless.
