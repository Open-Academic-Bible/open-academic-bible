# Acts of Peter

A second-century account of Peter's contest with Simon Magus in Rome and of his death. It contains the story in which Peter, leaving Rome, meets the Lord and asks "Where are you going?" (Quo vadis?), and the tradition that Peter was crucified head downward. Most of it survives in a Latin manuscript at Vercelli; the account of Peter's death survives in Greek.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> While Paul was staying in Rome and strengthening many people in the faith, it happened that Candida[^1-1-1], the wife of Quartus, who was in charge of the prisons[^1-1-2], listened to Paul, took his words to heart, and believed. When she had taught her husband too, and he had come to believe, Quartus allowed Paul to leave the city and go wherever he wished. Paul said to him, “If it is God’s will, he himself will show me.” After fasting for three days and asking the Lord for what would suit him best, Paul had a vision. The Lord said to him, “Paul, get up, and be a doctor with your own body[^1-1-3] to those who are in Spain.” He told the brothers and sisters what God had commanded, and with no hesitation he set about leaving the city. But as soon as Paul began to go, there was great weeping among the whole community, because they thought they would never see Paul again, and they even tore their clothes. They also remembered how often Paul had argued with the teachers of the Jews and defeated them, saying, “Christ, on whom your ancestors laid their hands, was doing away with their sabbath, their fasts, their festivals, and circumcision, along with human teachings and the other traditions.” The brothers and sisters kept begging Paul, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, to be away no longer than a year. They said, “We know your love for the community; do not forget us once you have arrived there, and do not begin to leave us like children without a mother.” While they were still pleading with him in tears, a sound came from heaven and a great voice said, “Paul, God’s servant, has been chosen for ministry for the rest of his life. By the hands of Nero, a lawless and wicked man, he will be brought to his end before your eyes.” The voice had come from heaven, and the believers were all the more strengthened.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> They brought Paul an offering of bread and water, so that he could pray over it and give a portion to each person. Among them was a woman named Rufina, who also wanted to receive the Eucharist from Paul’s hand. Paul, filled with the Spirit, said as she came forward, “Rufina, you are not coming to God’s altar as one who is worthy. You rise from the side of a man who is not your husband but an adulterer, and you are trying to take God’s Eucharist. Look, Satan will trouble your heart and expose you before the eyes of everyone who believes in the Lord, so that those who see and believe may know that they have put their faith in the living God, who searches hearts[^2-1-1]. If you repent of what you have done, he is faithful and able to wipe out your sins and set you free from this sin. But if you do not repent, then while you are still in the body, a devouring fire will take you, and the outer darkness for all ages.” At once she fell down, paralyzed from her head to the soles of her feet, and she could not speak, for her tongue was tied. When the believers and the new converts saw this, they beat their chests, remembered their earlier sins, and wept, saying, “We do not know whether the Lord will forgive the sins we committed in the past.” Then Paul asked for silence and said, “People who have only now begun to believe in Christ, if you do not hold fast to your former deeds and your ancestral tradition, and if you keep away from all deceit, anger, cruelty, adultery, and defilement, and from pride, jealousy, scorn, and hatred, then Jesus, the living God, will forgive you what you did in ignorance. So, servants of God, arm yourselves, each one, in your inner person[^2-1-2], with peace, patience, gentleness, faith, love, knowledge, wisdom, affection for the community, hospitality, mercy, self-control, purity, goodness, and righteousness. Then you will have as your leader for ever the firstborn of all creation[^2-1-3], and you will have power, in peace, with our Lord.” When they had heard this from Paul, they asked him to pray for them. Paul raised his voice and said, “Eternal God, God of the heavens, God of a majesty beyond words, you who have made all things firm by your word, who have laid the bond of your grace on every age, Father of your holy Son Jesus Christ, we ask you together, through your Son Jesus Christ, to strengthen the souls that once could not believe but now do. I was once a blasphemer[^2-1-4], and now I am blasphemed. I was once a persecutor, and now I suffer persecution from others. I was once Christ’s enemy, and now I pray to be his friend. I trust in his promise and in his mercy; I believe I am faithful and that I have received forgiveness for my earlier offenses. So I urge you too, brothers and sisters, to believe in the Lord, the almighty Father, and to put all your hope in our Lord Jesus Christ, his Son. No one will be able to tear those who believe away from his promise. Kneel together and commend me to the Lord as I set out toward another people, so that his grace may go ahead of me and arrange my journey well, and so that he may receive his holy and faithful vessels, who give thanks as I proclaim the Lord’s word, and that they may be firmly grounded.” The brothers and sisters, weeping for a long time and praying to the Lord with Paul, said, “Lord Jesus Christ, be with Paul, and bring him back to us whole. We know our weakness, which is still in us.”

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> While the people were praying, a great crowd of women knelt and begged the blessed Paul, and they kissed his feet and escorted him to the harbor. Dionysius and Balbus from Asia, Roman knights and distinguished men, were there, and so was a senator named Demetrius, who stayed close to Paul on his right and said, “Paul, I would leave the city and never part from you, if I were not a magistrate.” From the household of Caesar came Cleobius, Ifitus, Lysimachus, and Aristeus, and two women, Berenice and Philostrate, with the presbyter Narcissus[^3-1-1]. After they had escorted him to the harbor, a storm at sea threatened, so Narcissus sent the brothers and sisters back to Rome, so that anyone who wished could come down and listen to Paul until he sailed. When they heard this, the brothers and sisters went up into the city. Those who had stayed in the city were told, and soon the news had spread. Some came on pack animals, others on foot, others down the Tiber to the harbor. They stood firm in their faith for three days, and on the fourth day until the fifth hour they prayed together with Paul and made their offering[^3-1-2]. They loaded everything the voyage needed onto the ship and gave him two faithful young men to sail with him. Then they said farewell to him in the Lord and went back to Rome.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> A few days later a great disturbance broke out in the middle of the church. People were saying that they had seen marvels done by a man named Simon, who was from Aricia[^4-1-1]. Some added that he called himself a great power of God[^4-1-2] and that he did nothing without God. “Is he himself the Christ? But we believe in the one Paul preached to us. Through him we have seen the dead raised and people set free from all sorts of illnesses. This man is looking for a contest, we know that, for it was no small stir that he caused among us. Perhaps he will soon be coming to Rome.” The next day the crowd begged him with loud shouts, saying, “You are a god in Italy, you are the savior of the Romans. Hurry to Rome!” He spoke to the people in a thin voice and said, “Tomorrow, around the seventh hour, you will see me flying over the gate of the city, looking just as you see me now while I talk with you.” So, brothers and sisters, if you agree, let us go and watch more closely how this turns out. Everyone ran together and reached the harbor gate[^4-1-3]. When the seventh hour came, suddenly a cloud of dust was seen in the sky far away, like smoke, flashing with rays and flame. When it reached the gate, he suddenly disappeared. Afterward he appeared standing among the people. They all worshiped him, recognizing that he was the one they had seen the day before. Not a few of the believers stumbled[^4-1-4] over each other, especially because Paul was not in Rome, nor Timothy or Barnabas, who had been sent by Paul to Macedonia, and there was no one to strengthen them, least of all the recent converts. Simon grew more and more proud of what he did, and some of the people began daily to call Paul a magician, others a deceiver. A great multitude that had been established in the faith was scattered completely, except for the presbyter Narcissus[^4-1-5] and two women in the lodging of the Bithynians, and four others who stayed at home. These prayed and begged the Lord that Paul might return quickly, or that anyone else might come to visit his servants, because the devil was dissolving them with his wickedness.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> While they mourned and fasted, God was by then readying Peter for what lay ahead, in Jerusalem. When the twelve years that the Lord had commanded him were complete, Christ showed him a vision. He said, “Peter, the magician Simon, whom you drove out of Judea after proving him false, has gotten ahead of you in Rome. And you should know this at once: everyone who trusted in me he is undoing with his cunning and the power of Satan, by whose strength he shows that he is what he is. But do not delay. Set out tomorrow, and there you will come upon a ship ready and sailing for Italy. In a few days I will let you see my grace, which has no envy in it.” Warned by this vision, Peter told the brothers and sisters at once. He said, “It is necessary for me to go up to Rome to fight the enemy and adversary of the Lord and also of our community.” He descended to Caesarea[^5-1-1] and boarded a ship, though the ladder had already been pulled up, and he had no provisions with him. The captain, whose name was Theon, gave Peter a long look and said, “Everything we have is yours. What kindness would it be if we took on board a man just like us, in a difficult situation, and did not share all we have with you? Only let us sail safely.” Peter thanked him for the offer, but he himself fasted on the ship, sad at heart, yet again taking comfort that God had counted him worthy to serve him. After a few days the captain rose at mealtime and invited Peter to eat with him. He said, “Whoever you are, you are almost a stranger to me, but I take you for a servant of God. At midnight, as I stood at the helm, I heard a man’s voice from heaven calling, ‘Theon, Theon,’ twice. Then it said, ‘Of all those who sail with you, honor Peter above the rest. Through him you and the others will be saved without any harm, by a course you did not hope for.’” Peter believed that God wanted to show his care at sea to the people on the ship, and from then on he began to tell Theon about God’s great deeds, how the Lord had picked him out among the apostles, and what task had him sailing to Italy. Every day he shared the words of God with him. Looking at him, and seeing how he lived, Peter recognized that Theon shared his convictions in the faith, and was a worthy servant. When the ship lay becalmed in the Adriatic, Theon drew Peter’s attention to the calm and said, “If you judge me worthy of being marked with the Lord’s sign[^5-1-2], you have your chance now.” Everyone else on board had fallen asleep, drunk. Peter climbed down by a rope and baptized Theon into the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Theon came up from the water filled with joy, and Peter was glad too, because God had counted Theon fit to bear his name. When Theon had been baptized, a young man appeared in the same place, radiant and beautiful, saying to them, “Peace to you.” Peter and Theon at once climbed up and entered the cabin[^5-1-3]. Peter took bread and thanked the Lord, who had counted him worthy of this holy ministry and had let the young man appear to them with the greeting “Peace be with you.” He said, “You, the best and only holy one, you have appeared to us, God Jesus Christ. By your name this man has now been washed and sealed with your holy seal. So in your name I share your Eucharist with him, so that he may be your servant, perfected and without blame for ever.” While they feasted and rejoiced in the Lord, all at once a wind came up, not violent but steady, and it did not stop for six days and as many nights, until they reached Puteoli.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> When they put in at Puteoli[^6-1-1], Theon jumped from the ship and went to the lodging where he usually stayed, to get things ready for Peter’s arrival. His host was a man named Ariston, who always feared the Lord, and Theon had put himself in his care for the sake of the Name[^6-1-2]. When he reached the lodging and saw Ariston, Theon said to him, “God, who counted you worthy to serve him, has shared his grace with me too, through his holy servant Peter. He has sailed with me from Judea, commanded by our Lord to come to Italy.” When Ariston heard this, he threw himself on Theon’s neck, embraced him, and begged him to take him to the ship and show him Peter. He said, “Since Paul left for Spain, there has been no one among the brothers and sisters to give me rest. And a certain Jew has forced his way into the city, a man named Simon, and with his magic spells and his wickedness he has broken up the whole community here and there, so that even I fled from Rome, hoping that Peter would come. Paul had told us about him, and I myself saw much of him in a vision. Now I believe in my Lord that he is rebuilding his ministry, because every deception will be rooted out from his servants. Our Lord Jesus Christ is faithful, and able to restore our minds.” Hearing this from Ariston as he wept, Theon grew even stronger in spirit and more firmly grounded, because he understood that he had come to trust the living God. When they came together at the ship, Peter looked at them, and Ariston said, “You who share the holy mysteries and show the right way that is in the Lord Jesus Christ our God, who has shown us his coming through you: we have let go of everyone Paul handed over to us[^6-1-3], since he told us to expect you, because he counted us worthy to see his great and wonderful works through you. I beg you, then, hurry to the city. I left behind the brothers and sisters who had stumbled, whom I saw falling in the devil’s testing, and I fled here, telling them, ‘Within these months the mercy of our Lord will bring you his servant.’ For I had seen Paul in a vision, saying to me, ‘Ariston, flee the city.’ When I heard that, I believed at once and set out in the Lord, though I carry weak flesh. Day by day I stood on the shore and asked the sailors whether Peter had sailed with them. Now, with the grace of the Lord overflowing, I ask that we go up to Rome without any delay, so that the teaching of that wicked man does not gain more ground.” As Ariston said this with tears, Peter gave him his hand and raised him up. He said, “The one who tempts the whole world through his angels[^6-1-4] has got ahead of us. But he will put out Simon’s deceptions and place under the feet of those who believe in Christ, whom we proclaim, the one who has power to rescue his servants from every testing.” Theon said, “You never rested a single day on the ship in so great a sea, and now, after so rough a journey, do you want to set out straight from the ship? Stay and refresh yourself, and then go. From here to Rome the road is paved with flint, and I fear you may suffer from the jolting.” Peter answered them, “What if a millstone were hung around my neck[^6-1-5], as my Lord used to tell us about anyone who made one of the brothers stumble, and I were thrown into the depths? It will not be only a millstone, but something worse, far from those who have believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, if this persecutor of his servants has his way[^6-1-6].” Theon could not persuade him by any argument to stay even one day. Theon himself gave orders about everything on the ship, to be handled as best served his interests, and followed Peter to Rome, leading Ariston to the house of the presbyter Narcissus[^6-1-7].

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> The report spread through the city to the scattered brothers and sisters that Peter had come because of Simon, to expose him as a deceiver and a persecutor of good people. So the whole multitude ran together to catch sight of the Lord’s apostle, who would steady them in Christ. On the week’s first day[^7-1-1], when the crowd had gathered to see Peter, he began to say in a very loud voice, “People here who hope in Christ, you who for a short time have been through testing, learn the reason God sent his Son into the world and the reason he had him born of the virgin Mary. Would he have done it if it did not bring some grace or provision? He wished to remove every stumbling block, every ignorance, and every working of the devil, his first attempts and the strength by which he used to prevail before our God shone out in the world. People fell into death through ignorance, in many different illnesses, and almighty God, moved to pity, dispatched his Son to the world. I was beside him, and I walked on the water[^7-1-2]. I remain a witness of that, and I testify that he did all the signs and wonders that he did then. I admit it, dearest brothers and sisters: I was with him, and yet I denied him[^7-1-3], our Lord Jesus Christ, not just once but three times. For there were vicious dogs[^7-1-4] who surrounded me, as they had surrounded the Lord’s prophets. Yet the Lord did not count it against me. He had pity on my fleshly weakness, so that afterward I wept bitterly and mourned how weak my faith had been, since the devil had made a fool of me and I did not remember my Lord’s word. And now I say to you, people gathered under the name of Jesus Christ, that Satan has drawn his arrows against you, to make you stray from the way. But do not give up, brothers and sisters, and do not lose heart. Be strong, stand firm, and do not doubt. If Satan made me stumble, though the Lord held me in the greatest honor, so that I renounced the light of my hope[^7-1-5] and was driven to run as though I trusted a human being, what do you suppose he will do to you, who are newcomers? Did you imagine he would not try to overturn you, to turn you into enemies of God’s kingdom and hurl you into ruin by one last deception? Whoever he drives away from the hope of our Lord Jesus is a child of destruction[^7-1-6] for ever. So turn back, brothers and sisters chosen by the Lord, and be strengthened in the Lord almighty, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the one whom nobody has ever seen[^7-1-7] or can see, except the one who has believed in him. Understand where the testing has come from. I do not ask you to look to Christ, whom I proclaim, only through words; by deeds too, and by acts of great power, I urge you in the faith that is in Jesus, that none of you expect anyone else apart from this man who was despised and insulted by the Jews, this Nazarene who was crucified, died, and rose on the third day.”

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> The brothers and sisters repented and asked Peter to fight Simon, who claimed to be the power of God[^8-1-1]. He was staying in the house of the senator Marcellus, whom he had won over with his spells. They said, “Believe us, brother Peter[^8-1-2]: no one among human beings was ever wiser than this Marcellus. All the widows who hoped in Christ took refuge with him, and all the orphans were fed by him. What more can I say, brother? All the poor called Marcellus their patron, and his house had the name of a house for strangers and the poor. The emperor said to him, ‘I am keeping you away from every office, so that you will not strip the provinces to give gifts to the Christians.’ Marcellus answered, ‘What is mine is yours too.’ The emperor told him, ‘It would be mine if you kept it for me, but it is not mine, because you give it to whomever you please, and I do not know to what lowly people.’ We lay this before your eyes, brother Peter, to tell you how a man’s great mercy has been turned into blasphemy. Had that man not been led astray, we too would not have been separated from the holy faith of God our Lord. Now Marcellus is raging and regrets his generosity. He says, ‘I spent so much of my wealth for so long, believing, to no purpose, that I was giving it for knowledge of God!’ Any stranger who comes to the door of his house he beats with a stick and has thrown out, saying, ‘I wish I had not spent so much money on these impostors!’ He says even more in his blasphemy. But if any mercy of our Lord is in you, and any of the kindness that he commanded, help this man who gave alms to God’s servants in such great numbers.” When Peter saw this, he was struck with bitter grief and said, “What deceitful arts and testings the devil has! What plots and inventions of evil people! He stores up for himself a huge fire for the day of wrath[^8-1-3], he destroys simple people, he is a grasping wolf[^8-1-4], devouring and squandering eternal life! You entangled the first human being[^8-1-5] in desire and bound him with your ancient wickedness and with the chain of the body. You are the fruit of the tree of bitterness, wholly bitter, who sends all kinds of desires upon people. You forced Judas, my fellow disciple and fellow apostle, to act impiously and betray our Lord Jesus Christ, who will exact the penalty from you. You hardened the heart of Herod, and of Pharaoh, whom you set against Moses, the servant of God. You gave Caiaphas the nerve to hand our Lord Jesus Christ over to a lawless crowd[^8-1-6], and even now you shoot your poisoned arrows at innocent souls. Wicked one, enemy of everyone, be cursed away from the church of the Son of the holy almighty God, and like a brand thrown from the fire may you be put out by the servants of our Lord Jesus Christ. Let your blackness fall back on you and on your children, an evil seed. Let your wickedness and your threats turn back on you, and on you and your angels let your testings fall, beginning of malice, bottomless pit of darkness! Keep the darkness you have, for yourself and for the vessels you own. Leave those who are going to believe in God. Leave Christ’s servants and those who wish to serve in his army. Keep your garments of darkness for yourself. You knock for no reason at other people’s doors, for they are not yours but belong to Christ Jesus, who watches over them. You, grasping wolf, want to snatch sheep that are not yours but belong to Christ Jesus, who guards them with the greatest care.”

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> While Peter spoke with great sorrow of heart, many more were added to those who believed in the Lord. The brothers and sisters begged Peter to take on Simon and not let him go on troubling the people. Without delay Peter hurried out of the assembly and went to Marcellus’s house, where Simon was staying. A great crowd followed him. When he came to the door, he called the doorkeeper and said, “Tell Simon: ‘Peter, the one you fled from in Judea, is waiting for you at the door.’” The doorkeeper answered Peter, “Sir, I do not know whether you are Peter. I have my orders. He found out yesterday that you had entered the city, and he told me, ‘Whether it is day or night, at whatever hour he comes, say that I am not in.’” Peter said to the young man[^9-1-1], “You have done well to report what he forced you to say.” Then Peter turned to the people following him and said, “You are about to see something great and marvelous.” Peter looked around and saw a huge dog tied up with a heavy chain. He went over and untied it. As soon as it was loose, the dog received a human voice and said to Peter, “What do you order me to do, servant of the living God, whose name cannot be told?” Peter said to it, “Go in and say to Simon, in the middle of his gathering, ‘Peter says to you: come out in public. I came to Rome because of you, you scoundrel, who lead simple souls astray.’” At once the dog ran in, burst into the middle of the people with Simon, reared up on its front paws, and said in a loud voice, “Simon, Peter, the servant of Christ, who is standing at the door, says to you: ‘Come out in public. I came to Rome because of you, you worst of scoundrels, who deceive simple souls.’” When Simon heard this, and saw the incredible sight, the words he had used to deceive the bystanders left him, and they were all stunned.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> When Marcellus saw this, he went out to the door, threw himself at Peter’s feet, and said, “Peter, I embrace your feet, holy servant of the holy God. I have sinned much. If there is any true faith in you for the Christ whom you proclaim, if you remember his commandments, to hate no one and be unkind to no one, as I learned from Paul, your fellow apostle, then do not take my offenses into your heart, but pray for me to the Lord, the holy Son of God, whom I provoked to anger by persecuting his servants. Ask for me as a good steward of God[^10-1-1], so that he will not hand me over with Simon’s sins to eternal fire. Simon persuaded me to put up a statue to him, with the inscription ‘To Simon, the young god[^10-1-2].’ If I had known, Peter, that money could win him over, I would have given you all I have, with contempt for it, to save my soul. If I had children, I would have counted them as nothing, if only I could believe in the living Lord. I admit that he would not have deceived me, except that he said he was God’s power. But let me tell you, dearest Peter: I was not worthy to hear you, servant of God, and I was not yet established in faith in God that is in Christ, and that is why I stumbled. So I ask you not to be angry at what I am about to say. Our Lord, whom you proclaim in truth, said to your fellow apostles in your presence, ‘If you have faith like a grain of mustard seed[^10-1-3],’ and so on. And this Simon called Peter faithless, doubting in the waters[^10-1-4]. They understood. So if you, on whom he laid his hands, whom he chose, with whom he did marvels, were doubting, then I too, with this testimony in hand, repent and take refuge in your prayers. Receive my soul, which has fallen away from our Lord and from his promise. He is almighty and can forgive my sins.” Peter said in a loud voice, “To you, our Lord, be glory and splendor, almighty God, Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. To you be praise, glory, and honor for ever and ever. Amen. Holy Lord, in the sight of all who see, strengthen Marcellus and send your peace on him and on his house today. Whatever has been lost or has gone astray, you alone can turn them all back. We pray to you, Lord, shepherd of sheep long scattered[^10-1-5]: now receive one of your little sheep, and do not let him go on raving in error and ignorance any longer, but take him into the number of your flock. Yes, Lord, receive him, as he asks you with grief and tears.”

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> While Peter said this, and Marcellus embraced him, Peter turned to the crowd standing beside him and saw a young man in the crowd laughing, who had a very evil demon[^11-1-1] in him. Peter told him, “Whoever you may be who laughed, show yourself in public, in front of everyone standing here.” At this the young man flung himself against the wall and said, “Peter, a great quarrel is going on involving Simon and the dog you sent. Simon is telling the dog, ‘Deny that I am here.’ The dog says to him much more than you commanded. When it has finished the mystery you set for it, it will drop dead at your feet.” Peter said, “And you, whatever demon you are, come out of the young man by the authority of our Lord Jesus Christ, and do him no harm. Show yourself to everyone standing here.” As soon as the young man heard it, the demon went out of him. He seized a large marble statue that was standing in the house’s courtyard and smashed it to pieces with his heels. The statue was of Caesar. Marcellus saw this, struck his forehead, and told Peter, “A terrible crime has been committed. If one of the informers[^11-1-2] reports it to Caesar, he will inflict heavy penalties on us.” Peter said to him, “I do not see you now as you were a little while ago. You told me you were prepared to give away all your wealth to save your soul. Yet if you truly repent, and trust in Christ with your whole heart, take running water[^11-1-3] in your hands, pray to the Lord, and in his name sprinkle it over the broken pieces of the statue, and it will be intact as it was before.” Marcellus did not hesitate. He trusted with his whole heart, and before he took the water he looked up, with his hands raised, and said, “I trust in you, Lord Jesus Christ. Your apostle Peter is testing whether I believe rightly in your holy name. So I scoop up water in my hands and in your name sprinkle these stones, so that the statue may be whole as it was before. If it is your wish, Lord, that I stay in the body and suffer nothing from Caesar, let this stone be whole as it was before.” He splashed water on the stones, and the statue became whole. Peter gloried that Marcellus had not doubted in asking the Lord, and Marcellus was glad in the Spirit that such a sign, the first, had been done by his own hands. He believed from the depths of his heart in the name of Jesus Christ, God’s Son, through whom all impossible things are possible.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> But Simon, inside, said to the dog, “Tell Peter that I am not in.” The dog answered him in front of Marcellus: “You shameless wretch, worst enemy of all living creatures and of all who believe in Christ Jesus! Here is a mute animal, sent to you, which has received a human voice to accuse you and prove you a deceiver and a cheat. Have you been thinking for so long just to say, ‘Say that I am not in’? You think you can hide from the one who commanded me to speak against you to your face, and not for your sake but for the sake of those you were deceiving and sending to ruin? You will be cursed, then, you enemy and corrupter of the way of Christ’s truth, and you will face in the fire that never dies[^12-1-1] and in the outer darkness[^12-1-2] the evils that you have done.” After saying this, the dog went out, and the people followed it, leaving Simon alone. The dog came to Peter, who was sitting with the crowd that had come to see his face, and told the true angel and apostle of God[^12-1-3] what it had done: “Peter, you will have a great contest. You will convert many to the faith whom he has led astray. For this you will receive a reward from God for your work.” When the dog had said this, it fell at the apostle’s feet and died. When the great crowd saw the dog speaking, they were astonished. Some threw themselves at Peter’s feet, others said, “Show us another sign, so that we may believe in you as a servant of the living God. For Simon, too, did many signs in our presence.”

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> Peter turned and saw a smoked fish[^13-1-1] hanging in a window. He took it and said to the people, “If you see this fish now swimming in water like a living one, will you be able to believe in the one I proclaim?” They answered with one voice, “In your name, Jesus Christ, as long as there is still no faith, let it live before all of us and swim like a fish.” He threw the fish into a pool, and it came alive and began to swim. When the crowd saw the fish swimming, Peter did not pray only for the sake of seeming to work a phantom, but made it swim for a good while, so that it would draw crowds from all sides and show that the fish had been made alive. One person in the crowd even tossed bread to it, and they saw it whole. Many followed after seeing this and believed in the Lord, and they gathered by day and night in the house of the presbyter Narcissus[^13-1-2]. Peter explained the prophetic scriptures to them, and what our Lord Jesus Christ had done in word and in deed.

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> Day by day Marcellus grew firmer through the signs he saw done through Peter by the grace of Jesus Christ, which Christ had given him. Marcellus attacked Simon in his own house, where Simon was sitting in the dining room. He cursed him and said, “Worst enemy and plague of humankind, corrupter of my soul and my house, you who wanted to drive me away from Christ my Lord and savior!” He laid hands on him and ordered him thrown out of his house. The slaves, now given the power, abused him in every way. Some slapped his face, some beat him with a stick, some threw stones, and some emptied pots full of filth[^14-1-1] over his head, slaves who for his sake had earlier been bound for a long time because he had spoken evil of them to their master. Other fellow slaves taunted him: “Now, by God’s will, we pay you back what you deserve, because he has had mercy on us and on our master.” Simon, beaten up and driven from the house, ran to the house where Peter was staying. He stood at the door of the presbyter Narcissus[^14-1-2] and shouted, “Here I am, Simon. Come down, Peter, and I will show that you believed in a Jewish man, the son of a carpenter[^14-1-3].”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> When Peter was told that Simon had said this, he sent a woman with a nursing baby to him. He told her, “Go down quickly and you will find someone looking for me. You have no need to answer. Keep silent and listen to what the baby you are holding says to him.” The woman went down. The baby she was nursing was seven months old. It received a human voice and said to Simon, “You horror to God and to people, you destroyer of truth and of nature! In a short, a very short time you will be shown up, and after that eternal punishment awaits you. Born of a shameless father, you are a fruit who never grows roots for good, only for poison. Faithless race, left without every hope! You were not shamed when a dog proved you wrong. I, an infant, am compelled by God to speak, and even so you do not blush. But against your will, when the sabbath comes[^15-1-1], another will bring you into the Forum of Julius[^15-1-2], to prove what kind of man you are. So leave the door. Keep away from the innocent souls whom you were overturning and grieving in Christ. Your vile nature will be put on display, and your scheming cut short. Now I speak a final word: Jesus Christ says to you, ‘Be silent, silenced in my name, and leave Rome until the sabbath.’” Immediately he was struck dumb and left Rome until the sabbath, and stayed in a stable. The woman went back with the baby to Peter and reported to him and the other brothers and sisters what the baby had said to Simon. They glorified the Lord, who had shown these things to humankind.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> When night came, Peter saw Jesus, still awake, clothed in a garment of radiance, smiling, and saying to him, “Already a large crowd from the community has been brought back through you, and through the signs you have done in my name. When the sabbath comes you will have a contest of faith, and many more gentiles will be converted, and many Jews[^16-1-1], to me who was spat on[^16-1-2]. I will present myself to you whenever you ask me for signs and wonders, and you will convert many. But you will face opposition from the spells and magic inventions of Simon. So do not stop now, and strengthen in my name those I send to you.” When it was day, Peter told the brothers and sisters that the Lord had appeared to him and what he had commanded.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> “Believe me, brothers and sisters: I drove this Simon out of Judea, where he did a great deal of harm with his magic spells. He was staying in Judea with a woman named Eubola[^17-1-1], a person of great standing in this world, who had plenty of gold and pearls of no small value. Simon slipped in with two others like himself. No one in the household saw those two, only Simon. By magic they made off with every bit of the woman’s gold and vanished. When Eubola found out what had happened, she began to torture her household, saying, ‘You have used this man of God as your chance to rob me, because you saw him come in to me to pay respect to an unworldly woman. His name is similar to the Lord’s.’ I fasted for three days, praying that the matter would be brought into the open. I saw in a vision Italicus and Antulus[^17-1-2], men I had taught in the Lord’s name, and a naked boy in chains who gave me a loaf of fine wheat bread and said, ‘Peter, wait two more days, and you will witness God’s great deeds. Everything that vanished from Eubola’s house, Simon took by magic. He worked an illusion and made off with it together with two others. On the third day, about the ninth hour, you will spot them at the gate that leads to Naples, selling to a goldsmith named Agrippinus a golden figure of a satyr[^17-1-3] weighing two pounds, with a precious stone set in it. You do not need to touch it, so that you will not be defiled. But have some of the lady’s slaves with you, show them the goldsmith’s shop, and learn from them. Because of this many will believe in the Lord’s name, and what these men have often stolen by their cunning and malice will be proved in public.’ On hearing this, I went to Eubola and found her mourning, with her clothes torn. I said to her, ‘Eubola, get up from your grief, fix your face, tie up your hair, put on clothing that suits you, and pray to Jesus Christ the Lord, who judges every soul. He is the invisible Son of God, and through him salvation must come to you, if you truly repent with your whole heart of your earlier sins. Receive power from him. Look, the Lord says to you through me: you will find everything you have lost. And when you have received it, make sure he finds you, so that you can renounce this present world and seek eternal rest. So listen: have some of your people watch the gate that leads to Naples on the day after tomorrow, around the ninth hour. They will see two young men carrying a golden satyr set with stones, as the vision showed me, offering it to a buyer, a man named Agrippinus, who belongs to the household of godliness[^17-1-4] and of the faith that is in our Lord Jesus Christ. Through that man it will be made clear to you that you ought to trust the living God, not Simon the magician, the unstable devil, who wanted you tormented and your innocent household with you. With smooth speech he deceived you, talking only with his mouth about reverence for God, though he himself is full of irreverence. When you thought you were going to celebrate a joyful day, and you set up an idol and veiled it, and set out all your jewelry on a table, he brought in two young men that nobody in your household saw, by a magic spell, and they carried off your jewelry and were never seen again. But his scheme had no place to take hold. My God has made it plain to me, so that you would not be deceived or perish in Gehenna[^17-1-5] for the things you did against God in ungodliness. God is full of all truth and just to human beings, and apart from the one through whom what you lost was saved for you, there is no help. Now win your own soul.’ She threw herself down before my feet and cried, ‘Sir, I do not know who you are. I took him to be a servant of God, I gave him a part in looking after the poor, and I gave much through his hands, and much to him himself. What harm did I do him, that he would plot so much against my house?’ Peter said to her, ‘Trust is not to be given to words[^17-1-6] but to works and deeds.’ I took two of Eubola’s stewards and called on Agrippinus and said to him, ‘Be sure you recognize these men. Tomorrow two young men will come to you, wanting to sell you a gold satyr set with gems that belongs to their mistress. You will accept the offer when they come, and God will bring the rest to the test.’ The next day the lady’s stewards came around the ninth hour, and the young men came too, wanting to sell the golden satyr to Agrippinus. They were arrested at once, and the lady was informed. She went in distress to the governor and told him loudly what had happened to her. When the governor Pompeius[^17-1-7] saw the woman in such distress, who had never before appeared in public, he got up from the judgment seat at once, went into the headquarters[^17-1-8], and ordered the men brought in and questioned under torture. Under torture they admitted that they had served Simon, ‘who led us on with silver coins.’ Questioned longer, they said that everything Eubola had lost was hidden underground in a cave, and much else besides. When Pompeius heard that, he rose to go to the gate with the men, since they were slow in coming. He saw a large crowd approaching, with the two men in chains. Simon understood at once and ran away, and he has not appeared in Judea since. After Eubola had gotten back everything that was hers, she gave it to look after the poor. She came to believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, took comfort, and scorned and renounced this world. She gave to widows and orphans and dressed the poor, and after many years she fell asleep. These things, dearest brothers and sisters, took place in Judea, and so the angel of Satan[^17-1-9], as he is called, was driven out of there.”

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> “Dearest and most beloved brothers and sisters, let us fast together and pray to the Lord. The one who drove him out of there is able to root him out of here too. May he give us strength to resist him and show that he is Satan. For on the sabbath our Lord will bring him, against his will, into the Forum of Julius[^18-1-1]. So let us kneel to Christ, who hears us even if we do not call out. He sees us, though he is not seen with these eyes, but he is in us. If we are willing, he will not leave us. Let us cleanse our souls of every wicked temptation, and God will not depart from us. Even if we only nod with our eyes, he is there beside us.”

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> After Peter had said this, Marcellus came in and said, “I have cleaned my whole house of the traces of Simon and rooted out every bit of his wicked dust. I took water and called on the holy name of Jesus Christ, along with the other servants who belong to him, and sprinkled my whole house, all the dining rooms and every porch, as far as the outer door, and I said: ‘Lord Jesus Christ, I know that you are clean and untouched by any impurity. Let my enemy and adversary be driven from your sight.’ And now, blessed Peter, I have ordered the widows and the elders[^19-1-1] to come together at my house, which is open to all, to pray with us. Each of them will receive a coin[^19-1-2] in the name of the service, so that they may truly be called servants of Christ. Everything else for the service has been prepared. So I ask you, most blessed Peter, to join in their prayers, so that you may also enrich their prayers on my behalf. Let us go, then, and take Narcissus along, and whatever brothers and sisters are here.” Peter yielded to his simple wish and went out with him and the other believers.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> Peter went in and saw one of the older women, a widow who was blind, with her daughter who was giving her an arm and leading her into Marcellus’s house. Peter told her, “Come here, mother. From now on we have an inaccessible light that no darkness covers, and he says to you through me, ‘Open your eyes and see, and walk by yourself.’” At once the widow could see, and she saw Peter laying his hand on her. Peter went into the dining room and saw that someone was reading the gospel. He rolled up the scroll and said, “You who hope in Christ, learn how the holy scripture of our Lord ought to be proclaimed. By his grace we wrote down what we had begun, and though it may still seem weak to you, we wrote what human flesh can take in and carry. So first we must know God’s will and goodness. Long ago deception had flooded the world and was plunging many thousands of people into ruin, and the Lord, moved to show his mercy, appeared in another form and in the likeness of a human being, since neither the Jews nor even we were fit to be enlightened. Each of us saw as much as he was able to take in. Now I will expound for you what has just been read. Our Lord wanted me to see his majesty on the mountain[^20-1-1], along with the sons of Zebedee. I fell down as if I were dead, and shut my eyes, and heard a voice from him such as I cannot describe. I thought I had been blinded by his splendor. When I had caught my breath a little, I said to myself, ‘Maybe my Lord has brought me up here to blind me.’ And I said, ‘If this is your will, Lord, I do not resist.’ He took my hand and raised me. When I stood up, I saw him once more in the form that I could bear. So, dearest brothers and sisters, the merciful God carried our weaknesses and bore our failings, as the prophet said: ‘He bears our sins[^20-1-2] and suffers for us, while we thought he was in pain and held in the grip of wounds.’ For he is within the Father and the Father within him[^20-1-3], and he himself is the full measure of every majesty, who showed us all his good things. He ate and lived for our sake, though he himself felt neither hunger nor thirst. He bore insults and suffered for us. He died and was raised for our sake. He stood up for me when I sinned, and strengthened me with his greatness, and he will console you[^20-1-4] so that you may love him, this one who is great and small, beautiful and ugly, young and old, appearing in time and yet for ever invisible. Human hands did not hold him, and yet his servants do hold him. No flesh has seen him, yet now he is known. He is the word that was heard and is now among us, who knew no suffering and yet has now experienced suffering. He was never punished and now has been punished. He is before the ages and has been understood in time. He is the great beginning of every principle, yet was handed over to rulers. This Jesus you have, brothers and sisters: the door and the light, the way[^20-1-5] and the bread, the water and the life, the resurrection and the refreshment, the pearl[^20-1-6] and the treasure, the seed and the harvest, the mustard seed[^20-1-7] and the vine[^20-1-8], the plough, the grace, the faith, the word. He is all things, and nobody is greater than he. To him be praise for ever and ever. Amen.”

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> When the ninth hour was past, they got up to give thanks in prayer. Suddenly some of the older widows[^21-1-1], who were blind and did not believe, and whom Peter did not know, cried out to him, “We are sitting together, Peter, hoping in Christ Jesus. We pray, lord Peter[^21-1-2], that you will give us also his mercy and kindness.” Peter said to them, “If you have faith in Christ, if it is firm in you, then see in your mind what you do not see with your eyes. Let your ears seem closed, and let your eyes see nothing but human beings and oxen and mute stones and sticks. But now, Lord, may your sweet and holy name help these women. You touch their eyes, for you are powerful.” The dining room where they were shone as if lightning had struck, with a light like the light that comes among clouds, but not like that of the day. It was indescribable and invisible, a light that no one can describe. It lit us up so much that we collapsed in terror, calling out to the Lord, “Have mercy on us, Lord, your servants. Give us what we are able to bear, Lord, for we can neither see nor endure this.” While we lay there, only those widows who had been blind were standing. The bright light that had appeared to us entered their eyes and made them see. Peter said to them, “Tell us what you saw.” Some said, “We saw an old man, with an appearance we cannot describe to you.” Others said, “A young man.” Others said, “We saw a boy, gently touching our eyes[^21-1-3], and so our eyes were opened.” Peter praised the Lord and said, “You alone are Lord God. How many lips would we need to bring you praise? How can we thank you according to your mercy?” So, brothers and sisters, as I told you a little while ago, God is greater than our thoughts, as we have learned from these older widows, how they saw the Lord in different forms.

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> Peter exhorted them all to take in the Lord with their whole heart. Then he and Marcellus and the other brothers and sisters began to serve the Lord’s virgins[^22-1-1], and rested until morning. Marcellus said to the virgins, “Holy and pure virgins of the Lord, listen. You have a place to stay. What I call mine, whose is it if not yours? Do not leave, but take refreshment. For tomorrow is the sabbath, and Simon has a contest with Peter, God’s holy one. Just as the Lord has always stood by Peter, so now Christ will stand up for him as his apostle, to overcome that wicked enemy, the persecutor of the Lord’s truth. Look, my young men have come with the news that they have seen platforms being put up in the forum, and a crowd saying, ‘Tomorrow at daybreak two Jews are to compete here in a debate about God[^22-1-2].’ So let us keep watch until morning, asking and begging our Lord Jesus Christ to listen to our prayers for Peter.” Marcellus then turned and slept briefly, and woke up and said, “Apostle of Christ, Peter, let us go boldly to what lies ahead. Just now, when I turned to sleep for a short while, I saw you seated in a high place[^22-1-3] with a great crowd in front of you. And I saw a woman, utterly ugly, who had the look of an Ethiopian, not an Egyptian, but wholly black and filthy, dressed in rags, with an iron collar around her neck and chains on both her hands and her feet, dancing. When you saw me, you said in a loud voice, ‘Marcellus, all the strength of Simon and of his god is this woman. Cut off her head.’ I answered, ‘Brother Peter[^22-1-4], I am a senator from a noble family, and I have never stained my hands, not even killing a sparrow[^22-1-5].’ When you heard that, you began to shout, ‘Come, Jesus Christ, our true sword, and cut off not only the head of this demon, but hack all her limbs to pieces before all these people, whom I have put to the test in your service.’ At once a figure resembling you, Peter, took a sword and cut that demon to pieces. I gazed at the two of you, at you and at the one cutting up the demon, and I was amazed at how alike you were. I woke up, and I have reported these signs of Christ to you.” When Peter heard this, his spirit was even more filled, because Marcellus had seen such things, and because the Lord is always there for his own people. Glad and refreshed by these words, he rose and headed for the forum.

## Chapter 23

<sup>1</sup> The brothers and sisters gathered, along with all the Romans there. People took their places for a gold coin each[^23-1-1]. Senators, prefects, and officials all came as well. Peter arrived and stood in the middle, and they all shouted, “Show us who your God is, Peter, and what greatness he has, that gives you such confidence. Do not begrudge it to the Romans; they are lovers of the gods. We have had proof from Simon, and we have yours too. Prove to us, both of you, whom we should truly believe.” While they were saying this, Simon also arrived. He stood next to Peter, unsettled, and looked him over at first. After a long silence, Peter said, “People of Rome, be true judges between us. I say that I have believed in the living and true God, and I promise to give you proofs of him, which are already known to me, as many of you also testify. You can see that this man is now rebuked and silent, knowing that I drove him out of Judea because of the frauds he committed by magic against Eubola, a respectable and very simple woman. Driven out by me, he came here thinking he could hide among you, and look, he is standing right here beside me. Tell us, Simon: did you not fall at my feet and Paul’s in Jerusalem, when you saw the healings done by our hands, and say, ‘I beg you, take from me as much payment as you want[^23-1-2], so that I can lay on hands and do such mighty works’? When we heard that, we cursed you, saying, ‘Do you think you can test us, as though we wanted to own money?’ And now you feel no fear? My name is Peter, because the Lord Christ saw fit to call me ready for anything[^23-1-3]. For I trust in the living God, through whom I will put an end to your magic. Now let him do before you the marvels he did earlier. And what I have said about him now, will you not believe me?” Simon said, “You have the nerve to speak of Jesus of Nazareth, the son of a carpenter[^23-1-4] and a carpenter himself, whose family comes from Judea? Listen, Peter: the Romans have good sense; they are not fools.” He turned to the people and said, “Romans, is a god born? Is a god crucified? Anyone who has a master is not a god.” As he said this, many replied, “You speak well, Simon.”

## Chapter 24

<sup>1</sup> Peter said, “A curse on your words against Christ! You have the nerve to say this, when the prophet says of him, ‘Who can describe his origin?[^24-1-1]’ and another prophet says, ‘We saw him, and he had no form or beauty[^24-1-2].’ And: ‘In the last times a child will be born of the Holy Spirit[^24-1-3]; his mother does not know a man, and no one says he is his father.’ And again he says, ‘She gave birth and did not give birth[^24-1-4].’ And another prophet, honoring the Father, says, ‘We did not hear her voice[^24-1-5], and no midwife came in.’ Another prophet says, ‘Not born from a woman’s womb[^24-1-6], but he came down from a heavenly place.’ And: ‘A stone was cut out without hands[^24-1-7] and struck all the kingdoms.’ And another says about him, ‘The stone that the builders rejected[^24-1-8] has become the head of the corner,’ and he calls it an elect, precious stone. And another prophet says about him, ‘And look, I saw one like a son of man[^24-1-9] coming on a cloud.’ What more can I say? People of Rome, if you knew the prophetic scriptures, I would explain everything to you. But that will be opened up for you later. Now to you, Simon. Do one of the things with which you used to deceive them, and I will undo it through my Lord Jesus Christ.” Simon took courage and said, “If the prefect allows it...”[^24-1-10]

## Chapter 25

<sup>1</sup> The prefect wanted[^25-1-1] to show patience toward both of them, so that he would not seem to act unjustly. He brought forward one of his own household slaves and said to Simon, “Take this boy and put him to death.” To Peter he said, “You bring him back to life.” And to the people he said, “Let it be seen which of these is acceptable to God, the one who kills or the one who makes alive.” At once Simon spoke into the boy’s ear[^25-1-2]. Without a sound he silenced him, and the boy died. The people began to murmur. Then one of the widows who was being looked after at Marcellus’s house, standing behind the crowd, cried out, “Peter, servant of God, my son is dead, the only one I had!” The people made room and brought her to Peter. She threw herself at his feet and said, “I had one son[^25-1-3]. With his own shoulders he provided my food; he lifted me up and carried me. Now that he is dead, who will reach out a hand to me?” Peter said to her, “Go with these witnesses and bring your son, so that those who see may be able to believe that he has risen by the power of God, and so that Simon, seeing it, may fall[^25-1-4].” Peter said to the young men, “We need young men here, and ones who are willing to believe.” At once thirty young men stood up, ready to carry her or to bring her dead son. The widow had barely come back to herself when the young men lifted her up. She cried out, “Look, my son, the servant of Christ has sent for you,” tearing her hair and her face. When the young men arrived, they checked the boy’s nostrils to see whether he was really dead. Seeing that he was dead, they comforted his mother, saying, “If you truly believe in Peter’s God, we will pick him up and carry him to Peter, so that he may raise him and give him back to you.”

## Chapter 26

<sup>1</sup> While they were saying this, the prefect looked at Peter[^26-1-1] in the forum and said, “What do you say, Peter? Look, my slave lies dead, whom even the emperor is fond of, and I did not spare him. I had many other young men, but I trusted in you and in your Lord, whom you proclaim, to see whether you two are really reliable and true, and that is why I wanted this boy to die.” Peter said, “God is not tested[^26-1-2] or sized up. But if he is truly loved and worshiped, he hears those who are worthy. Since my Lord God, Jesus Christ, is now being tested among you, who does such great signs and wonders through me among the company of his sinners[^26-1-3], now, in the sight of everyone, Lord, through my voice raise up by your power the one whom Simon touched and killed.” And Peter said to the boy’s master, “Go, take his right hand, and you will have him alive and walking with you.” Agrippa the prefect ran to the boy, took his hand, and raised him up. When the crowds saw this, they all shouted, “One God, one God, the God of Peter!”

## Chapter 27

<sup>1</sup> In the meantime the widow’s son was carried in on a bier[^27-1-1]. Peter raised his eyes to heaven and stretched out his hands and said, “Holy Father of Jesus Christ, your Son, you who have taught us to look down on everything that is in the world and to follow you alone, you who are seen by only a few and wish to be known by many: shine around us, Lord, give us light, appear, and raise the son of this elderly widow[^27-1-2], who cannot help herself without her son. In the word of Christ my Lord I tell you, young man: get up and walk with your mother for as long as you are able to help her. After that you will serve me in a higher way, ministering in the lot of a deacon and a bishop[^27-1-3].” At once the dead man got up. The crowds saw it and were astonished, and the people cried out, “You are God the savior! You, Peter’s God, the invisible God, the savior!” They spoke among themselves, amazed at the might of a man who with a word called on his Lord, and they received it as a sanctifying thing[^27-1-4].

## Chapter 28

<sup>1</sup> As the news spread through the whole city, the mother of a certain senator came and pushed through the middle of the crowd. She threw herself at the feet of the servant of the merciful God and said, “I have learned from my people that by his grace you share this light with all who long for it. Share the light with my son too, for I know you begrudge it to no one. Do not turn away from a matron who is begging you.” Peter said to her, “Will you believe in my God?” Weeping, she said in a loud voice, “I believe, Peter, I believe!” The whole people shouted, “Give the mother back her son!” Peter said, “Let him be brought here, in front of all these people.” Then he turned to the people and said, “People of Rome, I too am one of you, and I follow this way as a sinner who has received mercy. So do not look at me as though I did what I do by my own power. It is the power of my Lord Jesus Christ, the judge of the living and the dead[^28-1-1]. I believe in him, and I am sent by him. Woman, you also go and have your son brought here, and he will rise.” The woman ran through the middle of the crowd and out into the open, full of joy, and in faith she came to her house. She had her young men carry him and came to the forum. She told the young men to put caps on their heads[^28-1-2] and walk in front of the bier, and everything she had meant to burn with her son’s body was to be carried before his bier. Peter saw this and had pity on her and on the body. As everyone mourned, she reached the crowd. A throng of senators and matrons followed, to see the wonders of God. Nicostratus, who had died[^28-1-3], was a man of great standing, very dear to the senate. They carried him in and set him down in front of Peter. Peter called for quiet and said in a very loud voice, “People of Rome, let there now be a fair judgment between me and Simon. Decide which of us belongs to God, and believe in him as God’s angel. If he cannot do it, I will call on my God and give the son back alive to his mother. Then believe that the one staying among you is a magician and a deceiver.” When all heard this, they thought what Peter said was fair. They urged Simon on: “Now, if you have anything in you, show it openly. Either win or be defeated. Why are you standing there? Go on, begin!” When Simon saw that everyone was pressing him, he stood in silence. When he saw that the people had fallen silent and were looking at him, he called out, “People of Rome, if you see the dead man rise, will you throw Peter out of the city?” The whole people said, “We will not only throw him out, we will burn him in the flames this very hour.” Simon went to the head of the dead man, bent down, and rose up three times. He showed the people the dead man lifting his head and moving it, opening his eyes and bowing gently toward Simon[^28-1-4]. At once the people began to demand wood and torches to burn Peter. But Peter, taking power from Christ, raised his voice and said to those shouting, “People of Rome, how foolish and empty you are, while your eyes and ears and hearts are blind! How long will your understanding stay in darkness? Do you not see that you have been bewitched, and think a dead man has risen who has not lifted himself up? I had enough reason to stay silent and die without speaking, leaving you in the illusions of this world. But I have before my eyes the punishment of fire that cannot be put out. If this is what you want, let the dead man speak. Let him get up, if he lives. Let him untie with his own hands the cloth that binds his jaw. Let him call for his mother, and ask you as you shout, ‘Why are you shouting?’ Let him beckon to us with his hand. And if you want to see that he is dead and that you are bewitched, let the man who persuaded you to turn from Christ step away from the bier, and you will see the dead man just as you saw him brought in.” Agrippa the prefect could stand it no longer, and he pushed Simon away with his own hands, and the dead man lay just as he had been. The people, enraged, turned away from Simon’s magic and began to shout, “Hear us, Caesar! If the dead man does not rise now, let Simon burn instead of Peter, for he has truly blinded us!” Peter stretched out his hand and said, “People of Rome, be patient. I do not say that if the boy rises Simon should be burned, for if I say it, you will do it.” The people shouted, “Even if you do not want it, Peter, we will do it.” Peter said to them, “If you persist in this, the boy will not rise. We do not know how to repay evil with evil[^28-1-5]. We have learned to love our enemies and pray for our persecutors[^28-1-6]. If this man can still repent, all the better, for God does not remember wrongs. Let him come, then, into the light of Christ. If he cannot, let him have the share of his father the devil[^28-1-7]. But your hands must not be stained.” After saying this to the people, he went to the boy. Before raising him, he said to the mother, “These young men whom you set free in honor of your son might be hurt in spirit when they see your son rise, if they were then to serve him again. So let them all remain free and receive their food as they used to. Your son is going to rise, and they will be with him.” Peter looked at her for a long time, to see what she was thinking. The boy’s mother said, “What else can I do? Before the prefect I declare that whatever I was going to burn on my son’s body, they may keep.” Peter said, “Let the rest be distributed to the widows.” Then Peter, glad in his heart, said in the Spirit[^28-1-8], “Merciful Lord Jesus Christ, appear to your Peter as he calls on you, as you have always shown him mercy and goodness. In the presence of all these people who have obtained their freedom, so that they may serve you, let Nicostratus rise now.” Peter touched the boy’s side and said, “Get up.” The boy rose, took up his clothes, sat up, untied the cloth from his jaw, and asked for other clothes. He came down from the bier and said to Peter, “Man of God, let us go to Christ our Lord, whom I saw speaking with you. He said to you, showing me to you, ‘Bring him to me now, for he is mine.’” When Peter heard this from the boy, his spirit was strengthened all the more, with the Lord’s help. He said to the people, “People of Rome, this is how the dead are raised: they talk, they walk, they live for as long as God wishes. Now, you who have come together to watch, if you do not turn from these evils of yours, from all the gods you manufacture[^28-1-9], from all uncleanness and desire, you will not gain life.”

## Chapter 29

<sup>1</sup> From that hour they worshiped him like a god, falling at his feet, and they brought the sick they had at home to be healed. When the prefect saw such a crowd, he gestured to Peter, as he looked toward him, to withdraw. Peter told the people to come to Marcellus’s house. The boy’s mother begged Peter to set foot in her house too. But Peter had arranged to be at Marcellus’s on the Lord’s day[^29-1-1] to see the widows, as Marcellus had promised, so that they could be served by his own hand. So the boy who had risen said, “I will not leave Peter.” His mother, glad and rejoicing, went to her own house. The next day, after the sabbath, she came to Marcellus’s house and brought Peter two thousand gold pieces[^29-1-2], saying to him, “Divide this among the virgins of Christ[^29-1-3] who serve him.” But the boy who had risen from the dead saw that she had given to no one else, and he went to the house and opened the cabinet. He offered four thousand gold pieces himself and said to Peter, “Look, I who have been raised offer a double gift, and from this day on I offer myself as a sacrifice to God, speaking.”

## Chapter 30

<sup>1</sup> On the Lord’s day Peter was speaking to the brothers and sisters and urging them toward faith in Christ. Many senators were present, along with a good number of knights and wealthy women and matrons who were being steadied in the faith. One woman there, very rich, bore the name Chryse[^30-1-1], because every vessel she owned was of gold. From the day she was born she had never used a vessel of silver or glass, but only of gold. She said to Peter, “Peter, servant of God, the one you call God stood beside me in a dream and said, ‘Chryse, take ten thousand gold pieces[^30-1-2] to Peter my servant, for you owe them to him.’ So I have brought them, afraid that some harm might come to me from the one who appeared to me, who then went away into heaven.” After saying this, and setting the money down, she left. Peter saw it and praised the Lord, because those in distress would now be relieved. Some of those present said to him, “Peter, was it not wrong to accept this money from her? She is notorious in all Rome for sexual immorality[^30-1-3], and she does not stay with one man; she even goes to her own young slaves[^30-1-4]. Do not share the table of Chryse. Let what came from her be sent back to her.” When Peter heard this, he laughed and said to the brothers and sisters, “What she is like in the rest of her life, I do not know. But I did not take this money for no reason. She brought it as a debtor of Christ, and she is giving it to Christ’s slaves, for he himself provided for them.”

## Chapter 31

<sup>1</sup> They also brought him the sick on the sabbath, begging him to heal them of their diseases. Many people were cured, paralytics and gout sufferers, people with fevers that came every second or fourth day[^31-1-1], and people with every kind of bodily illness, believing in the name of Jesus Christ. Every day very many more were joined to the grace of the Lord. After a few days, Simon the magician told the crowd he would expose Peter as someone who put his faith not in a genuine God but in a delusion. He produced many illusions, and the disciples who were already firmly grounded laughed at him. In the dining rooms he had certain spirits appear to be brought in to them, which only appeared and were not truly there. What more is there to say? Though he had been refuted many times for his magic, he made lame people appear healthy for a short time, and blind people likewise, and he seemed to bring many dead people to life and make them move, as he did with Stratonicus[^31-1-2]. Peter followed behind and exposed all this in front of the onlookers. Simon was always being disgraced and laughed at by the crowd of Romans, and he was distrusted because he did not succeed in what he promised to do. In this state he told them, “Romans, you suppose now that Peter has defeated me, as though he were more powerful, and you give him more of your attention. You are mistaken. Tomorrow I will leave you, the godless and most impious, and flee to the God whose power I am[^31-1-3], although enfeebled. If you have fallen, look, I am the one who stands[^31-1-4]. I will go up to the Father and say to him, ‘They wanted to bring me down too, your standing son, but I did not give in to them; I went back to myself.’”

## Chapter 32

<sup>1</sup> On the next day a larger crowd gathered on the Sacred Way[^32-1-1] to see Simon fly. Peter, who had seen a vision, also came to the place, in order to expose him in this too. When Simon first came to Rome, he amazed the crowds by flying, but Peter, who was to refute him, was not yet staying in Rome, and the man had so deceived people with his illusions that some were beside themselves over him. Standing now on a high place and spotting Peter, he started to speak: “Peter, now more than ever, as I rise in full view of all these people, I tell you: if your God has power, the one the Jews put to death, and who had you, his chosen ones, stoned, let him prove that faith in him comes from God. Let this make it plain whether it deserves the name of God. I will go up and show myself to this whole crowd, to show who I am.” And look: he was lifted up to the height, and everyone could see him borne over the whole of Rome, above its temples and hills. The believers turned their eyes to Peter. Peter, looking at what was happening, called to the Lord Jesus Christ, “If you permit him to carry out what he has undertaken, all who have put their faith in you will be made to stumble, and the signs and wonders that you granted them through me will find no belief. Hurry, Lord, with your grace. Let him fall from above and be crippled, and not die, but let his leg be broken in three places.” He fell from above and his leg was broken in three places. Then they stoned him, and everyone went off to their own homes, from then on all trusting Peter. One of Simon’s friends, named Gemellus, from whom Simon had taken a great deal of money and whose wife was Greek, hurried along the road and saw Simon with his leg broken. He said, “Simon, if the power of God is shattered, will God himself, whose power you are, not be blinded?” Gemellus ran off after Peter, saying to him, “I too long to be counted among those who believe in Christ[^32-1-2].” Peter said, “Why this jealousy, my brother[^32-1-3]? Come and sit by me.” Simon, in his misfortune, found some people to carry him by night on a litter from Rome to Aricia. He stayed there and was taken from there to another place[^32-1-4] on a charge of magic, and there, under the surgeon’s knife[^32-1-5], the devil’s angel Simon reached the end of his life.

## Chapter 33

<sup>1</sup> Peter was living in Rome, rejoicing with the brothers and sisters in the Lord and giving thanks night and day for the daily crowd that was being brought to the holy name by the Lord’s grace. The concubines of the prefect Agrippa also came together to Peter. There were four of them: Agrippina, Nicaria, Euphemia, and Doris. They heard the word about purity and all the sayings of the Lord, and their souls were struck. They agreed with one another to stay pure and to stay away from Agrippa’s bed, and he pestered them. Agrippa was at a loss and was grieved about them, especially these women. So he had them watched and, sending someone after them to see where they went, he learned that they went to Peter. When they came to him, he said to them, “If you keep company with Peter, I will destroy you, and I will burn him alive.” But they were ready to suffer any evil at Agrippa’s hands, so long as they would no longer be driven wild by passion[^33-1-1], being strengthened by the power of Jesus.

## Chapter 34

<sup>1</sup> A woman named Xanthippe, strikingly beautiful, the wife of Albinus, a friend of Caesar, came to Peter with the other women and kept apart from Albinus. He was beside himself with rage, and he was in love with Xanthippe. He could not understand why she would not even sleep in the same bed with him, and he grew savage as a wild animal, wanting to do away with Peter, because he knew Peter was to blame for the separation of their bed. Many other women too, who had fallen in love with the word of purity[^34-1-1], left their husbands, and husbands kept apart from their wives, because they wanted to live reverently and chastely before God. With a huge uproar in Rome, Albinus told Agrippa how things stood with him: “Either avenge me on Peter, who has taken my wife, or I will avenge myself.” Agrippa said the same had been done to him, since Peter had taken his concubines away. Albinus said to him, “Then why are you waiting, Agrippa? Let us find him and kill him as a meddler[^34-1-2], so that we can get our wives back, and so that we can avenge those who are not able to kill him, whose wives he has also taken away.”

## Chapter 35

<sup>1</sup> While they were making these plans, Xanthippe learned of her husband’s plot with Agrippa, and sent word to Peter so that he would leave Rome. The other brothers and sisters, together with Marcellus, urged him to go out. Peter said to them, “Are we to run away, brothers and sisters?” They said, “No, but so that you can still serve the Lord.” Persuaded by the believers, he went out alone, saying, “Let none of you leave with me. I will go out alone, having changed my appearance.” As he was going out through the gate, he saw the Lord entering Rome. Peter said to him, “Lord, where are you going?[^35-1-1]” The Lord said to him, “I am entering Rome to be crucified.” Peter said to him, “Lord, are you being crucified again?” He said to him, “Yes, Peter, I am being crucified[^35-1-2] again.” Peter came to himself[^35-1-3] and watched the Lord go up into heaven. Then he turned back to Rome, rejoicing and glorifying the Lord, because he had said, “I am being crucified,” which was about to happen to Peter.

## Chapter 36

<sup>1</sup> He went up again to the brothers and sisters and told them what he had seen. They grieved in their souls, weeping and saying, “We beg you, Peter, think of us who are younger.” Peter said to them, “If it is the Lord’s will, it will come about even if we do not want it. As for you, the Lord is able to establish you in his faith, to lay your foundations and widen your reach in him, you whom he himself planted, so that you may plant others through him. As for me, as long as the Lord wishes me to be in the flesh I will not resist, and if he wishes to take me again, I rejoice and am glad.” While Peter was still speaking, soldiers took him and brought him to Agrippa. In his illness[^36-1-1], he ordered Peter crucified, on a charge of godlessness. The entire crowd of believers ran together, rich and poor, orphans and widows, weak and strong, wanting to see Peter and snatch him away, while the crowds shouted with one unstoppable voice, “What wrong has Peter done, Agrippa? What harm has he done you? Tell the Romans!” Others said, “We should be afraid that if this man dies, his Lord will destroy us all.” Peter came to the place, quieted the crowd, and said, “People who serve as soldiers for Christ, who hope in Christ, remember my signs and wonders, remember God’s sympathy and how many healings he has done for you. Wait for him as he comes to repay each person according to their deeds[^36-1-2]. And now do not be bitter toward Agrippa, for he is the servant of his father’s working[^36-1-3]. This will certainly happen, for the Lord has shown me what is going to occur. But why am I delaying and not going to the cross?”

## Chapter 37

<sup>1</sup> When he came up and stood at the cross, he began to speak. “O name of the cross, hidden mystery! O grace beyond description, spoken in the name of the cross! O nature of humankind, which cannot be separated from God! O unspeakable and inseparable friendship, which cannot be shown forth through unclean lips! Now, standing at the end of my release from this place, I press you hard: I will make known what you are. I will not be silent any longer about the mystery of the cross, long shut up in my soul and concealed. You who hope in Christ, do not let the cross be for you what appears to the eye. It is something other than this appearance, in keeping with the passion of Christ. And now above all, since those of you who are able can hear, and I am in the last and final hour of my life, listen. Separate your souls from every sense, from everything that appears and is not truly real. Make these eyes of yours blind, make these ears of yours deaf, put away the deeds that are seen, and you will perceive the mystery of your salvation in Christ[^37-1-1]. And let this be said to you as to those who receive bodies[^37-1-2]. It is time, Peter, to hand over your body to those who take it. So receive it, you to whom it belongs. I ask you, executioners, to crucify me this way, head downward, and not otherwise. Why, I will tell those who listen.”

## Chapter 38

<sup>1</sup> When they had hung him up in the way he asked, he started to speak again. “People for whom hearing is natural, give ear to what I am about to declare to you now, here where I hang. Know the mystery of all nature and the origin of all things, how it came about. The first human being, whose kind I bear in my form, was brought down head first, and showed a kind of birth that had not existed before, for it was lifeless, without movement. He was dragged down and cast his own beginning down to the earth, and so he established this whole arrangement of the world, hung up as an image of the calling[^38-1-1]. In it he displayed the right as left and the left as right, and reversed all the marks of their nature, so that things that are not good were thought good, and things that are truly bad were thought excellent. About these the Lord speaks in a mystery, ‘If you do not make the right into the left[^38-1-2] and the left into the right, and the above into the below, and the back into the front, you will not know the kingdom.’ I have brought this thought forward for you, and the shape in which you see me hanging is the outline of that first human being[^38-1-3] who came into birth. You, then, my beloved, both those who hear now and those who will hear, must forget the first error and run back to where you started. You should step up onto the cross of Christ, who is the stretched word, single and alone. The Spirit[^38-1-4] says of him, ‘What is Christ if not the word, the voice of God?’ So the word is this vertical wood where I hang crucified; the sound is the crosspiece, human nature; and the nail that fastens the crosspiece to the upright at the center is the turning back and repentance of humankind.”

## Chapter 39

<sup>1</sup> “Since you have made these things known to me and revealed them, word of life, wood that I have just now called by that name, I thank you, not with these lips that are nailed fast, nor with the tongue through which truth and falsehood come out, nor with this speech that arises from the craft of material nature. I thank you, King, with that voice which is understood through silence, which is not heard in public, which does not come forth through the organs of the body, which does not travel into ears of flesh, which is not heard by a perishable substance, which does not belong to the world or fall on the earth, which is not written in books, which belongs to one person and not to another. With this, Jesus Christ, I thank you, with the silence of a voice, with which the spirit in me[^39-1-1] loves you, speaks to you, sees you, and pleads with you. You are known by spirit alone. You are my father, you my mother, you my brother, you my friend, you my slave, you my steward. You are the All, and the All is in you[^39-1-2]. You are, and there is nothing else that is, except you alone. So take refuge in him, brothers and sisters, and when you learn that you exist only in him, you will obtain what he promises you, ‘what no human eye has seen[^39-1-3], no ear has heard, and what has not entered the heart of any person.’ We ask, then, for what you promised to give us, undefiled Jesus. We praise you, we thank you, and we confess you, while we are still weak human beings glorifying you, because you alone are God, and no other. To you be glory, now and to all the ages of ages. Amen.”

## Chapter 40

<sup>1</sup> When the crowd standing by called out the Amen with a great sound, at that very Amen Peter handed his spirit over to the Lord. Marcellus did not ask anyone’s permission, which was not allowed, but saw that the blessed Peter had breathed his last. With his own hands he took him down from the cross and washed him in milk and wine. He ground up seven minas of Chian mastic[^40-1-1], with fifty more of myrrh, aloes, and leaf[^40-1-2], filled a very costly marble coffin with Attic honey[^40-1-3], and laid the body in his own tomb. That night Peter stood by Marcellus and said, “Marcellus, have you heard the Lord say, ‘Let the dead bury their own dead[^40-1-4]’?” When Marcellus said, “Yes,” Peter said to him, “What you have spent on the dead, then, you have lost. For though you are alive, you have cared for the dead like a dead man.” Marcellus woke up and told the brothers and sisters about Peter’s appearance. He remained with those who had been made firm in faith in Christ by Peter, and was himself strengthened still more, until Paul arrived in Rome.

## Chapter 41

<sup>1</sup> When Nero later learned that Peter had left this life, he blamed the prefect Agrippa, because Peter had been put to death without his approval. He had wanted to punish him with a longer and heavier penalty, for Peter had won some of Nero’s own people as disciples and drawn them away from him. So Nero was furious, and for quite some time did not speak to Agrippa, since he was looking for ways to destroy all the believers Peter had made disciples. One night he saw someone flogging him, who said, “Nero, you can no longer persecute or destroy the servants of Christ. Take your hands off them.” Nero was terrified by such a vision and let the disciples be at that time, the time at which Peter also departed this life. And from then on the brothers and sisters were of one mind, rejoicing and exulting in the Lord, and they glorified the God and Savior[^41-1-1] of our Lord Jesus Christ, together with the Holy Spirit, to whom glory belongs for ever and ever. Amen.
