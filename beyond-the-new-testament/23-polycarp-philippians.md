# Polycarp to the Philippians

A letter from Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, to the church at Philippi, written around the time of Ignatius's journey, perhaps soon after 110. It is full of echoes of the New Testament letters. The Greek breaks off before the end; the last chapters survive in Latin.

<sup>1</sup> Polycarp and the presbyters with him, to the church of God that lives as a resident alien at[^0-1-1] Philippi: may mercy and peace from God Almighty and from Jesus Christ our Savior be multiplied to you.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> I rejoiced greatly with you in our Lord Jesus Christ because you welcomed the likenesses of true love, and, as the opportunity fell to you, sent on their way those who were wrapped in chains fit for saints, which are the crowns of those truly chosen by God and by our Lord.

<sup>2</sup> I rejoice also that the firm root of your faith, proclaimed from ancient times, remains to this day and bears fruit for our Lord Jesus Christ, who endured, for our sins, even to the point of death, and whom God raised, having loosed the pains of Hades[^1-2-1].

<sup>3</sup> In him, though you have not seen him[^1-3-1], you believe with a joy that cannot be expressed and is full of glory, a joy many long to enter, knowing that you have been saved by grace[^1-3-2], not from works, but by the will of God through Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> Therefore tuck up your robes[^2-1-1] and serve God with reverence and truth. Put away the hollow talk and the delusion of the masses. Put your faith in the God who raised our Lord Jesus Christ from the dead, giving him glory and a throne at his right. To him everything in heaven and earth is subject[^2-1-2]; every creature that breathes worships him; he is coming as judge of the living and the dead[^2-1-3]; and God will hold those who disobey him to account for his blood.

<sup>2</sup> The one who raised him from the dead will raise us also[^2-2-1], if we do his will, walk in his commandments, and love what he loved, keeping away from all injustice, greed, love of money, slander, and false testimony. We must not pay back evil for evil[^2-2-2], or insult for insult, or blow for blow, or curse for curse,

<sup>3</sup> but remember what the Lord said as he taught: “Do not judge[^2-3-1], so that you may not be judged; forgive, and you will be forgiven; show mercy, so that you may receive mercy; the measure you give will be the measure you get.” And: “Blessed are the poor[^2-3-2], and those persecuted for the sake of righteousness, for theirs is the kingdom of God.”

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> I am writing you these things about righteousness, brothers and sisters[^3-1-1], not because I took it upon myself, but because you invited me first.

<sup>2</sup> Neither I nor anyone like me is able to keep up with the wisdom of the blessed and glorious Paul. He lived among you in the sight of the people of that day and taught the word about truth with precision and firmness; and when he was absent he sent you letters. If you pore over them, you will be able to grow in the faith that has been handed to you,

<sup>3</sup> which is the mother of us all[^3-3-1], with hope following and with love for God, for Christ, and for neighbor leading the way. For if anyone is within these, that person has fulfilled the commandment[^3-3-2] of righteousness, since whoever has love is far from all sin.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> Love of money[^4-1-1] is where all troubles begin. We came into the world with nothing and can carry nothing out; so let us take up righteousness as our armor[^4-1-2], and first of all train ourselves to live by the Lord’s commandment.

<sup>2</sup> then to teach our wives to walk in the faith, love, and purity given to them, cherishing their own husbands in all truth and loving everyone else equally, with complete self-control, and to educate the children in the discipline of the fear of God;

<sup>3</sup> and to teach the widows[^4-3-1] to be sensible about the faith of the Lord, to intercede constantly for everyone, to stay far from all slander, gossip, false testimony, love of money, and every evil, knowing that they are God’s altar[^4-3-2], that everything is inspected, and that nothing escapes him, neither reasonings nor thoughts nor any of the secrets of the heart.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Since we know that God is not mocked[^5-1-1], we ought to walk in a way worthy of his commandment and glory.

<sup>2</sup> Deacons likewise have to be above reproach[^5-2-1] in the sight of his righteousness, since they serve God and Christ, not people. They must not be slanderers or speak out of both sides of the mouth; they must not love money; they must be self-controlled in all respects, tenderhearted, conscientious, walking by the truth of the Lord, who became everyone’s servant[^5-2-2]. If we please him now, we will gain the age to come too, just as he promised to raise us from the dead and, if we conduct ourselves in a way worthy of him, to let us rule with him[^5-2-3], provided that we have faith.

<sup>3</sup> Young men must be equally blameless in everything. Above all, they should plan ahead for purity and rein themselves in from every evil. It is good to be held back from the passions of the world, because every passion wars against the spirit[^5-3-1]. Neither the sexually immoral, nor male prostitutes, nor men who have sex with men[^5-3-2] will inherit God’s kingdom, nor will those who commit outrages. For that reason we must stay away from all of this and be subject to the presbyters and deacons as to God and Christ. The virgins must live with a pure conscience, free of blame.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> The presbyters, too, must be compassionate, merciful to all, turning back those who have strayed[^6-1-1], visiting all who are sick, not neglecting a widow, an orphan, or a poor person, but always providing for what is good[^6-1-2] in the sight of God and people. They must refrain from all anger, partiality, and unjust judgment, stay far from all love of money, not be quick to believe anything against anyone, not be harsh in judgment, and know that we are all debtors of sin.

<sup>2</sup> If we are asking the Lord to forgive us[^6-2-1], we owe forgiveness too. We stand in full view of the Lord and God, and everyone must appear before Christ’s tribunal[^6-2-2], each person answering for themselves.

<sup>3</sup> That is how we should be his slaves, with fear and every kind of reverence, just as he himself charged us, and as the apostles who brought us the good news and the prophets who announced our Lord’s coming in advance have taught. Be eager for what is good. Avoid scandals, false brothers and sisters[^6-3-1], and those who wear the Lord’s name as a mask, who mislead empty-headed people.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> Anyone who refuses to confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh is the antichrist[^7-1-1]; anyone who refuses to confess the witness of the cross belongs to the devil; and anyone who bends the Lord’s sayings toward their own passions, claiming there is no resurrection and no judgment, is Satan’s eldest son.

<sup>2</sup> So let us abandon the pointless talk of the crowd and their false teachings and go back to the message passed on to us from the start. Let us keep clear heads for prayer[^7-2-1], hold fast to fasting, and with our petitions ask the God who sees all not to bring us into testing[^7-2-2], as the Lord said, “The spirit is eager[^7-2-3], but the flesh is weak.”

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Let us persevere without ceasing in our hope and in the pledge of our righteousness, who is Christ Jesus. He carried our sins[^8-1-1] in his own body up to the tree; he committed no sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth; but he endured everything for us, so that we might live in him.

<sup>2</sup> Let us become imitators of his endurance, and if we suffer for his name, let us glorify him. This is the example[^8-2-1] he set for us in his own person, and this is what we have believed.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> I appeal to every one of you to listen to the message of righteousness and to practice complete endurance. You have watched it happen before your eyes, not only in the blessed Ignatius, Zosimus, and Rufus, but also in others from your own community, and in Paul himself and the rest of the apostles.

<sup>2</sup> You can be certain that none of these ran in vain[^9-2-1]; they ran in faith and righteousness, and they are now in the place they earned beside the Lord, with whom they also suffered. They did not love the present age[^9-2-2], but the one who died for our sake and was raised by God for us.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> So stand firm in this[^10-1-1] and follow the Lord’s example, steady in faith and unchangeable, lovers of the community of brothers and sisters, loving one another, joined together in truth, going ahead of one another in the Lord’s gentleness, despising no one.

<sup>2</sup> Do not delay doing good when you are able, for charity rescues from death[^10-2-1]. Be subject to one another, all of you, keeping your behavior beyond reproach among the gentiles[^10-2-2], so that your good works earn you praise and the Lord is not slandered because of you.

<sup>3</sup> But woe to the one through whom the name of the Lord is blasphemed[^10-3-1]. So teach self-control to everyone, and live by it yourselves.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> I am deeply grieved about Valens, who was once made a presbyter among you, that he understands so little the place that was given to him. So I advise you to keep away from greed and to be pure and truthful. Keep away from every evil.

<sup>2</sup> How could someone who cannot control themselves in this matter preach to others? Anyone who does not keep away from greed will be polluted by idolatry and be judged like the gentiles, who do not know the Lord’s judgment. Or have we forgotten that the saints will judge the world[^11-2-1]? That is Paul’s teaching.

<sup>3</sup> I have noticed or heard nothing of the kind among you, among whom the blessed Paul labored, you who are named at the beginning of his letter[^11-3-1]. He boasts about you in all the churches, which alone had known the Lord at that time; we had not yet known him.

<sup>4</sup> I am therefore deeply sorry for him and for his wife, brothers and sisters[^11-4-1], and I pray that the Lord gives them true repentance. For your part, keep a clear head here: do not treat such people as enemies, but call them back as members who are ailing and wandering[^11-4-2], so that you save the whole body that you are. In doing so you build yourselves up.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> I am sure that you have practiced hard in the holy scriptures, and that nothing in them is hidden from you. That privilege has not been given to me. So, as this passage says, “Be angry, yet do not sin[^12-1-1],” and “Let the sun not set on your anger.” Happy is the one who remembers it, and I believe that you do.

<sup>2</sup> May God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, and Jesus Christ himself, the Son of God and our everlasting high priest[^12-2-1], make you grow in faith and truth, in complete gentleness, free of anger, with patience, steadiness, forbearance, and purity. May he give you a place and share among his saints[^12-2-2], and us with you, and everyone under heaven who is going to believe in our Lord and God Jesus Christ and in his Father, who raised him from the dead.

<sup>3</sup> Pray for all the saints. Pray too for kings[^12-3-1], powers, and rulers, and for those who persecute and hate you, as well as for the enemies of the cross[^12-3-2], so that your fruit will be there for all to see, and you will be whole in him.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> You and Ignatius wrote to me[^13-1-1] that if anyone goes to Syria, he should also take your letters along. I will do this if I find a suitable opportunity, either I myself or someone I send as an envoy on your behalf as well.

<sup>2</sup> We have sent you the letters of Ignatius that were sent to us by him, and others that we had with us, as you instructed. They are attached to this letter, and you will be able to benefit greatly from them. They contain faith, endurance, and all the building up that pertains to our Lord. And concerning Ignatius himself and those with him, let us know whatever you learn with greater certainty[^13-2-1].

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> I have written this to you[^14-1-1] through Crescens, whom I recently commended to you and now commend again. He lived among us blamelessly, and I believe he will do so among you as well. You will have his sister commended to you when she comes to you. Farewell in the Lord Jesus Christ, in grace, with all of yours. Amen.
