## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Paul was on his way up to Iconium after his flight from Antioch, and Demas and Hermogenes[^1-1-1] the coppersmith joined him on the road. They were brimming with hypocrisy and kept appealing to Paul, as people do who love someone. But Paul, with his eye on nothing but the goodness of Christ, did them no harm and was extremely fond of them. He made every word of the Lord sweet to them, together with the teaching and the interpretation of the gospel[^1-1-2], the birth and the resurrection of the Beloved[^1-1-3], and he recounted to them word for word the great deeds of Christ, as these had been revealed to him.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> A man named Onesiphorus[^2-1-1], hearing that Paul was coming to Iconium, went out with his children Simmias and Zeno and his wife Lectra to meet Paul and welcome him as a guest, for Titus had told him what Paul looked like. He had not seen him in the flesh, only in the spirit.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> He went along the royal road that leads to Lystra and stood waiting for him, looking at everyone who came by, as Titus had described. And he saw Paul coming: a man small in stature, with a bald head and bowed legs, in good condition[^3-1-1], with eyebrows that met and a nose slightly hooked[^3-1-2], full of grace. At one moment he appeared like a human being, and at another he had the face of an angel.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> Seeing Onesiphorus, Paul smiled, and Onesiphorus said, “Hello, servant[^4-1-1] of the blessed God!” “Grace to you and your household,” Paul replied. But Demas and Hermogenes were envious and put on an even greater show of hypocrisy, so that Demas said, “Are we not people of the blessed one too? Why did you not greet us the same way?” Onesiphorus replied, “I do not see any fruit of righteousness in you. But if you are somebody[^4-1-2], come to my house as well and rest.”

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> When Paul had entered the house of Onesiphorus, there was great joy, with kneeling and the breaking of bread and a word of God about self-control and the resurrection. Paul said, “Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God[^5-1-1]. Blessed are those who have kept the flesh pure, for they will become a temple of God. Blessed are the self-controlled[^5-1-2], for God will speak with them. Blessed are those who have taken leave of this world[^5-1-3], for they will please God. Blessed are those who have wives as though they had none, for they will inherit God[^5-1-4]. Blessed are those who have the fear of God, for they will become angels of God.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> “Blessed are those who tremble at the words of God[^6-1-1], for they will be comforted. Blessed are those who have received the wisdom of Jesus Christ, for they will be called children[^6-1-2] of the Most High. Blessed are those who have kept the baptism, for they will find rest with the Father and the Son. Blessed are those who have taken in the understanding of Christ, for they will be in the light. Blessed are those who for the love of God have gone out of the fashion of the world, for they will judge angels[^6-1-3] and be blessed at the right hand of the Father. Blessed are the merciful, for they will receive mercy[^6-1-4] and will not see the bitter day of judgment. Blessed are the bodies of virgins, for they will please God and will not lose the reward for their purity; for the word of the Father will become for them a work of salvation on the day of his Son, and they will have rest for ever and ever.”

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> While Paul was saying these things in the middle of the church[^7-1-1] in the house of Onesiphorus, a virgin named Thecla, whose mother was Theocleia and who was engaged to a man named Thamyris, sat at the nearby window of the house and listened night and day to what Paul was saying about purity. She did not turn away from the window, but was drawn on by faith, rejoicing greatly. And when she saw many women and virgins going in to Paul, she longed to be counted worthy to stand in Paul’s presence and hear Christ’s word; for she had not yet seen Paul’s features, but only heard his word.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Since she would not leave the window, her mother sent for Thamyris, and he came overjoyed, as though he were already taking her in marriage. Thamyris said to Theocleia, “Where is my Thecla?” And Theocleia said, “I have a strange story to tell you, Thamyris. For three days and three nights Thecla has not got up from the window, not even to eat or drink, but gazes as if at a delightful sight, so devoted is she to a foreigner who teaches deceitful and varied words. I am amazed that a girl of such modesty is so badly troubled.”

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> “Thamyris, this man is upsetting the city of the Iconians, and your Thecla as well. All the women and young men are going in to him and learning from him that, as he puts it, there is one God alone to be feared, and that we must live in purity. And my daughter, tied to the window like a spider by what he says, is gripped by a strange craving and a dreadful passion. She hangs on his words, and the girl has been taken captive. Go to her yourself and speak to her; she is engaged to you.”

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> Thamyris went up to her, torn between love for her[^10-1-1] and fear at her stupor, and said, “Thecla, my betrothed, why do you sit like this? What kind of feeling has you so stunned? Turn to your own Thamyris and feel ashamed.” Her mother echoed him: “Daughter, why are you sitting here looking at the floor, with no answer for anyone, like a person out of her mind?” All of them wept bitterly: Thamyris because he was losing a bride, Theocleia a child, and the maids their mistress. The house was filled with confusion and mourning. Yet through all of it Thecla never turned aside, but stayed intent on the word of Paul.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> Thamyris sprang up and went out into the street. He kept watch on the people going in to Paul and coming out. And he saw two men locked in a bitter quarrel, and said to them, “Gentlemen, who are you? And tell me, who is the man inside with you, who leads astray the souls of young men and deceives virgins, so that no weddings take place and everyone stays as they are? I promise to give you a great deal of money if you tell me about him; I am the foremost man in the city.”

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> Demas and Hermogenes told him, “We do not know who he is. But he robs young men of wives and virgins of husbands, saying, ‘There is no resurrection for you unless you stay pure and do not defile your flesh, but keep it chaste.’”

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> Thamyris said to them, “Come, gentlemen, to my house and rest with me.” And they went to a costly dinner, with plenty of wine, great wealth, and a splendid table. Thamyris gave them drink, because he loved Thecla and wanted to get her as his wife. And at the dinner Thamyris said, “Gentlemen, tell me what his teaching is, so that I may know it too; for I am in no small distress about Thecla, because she is so fond of the foreigner, and I am being cheated of marriage.”

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> Demas and Hermogenes said, “Bring him before Castellius[^14-1-1] the governor, charging that he is winning the crowds over to the new teaching of the Christians; that will finish him off, and you will have your wife Thecla. As for us, we will explain to you what he calls the resurrection: it has already taken place in the children we have[^14-1-2], and we rise again when we come to know the true God.”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> When Thamyris heard this from them, he was filled with jealousy and rage, and rising early in the morning he went to the house of Onesiphorus with magistrates and officers and a good-sized crowd carrying clubs. He said to Paul, “You have ruined the city of the Iconians, and the woman engaged to me, so that she will not have me. Let us go to the governor Castellius.” And the whole crowd said, “Take away the sorcerer! He has ruined all our wives!” And the crowds were persuaded.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> Standing before the tribunal, Thamyris cried out at the top of his voice, “Proconsul[^16-1-1], we do not know where this man has come from, but he forbids virgins to marry! Let him tell you why he teaches this.” Demas and Hermogenes whispered to Thamyris, “Tell him that he is a Christian; that is how you will destroy him.” The governor, however, held to his own judgment, and he summoned Paul and asked, “Who are you, and what do you teach? The charges against you are not small.”

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> Paul raised his voice and said, “If I am being questioned today about what I teach, listen, proconsul. The living God, the God of vengeance, the jealous God, the God who needs nothing, who wants the salvation of people, sent me to tear them away from corruption and uncleanness and from every pleasure and from death, so that they would sin no more. For this reason God sent his own servant[^17-1-1], whom I proclaim and teach, that people might place their hope in him, who alone had compassion on a world gone astray, so that people would no longer be under judgment but would have faith and the fear of God, and knowledge of holiness, and love of truth. So if I teach what God has revealed to me, what wrong am I doing, proconsul?” When the governor heard this, he ordered Paul to be bound and taken to prison, until he had time to listen to him more carefully.

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> During the night Thecla took off her bracelets and gave them to the doorkeeper, and when the door had been opened for her she went off to the prison; and she gave the jailer a silver mirror and went in to Paul. She sat down at his feet and heard the great deeds of God. Paul was not afraid of anything, but conducted himself with the boldness that comes from God; and her faith grew as she kissed his chains.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> Meanwhile Thecla’s family and Thamyris were looking for her, hunting her through the streets as if she had been lost, until one of the doorkeeper’s fellow slaves told them she had left by night. They questioned the doorkeeper, who said she had gone to the foreigner in the prison. They went where he said and found her, so to speak, chained to him by her affection. They left there, rounded up the crowds, and informed the governor.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> He ordered Paul to be brought to the tribunal; but Thecla rolled about on the spot where Paul had sat teaching in the prison. So the governor ordered her to be brought to the tribunal too, and she went off with joy, exulting. When Paul was brought forward again, the crowd shouted all the louder, “He is a sorcerer! Take him away!” But the governor listened gladly to Paul speak about the holy works of Christ. After consulting, he called Thecla and said, “Why do you not marry Thamyris, as the law of the Iconians requires?” She stood there gazing at Paul. When she gave no answer, her mother Theocleia cried out, “Burn the lawless girl! Burn the bride who will not wed, in the middle of the theater, so that all the women taught by this man will be afraid!”

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> The governor was deeply troubled. He had Paul flogged and drove him out of the city, but condemned Thecla to be burned. At once the governor got up and went off to the theater, and the whole crowd went out to the spectacle, as if compelled[^21-1-1]. And just as a lamb in the wilderness looks around for the shepherd, so Thecla looked for Paul. She gazed into the crowd and saw the Lord sitting there in the likeness of Paul, and she said, “As though I could not hold out, Paul has come to see me.” And she kept her eyes on him; but he went off into heaven.

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> The boys and girls carried in wood and straw to burn Thecla. When she was brought in naked, the governor wept and marveled at the power in her. The executioners spread out the wood and told her to step onto the pyre. Making the sign of the cross[^22-1-1], she stepped onto the logs, and they lit it. A great fire blazed up, yet it did not touch her. For God, moved with pity, made a roar beneath the ground, and a cloud full of water and hail hung overhead and poured out its entire contents, so that many were put in danger and some died; and the fire was put out, and Thecla was saved.

## Chapter 23

<sup>1</sup> Paul was meanwhile fasting in an open tomb[^23-1-1] along the road from Iconium to Daphne, together with Onesiphorus, his wife, and his children. When many days had gone by in their fasting, the children told Paul, “We are starving.” And they had nowhere to get money for bread, for Onesiphorus had abandoned the goods of this world and followed Paul with his entire family. Paul pulled off his cloak and told one of them, “Go, boy, buy a good many loaves and bring them back.” As the boy was buying, he noticed Thecla, a neighbor of theirs, and in astonishment cried, “Thecla, where are you headed?” “I am chasing after Paul,” she said, “now that I have been saved from the fire.” The boy said, “Come here; I will lead you to him. He is groaning over you; he has been praying and fasting for six days now.”

## Chapter 24

<sup>1</sup> When she came to the tomb, Paul had bent his knees and was praying, “Father of Christ, do not let the fire touch Thecla; but be at her side, for she is yours.” She, standing behind him, cried out, “Father, who made heaven and earth, Father of your beloved servant[^24-1-1] Jesus Christ, I bless you that you saved me from the fire, so that I might see Paul.” Paul got up, saw her, and said, “God who knows hearts[^24-1-2], Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, I bless you, because you quickly gave me what I asked for and listened to me.”

## Chapter 25

<sup>1</sup> There was much love inside the tomb, as Paul, Onesiphorus, and everyone rejoiced. They had five loaves, vegetables, and water and salt[^25-1-1], and they were glad at the holy works of Christ. Thecla said to Paul, “I will cut my hair short and follow you wherever you go.” But he said, “The time is shameful, and you are beautiful. I fear that another temptation worse than the first will take you, and you will not endure it but will behave like a coward.” Thecla said, “Only give me the seal in Christ[^25-1-2], and no temptation will touch me.” And Paul said, “Thecla, be patient, and you will receive the water[^25-1-3].”

## Chapter 26

<sup>1</sup> Paul sent Onesiphorus and his whole household back to Iconium, and then took Thecla and went into Antioch. As soon as they entered, a Syriarch[^26-1-1] named Alexander saw Thecla and fell in love with her, and he pleaded with Paul, offering money and gifts. But Paul said, “I do not know the woman you are talking about, and she is not mine.” Being a man of great influence, he put his arms around her himself in the street; but she would not stand for it and looked for Paul. She cried out bitterly, “Do not force the stranger! Do not force God’s servant[^26-1-2]! I am a leading woman of Iconium, and because I would not marry Thamyris I have been thrown out of the city.” And she seized Alexander, tore his cloak, pulled the crown from his head, and made a public spectacle of him[^26-1-3].

## Chapter 27

<sup>1</sup> He loved her, but he was also ashamed at what had happened to him, and he brought her before the governor; and when she admitted that she had done these things, he sentenced her to the wild beasts. The women were stunned and shouted by the tribunal, “An evil verdict! An impious verdict!” But Thecla asked the governor to let her remain pure until she should fight the beasts. And a wealthy woman named Tryphaena, a queen[^27-1-1], whose daughter had died, took her into her care and had her as a comfort.

## Chapter 28

<sup>1</sup> When the beasts were paraded, they tied her to a fierce lioness, and Queen Tryphaena followed along. But the lioness, while Thecla sat on her, licked her feet, and the whole crowd was astonished. The charge written on her placard was “Temple robber[^28-1-1].” The women with their children cried out from above, “O God, an impious judgment is being made in this city!” After the parade Tryphaena took her in again; for her daughter Falconilla, who was dead, had said to her in a dream, “Mother, you will have the desolate stranger Thecla in my place, so that she may pray for me and I may be moved to the place of the righteous.”

## Chapter 29

<sup>1</sup> On bringing her back from the procession, Tryphaena was sorrowful because Thecla was to fight the beasts the following day; yet she was also devoted to her, as to her own Falconilla, and she said, “Thecla, my child, my second one, come and pray for my daughter, that she may live forever. I saw this in a dream.” Without delay Thecla raised her voice and said, “My God, Son of the Most High, who are in heaven, give her what she desires: let her daughter Falconilla live forever.” While Thecla spoke, Tryphaena grieved, thinking of such beauty being thrown to beasts.

## Chapter 30

<sup>1</sup> When morning came, Alexander arrived to take her away—for he was the one giving the hunt—saying, “The governor is seated, and the crowd is clamoring for us. Let me take away the beast fighter.” But Tryphaena cried out so loudly that he fled. She said, “A second mourning for my Falconilla has come upon my house, and there is no one to help, neither a child, for she is dead, nor a relative, for I am a widow. God of Thecla, my child, help Thecla!”

## Chapter 31

<sup>1</sup> The governor sent soldiers to fetch Thecla, but Tryphaena would not give her up. She took Thecla by the hand herself and led her up, saying, “I carried my daughter Falconilla to the tomb; and you, Thecla, I am leading to a fight with beasts.” Thecla wept bitterly and cried out to the Lord, “Lord God, in whom I trust, in whom I have taken refuge, you who saved me from the fire, grant Tryphaena her reward for her kindness to your servant[^31-1-1] and for keeping me pure.”

## Chapter 32

<sup>1</sup> Then there was an uproar, with the roaring of the beasts and the shouting of the people and of the women sitting together, some saying, “Bring in the temple robber!” and others saying, “Let the city be destroyed for this lawlessness! Take us all away, proconsul! A bitter sight, an evil verdict!”

## Chapter 33

<sup>1</sup> Thecla was taken from Tryphaena’s hand, stripped, given a belt, and thrown into the stadium. Lions and bears were let loose on her. But a fierce lioness ran up and lay down at her feet, and the crowd of women shouted aloud. A bear ran at her, but the lioness leapt out to meet it and tore the bear apart. Then a lion trained to attack people, which belonged to Alexander, ran at her; and the lioness grappled with the lion and was killed along with it. The women mourned all the more, because the lioness that had helped her was dead.

## Chapter 34

<sup>1</sup> Then many more beasts were sent in. She stood there with her arms outstretched, praying; and when she had finished praying, she turned and saw a great ditch filled with water. “Now,” she said, “is the moment to bathe.” In she plunged, crying, “In the name of Jesus Christ I am baptized on my last day[^34-1-1]!” At the sight, the women and the entire crowd wept and begged, “Do not throw yourself into the water!” The governor himself shed tears, because seals were going to feed on[^34-1-2] such beauty. But into the water she went, in the name of Jesus Christ, and the seals, when they saw the glare of fiery lightning, floated up dead. A cloud of fire surrounded her, so that the animals could not touch her and no one could see her naked.

## Chapter 35

<sup>1</sup> As still more terrifying beasts were sent in, the women raised a wail, and they threw down leaves, nard, cassia, and amomum[^35-1-1], until the air was full of perfumes. All the beasts that were thrown in lay as if gripped by sleep and did not touch her. Then Alexander said to the governor, “I have some very frightening bulls; let us tie the beast fighter to those.” The governor grimly agreed: “Do as you like.” They tied her by the feet between the bulls and set red-hot irons beneath their private parts[^35-1-2], so that the bulls, thrown into a greater frenzy, would kill her. The bulls bounded about, but the flame blazing around her burned through the ropes, and she was as if never bound.

## Chapter 36

<sup>1</sup> Tryphaena collapsed where she stood by the arena, at the barriers[^36-1-1], so that her maids cried, “Queen Tryphaena has died!” The governor called off the games, and all the city was thrown into panic. Alexander fell at the governor’s feet and said, “Pity me and the city, and let the beast fighter go, or the city will be destroyed with her. If Caesar hears about this, he will perhaps destroy us and our city together, because his kinswoman, Queen Tryphaena, has died at the barriers.”

## Chapter 37

<sup>1</sup> The governor called Thecla out from among the beasts and asked her, “Who are you? What power do you have about you, that none of the beasts laid a paw on you?” “I am a slave of the living God,” she answered. “As for what I have about me: I believed in the one in whom God was well pleased[^37-1-1], his Son, and for his sake not one of the beasts touched me. He alone is the way of salvation[^37-1-2] and the basis of immortal life. For those battered by storms he is a refuge, for the oppressed a relief, for the hopeless a shelter; in short, anyone who does not believe in him will not live, but will die forever.”

## Chapter 38

<sup>1</sup> When the governor heard this, he ordered clothes to be brought and said, “Put on the clothes.” But she said, “The one who clothed me when I was naked among the beasts will clothe me with salvation on the day of judgment.” And she took the clothes and put them on.

<sup>2</sup> Immediately the governor sent out a public proclamation, saying, “I release to you Thecla, the servant[^38-2-1] of God, who worships God.” All the women shouted with a loud voice, and as with one mouth gave praise to God, saying, “There is one God, who has saved Thecla!” so that the whole city shook at the sound.

## Chapter 39

<sup>1</sup> When Tryphaena heard the good news, she came out with a crowd to meet her, and she embraced Thecla and said, “Now I believe that the dead are raised! Now I believe that my child is alive! Come inside, and I will sign over to you everything I own.” So Thecla went in with her and stayed in her house for eight days, teaching her the word of God, so that most of the maids also believed, and there was great joy in the house.

## Chapter 40

<sup>1</sup> Thecla missed Paul and searched for him, sending messengers everywhere, and word came that he was in Myra. So she took some young men and maids, tied up her clothing, sewed her tunic into a cloak in a man’s style, and set out for Myra. She found Paul preaching the word of God and went up to him. He was stunned to see her and the crowd with her, and thought another temptation had come upon her. She realized this and told him, “I have been washed clean[^40-1-1], Paul. The one who worked with you for the gospel has worked with me for my washing.”

## Chapter 41

<sup>1</sup> Paul took her by the hand and brought her to the house of Hermias, where he listened to her whole story; he marveled at length, and the listeners gained strength and prayed for Tryphaena. Then Thecla got up and told Paul, “I am going to Iconium.” “Go,” said Paul, “and teach God’s word.” Tryphaena had sent plenty of clothing and gold along with her, so that she could leave some with Paul to serve the poor.

## Chapter 42

<sup>1</sup> She herself went off to Iconium. She entered the house of Onesiphorus and fell down on the floor where Paul had sat teaching the words of God, and she wept and said, “My God, and God of this house, where the light shone on me; Christ Jesus, Son of God, my helper in prison, my helper before governors, my helper in the fire, my helper among the beasts: you yourself are God, and to you be the glory forever. Amen.”

## Chapter 43

<sup>1</sup> There she found Thamyris dead and her mother still living. She called her mother to her and said, “Theocleia, mother, can you believe that the Lord is alive in heaven? If it is money you crave, it is the Lord who will give it to you, through me; and if it is your child you crave, here I am beside you.”

<sup>2</sup> And having given this testimony, she went off to Seleucia, and after enlightening[^43-2-1] many with the word of God, she fell asleep in a good sleep.
