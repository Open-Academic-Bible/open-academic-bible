# Acts of Thomas

A third-century narrative, probably from Syria, of the apostle Thomas's mission to India. It survives complete in Syriac and Greek and includes the "Hymn of the Pearl," one of the best-known early Christian poems. Its strong call to sexual abstinence made it popular with ascetic groups.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> In those days all of us apostles were together in Jerusalem: Simon called Peter and his brother Andrew; Zebedee’s son James and his brother John; Philip and Bartholomew; Thomas, and Matthew the tax collector; Alphaeus’s son James and Simon the Cananaean[^1-1-1]; and Judas, James’s brother[^1-1-2]. We shared out the regions of the world, so that each of us could travel to the area allotted to him, among the people to whom the Lord was sending him. Judas Thomas, also called Didymus[^1-1-3], drew India by lot. He did not want to go, claiming that his bodily weakness made travel impossible, and asking, “How can a Hebrew like me go to the Indians and announce the truth?” As he was pondering and protesting like this, the Savior appeared to him[^1-1-4] in the night and said, “Thomas, have no fear. Travel to India and proclaim the word there, for my grace goes with you.” Still he would not listen. “Send me anywhere you like,” he said, “but not there. I will not go to the Indians.”

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> While he was still muttering these thoughts, a merchant from India happened to be there. His name was Abbanes, and King Gundaphorus[^2-1-1] had sent him with instructions to purchase a carpenter and bring him back. The Lord noticed him strolling through the marketplace at noon and asked, “Are you looking to buy a carpenter?” “I am,” said the man. The Lord answered, “One of my slaves is a carpenter, and I am ready to sell him.” Saying this, he pointed to Thomas some way off, settled on three pounds[^2-1-2] of uncoined silver[^2-1-3], and drew up a contract: “I, Jesus, son of the carpenter Joseph, acknowledge that I have sold my slave, named Judas, to you, Abbanes, a merchant in the service of Gundaphorus, king of the Indians.” Once the deal was done, the Savior took Judas, also known as Thomas, and brought him to the merchant Abbanes. Seeing him, Abbanes asked, “Is that your owner?” “Yes,” replied the apostle, “he is my Lord.” “Then I bought you from him,” said Abbanes. The apostle said nothing.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Early the next morning the apostle prayed and begged the Lord, and said, “I will go where you want, Lord Jesus. Let your will be done.” He went to Abbanes the merchant, bringing nothing with him except his price. For the Lord had given it to him with the words, “Let your price be with you, together with my grace, wherever you go.” The apostle found Abbanes just then loading his baggage on the ship, and began to help him load. When they had boarded and taken their seats, Abbanes began to question the apostle: “What trade do you know?” He said, “In wood I make plows, yokes, balance-beams[^3-1-1], boats, oars for boats, masts, and pulleys. In stone I make pillars, temples, and royal palaces.” Abbanes the merchant said to him, “That is exactly the kind of craftsman we need.” So they set sail. The wind was in their favor, and they sailed eagerly on until they reached Andrapolis[^3-1-2], a royal city.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> They left the ship and went into the city, and there all around them they heard the sound of pipes, water-organs[^4-1-1], and trumpets. The apostle asked, “What is this festival in the city?” The people there told him, “The gods have brought you too, so that you can join the feasting in this city. The king has an only daughter, and today he is giving her to a husband in marriage. The joy and the public celebration you see today are for her wedding. The king has sent heralds to announce everywhere that everyone must attend the wedding, rich and poor, slave and free, foreigner and citizen. Anyone who declines[^4-1-2] and fails to attend will answer to the king.” When Abbanes heard this, he said to the apostle, “Let us go as well, so that we do not offend the king, especially since we are foreigners.” The apostle said, “Let us go.” They found lodging in an inn, rested a little, and went to the wedding. When the apostle saw that everyone was reclining at table, he reclined too, in the middle of the room. Everyone stared at him as if he were a stranger who had come from a foreign land. Abbanes the merchant, as his master, reclined in a different place.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> When they had eaten and drunk, the apostle tasted nothing. The people around him asked, “Why did you come here if you will neither eat nor drink?” He answered, “I came here for something greater than food and drink, and to carry out the king’s wishes. Heralds proclaim what the king says, and whoever does not listen to the heralds will be liable to the king’s judgment[^5-1-1].” After they had eaten and drunk, garlands and perfumed oil were brought in. Each guest took some oil. One smeared it on his face, another on his beard, another on other parts of his body. But the apostle anointed only the crown of his head[^5-1-2], put a little on his nostrils, let some drip into his ears, dabbed it on his teeth, and carefully rubbed it over his heart. The garland brought to him, woven from myrtle and other flowers, he took and put on his head. In his hand he took a reed branch[^5-1-3] and held it. The flute-girl was going around everyone with her pipes in her hands, playing. When she came to where the apostle was, she stood over him and played toward his head for a long time. That flute-girl was a Hebrew by birth.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> While the apostle gazed at the floor, one of the wine-pourers stretched out an arm and struck him. The apostle lifted his eyes, looked straight at his attacker, and said, “My God will pardon you for this injustice in the world to come, but in the present world he will display his wonders through you, and before long I will watch the very hand that hit me being dragged away by dogs.” After saying this he started to chant this song: “The girl is the daughter of light[^6-1-1], and the splendor of kings dwells and rests upon her. Her appearance is delightful, bright with shining beauty. Her robes are like the flowers of spring, and a sweet perfume drifts out from them. On her head sits the king, feeding with his ambrosia those who rest upon him. Truth is set upon her head, and her feet show forth joy. Her mouth is open, and it suits her well: thirty-two[^6-1-2] are the ones who sing her praise. Her tongue is like the door’s curtain, which stirs for those who go in. Her neck takes the shape of a stair that the first builder[^6-1-3] made. Her two hands signal and point toward the dance of the blessed ages, announcing it, and her fingers show the gates of the city. Her bridal room is radiant, breathing the scent of balsam and every spice, and sending out the sweet odor of myrrh and spikenard[^6-1-4]. Inside, myrtles and many fragrant flowers are laid out, and the doorposts[^6-1-5] are decorated with reeds.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> “Her groomsmen surround her, seven in number, whom she herself has chosen. Her bridesmaids are seven, and they dance before her. Twelve in number are those who stand before her and serve her, fixing their gaze and their attention on the bridegroom so that they may be enlightened by the sight of him. They will be with him forever in that eternal joy, and will be at that wedding where the great[^7-1-1] gather, and will stay at the feast of which the eternal ones are counted worthy. They will put on royal clothing and wear shining robes, and both will be in joy and exultation, and glorify the Father of all. They received his radiant light and were illuminated by the sight of their lord. They received his ambrosia[^7-1-2] as food, which never runs short, and they drank of the wine that gives them neither thirst nor craving. With the living Spirit they glorified and sang praise to the Father of truth and the Mother of wisdom[^7-1-3].”

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> When he had sung and finished this song, everyone present looked at him. He fell silent. They noticed that his appearance had changed[^8-1-1], but they could not understand what he had said, since he was a Hebrew and had spoken in Hebrew. Only the flute-girl heard it all, because she was a Hebrew by birth. She moved away from him and played for the others, but her eyes kept returning to him, for she was very fond of him as a man of her own people, and he was also better looking than anyone else there. When the flute-girl had finished playing for everyone, she sat down facing him, gazing intently at him. He, however, looked at nobody and paid attention to nobody, but kept his eyes fixed on the ground, waiting for the moment when he could leave. Meanwhile the cupbearer who had struck him went down to the spring to draw water. There happened to be a lion there, and it killed him and left him lying where he fell, with his limbs torn apart. Dogs promptly seized the pieces, and one black dog took his right hand in its mouth and carried it into the dining hall[^8-1-2].

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> When they saw it they were all stunned, and began asking whose hand it was that was missing from among them. When it became clear that it was the hand of the cupbearer who had struck the apostle, the flute-girl smashed her pipes and threw them down, went to the apostle’s feet, sat down, and said, “This man is either a god or an apostle of God[^9-1-1]. I heard him say in Hebrew to the cupbearer, ‘I will soon see the hand that struck me dragged along by dogs.’ You have just seen it for yourselves. It happened exactly as he said.” Some believed her, and some did not. The king heard about this, came to the apostle, and said, “Get up and come with me, and pray for my daughter. She is my only child, and today I am giving her in marriage.” The apostle did not want to go with him, because the Lord had not yet been revealed to him there. But the king took him against his will to the bridal chamber to pray for the couple.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> The apostle stood and began to pray, saying, “My Lord and my God, fellow traveler of your slaves[^10-1-1], guide and director of those who believe in you, refuge and rest of the afflicted, hope of the poor and ransomer of captives, physician of souls lying sick, and savior of all creation, you who give life to the world and strengthen souls, you know what is to come, and you bring it about through us. Lord, you reveal hidden mysteries and make known words that cannot be spoken. Lord, you are the planter of the good tree, and by your hands all good works are brought forth. Lord, you are in all things, pass through all things, and are present in all your works, made known by the activity of them all. Jesus Christ, compassionate son and perfect savior, Christ, son of the living God, undaunted power that overthrew the enemy, voice heard by the rulers, who shook all their powers, envoy sent from on high who descended as far as Hades[^10-1-2], you who opened its doors and led out those who had been shut up for many ages in the storeroom of darkness[^10-1-3], and showed them the way up to the heights. I beg you, Lord Jesus, as I offer this petition on behalf of these young people, that you would do for them what helps them, benefits them, and serves their good.” He laid his hands on them and said, “The Lord will be with you,” and left them there and went away.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> The king requested the groomsmen to leave the bridal room. After all had gone out and the doors had been closed, the bridegroom raised the curtain[^11-1-1] of the chamber to lead his bride to himself. There he saw the Lord Jesus in the likeness of Judas Thomas, talking with the bride, the very apostle who shortly before had blessed them and left. The bridegroom asked him, “Did you not leave ahead of everyone? How have you ended up here?” The Lord replied, “I am not Judas, the one called Thomas. I am his brother[^11-1-2].” The Lord sat down on the couch, told them to take the stools, and began to speak to them.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> “Children, bear in mind what my brother told you, and who he put you in the care of. And understand this: if you break away from this filthy partnership[^12-1-1], you will become holy temples, pure, released from afflictions and pains, seen and unseen, and you will not take on the cares of livelihood and offspring, which end in ruin. If you do have many children, you turn greedy and grasping on their account, beating orphans and defrauding widows, and in doing so you bring the worst punishments on yourselves. Most children turn out to be good for nothing, troubled by demons, some openly and some secretly. They become epileptic[^12-1-2], or half-withered, lame, deaf, mute, paralyzed, or simple-minded. Even if they are healthy, they will again be useless, committing worthless and disgusting acts. They turn up in adultery, murder, theft, or sexual immorality[^12-1-3], and in all of these you will be broken. But if you are convinced, and keep your souls pure for God, living children[^12-1-4] will be yours, whom these injuries do not touch, and you will be free from worry, living an untroubled life without grief or anxiety, waiting to obtain that marriage which cannot decay and which is real. You will attend as groomsmen in it, going into that chamber filled with immortality and light.”

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> When the young people heard this, they believed the Lord and handed themselves over to him. They kept away from filthy desire[^13-1-1], and stayed in that place through the night. The Lord left their presence after telling them, “The grace of the Lord will be with you.” At daybreak the king arrived, had a table set, and brought it in before the bridegroom and the bride. He found them sitting opposite each other, the bride’s face uncovered[^13-1-2] and the bridegroom looking very cheerful. The mother went up to the bride and said, “Why are you sitting like this, child? Have you no shame? You act as if you had been living with your husband for a long time.” Her father said, “Do you not even cover yourself, because of your great love for your husband?”

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> The bride answered, “Father, it is true that I am in great love. I pray to my Lord that the love I felt this night will stay with me, and I will ask for the man I came to know[^14-1-1] today. So I will no longer cover myself, because the mirror of shame[^14-1-2] has been taken away from me. I am no longer ashamed or embarrassed, because the work of shame and embarrassment has gone far from me. I am not bewildered, because bewilderment did not stay with me. I am cheerful and glad, because the day of my joy was not disturbed. I have despised this husband and this marriage that pass away before my eyes, because I have been fitted to a different marriage. I did not unite with a man of the moment, whose end is lust and bitterness of soul, because I have been yoked to a true husband.”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> While the bride was still saying more than this, the bridegroom spoke up: “I thank you, Lord, who were proclaimed through the foreign man[^15-1-1] and found among us. You put me far from corruption and sowed life in me. You freed me from this disease, so hard to cure and treat and lasting for ever, and established in me a sound and sober health. You showed yourself to me and revealed to me everything about my condition. You redeemed me from falling and brought me to something better. You set me free from passing things and judged me worthy of what is immortal and eternal. You humbled yourself to reach me and my smallness, so that you could present me to your greatness and unite me to yourself. You did not hold back your own compassion from me, who was perishing, but showed me how to search for myself and to know who I was, and who and how I am now, so that I may become again what I was[^15-1-2]. I did not know you, but you looked for me. I did not understand you, but you took me to yourself. I have felt you, and now I cannot forget you. Love for you seethes in me. I cannot speak of you as I should. What I am able to say about you is brief and very little, and does not match your glory. But you do not blame me for speaking boldly to you even about what I do not know, for it is because of your love that I say even this.”

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> When the king heard this from the bridegroom and the bride, he tore his clothes and said to those standing near him, “Go out quickly and search the whole city. Seize that sorcerer[^16-1-1] who came to this city at an evil hour, and bring him to me. I led him into my house with my own hands, and I told him to pray for my unlucky daughter. Whoever finds him and brings him to me, I will give anything he asks.” They went out and searched but did not find him, because he had already sailed away. They also went to the inn where he had stayed, and there they found the flute-girl weeping and distressed that he had not taken her with him. They told her what had happened with the young couple. She was overjoyed to hear it, put away her grief, and said, “Now I too have found rest here.” She got up and went to them, and stayed with them for some time, until they had also taught the king. Many of the brothers and sisters[^16-1-2] gathered there too, until they heard the report that the apostle had come to the cities of India and was teaching there. They went and joined him.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> When the apostle arrived in the cities of India together with the merchant Abbanes, Abbanes went to pay his respects to King Gundaphorus, and reported on the carpenter he had brought along. The king, delighted, ordered him to be brought before him. Once the apostle had entered, the king asked, “What is your trade?” “Carpentry and construction,” replied the apostle. “And what can you make out of wood, and what out of stone?” asked the king. The apostle said, “Out of wood I make plows, yokes, balance-beams[^17-1-1], pulleys, ships, oars, and masts; out of stone, pillars, temples, and royal halls.” “Could you build me a palace?” said the king. “Yes,” he answered. “I can build and equip it; that is why I came, to build and do carpentry.”

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> The king took him and went outside the city gates. On the road he began discussing with him the building of the palace and how the foundations should be laid, until they came to the place where he wanted the building to stand. He said, “I want the building to be here.” The apostle said, “Yes, this place is suitable for building.” The site was wooded, and there was plenty of water there. The king said, “Start building.” But the apostle said, “I cannot start building at this time of year.” “When can you start?” the king asked. The apostle said, “I will begin in Dius and finish in Xanthicus[^18-1-1].” The king was amazed and said, “Every building is put up in summer. Can you build and complete a palace in the middle of winter?” The apostle said, “It has to be this way. There is no other way.” The king said, “Then if that is what you have decided, draw me a plan of how the work will go, since I will not be back here for some time.” The apostle took a reed and marked out the plan, measuring the site. He set the doors to face the rising sun, toward the light, and the windows toward the west, to the winds. He put the bakehouse[^18-1-2] on the south side, and the water channel[^18-1-3] for the household on the north. The king looked at it and said to the apostle, “You truly are a craftsman, and it is right for you to serve kings.” He left him a great deal of money and went away.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> From time to time the king sent silver and provisions, both for the apostle’s own upkeep and for the rest of the workmen. Thomas took everything he received and administered it, traveling around the towns and nearby villages, distributing it and providing acts of piety[^19-1-1] for the poor and the afflicted, and giving them relief. He said, “A king knows how to win royal rewards, but for now the poor must be given rest.” After this the king dispatched an envoy to the apostle with a letter: “Let me know how far you have got, what I should send you, and what you need.” The apostle sent back word that the palace was built[^19-1-2] and only the roof remained. When the king heard it, he sent more gold and unstamped silver[^19-1-3], with a letter: “If the palace stands complete, let the roof go on.” The apostle said to the Lord, “Lord, I thank you in every way, because you died for a little while so that I might live in you forever, and because you sold me so that through me you could free many.” He never stopped teaching and refreshing the afflicted. He told them, “The Lord has made this provision for you, and he himself supplies each person’s food. He is the feeder of orphans and manager of widows’ affairs, and for all who are afflicted he becomes relief and rest.”

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> When the king returned to the city, he asked his friends about the palace that Judas, also called Thomas, was building for him. They told him, “He has neither built a palace nor done anything else that he promised to do. He goes around the cities and regions, gives everything he has to the poor, teaches a new and single God[^20-1-1], heals the sick, drives out demons, and does many other amazing things. We think he is a magician[^20-1-2]. Yet his compassion and his cures, which he performs without payment, and also his simplicity, gentleness, and faith, show that he is either a righteous man or an apostle of the new God whom he proclaims. For he fasts constantly and prays, eats only bread with salt, drinks only water, wears one cloak in fair weather and in winter alike, takes nothing from anyone, and gives away to others what he has.” When the king heard this, he rubbed his face with his hands and shook his head for a long time.

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> He sent for the merchant who had brought the apostle, and for the apostle, and said to him, “Have you built me the palace?” “Yes, I have built it,” he said. The king said, “When, then, shall we go and look at it?” The apostle answered, “You cannot see it now, but you will see it when you depart from this life.” The king was furious and ordered the merchant and Judas, also called Thomas, to be put in chains and thrown into prison, until he could investigate and find out who had been given the king’s money, and then destroy him together with the merchant. The apostle went off to the prison rejoicing, and said to the merchant, “Do not be afraid of anything. Only believe in the God proclaimed through me, and you will be set free from this world, and from the coming age you will receive life.” The king meanwhile considered what death to put them to. He decided to skin them alive and burn them with fire. That same night the king’s brother Gad[^21-1-1] fell ill. Because of the grief and the insult the king had suffered, he was greatly weighed down, and he sent for the king and said, “Brother, I entrust my household and my children to you. I am grieved by the abuse that has been done to you, and now I am dying. If you do not bring punishment down on the head of that magician, you will not give my soul rest in Hades[^21-1-2].” The king said to his brother, “I have been thinking all night[^21-1-3] how to put him to death. This is what seemed best to me: to skin him and burn him in the fire, along with the merchant who brought him.”

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> As they were talking, the soul of his brother Gad left him. The king grieved deeply for Gad, because he loved him greatly, and ordered that he be laid out in royal garments of great price. Meanwhile angels took the soul of Gad, the king’s brother, and carried it up to heaven. They showed him the various places and dwellings and asked, “Where would you like to live?” As they approached the house that the apostle Thomas had built for the king, Gad caught sight of it and said to the angels, “Please, my lords, allow me to stay in one of the rooms on the ground floor[^22-1-1] here.” They replied, “You cannot live in this house.” “Why not?” he asked. They said, “This is the palace that the Christian[^22-1-2] built for your brother.” He said, “Then I beg you, my lords, let me return to my brother and buy this palace from him. He does not know what it is like, and he will sell it to me.”

## Chapter 23

<sup>1</sup> Then the angels let Gad’s soul go. While they were putting the burial robe on him, his soul returned to him, and he told those standing around, “Summon my brother, because I have one request to make of him.” They at once carried the good news to their king: “Your brother has revived.” The king sprang up and hurried to his brother with a large crowd. He went in and stood beside the bed, dumbstruck, unable to say a word. His brother said, “Brother, I know and am convinced that if anyone had asked you for half of your kingdom for my sake, you would have given it. So I ask for one favor: sell me something I want from you.” The king replied, “What is it that you want me to sell you?” “Swear to me that you will do it,” said Gad. The king swore: “Anything of mine[^23-1-1] you ask for, I will give you.” Gad said, “Sell me that palace of yours in the heavens.” The king said, “How could I own a palace in heaven?” Gad answered, “The one built for you by that Christian who is now in jail, the one the merchant bought from someone called Jesus and brought to you. I mean the Hebrew slave you wanted to punish, thinking he had cheated you. I was upset over him and died, and now I have revived.”

## Chapter 24

<sup>1</sup> Then the king, coming to his senses[^24-1-1], understood the things that mattered to him and the eternal blessings to come. He said, “I cannot sell you that palace. But I pray to enter it, live there, and be counted worthy of its inhabitants. If you really want to buy a palace like that, look, the man is alive and will build you one better than that.” At once he sent and brought the apostle out of prison, together with the merchant who had been locked up with him. He said, “I beg you, as a man begging the servant of God[^24-1-2], to pray for me, and to plead with him whose servant you are, that he will forgive me and overlook what I did to you or planned to do. Make me worthy to be a resident of that dwelling for which I did no work, though you built it for me by your labor alone, with the grace of your God working with you. Let me too become a servant and serve this God whom you proclaim.” His brother also fell down before the apostle and said, “I beg and implore you before your God that I may be made worthy of his service and ministry, and that I may be granted a share in what his angels showed me.”

## Chapter 25

<sup>1</sup> The apostle, overcome with joy, said, “I give thanks to you, Lord Jesus, because you have revealed your truth among these people. You alone are the God of truth, and no other. You know everything that most people do not know. Lord, in everything you show compassion and spare human beings. People, because of the error in them, overlooked you, but you did not overlook them. Now, at my asking and begging, receive the king and his brother, and mix them into your flock. Cleanse them with your washing and anoint them with your oil, free from the error that surrounds them. Guard them from the wolves, and bring them to your pastures. Give them drink from your ambrosial spring[^25-1-1], which is never muddied and never runs dry. They plead with you and beg you, and want to become your helpers and servants[^25-1-2]. For this they are even willing to be pursued by your enemies, hated and insulted and killed on your account, just as you suffered all this for us, so that you might gain us for yourself, since you are Lord and truly the good shepherd[^25-1-3]. Grant them to have their confidence in you alone, and the help and hope of their salvation that they await from you alone. Let them be made firm in your mysteries, receive the perfect blessings of your gifts and presents, flourish in serving you, and bear their fruit to the full in your Father.”

## Chapter 26

<sup>1</sup> King Gundaphorus and his brother Gad were fully devoted to the apostle. They followed him and never left his side. They too supplied those in need, giving to all and giving rest to all. They begged him to let them also receive the seal of the word[^26-1-1], saying, “Our souls are free of other concerns and eager for God, so give us the seal. For we heard you say that the God you proclaim knows his own sheep by his seal.” The apostle said to them, “I am glad, and I ask you to receive this seal and share with me in this thanksgiving[^26-1-2] and blessing of the Lord, and to be made complete through it. For this is the Lord and God of all, Jesus Christ, whom I proclaim, and he is the Father of truth, in whom I have taught you to believe.” He told them to bring oil, so that they might receive the seal through the oil. They brought the oil and lit many lamps, for it was night[^26-1-3].

## Chapter 27

<sup>1</sup> The apostle stood up and sealed them[^27-1-1]. The Lord was revealed to them through a voice, saying, “Peace to you, brothers.” They heard only his voice and did not see his form, for they had not yet received the added sealing of the seal. The apostle took the oil, poured it over their heads, anointed and rubbed them, and began to say: “Come, holy name of the Christ, above every name. Come, power of the Most High, and perfect compassion. Come, highest gift. Come, compassionate mother[^27-1-2]. Come, partnership of the male[^27-1-3]. Come, you who reveal the hidden mysteries. Come, mother of the seven houses[^27-1-4], so that your rest may be in the eighth house. Come, elder of the five members[^27-1-5]: mind, thought, reflection, consideration, reasoning. Share with these young men. Come, Holy Spirit, and cleanse their kidneys[^27-1-6] and their heart, and put the added seal on them in the name of the Father and Son and Holy Spirit.” When they had been sealed, a youth appeared to them holding a burning torch, so that their lamps grew dim in the glare of its light. He went out and was no longer visible to them. The apostle said to the Lord, “Your light, Lord, is more than we can take in. We cannot bear it, for it is greater than our sight.” When dawn came and it grew light, he broke bread and made them sharers in the thanksgiving of Christ. They rejoiced and were glad. Many others also came to believe, were added to them, and entered the refuge of the Savior.

## Chapter 28

<sup>1</sup> The apostle did not stop preaching. He said to them, “Men and women, boys and girls, young men and virgins, in the prime of life and in old age, whether slaves or free, keep away from sexual immorality[^28-1-1], greed, and slavery to the belly[^28-1-2]. For under these three headings all lawlessness comes about. Sexual immorality cripples the mind and darkens the eyes of the soul. It stands in the way of the body’s proper conduct, turns the whole person to weakness, and throws the whole body into sickness. Insatiable greed puts the soul in fear and shame. It lives inside the body and plunders what belongs to others, and it is under this suspicion: if it gives back what belongs to others, it is put to shame in front of the owners. Slavery to the belly throws the soul into worries, anxieties, and griefs, fretting in case it comes to lack these things and is seen to be far from them. When you are rid of these things you become free of anxiety, grief, and fear, and what the Savior said stays with you: ‘Do not worry about tomorrow[^28-1-3], for tomorrow will worry about itself.’ Remember also the saying that was spoken earlier: ‘Look at the ravens[^28-1-4], and consider the birds of the sky: they neither sow nor reap nor gather into storehouses, and God provides for them. How much more for you, people of little faith?’ Instead, welcome his coming, put your hopes in him, and believe in his name. For he is the judge of the living and the dead[^28-1-5], and he gives to each person according to their deeds. At his coming and later appearing no one will have any defense to offer, as someone about to be judged by him who claims not to have heard. For his heralds are proclaiming in the four regions[^28-1-6] of the world. So change your minds and believe the promise. Take up the yoke of gentleness and the light burden[^28-1-7], so that you may live and not die. Acquire these things; hold on to them. Come out of the darkness, so that the light may welcome you. Come to the one who is truly good, so that you may receive grace from him and set his sign in your souls.”

## Chapter 29

<sup>1</sup> When he had said this, some of those standing there said to him, “It is time for a debtor to pay back what he owes[^29-1-1].” He answered them, “The lord of the debt always wants to take back more than is due, but let us give him what is owed.” He blessed them, took bread, oil, vegetables, and salt, blessed these, and gave them out. He himself kept to his own fast[^29-1-2], for the Lord’s day was about to dawn. That night, as he slept, the Lord came and stood at his head and said, “Thomas, get up early, bless everyone after the prayer and the service, and go two miles along the eastern road. There I will show you my glory. Because of your going, many will take refuge with me, and you will expose the nature and power of the enemy.” The apostle got up from sleep and said to the brothers and sisters who were with him[^29-1-3], “Children and brothers and sisters, the Lord wants to accomplish something through me today. Let us pray and ask him that nothing at all stand in our way toward him, but that now, as always, his purpose and will be done through us.” When he had said this, he laid his hands on them and blessed them. He broke the bread of the thanksgiving[^29-1-4] and gave it to them, saying, “May this thanksgiving be for you a source of compassion and mercy, not of judgment and retribution.” And they said, “Amen.”

## Chapter 30

<sup>1</sup> The apostle set out to go where the Lord had told him. Near the second mile, after turning a little off the road, he saw the body of a handsome young man lying there. He said, “Lord, is this why you brought me out to come here, so that I would see this trial[^30-1-1]? Then let your will be done as you wish.” He began to pray: “Lord, judge of the living and the dead, of the living who stand here and the dead who lie here, master and father of all. You are father not of souls that are in bodies but of those that have left them, and of souls that are in defilements[^30-1-2] you are master and judge. Come in this hour in which I call on you, and show your glory on this man lying here.” Then he turned to those following him and said, “This did not happen for nothing. The enemy has been at work and caused it, so that he could attack through him. And notice that he has not used any other kind of creature[^30-1-3], or worked through any other animal, but only one that is subject to him.”

## Chapter 31

<sup>1</sup> As he said this, an enormous serpent[^31-1-1] emerged from its den, striking its head and whipping its tail against the ground, and said to the apostle in a loud voice, “I will tell you openly why I killed this man, since you have come here for this reason, to expose what I have done.” “Yes, speak,” said the apostle. The serpent said, “A beautiful woman lives in the village opposite. As she walked past me, I saw her and desired her. I went after her and kept watch, and I found this young man kissing her. He had intercourse with her[^31-1-2] and did other shameful things with her. It would have been simple to tell you this, for I know you are the twin of Christ[^31-1-3], who is always wiping out our kind. But I did not want to frighten her, so I did not kill him at that moment. I waited, and when he came by in the evening, I hit him and killed him, all the more because he had the nerve to do this on the Lord’s day[^31-1-4].” The apostle questioned him: “Tell me from what seed you come and what family you belong to.”

## Chapter 32

<sup>1</sup> The serpent said, “I am a creeper of the creeping kind, and a harmful one of the harmful kind. I am the son of the one who harmed and struck the four brothers who stand[^32-1-1]. I am the son of the one who sits on a throne in the part of the world under heaven, and takes back what is his from those who borrow. I am the son of the one who girds the sphere[^32-1-2]. I am the kin of the one who is outside the ocean, whose tail lies in his own mouth[^32-1-3]. I am the one who went through the fence into the garden[^32-1-4] and spoke with Eve what my father commanded me to say to her. I am the one who set Cain on fire to kill his own brother, and because of me thorns and thistles grew on the earth. I am the one who threw the angels[^32-1-5] down from above and tied them to the desires of women, so that children of the earth would be born from them and I could carry out my will in them. I am the one who hardened Pharaoh’s heart to kill the children of Israel and enslave them under a yoke of harshness. I am the one who led the crowd astray in the desert when they made the calf. I am the one who inflamed Herod and fired up Caiaphas to the lie of false accusation before Pilate, for this was fitting for me. I am the one who stirred up Judas and bought him to hand Christ over to death. I am the one who lives in the abyss of Tartarus[^32-1-6] and holds it, but the Son of God wronged me against my will, and chose his own out of my possession. I am the kin of the one who is going to come from the east, to whom also power is given to do what he wants on the earth.”

## Chapter 33

<sup>1</sup> After the serpent had said this, with the whole crowd listening, the apostle raised his voice and said, “Be quiet now, you shameless creature, and be ashamed as you die completely. The end of your destruction has arrived. Do not dare to talk about what you did through those who have become subject to you. In the name of that Jesus who, even until now, struggles against you for the sake of his own people, I order you to suck out your poison, which you injected into this man, draw it up, and take it from him.” The serpent said, “The time of our end has not yet come, as you say. Why do you force me to take back what I injected into him, and to die before my time? When my own father draws up and sucks out what he threw into creation, then his end will come.” The apostle said to him, “Then show me now the nature of your father[^33-1-1].” The serpent came up, put his mouth on the young man’s wound, and sucked the venom out of him. Little by little the color of the young man, which had been like purple, grew pale, while the serpent swelled. When the serpent had drawn all the venom into himself, the young man leaped up and stood, ran to the apostle’s feet, and fell down. The swollen serpent burst with a crack and died, and his poison and gall[^33-1-2] poured out. In the place where his poison was spilled, a great chasm opened, and the serpent was swallowed up in it. The apostle said to the king and his brother, “Put workmen to work and fill that place, lay foundations, and build houses on top, so that it may be a dwelling for strangers[^33-1-3].”

## Chapter 34

<sup>1</sup> The young man said to the apostle with many tears, “How have I wronged you? You are a man who has two forms[^34-1-1], and wherever you wish, there you are found, held back by nobody, as I see. For I saw that man who stands beside you, as I stand beside you now, who said to you, ‘I have many marvels to show through you and great works to carry out through you, for which you will receive a reward. You will make many people live, and they will be at rest in eternal light like God’s children. So bring this young man to life, who was struck down by the enemy, and be his guardian[^34-1-2] at all times.’ You have done well to come here, and you will do well to go back to him, and he will never leave you at any time. As for me, I have become free of anxiety and reproach. Light has dawned on me out of the anxiety of the night, and I am at rest from the labor of the day. I have been freed from the one who provoked me to do these things, and I sinned against the one who taught me the opposite. I have lost that kinsman of the night who forced me to sin by his deeds, and have found my kinsman of the radiant light. I have lost the one who darkens and dims his own subjects so that they do not know what they are doing, so that they will not be ashamed of their works and give them up, and their deeds come to an end. I have found the one whose works are light and whose deeds are truth, and whoever does these things does not regret them. I have been freed from the one for whom falsehood is a lasting companion, whose covering is darkness in front, while shameless disgrace trails behind in idleness. I have found the one who shows me good things so that I can take hold of them, the son of truth, a kinsman of concord, who drives away the mist and gives light to his own creation, heals its wounds, and overthrows its enemies. But I beg you, man of God, let me see him again, and look on the one who has now become hidden from me, so that I may also hear his voice. Its wonder I cannot describe, for it does not belong to the nature of this bodily organ[^34-1-3].”

## Chapter 35

<sup>1</sup> The apostle answered him, “If you keep away from the things you have come to know, as you have said, and learn who it is that did this in you, and become a listener to him whom you now seek with your burning love, you will see him and be with him forever, rest in his rest, and be in his joy. But if you become careless toward him and turn back to your earlier deeds, and neglect that beauty and that radiant face that has now been shown to you, and let the shining of his light, which you now long for, slip away from you, you will lose not only this life[^35-1-1] but the one to come as well. You will go off to the one you said you had lost[^35-1-2], and you will no longer see the one you said you had found.”

## Chapter 36

<sup>1</sup> When the apostle had said this, he went toward the city, holding the young man’s hand. He said, “What you have seen, child, is little out of the many things God has. He does not give us good news about these visible things, but promises us greater things. As long as we are in the body, we cannot say or show what he will give to our souls. If we say he gives us light, that is the visible light, and we have it. If we say wealth, which exists and shows itself in this world, we can name it and we do not need it, since it has been said, ‘A rich person will enter[^36-1-1] the kingdom of heaven only with difficulty.’ If we mention the clothing worn by those who live in luxury in this life, it has a name, and it has been said, ‘Those who wear soft clothing[^36-1-2] are in the houses of kings.’ If we mean costly banquets, we have received a command to be on guard against them, not to be weighed down[^36-1-3] by drinking bouts, drunkenness, and the cares of ordinary life, as it was said, ‘Do not worry about your life[^36-1-4], what you will eat or what you will drink, or about your body, what you will wear, for life is more than food and the body more than clothing.’ If we mean this temporary rest, a judgment has been set for that as well. But we speak about the world above, about God and angels, about watchers[^36-1-5] and holy ones, about ambrosial food and the drink of the true vine, about clothing that lasts and does not wear out, about things that no eye has seen[^36-1-6] and no ear has heard, which have not even entered the heart of sinful people, what God has prepared for those who love him. These are what we talk about and what we announce as good news. So you also believe in him, so that you may live, and put your trust in him, and you will not die. For he is not won over by gifts for you to bring to him, and he does not need sacrifices for you to sacrifice to him. Look to him and he will not overlook you. Turn back to him and he will not abandon you. His beauty and loveliness will make you long to love him. But he does not allow you to turn yourself away from him.”

## Chapter 37

<sup>1</sup> As the apostle was saying this to the young man, a large crowd gathered around. Looking up, the apostle saw them straining to see him and climbing up onto raised spots[^37-1-1]. He said to them, “People who have come to the gathering of Christ[^37-1-2] and want to put your faith in Jesus, learn from this. You see that unless you stand up higher, you cannot see me, small as I am, and cannot observe me, though I am like you. If you cannot see someone like yourselves unless you lift yourselves a little off the ground, how will you see the one who spends his time on high and is now to be found in the depths, unless you first raise yourselves out of your earlier way of living? Rise above your useless actions, your desires that do not last, the wealth you leave behind here, the possessions from the earth that wear out, the clothes that rot, the beauty that ages and fades, and, more than all that, the entire body in which all of these are kept, which grows old and turns to dust, going back to its own nature. For it is the body that holds all of these things up. Instead, put your faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, whom we proclaim, so that your hope may rest on him and you may have life in him for ever and ever. May he travel with you[^37-1-3] through this land of error and be a port for you in this noisy sea. May he be a gushing spring for you in this parched country, a storehouse[^37-1-4] full of food in the place where people are hungry, rest for your souls, and a healer of your bodies too.”

## Chapter 38

<sup>1</sup> When the crowd that had gathered heard this, they wept and said to the apostle, “Man of God, we do not dare to say that we belong to the God you proclaim, because the deeds we have done are foreign to him[^38-1-1], and do not please him. But if he feels compassion for us, shows us mercy, and rescues us, overlooking our earlier deeds and setting us free from the evils we committed while in error, and if he does not hold them against us or remind us of our earlier sins, we will become his servants and will do his will to the end.” The apostle answered them, “He does not condemn you, nor does he count against you the sins you committed while in error. He overlooks the offenses[^38-1-2] you committed out of ignorance[^38-1-3].”

## Chapter 39

<sup>1</sup> While the apostle was still standing in the road, speaking to the crowd, the foal of a wild donkey[^39-1-1] came up and stood in front of him. Opening its mouth[^39-1-2], it said, “Twin of Christ, apostle of the Most High, fellow initiate[^39-1-3] in the secret word of Christ, recipient of his hidden sayings, partner of God’s Son, you who were free and became a slave[^39-1-4], and who, sold, brought many into freedom. Relative of the great family that condemned the enemy and ransomed its own, you who have become the reason many in the land of the Indians have life. You came to people who had gone astray, and through your appearance and your godly words they are now turning to the God of truth who sent you. Get on, sit on my back, and rest until you reach the city.” The apostle replied, “O Jesus Christ, you who understand perfect compassion[^39-1-5]! O stillness and quiet, now spoken even by creatures without reason! O secret rest, shown in action, our savior and provider, who watch over us and give us rest in bodies not our own! Savior of our souls! Fountain that is sweet and never fails, spring that is steady, clean, and never muddied! Protector and helper in the fight for your own slaves, who turn the enemy aside and chase him from us, who fight many battles on our behalf and make us victorious in everything! Our true and unbeaten athlete, our holy and triumphant commander! Glorious one, who give your own a joy that never passes away and a comfort with no sorrow in it at all! Good shepherd, who handed yourself over[^39-1-6] for your own sheep, conquered the wolf, ransomed your own lambs, and led them to good grazing! We glorify and sing to you, and your unseen Father, and your Holy Spirit, and the mother of all creation[^39-1-7].”

## Chapter 40

<sup>1</sup> When the apostle had said this, the whole crowd present looked at him, expecting to hear how he would answer the colt. The apostle stood for a long time as if stunned, then looked up to heaven and said to the colt, “Whose are you, and what are you? What has come from your mouth is astonishing and strange, and hidden from most people.” The colt answered, “I am from the family that served Balaam[^40-1-1], and your Lord and teacher also sat on one[^40-1-2] that belonged to my family. I too have now been sent to give you rest as you sit on me, so that I may receive faith[^40-1-3], and be given that share which I am about to gain through the service I give you. When I have served you, it will be taken up from me.” The apostle said to him, “He who has granted you this gift is able to bring it to completion in you and in those of your family. As for this mystery, I am weak and powerless.” And he did not want to sit on the colt. But the colt begged and pleaded with him, swearing an oath[^40-1-4], to be blessed by him. Then the apostle climbed up and sat. They went along with him, some walking ahead of him and some following. Everyone ran, wanting to see how it would end and how he would dismiss the colt.

## Chapter 41

<sup>1</sup> When he got near the city gates, he climbed down and said, “Go away and stay safe where you were.” The colt at once collapsed on the ground at the apostle’s feet and died. Everyone there was sad and said to the apostle, “Give him life and raise him up.” He answered them, “I could raise him in the name of Jesus Christ, but in every way this is best[^41-1-1]. The one who gave him the power of speech could have kept him from dying. I am not raising him, not because I am unable, but because this is what is useful and good for him.” He instructed those present to dig a grave and bury its body, and they did what he commanded[^41-1-2].

## Chapter 42

<sup>1</sup> The apostle went into the city, with the whole crowd following him. He was thinking of going to the parents of the young man he had restored to life after the serpent killed him, for they had begged him earnestly to come to them and enter their house. But a very beautiful woman suddenly let out a loud cry: “Apostle of the new God[^42-1-1], who came to India, and slave[^42-1-2] of that holy and only good God! Through you he is proclaimed as the savior of the souls who come to him, and through you the bodies of those tormented by the enemy are healed. You have become the cause of life for everyone who turns to him. Order me to be brought before you so that I may tell you what has happened to me. Perhaps through you I may find hope, and these people standing beside you may have better hope in the God you proclaim. I have been tormented by the adversary[^42-1-3] for no small time, five years now. At first I sat quietly, as a woman does[^42-1-4], and peace surrounded me on every side. I worried about nobody, for I had no concern for anything else.”

## Chapter 43

<sup>1</sup> One day, as I was coming out of the bath, a man met me, agitated and distraught. His voice and speech seemed to me extremely faint and thin. He stood in front of me and said, “You and I will be joined in one love, and we will unite as a husband does with a wife.” I answered him, “I have never had relations with my betrothed, because I refused to marry. How can I give myself to you, when you want me to join you in adultery?” With that I walked on, and I said to the maid who was with me, “Did you see that young man and his shamelessness, how he spoke so boldly to me without any embarrassment?” But she said, “I saw an old man talking to you.” When I got home and had eaten, my mind began to suspect something, especially because he had appeared to me in two shapes[^43-1-1]. With this thought in my head I fell asleep. He came that night and forced his filthy union on me. I saw him by day as well, and ran from him, but at night, which suited his kind[^43-1-2], he would come and abuse me. Now, as you see, he has been pestering me for five years and has not left me alone. But I know and am convinced that demons, spirits, and avenging powers are subject to you and tremble at your prayer. So pray for me and drive away the demon who torments me, so that I too may be free, be gathered back into my original nature, and receive the gift given to my kin.

## Chapter 44

<sup>1</sup> The apostle said, “What relentless evil! What impudence from the enemy! O envious one, who is never at rest! O ugly one, who subjects the handsome! O creature of many shapes, who appears however he wishes, though his essence cannot change! O deceitful and faithless one! O bitter tree, whose fruits are just like itself! O devil, who fights on behalf of what belongs to others[^44-1-1]! O error that lives by shamelessness! O wickedness that slithers like a snake, and this one’s own kin[^44-1-2]!” As the apostle said this, the hostile one came and stood before him, though no one saw him except the woman and the apostle. Then, speaking in a very loud voice,

## Chapter 45

<sup>1</sup> he said in everyone’s hearing, “What do we have to do with you, apostle of the Most High? What do we have to do with you, slave of Jesus Christ? What do we have to do with you, counselor of the holy Son of God? Why do you want to destroy us before our time has come? Why do you want to take away our authority? Until this very hour we had hope and a season still left to us. What do we have to do with you? You have authority over what is yours, and we over what is ours. Why do you want to use tyranny against us, when you yourself teach others not to use it? Why do you reach for what belongs to others, as if you were not content with your own? Why do you make yourself like the Son of God[^45-1-1], who wronged us? You resemble him closely, as if you had been born from him. We had thought we would put him under the yoke, as we did the rest, but he turned and took us into his power. We did not know him. He deceived us with his ugly appearance, his poverty, and his need; seeing him like that, we took him for a man clothed in flesh, not knowing that he is the one who gives life to people. He gave us authority over what is ours, and told us, in the time we have, not to let go of it but to go about among our own. But you want more than is right and more than you were given: to take us over and wear us down.”

## Chapter 46

<sup>1</sup> After saying this, the demon began to cry. “I am leaving you, my loveliest spouse,” he said, “whom I found long ago and in whom I found rest. I give you up, my steadfast sister, my beloved, with whom I was pleased. I do not know what to do, or whom to call to listen and defend me. I know: I will travel to places where this man’s reputation has never been heard, and maybe I will call you, my beloved, by another name[^46-1-1].” Raising his voice, he said, “Remain in peace, since you have fled for safety to one greater than I. I will go and look for someone like you, and if I find nobody, I will come back to you. For I know that while you are close to this man you have shelter in him, but once he departs you will return to how you were before he showed up. You will forget him, and I will find my moment and my freedom to speak. For now, I am afraid of the name of the one who rescued you.” Having said this the demon disappeared, and after he had gone, only fire and smoke could be seen there. All who stood nearby were thunderstruck.

## Chapter 47

<sup>1</sup> When the apostle saw this, he said to them, “That demon showed nothing strange or foreign, only his own nature, in which he will also be burned up. The fire will consume him, and his smoke will be scattered.” Then he began to pray: “Jesus, the hidden mystery that was revealed to us, you are the one who has shown us a great many mysteries. You set me apart from all my companions and spoke three words[^47-1-1] to me that set me on fire, and I cannot tell them to others. Jesus, a man murdered, dead, and buried! Jesus, God from God, savior who makes the dead live and heals the sick! Jesus, who was in need like a poor man[^47-1-2], yet saves as one who needs nothing; who hunted fish for the morning meal[^47-1-3] and the evening meal, and satisfied everyone with a little bread! Jesus, who rested from the weariness of the road as a human being, and who walked on the waves like a god!”

## Chapter 48

<sup>1</sup> “Jesus Most High, voice that rose from the perfect compassion[^48-1-1], savior of all, right hand of the light, who turn the evil one back upon his own nature and gather all his nature into one place; you of many shapes, only-begotten, firstborn among many brothers and sisters[^48-1-2], God from the Most High God, the human being despised until now! Jesus Christ, you who do not overlook us when we call on you, who became the occasion of human life for all, who for our sake was judged and shut in prison, and who free all who are in chains; who were called a deceiver, and ransom your own from deception: I ask you on behalf of these who stand here and believe in you. They long to receive your gifts. They are full of hope in your help and hold fast to your refuge in your greatness. They are ready to listen to the words we speak to them. Let your peace come and settle in them, and let it renew them from their former deeds, so that they strip off the old self[^48-1-3] with its practices and put on the new one that I now announce to them.”

## Chapter 49

<sup>1</sup> He laid his hands on them and blessed them, saying, “The grace of our Lord Jesus will be with you forever.” And they said, “Amen.” The woman begged him, “Apostle of the Most High, give me the seal[^49-1-1], so that enemy cannot come back to me.” So he had her come close, laid his hands on her, and sealed her[^49-1-2] in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Many others were sealed along with her. Then the apostle told his deacon to set out a table. They put out a bench they found there, and he spread a linen cloth over it and laid the bread of blessing on it. The apostle stood beside it and said, “Jesus, you have counted us worthy to share in the thanksgiving of your holy body and blood[^49-1-3]. Here we are, daring to approach your thanksgiving and to call on your holy name. Come and share with us[^49-1-4].”

## Chapter 50

<sup>1</sup> He began to say, “Come, perfect compassion[^50-1-1]. Come, fellowship of the male. Come, you who know the mysteries of the chosen one. Come, you who share in all the struggles of the noble athlete. Come, stillness[^50-1-2] that reveals the great things of all greatness. Come, you who bring hidden things to light and make the unspeakable plain, holy dove who bears the twin young. Come, hidden mother. Come, you who are plain to see in your deeds and who give joy and rest to those joined to you. Come and share with us in this thanksgiving that we celebrate in your name, and in the love meal[^50-1-3] for which we have gathered at your call.” After saying this he marked the bread with a cross, broke it, and began to hand it out. First he gave it to the woman, saying, “May this bring you forgiveness of sins and of eternal offenses[^50-1-4].” After her he gave it to all the others who had received the seal[^50-1-5].

## Chapter 51

<sup>1</sup> There was a young man[^51-1-1] who had committed a forbidden act. He came forward and took the thanksgiving into his mouth, and both his hands withered, so that he could no longer lift them to his mouth. The people standing there saw him and reported what had happened to the apostle. The apostle called him and said, “Tell me, child, and do not be ashamed, what you have done and why you came here. The Lord’s thanksgiving has exposed you. This gift, as it passes among many, heals above all those who come with faith and love, but it has dried you up, and what has happened did not happen without some cause.” The young man, exposed by the Lord’s thanksgiving, came and fell at the apostle’s feet and begged, “I have done an evil thing, though I thought I was doing something good. I was in love with a woman who lived at an inn outside the city, and she loved me too. When I heard you and believed that you were proclaiming a living God, I came and received the seal[^51-1-2] from you along with the others. You said, ‘Whoever shares in a defiled union, especially adultery, will not have life with the God I proclaim.’ Because I loved her very much, I begged her and urged her to live with me in chastity and pure conduct, as you yourself teach, but she refused. When she would not agree, I took a sword and killed her, for I could not bear to see her commit adultery with another man.”

## Chapter 52

<sup>1</sup> On hearing this the apostle said, “O mad union, how far you carry shamelessness! O uncontrollable desire, how did you drive this man to such acts? O serpent’s work, how you rage at your own people!” He then asked for water to be brought to him in a bowl. When it came, he said, “Come, you waters from the living waters, you who are from what truly is[^52-1-1], sent to us; rest sent to us from the rest; saving power coming from that power that overcomes all things and bends them to its own will: come and take up residence in these waters, so that the gift of the Holy Spirit may reach its fulfillment in them.” To the young man he said, “Go and rinse your hands in this water.” When he had washed them, they were restored. The apostle asked him, “Do you trust in our Lord Jesus Christ, that he can do all things?” He replied, “I am of little account, but I trust. I did this thinking it was something good. As I told you, I pleaded with her, but she refused to listen to me and to keep herself pure.”

## Chapter 53

<sup>1</sup> The apostle said to him, “Come, let us go to the inn where you committed this deed, and see what took place.” The young man led the way, ahead of the apostle. On reaching the inn, they found her lying there. When the apostle saw her he was saddened, for she was a lovely girl. He ordered her to be carried into the center of the inn; they put her on a cot[^53-1-3], took her out, and laid her down in the middle of the inn’s yard. The apostle placed his hand on her and began, “Jesus, you appear to us always, for you wish us to seek you at every time. You yourself gave us this authority to ask and receive, and you not only allowed it but also taught us to pray. Our bodily eyes do not see you, but you are never hidden from the eyes of our soul; your appearance is concealed, yet your works make you visible to us. Through your many works we have recognized you as far as we can hold, and you have handed us your gifts with no limit, saying, ‘Ask and it will be given to you[^53-1-2]; seek and you will find; knock and it will be opened to you.’ So we make our request, aware of our sins[^53-1-1]. We do not ask you for riches, gold, silver, property, or anything else that grows from the earth and returns to it. What we ask and plead for is this: that you raise up this woman who lies here, by your power and in your holy name, so that those standing by may glorify you and believe.”

## Chapter 54

<sup>1</sup> After making the sign[^54-1-1] over the young man, he said to him, “Go, take hold of her hand and say to her: ‘I killed you with iron, by my own hands, and by my own hands, in the faith of Jesus, I raise you.’” The young man went up and stood by her and said, “Christ Jesus, I have put my faith in you.” Then he turned to the apostle Judas Thomas and said, “Join me in prayer, so that my Lord, the one I am calling on, will come to help me.” He put his hand in her hand and said, “Come, Lord Jesus Christ: give life to her, and to me the guarantee of faith[^54-1-2] in you.” As soon as he pulled her hand, she sprang up and sat there, staring at the great crowd around her. She also saw the apostle standing opposite, left the cot, leapt out, fell at his feet, and grasped his clothing. “Please, my lord,” she said, “where is the other one who was with you, who would not let me remain in that dreadful, cruel place, but gave me over to you with the words, ‘Take charge of this woman so she can be completed and later brought together into her own place’?”

## Chapter 55

<sup>1</sup> The apostle said to her, “Tell us where you have been.” She answered, “You who were with me, to whom I was handed over: do you want to hear?” And she began: “A man took charge of me[^55-1-4], repulsive to look at, black from head to foot, his clothing filthy. He led me away to a place with many chasms, from which a great stench and a loathsome smell rose. He made me look down into each chasm. In the first[^55-1-1] I saw a blazing fire, and wheels of fire were running there, and souls hung on those wheels, smashing against one another. There was a great deal of screaming and wailing, and no one to set them free. The man told me, ‘These souls are of your own kind. They were handed over to punishment and torment for a number of days, and then others are brought in to replace them, and these in turn go to another place. These are the ones who exchanged the union of man and woman[^55-1-2].’ Then I looked more closely and saw infants piled on top of one another, struggling as they lay one over another. He answered me, ‘These are the children of those people, and that is why they were put here, as a witness against them[^55-1-3].’”

## Chapter 56

<sup>1</sup> “He led me to another chasm[^56-1-4]. I looked down and saw mud and worms welling up, with souls rolling around in it, and the loud grinding of teeth could be heard coming from them. The man said to me, ‘These are the souls of women who abandoned their husbands, committed adultery with others, and were brought to this torment.’ He showed me another chasm. I looked into it and saw souls hanging, some by the tongue, some by the hair, some by the hands, and some upside down by the feet, smoking with smoke and sulfur. The man who was with me explained, ‘The souls hanging by the tongue are slanderers[^56-1-1], who spoke false and shameful words and felt no shame. Those hanging by the hair are the ones who never blushed[^56-1-2], who had no modesty at all and went about the world with heads uncovered. Those hanging by the hands are the ones who seized and stole what belonged to others. They never gave anything to people of modest means[^56-1-3] and never helped the afflicted. They did this because they wanted to take everything, and they gave no thought at all to justice or the law. Those hanging upside down by the feet are the ones who ran quickly and eagerly along evil roads and disorderly paths. They did not visit the sick and did not escort those leaving this life. That is why each soul receives back what it has done.’”

## Chapter 57

<sup>1</sup> “Again he led me away and showed me a very dark cave, breathing out a heavy stench. Many souls peered out of it, wanting to get some of the air, but their guards would not let them look out. The man with me said, ‘This is the prison of the souls you saw. When each of them has completed the punishments for what she did, others come after her. Some are used up completely, and some are handed over to other punishments[^57-1-1].’ Those guarding the souls in the dark cave said to the man who had brought me, ‘Give her to us, so that we can put her in with the others until the time comes for her to be handed over to punishment.’ He answered them, ‘I will not give her to you. I am afraid of the one who handed her to me. I was not ordered to leave her here, and I am taking her back with me until I receive a command about her.’ He took me and led me up to another place, where people were being cruelly tortured. Then the one who looks like you[^57-1-2] took me and handed me over to you, telling you, ‘Take this woman, for she is one of the flock that has gone astray.’ So I was taken by you, and now I am here in front of you. I beg and plead with you that I may not go to those places of punishment that I saw.”

## Chapter 58

<sup>1</sup> The apostle said, “You have heard what this woman described. These are not the only punishments; there are others worse than these. If you do not turn to this God whom I proclaim, and keep away from your former works and the deeds you did without knowledge, you will find your end in these punishments. So believe in Jesus Christ, and he will forgive you the sins you committed before this, cleanse you of all the bodily desires that stay behind on the earth, and heal you of the failings that follow after you, go away with you, and are found before you. Each one of you, take off the old self and put on the new[^58-1-1], and leave behind your former way of life and conduct. Those who steal must steal no longer[^58-1-2], but must live by toiling and working. Adulterers must no longer practice sexual immorality, so that they do not hand themselves over to eternal punishment, for with God adultery is far worse than other evils. Put away greed, lying, drunkenness, and slander, and do not repay evil with evil[^58-1-3]. All of this is foreign and alien to the God I proclaim. Live instead by faith, gentleness, holiness, and hope, in which God delights, so that you may become his own, receiving from him the gifts that only a few people receive.”

## Chapter 59

<sup>1</sup> So all the people believed and gave their souls in obedience to the living God and to Christ Jesus, delighting in the blessed works of the Most High and in his holy service. They brought large sums of money for the support of widows, for he had gathered the widows together in the cities and sent supplies to every one of them by his own deacons, both clothing and food. He himself never stopped preaching and telling and showing them that this is Jesus the Christ, about whom the scriptures had proclaimed that he would come, be crucified, and be raised from the dead after three days. Beginning with the prophets[^59-1-1], he went on to show them clearly what concerned the Christ: that he had to come, and that in him everything foretold about him would be fulfilled. His fame spread through all the cities and regions. Everyone who had sick people or people troubled by unclean spirits brought them, and some they even laid on the road where he was about to pass, and he healed them all by the power of the Lord. Then all those he had healed said together in one voice, “Glory to you, Jesus, who gave healing to all of us equally through your slave[^59-1-2] and apostle Thomas. Now we are well and glad, and we ask to belong to your flock and be counted among your sheep. Receive us, Lord, and do not hold against us our offenses and the earlier failings we committed in ignorance.”

## Chapter 60

<sup>1</sup> The apostle said, “Glory to the only-begotten of the Father. Glory to the firstborn of many brothers and sisters[^60-1-1]. Glory to you, defender and helper of those who come to your refuge: you who never sleep and who wake those who sleep, who live and give life to those lying in death. God Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, redeemer and helper, refuge and rest of all who labor in your work, who give healing to those who for the sake of your name bear the burden of the day and the scorching heat[^60-1-2]: we thank you for the gifts you gave us, for the help you granted us, and for your plan of salvation[^60-1-3] that came to us from you.”

## Chapter 61

<sup>1</sup> “Bring these things to completion in us, all the way to the end, so that we may have confidence in you. Look on us, because for your sake we left our homes and our families, and for your sake we have gladly and willingly become strangers. Look on us, Lord, because we left our own property for you, so that we might gain you, the possession that cannot be taken away. Look on us, Lord, because we left our blood relatives, so that we could be mixed into your family. Look on us, Lord, who left our fathers, our mothers, and those who raised us, so that we might see your Father and be filled with his divine delight. Look on us, Lord, because for your sake we left our wives in the flesh[^61-1-1] and our earthly offspring, so that we might share in that lasting and true partnership, and bring forth true offspring, whose nature is from above, whom no one can take from us, with whom we remain and who remain with us.”

## Chapter 62

<sup>1</sup> While the apostle Judas Thomas was announcing God’s word across India, a commander[^62-1-2] serving King Misdaeus[^62-1-1] approached him and said, “News has reached me that you accept no pay from anyone, but give what you own to the needy. If you took wages I would have sent you a considerable amount and not come myself, for the king does nothing apart from me. My possessions are many; I am wealthy, one of the richest men in India. And I have never harmed anyone, yet the reverse has happened to me. I have a wife and a daughter by her. I am very fond of her, as nature asks, and have never sampled another woman. It happened that a wedding was held in our city, and the hosts were dear friends of mine. They came to me and urged me, inviting her and her daughter. Since they were such close friends, I was unable to decline. So I sent her, although she was unwilling to go, and I sent many servants along with them. They went off, she and her daughter, wearing a great deal of finery.”

## Chapter 63

<sup>1</sup> When evening came and it was time to leave the wedding, I sent lamps and torches to meet them. I stood in the road, watching for the moment when she would arrive and I would see her with my daughter. As I stood there, I heard the sound of wailing, and “Woe to her!” rang out from every mouth. My slaves[^63-1-1] came to me with their clothes torn, to report what had happened. “We saw a man,” they said, “and a boy with him. The man laid his hand on your wife and the boy on your daughter, and the women ran away from them. We struck at the pair with our swords, but our swords fell to the ground. In that same hour the women collapsed, grinding their teeth and beating their heads on the ground. Knowing this, we came to tell you.” When I heard this from the slaves, I tore my clothes and struck my face with my hands, and as if I had gone mad I ran down the road. I went and found them thrown down in the marketplace[^63-1-2]. I picked them up and brought them to my house, and after a long while they came to their senses, recovered, and sat up.

## Chapter 64

<sup>1</sup> I began to question my wife. “What has happened to you?” She said to me, “Do you not know what you did to me? I begged you not to send me to the wedding, because I was not well in my body. As I went along the road, near the aqueduct where the water ran, I saw a black man standing opposite me, grunting and tossing his head at me[^64-1-1], and a boy like him standing beside him. I said to my daughter, ‘Look at those two ugly men, whose teeth are like milk and whose lips are like soot.’ We left them and went on past the aqueduct. At sunset, as we left the wedding and were passing by with the young men, we came close to the aqueduct and my daughter saw them first. She slipped away and ran to me. After her, I too saw them coming toward us, and we fled from them. But the boys who were with us[^64-1-2] struck us and knocked down both me and my daughter.” As she told me this, the demons came upon them again and threw them down. From that hour they have been unable to go outside, shut up in one room or the next[^64-1-3]. I suffer greatly and am distressed because of them, for the demons throw them down wherever they find them, and strip them naked. I beg and plead with you before God: help me and have mercy on me. For three years now a table has not been set in my house, and my wife and my daughter have not sat down at table. I grieve above all for my wretched daughter, who has seen nothing good at all in this world.

## Chapter 65

<sup>1</sup> When the apostle heard this from the commander, he was deeply sorry for him. He said to him, “Do you believe that Jesus heals them?” “Yes,” said the commander. The apostle said, “Then entrust yourself to Jesus, and he himself will heal them and help them.” The commander said, “Show him to me, so that I can plead with him and believe in him.” The apostle said, “He is not seen by these bodily eyes. He is found with the eyes of the mind[^65-1-2].” So the commander raised his voice and said, “I believe you, Jesus, and I beg and plead with you: help the little faith I have in you.” The apostle ordered Xenophon[^65-1-1] the deacon to bring everyone together. When the whole crowd had assembled, the apostle stood in their midst and said,

## Chapter 66

<sup>1</sup> “My children, brothers and sisters[^66-1-1], you who have believed in the Lord, stay firm in this faith, bringing the good news of Jesus, whom I have announced to you, and keep your hopes fixed on him. Do not fall away from him, and he will not abandon you. While you sleep, in this sleep that weighs down all who lie in it, he does not sleep but keeps watch over you. When you are sailing at sea, in danger, with no one able to help, he walks on the water and comes to your aid. I am leaving you now, and it is unclear whether I will see you again in the flesh. So do not become like the people of Israel, who looked away from their shepherds for a short while and stumbled. I am leaving the deacon Xenophon with you in my place, for he, like me, proclaims Jesus. Neither he nor I am anything, but only Jesus. I too am a human being clothed in a body, a son of man like one of you. I have no wealth, as some do. Wealth condemns those who own it; it is utterly without worth[^66-1-2] and is left behind on the earth from which it came, while the offenses and stains of sin that come to people through it go away with them. Rich people are rarely found giving alms. But the merciful, and those who are humble in heart[^66-1-3], will inherit the kingdom of God. Beauty does not last for people, and those who trust in it will be put to shame when old age suddenly overtakes them. Everything has its season: in season it is loved, in season it is hated. So let your hope be in Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who is always loved and always longed for. Remember us as we remember you. For if we do not carry out the burden of the commandments, we are not worthy to be heralds of this name, and later we will pay the penalty on our own heads[^66-1-4].”

## Chapter 67

<sup>1</sup> When he had prayed together with them, remaining a long time in prayer and petition, and had placed them in the Lord’s care, he said, “Lord, master of every soul that lives in a body; Lord, Father of the souls that rest their hopes on you and look for your mercies; you who buy your own people back from error and release from slavery and ruin those who obey and come to your shelter: be with the flock of Xenophon. Anoint it with holy oil[^67-1-1], heal its sores, and keep it safe from the wolves that snatch.” Laying his hand on them, he said, “The Lord’s peace will rest on you and will accompany us on our way.”

## Chapter 68

<sup>1</sup> So the apostle set out on his journey, and everyone went with him part of the way in tears, making him promise to keep them in mind in his prayers and not forget them. He mounted and took his seat in the carriage, with all the brothers and sisters left behind. The commander[^68-1-1] came forward and roused the driver. “I ask and pray,” he said, “to deserve a seat below his feet. I will be his driver along this road, so that he in turn may guide me on that other road that few travel[^68-1-2].”

## Chapter 69

<sup>1</sup> When they had traveled about two miles, the apostle pleaded with the commander, made him get up, and had him sit down beside him, letting the driver go back to his own seat. As they went along the road, it happened that because of the great heat the animals became worn out and could not move at all. The commander became very upset and downcast, thinking of running on his own feet and bringing other animals for the carriage. But the apostle said, “Do not let your heart be troubled[^69-1-1] or afraid. Believe in Jesus Christ, whom I have announced to you, and you will see great wonders.” He looked and saw a herd of wild donkeys grazing beside the road. He said to the commander, “If you have believed in Christ Jesus, go to that herd of wild donkeys and say, ‘Judas Thomas, apostle of Christ the new God[^69-1-2], tells you: Let four of you come, whichever we need.’”

## Chapter 70

<sup>1</sup> The commander went, afraid, for there were many of them. As he left they came out to meet him, and when they were close he said to them, “Judas Thomas, apostle of the new God, commands you: Let four of you come, whichever I need.” When the wild donkeys heard this, they ran toward him with one accord, and when they arrived they bowed down to him[^70-1-1]. The apostle said to them, “Peace to you. Four of you, take the yoke in place of these animals that have given out.” Each of them came forward and pressed to be yoked. There were four stronger ones there, and they were yoked. Of the others, some went ahead and some followed. After they had gone a little way, he released the colts[^70-1-2], saying, “To you, fellow dwellers of the wilderness, I say: go back to your pastures. If I had needed all of you, all of you would have come with me. But now go to the place where you live.” And they went away quietly, until they were out of sight.

## Chapter 71

<sup>1</sup> As the apostle, the commander, and the driver went on, the wild donkeys pulled steadily and quietly, so as not to disturb the apostle of God. When they were near the city gate, they turned aside and stopped in front of the doors of the commander’s house. The commander said, “I am not able to explain what has happened, but when I have seen how it ends, then I will tell it.” The whole city came out when they saw the wild donkeys in the yoke. They had also heard the report that the apostle was going to stay there. The apostle asked the commander, “Where is your home, and where are you taking us?” He said to him, “You yourself know that we are standing at its doors, and these animals who came with you at your command know it better than I do.”

## Chapter 72

<sup>1</sup> After this he got down from the carriage. Then the apostle started to speak: “Jesus Christ, blasphemed in this land because they do not know you; Jesus, whose reputation is foreign in this city; Jesus, who receives[^72-1-1] every apostle in each land and city, and through whom all who deserve you are glorified; Jesus, who took on a form[^72-1-2] and became human, showing yourself to all of us lest you separate us from your own love: Lord, you gave yourself on our behalf, redeemed us with your blood[^72-1-3], and made us your precious possession. What can we give you, Lord, as a return for the life[^72-1-4] you gave for us? What we want is what you grant us: that we may ask you and live.”

## Chapter 73

<sup>1</sup> After he spoke, many people came together from all directions to see the apostle of the new God. The apostle spoke again: “Why are we just standing here? Lord Jesus, the hour has come. What do you need done? Order the fulfillment of what must happen.” The wife of the commander and his daughter were so crushed by the demons that their family believed they would never rise again. The demons did not allow them to take any share of anything, but flung them onto their beds, knowing nobody, up to the day the apostle arrived. He said to one of the wild donkeys in the yoke on the right, “Walk into the courtyard, stand inside, call out the demons, and say to them: Judas Thomas, apostle and disciple of Jesus Christ, orders you: Come out here! I was sent on your account, and to those of your family, to destroy you and chase you off to your own territory, until the end of time arrives and you descend into your own deep darkness.”

## Chapter 74

<sup>1</sup> The wild donkey went in, with a large crowd following, and said, “I am speaking to you, enemies of Jesus called Christ; to you, who close your eyes so as not to see the light, since your wretched nature cannot be turned toward the good. I am speaking to you, offspring of Gehenna[^74-1-2] and perdition, children of that one who until now has never ceased his evil, who ever renews his operations and whatever fits his own being. I am speaking to you, most brazen ones, who ruin yourselves. As for your destruction and end, what can I say, or how can I explain? I do not know, because there is so much that cannot be counted even by ear. Your acts are worse than the penalty kept in store for you[^74-1-1]. And you, demon, and your son who goes along with you, I address too, for now I am sent against you. But why say much about your nature and root, which you yourselves know and are brazen about? Judas Thomas, apostle of Christ Jesus, who has been sent here out of great love and goodwill, tells you: Step out in front of all the people gathered here, and tell me what family you come from.”

## Chapter 75

<sup>1</sup> At once the woman came out with her daughter, both half dead and disgraced. When the apostle saw them he was grieved, especially for the girl, and he said to the demons, “May no atonement or sparing ever come to you, for you do not know how to spare or to show pity. But in the name of Jesus, withdraw from them and stand to one side.” When the apostle said this, the women fell down and lay as if dead, with no breath and no voice. The demon answered in a loud voice, “Have you come here again, you who mock our nature and our race? Have you come again, you who wipe out our craft? I suppose you will not let us exist on the earth at all. But this you cannot do at the present time.” The apostle guessed that this demon was the one who had been driven out of that other woman[^75-1-1].

## Chapter 76

<sup>1</sup> The demon said, “I beg you, allow me to go off and live wherever you wish, and to take orders from you, and then I will not fear the ruler who has authority over me. Just as you came to proclaim good news, so I came to destroy. Just as, if you do not carry out the will of the one who sent you, he puts the punishment on your head, so too, if I do not do the will of the one who sent me, before the season and the appointed time, I am sent back to my own nature. Just as your Christ helps you in what you accomplish, so my father helps me in what I accomplish. Just as he prepares vessels for you[^76-1-1], those worthy of your dwelling, so he searches out vessels for me, through which I complete his works. Just as he feeds and manages his subjects, so he prepares punishments and torments for me, along with the dwellings that have become mine[^76-1-2]. Just as he gives you eternal life as the payment for your work, so he gives me eternal destruction as the return for my deeds. Just as you find rest in your prayer, your good works, and your spiritual praises, so I find rest in murders, adulteries, and sacrifices made with wine on altars[^76-1-3]. Just as you turn people to eternal life, so I turn those who obey me to eternal destruction and punishment. You receive your own, and I receive mine.”

## Chapter 77

<sup>1</sup> When the demon had said this and much else, the apostle answered, “Jesus orders you and your son, through me, never again to enter a place where people live. Come out, go away, and make your home far from where humans dwell.” The demons said to him, “Your order to us is cruel. But what will you do about the ones now hidden from you? Those who made all the idols[^77-1-1] are happier with them than with you. Most people bow to many of them and carry out their wishes, offering sacrifices to them and presenting food, with libations of wine and water and with votive gifts[^77-1-2].” The apostle said, “These too will now be abolished along with their works.” The demons suddenly vanished, and the women lay on the ground like the dead, without a sound.

## Chapter 78

<sup>1</sup> The wild donkeys stayed facing one another, not separating. Then the one who had been given speech through the Lord’s power, while everyone was silent and looking to see what they would do, said to the apostle, “Why are you standing around doing nothing, apostle of Christ the Most High, who waits for you to ask him for the noblest lessons? Why hold back[^78-1-1]? Your teacher is willing to display his great works by your hands. Why are you still, herald of the hidden one? Your lord wants to make the unspeakable things known through you, keeping them for those who deserve to hear them. Why are you silent, you who perform great works in the Lord’s name? Your master pushes you forward and breeds courage in you. So have no fear: he will not let go of a soul that belongs with you by birth. Start calling on him, and he will gladly hear you. Why do you stand amazed at every one of his deeds and works? These are small things compared with what he has shown through them. What will you say, then, of his great gifts? You cannot finish telling them. Why do you marvel at the healings of the body[^78-1-2] that he carries out? You know the other healing of his, the firm and lasting one, which he brings forth out of his own being. Why do you fix your eyes on this fleeting life and think nothing about the eternal[^78-1-3]?”

## Chapter 79

<sup>1</sup> “To you in the crowd standing near, who expect the fallen women to be raised up, I say: trust the apostle of Jesus Christ. Trust the teacher of truth. Trust the one who reveals truth to you. Trust Jesus. Put your trust in the Christ who was born[^79-1-1], so that those who are born may live through his life; who was nursed through infancy so that perfection could be seen in his human nature. He gave instruction to his own disciples, for he is the teacher of truth and the tutor of the wise. He also presented the gift in the temple[^79-1-2], to show that all offering is made holy. This man is his apostle, the one who demonstrates truth. This is the one who does the will of him who sent him. But false apostles and prophets of lawlessness are coming, and their end will fit their actions. They proclaim and legislate that people should run from godlessness, yet they are always caught in sins; they wear clothes of sheep, but inside they are wolves who seize[^79-1-3]. They are not content with a single wife and ruin many women. Claiming to despise children, they ruin many young people[^79-1-4], and they will pay for it. They are not satisfied with their own property but want everything useful to serve them alone, claiming to be his disciples. Their mouths utter one thing while their hearts think another. They tell others to be on guard against the wicked, while they themselves do nothing good. They are considered moderate and tell others to keep away from sexual immorality, stealing, and greed, yet they behave this way in secret, teaching others not to do these things.”

## Chapter 80

<sup>1</sup> When the wild donkey had finished speaking, everyone stared at him. When he fell silent, the apostle said, “Jesus, what am I to think of your loveliness, and what can I say about you? I do not know; or rather, I am unable, for I cannot find words for it, Christ who are at rest, the only wise one[^80-1-1], who alone sees into hearts and grasps what the mind is reasoning. Glory be to you, gentle and merciful. Glory be to you, wise Word. Glory be to your compassion, brought forth for our sake[^80-1-2]; glory be to your mercy, spread out above us; glory be to your greatness, shrunk for our sake; to your kingship on high, lowered on our account; to your strength, diminished for us; to your divinity, which showed itself in the likeness of human beings for us; to your humanity, which died so that it might make us alive; to your rising from the dead, since by it our souls are raised up and given rest; glory and praise to your going up into the heavens, for by it you have shown us the road up to the heights, promising that we would take seats at your right hand and judge Israel’s twelve tribes[^80-1-3]. You are the Word of the Father from heaven. You are reason’s hidden light, the one who points out the road of truth, who hunts down the darkness and erases error.”

## Chapter 81

<sup>1</sup> After saying this, the apostle stood over the women and said, “My Lord and my God, I am not divided from you[^81-1-1], and I do not call on you in unbelief. You are always our helper, our support, and the one who sets us upright, breathing your own power into us, encouraging us, and giving confidence in love to your own slaves. I ask you: let these souls be healed and rise up and be as they were before they were struck by the demons.” As he said this, the women turned and sat up. The apostle told the commander to have his servants take them in[^81-1-2]. When they had gone inside, the apostle said to the wild donkeys, “Follow me.” At his word they followed as far as outside the gates, and when they were outside he said to them, “Go in peace to your pastures.” The wild donkeys left willingly. The apostle stood watching over them so that no one would harm them, until they had gone far off and could no longer be seen. Then the apostle returned with the crowd to the commander’s house[^81-1-3].

## Chapter 82

<sup>1</sup> It happened that a woman, the wife of Charisius, a close relative of the king, whose name was Mygdonia, came to watch and see this new name, the new God who was being proclaimed, and the new apostle who had arrived in their land. Her own slaves were carrying her, but because of the huge crowd and the cramped space they could not bring her to him. So she sent word to her husband to send her more attendants. They came and reached her, crushing the people and beating them. When the apostle saw this he said to them, “Why are you trampling people who come to hear the word, and who are so eager? You want to be near me, yet you stay far away. It was said of the crowd that came to the Lord, ‘You have eyes but do not see[^82-1-1], and ears but do not hear.’ And he told the crowds, ‘Let anyone with ears to hear, listen[^82-1-2],’ and, ‘Come to me[^82-1-3], all who toil and are heavily loaded, and I will give you rest.’”

## Chapter 83

<sup>1</sup> Looking at the men who were carrying her, he said to them, “The blessing and the counsel[^83-1-1] promised to those people were spoken about you as well, since you are the ones now weighed down. You know how to carry loads that are hard to bear, and you are driven along at her command. You never stop passing judgment on your neighbor, and you do not understand that these people feel just as you do, and that each of you has one and the same nature. Just as you know how to heap burdens on animals and pitilessly pile weights on them, so too those who have power over you assume that you are not human as they are. But whether slave or free, rich or poor, all are the same in this life. Wealth will not release the rich from any debt, and poverty will not rescue the poor from judgment. We have not been given a command we cannot carry out, nor has he laid on us burdens we cannot bear. He has not asked us for the kind of building that people construct, or to quarry stones and put up houses as your craftsmen do with their own skill. The command we have received from the Lord is this: that we must never do to another what displeases us[^83-1-2] when it is done to us, and that we must consider beforehand what kind of judgment awaits us there, and how great it is. We tend to judge our neighbor’s affairs, though we know very well how to manage our own.”

## Chapter 84

<sup>1</sup> “So I urge you to keep away from all of this[^84-1-1], first of all from adultery, for it is the starting point of every evil. Next keep away from theft, which lured Judas Iscariot[^84-1-2] and led him to hang himself. All who live in greed fail to see what they are doing. Keep away also from boasting and from every shameful act, especially those of the body, through which eternal condemnation comes. For this is the mother city[^84-1-3] of all evils. It also leads the proud into tyranny, dragging them down into the depths and getting them under its power, so that they cannot see what they are doing. That is why what they do is hidden from them.”

## Chapter 85

<sup>1</sup> “But you, become pleasing to God in everything good, in meekness and quietness[^85-1-1]. God spares such people, gives them eternal life, and makes death a thing of scorn. In gentleness[^85-1-2], which follows every good thing, they defeat every enemy and alone receive the crown of victory. In quietness they hold out a hand to the poor, fill the need of those who lack, and bring and share what they have with those in want, above all with those who live in holiness. This way of life is chosen by God and leads into eternal life. It is the mother city of all good things in God’s sight, for those who do not compete in Christ’s stadium will never gain holiness. Holiness appeared from God, putting an end to sexual immorality, overthrowing the enemy, and pleasing God. It is an undefeated athlete, with rank from God, praised by many. It is an envoy of peace, announcing peace. Whoever acquires it stays free of worry, pleasing the Lord and waiting for the time of release[^85-1-3]. For nothing improper is ever done, and it gives life, rest, and joy to all who possess it.”

## Chapter 86

<sup>1</sup> “Meekness has subdued death and put it under its authority. Meekness has enslaved the enemy. Meekness, besides, is a good yoke. Meekness fears no one and does not stand against the many. Meekness is peace, joy, and the exultation of rest. So stay in holiness, accept freedom from worry, and draw near to meekness, for in these three chief points the Christ I proclaim to you is pictured. Holiness is Christ’s temple, and whoever lives in it gains a dwelling place. He fasted for forty days and forty nights[^86-1-1], tasting nothing, and whoever guards holiness will live in it as on a mountain. Meekness is his pride. For he said to Peter, our fellow apostle, ‘Turn your sword back[^86-1-2] and return it to its sheath. If I had wanted to do this, could I not have called on my Father for more than twelve legions of angels?’”

## Chapter 87

<sup>1</sup> While the apostle was saying this, with the whole crowd listening, people trampled one another in the crush. Then the wife of Charisius, the king’s relative, leapt out of her carrying chair and threw herself on the ground before the apostle. She clutched his feet and begged, “Disciple of the living God, you have come to a deserted land. We live in a wilderness, like animals in the way we conduct ourselves. Now through your hands we shall be saved. So I beg you, take thought for me and pray for me, so that the compassion of the God you proclaim may come to me, and I may become his dwelling, be reconciled through prayer, hope, and faith in him, receive the seal[^87-1-1], become a holy temple, and have him live in me.”

## Chapter 88

<sup>1</sup> The apostle answered, “I make my prayer and plea for all of you, brothers who believe in the Lord and sisters who place their hope in Christ: may the word of God make its home in every one of you. The choice is not ours, since we hold no authority over them.” Then he began to speak to Mygdonia: “Rise from the ground and gather yourself[^88-1-1]. This borrowed finery will do you no good, neither your body’s beauty nor your garments, neither your reputation for high standing nor the might of this world, neither this defiling union with your husband, if you are cut off from true fellowship. The show of adornment comes to nothing, the body ages and changes, clothes wear out, and authority and lordship vanish with them, leaving a person answerable for the life many have led in them[^88-1-2]. The union that produces children passes away too, since it amounts to a condemnation. Only Jesus remains forever, and those who hope in him.” When he had said this, he told the woman, “Depart in peace; the Lord will count you worthy of his own mysteries[^88-1-3].” But she answered, “I fear leaving, in case you abandon me and go off to another nation.” The apostle replied, “Even if I go, I will not leave you by yourself, for Jesus in his compassion will be with you.” She fell to the ground, bowed before him, and returned home.

## Chapter 89

<sup>1</sup> Charisius, a relative of King Misdaeus, bathed, came upstairs, and reclined to dine. He asked where his wife was, since she had not come to meet him from her bedroom as she usually did. Her maids told him, “She is unwell.” He rushed in, went into the bedroom, and found her lying on the bed, covered up. He uncovered her and kissed her, saying, “Why are you so sad today?” She said, “I am unwell.” He said to her, “Why did you not keep up the dignity of a free woman and stay in your house? Instead you went out, listened to empty talk, and watched magical performances[^89-1-1]. Get up and dine with me, for I cannot eat without you.” She answered, “Today I must decline, for I am terribly afraid.”

## Chapter 90

<sup>1</sup> When Charisius heard this from Mygdonia, he refused to go out to dinner and ordered his household to bring her in to dine with him. When they led her in, they urged her to join him, but she begged off. Since she would not, he ate alone, saying to her, “For your sake I turned down dinner with King Misdaeus, and you would not dine with me?” She said, “Because I am unwell.” Then Charisius got up and, as he usually did, wanted to go to bed with her. But she said, “Did I not tell you that I am declining today?”

## Chapter 91

<sup>1</sup> After hearing this, he went off and lay down on a different bed. When he woke from his sleep he said, “My lady Mygdonia, listen to the dream I had. I saw myself reclining near King Misdaeus, with a table of every kind of food set beside us. I saw an eagle come down from heaven and snatch away two partridges from in front of me and the king, and it carried them into its own heart[^91-1-1]. Then it flew around above us and came back. The king commanded that a bow be loosed at it, but the eagle again snatched a dove and a turtledove from in front of us. The king sent an arrow after it, and the arrow passed through it from side to side without harming it. Unhurt, the eagle soared up to its own nest. I woke up afraid and troubled, because I had tasted the partridge, and I was not allowed to bring any more to my mouth.” Mygdonia said to him, “Your dream is a good one. You eat partridges every day, but this eagle had never tasted one until now.”

## Chapter 92

<sup>1</sup> At dawn Charisius got up and dressed, and put his left shoe on his right foot. He stopped and said to Mygdonia, “What is the meaning of this? First the dream, and now this happening.” Mygdonia said to him, “This too is not bad. It seems to me quite excellent, for from a bad thing it will turn out better.” He washed his hands and left to greet King Misdaeus.

## Chapter 93

<sup>1</sup> Mygdonia too rose early and went to greet the apostle Judas Thomas. She found him conversing with the commander and the whole crowd, advising them about the woman who had received the Lord in her soul and whose housemate she was[^93-1-1]. The commander said, “She is the wife of Charisius, relative of King Misdaeus. Her husband is a hard man; whatever he tells the king, the king obeys. He will not let her keep to the resolve she has declared. He has often praised her before the king, saying no other woman matches her in affection. So everything you say to her is foreign to her.” The apostle replied, “If the Lord has truly and securely dawned in her soul, and she has received the seed that was sown[^93-1-2], she will not worry about this passing life or fear death, and Charisius will not be able to harm her. The one she has welcomed into her soul is greater, if she has truly welcomed him.”

## Chapter 94

<sup>1</sup> Hearing this, Mygdonia told the apostle, “Truly, my lord, the seed of your words has taken root in me, and I will bear fruit worthy of that seed.” The apostle said, “Lord, souls confess you and thank you, for they are yours; bodies thank you too, which you thought fit to become dwelling places for your heavenly gift.” Then he said to all who stood by, “Blessed are the saints whose souls have never passed sentence on themselves; having won such souls, they are not divided against themselves. Blessed are the spirits of the saints, who have received the whole heavenly crown from the age set for them. Blessed are the bodies of the saints, for they were thought fit to become temples of God, so that Christ may dwell in them. Blessed are you, for you have the authority to release people from their sins[^94-1-1]. Blessed are you if you do not lose what you were handed, but depart rejoicing and take it up with you. Blessed are you saints, for you are allowed to ask and to receive[^94-1-2]. Blessed are you who are meek, for God has judged you fit to inherit the heavenly kingdom[^94-1-3]. Blessed are the meek, for you are the ones who have defeated the evil one. Blessed are the meek, for you will see the Lord’s face[^94-1-4]. Blessed are you who go hungry for the Lord[^94-1-5], for rest is stored up for you, and your souls already exult. Blessed are you who are calm, since you will be freed from sin and from the payment due to the living, the pure and the impure[^94-1-6].” When the apostle had said this with the whole crowd listening, Mygdonia was made firmer in faith, in the glory and greatness of Christ.

## Chapter 95

<sup>1</sup> Charisius, who was both kinsman and friend of the king, came home for the midday meal and found his wife absent from the house. He asked everyone in his household, “Where has your lady gone off to?” One of them answered, “She has gone to that foreigner.” When he heard this from his slave, he was furious with the rest of his household for not reporting the matter at once. He sat down to wait for her. When evening came and she walked into the house, he said to her, “Where were you?” She answered, “At the doctor’s.” He said, “Is that stranger a doctor?” She said, “Yes, he is a doctor of souls. Most doctors tend bodies that are wasting away, but he tends souls that do not decay.” Hearing this, Charisius was seething inside against Mygdonia over the apostle. But he gave her no answer, because he was afraid; she was his superior in both wealth and judgment. He went off to dinner while she went into her bedroom. He told the servants, “Summon her to dinner,” but she refused.

## Chapter 96

<sup>1</sup> When he learned that she would not come out of the bedroom, he went in and said, “Why do you refuse to dine with me, and perhaps to share my bed as usual? My suspicion grows on this point too. I have heard that this magician and deceiver teaches that no man should live with his own wife, and so he overturns what nature knows how to demand and what the deity himself established by law.” While Charisius spoke, Mygdonia kept silent. He addressed her again: “Mygdonia, my lady and companion, do not be misled by deceptive, hollow words, or by the magic I hear he practices in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Nobody in this world has ever been known to raise a corpse, yet the stories I hear about him say he raises the dead. As for the fact that he does not eat or drink, do not imagine he abstains out of righteousness. He does so because he possesses nothing. What else could he do, when he lacks even a day’s bread? He wears a single garment because he is poor. And his taking nothing from anyone: he knows he is a healer[^96-1-1], and this has certainly become his custom.”

## Chapter 97

<sup>1</sup> After Charisius said this, Mygdonia stayed silent like a rock, praying for the dawn to break so she could go to the apostle of Christ. Charisius left her and went off to dinner in low spirits; he was worried, because it was his habit to sleep with her. When he had gone out, she knelt down and prayed, “Lord God, master, merciful Father, savior Christ, give me the strength to overcome the shamelessness of Charisius, and let me keep the holiness in which you delight, so that through it I too may find eternal life.” After this prayer she lay down on the bed, covered up.

## Chapter 98

<sup>1</sup> When Charisius had finished dinner, he came and stood over her. She cried out, “You have no place with me any longer. My Lord Jesus, who is with me and rests in me, is stronger than you.” He laughed and said, “You do well to mock, speaking like this about that sorcerer. And you do well to laugh at him when he says, ‘You will have no life with God unless you purify them[^98-1-1].’” After saying this he tried to lie down with her. But she could not endure it and screamed bitterly, “I call on you, Lord Jesus; do not abandon me, for I have taken refuge in you. I have learned that you are the one who searches for those caught in ignorance and rescues those held in error. Now I ask this of you, whose fame I heard and in whom I believed: come to my aid and save me from the shamelessness of Charisius, so that his foulness does not gain mastery over me.” Then, striking her own hands, she fled from him naked. As she went out she pulled down the curtain of the bedroom, wrapped herself in it, and went to her old nurse, and there she fell asleep.

## Chapter 99

<sup>1</sup> Charisius spent the whole night in despair, striking his face with his hands. He wanted to go at that very hour and report to the king the violence that had come upon him. But he reasoned with himself, “If my terrible distress forces me to go to the king right now, who will bring me in to see him? I know that my arrogance, my pride, and my haughtiness have brought me to this dishonor and cast me down into this smallness, so that Mygdonia, my own sister[^99-1-1], would refuse any union with me and leave me. Even if the king himself were standing at my doors at this hour, I would not go out and answer him. No, I will wait until daybreak. I know that whatever I ask of the king, he grants me. I will speak about the madness of that stranger, who uses tyranny to cast down the great and distinguished into the depths. It does not grieve me that I have lost her company; I grieve for her, because her great soul has been diminished. She is a woman of such good bearing, whom no member of her household ever condemned, and she ran out of her own bedroom naked. I do not know where she has gone. Perhaps that sorcerer has driven her out of her mind, and she has run frantic into the marketplace looking for him. For nothing seems lovely to her any longer except him and what he has said.”

## Chapter 100

<sup>1</sup> After saying this he began to wail, “Woe to me, my wife, and woe to you as well, for I have lost you too soon. Woe to me, my dearest, for you are better than my whole family. I had neither son nor daughter by you, to give me comfort. You did not live with me even a full year before an envious eye took you[^100-1-1] away from me. What sort of envious eye did this? What wicked demon persuaded you? I would sooner have seen death take you than see this drama played out against me, and at the hands of a stranger who may even be a runaway slave. I will not let my feet rest until I destroy him and take vengeance for this night. I will not even try to please Misdaeus the king unless he grants me vengeance for the stranger’s life[^100-1-2]. The one behind all this is Siphor the commander, who brought him here. Because of him the stranger appeared in this place and stays with him. Many go in and out to him, and he teaches them a new teaching, saying that no one can live unless they are freed from all their possessions and renounce them as he has. He is eager to make many into his partners.”

## Chapter 101

<sup>1</sup> While Charisius was thinking these things over, day dawned. He rose, put on cheap clothes and shoes, and set out gloomy and downcast to greet the king. When the king saw him, he said, “What makes you so sad, and why do you arrive looking like this? I see that your face has changed too.” Charisius told the king, “I have a strange matter to lay before you, a new kind of ruin, brought from India by Siphor: a Hebrew magician who stays in his house and never leaves his side. Many go in to him, and he teaches them about a new god and lays on them new laws that have never been heard before. He says, ‘You cannot enter the eternal life that I proclaim to you unless you separate from your own wives, and the women likewise from their own husbands.’ As it happens, my wretched wife went to him and became a hearer of his words. She believed them, and in the night she left me and rushed off to the stranger. So send for Siphor and that magician who hides in his house, and bring punishment down on their heads, so that all of our nation will not be destroyed.”

## Chapter 102

<sup>1</sup> When Misdaeus, his friend, heard this, he told him, “Do not grieve, and do not lose heart. I will send for him and avenge you. You will have your wife again, and I will take vengeance as well for the others who cannot manage it themselves.” Then the king came out and sat on the tribunal, and from his seat ordered Siphor the commander to be called. The messengers went to his house and found him seated at the apostle’s right, with Mygdonia at his feet and the whole crowd hanging on the apostle’s words. Coming forward, the king’s men told Siphor, “Here you sit listening to empty talk, while King Misdaeus, in his anger, is considering your destruction on account of this magician and deceiver you took into your house.” On hearing this, Siphor was dismayed. It was not the king’s threat to himself that troubled him but the apostle, since the king’s opinion of him was the opposite of the truth. He said to the apostle, “I am grieved for you. From the start I told you that the woman is the wife of Charisius, the king’s relative and friend, that he will not permit her to carry out her promise, and that the king grants him whatever he requests.” The apostle said to Siphor, “Have no fear. Put your faith in Jesus, who pleads for all of us, for we have gathered around his place of refuge.” When Siphor heard this, he wrapped his cloak around him and set off for King Misdaeus.

## Chapter 103

<sup>1</sup> The apostle questioned Mygdonia, “What has caused your husband’s anger, so that he has devised all this against us?” She said, “It is because I did not hand myself over to his destruction. At this late stage he wanted to subdue me and subject me to the passion he serves. But the one to whom I entrusted my soul rescued me from his hands. I fled from him naked and slept at my nurse’s house. I do not know what came over him, or why he has schemed all this.” The apostle said, “This will do us no harm. Trust in Jesus, and he will turn back Charisius’s anger, his madness, and his frenzy. He himself will be your companion on the dreaded road[^103-1-1], and he himself will lead you into his kingdom. He will bring you into eternal life, giving you a boldness that neither passes away nor changes.”

## Chapter 104

<sup>1</sup> Siphor stood before the king, who questioned him: “Who is the magician you have lodged in your house, where is he from, and what does he teach?” Siphor answered, “Majesty, you should know how much toil and grief my friends and I endured over my wife, whom you know and many others remember, and over my daughter, whom I value above all I own. What an ordeal I suffered! I became a joke and a curse throughout our land. Then I heard of this man’s fame and went to him and begged for his help, and I took him and brought him here. On the way I witnessed amazing, astonishing things, and here many people heard of the wild donkey[^104-1-1] and the demon he drove out. He cured my wife and daughter, and they are well now. He did not ask for payment; what he demands is faith and holiness, so that people share with him in what he does. His teaching is to worship and revere one God, master of all things, and Jesus Christ his son, in order to gain eternal life. His food is bread and salt, his drink is water, taken from one evening to the next, and he prays at length. Whatever he asks his God for, he receives. He teaches that this God is holy, good, and powerful, and that his son Christ is life and the giver of life. So he urges those around him to approach him with holiness, purity, love, and faith.”

## Chapter 105

<sup>1</sup> When King Misdaeus heard this from Siphor, he sent many soldiers to the house of Siphor the commander, to bring Thomas the apostle and everyone found there. The men who were sent went inside and found him teaching a great crowd, with Mygdonia sitting at his feet. When they saw the large crowd around him, they were afraid. They went back to their king and said, “We did not dare say anything to him. A great crowd was around him, and Mygdonia sat at his feet, listening to what he said.” When King Misdaeus and Charisius heard this, Charisius leapt up from before the king, pulled along a large crowd[^105-1-1], and said, “I will bring him to you, king, and Mygdonia too, whose mind he has stolen.” He came to the house of Siphor the commander in a rage and found him teaching. Mygdonia was not there, for she had gone back to her house, knowing that her husband had been told she was there.

## Chapter 106

<sup>1</sup> Charisius shouted at the apostle, “Get up, you wicked man, you destroyer, enemy of my house! Your sorcery does not harm me, for I will bring your sorcery down on your own head.” As he said this, the apostle turned to him and said, “Your threats will rebound on you. You will not harm me at all, for the Lord Jesus Christ, in whom I place my hopes, is greater than you, your king, and every soldier of yours.” Charisius took a short cloak from one of his household and threw it around the apostle’s neck, saying, “Drag him away. Let me see whether his god can rescue him from my hands.” They dragged him off to King Misdaeus. The apostle stood in front of the king, who said to him, “Say who you are, and by what power you do all this.” The apostle stayed silent. The king ordered his attendants to give him one hundred and twenty-eight lashes[^106-1-1], then lock him in prison in chains. They bound him and took him away. The king and Charisius discussed how to put him to death, but the crowd worshiped him like a god. So they had it in mind to say, “The stranger insulted the king and is a deceiver.”

## Chapter 107

<sup>1</sup> As the apostle went off to the prison, rejoicing and exulting, he said, “I thank you, Jesus, because you have made me worthy not only of faith in you but also of enduring much for your sake. So I give you thanks, Lord, that you took thought for me and gave me endurance. I thank you, Lord, that because of you I have been called a sorcerer and a magician. Receive me, then, among the blessings of the modest, the rest of the weary, and the blessings of those whom people hate[^107-1-1], persecute, and insult, speaking evil words against them. For see, because of you I am hated. See, because of you I have been cut off from the many, and because of you they call me something I am not.”

## Chapter 108

<sup>1</sup> While he was praying, all the prisoners watched him, and they begged him to pray for them too. After his prayer he took a seat and started to recite a psalm like this[^108-1-1]: “When I was a speechless infant in my father’s palace, resting in the abundance and comfort of my nurses, my parents provisioned me and dispatched me from the East, our native land. Out of the riches of their treasuries they assembled a load for me, large yet light, to be carried by me alone. The load was gold from the upper lands[^108-1-2], silver from the great treasuries, chalcedony stones from India, and pearls from Kosan[^108-1-3]. They armed me with adamant[^108-1-4], and they dressed me in a robe set with gems and spangled with gold, which they had fashioned in their affection for me, and in a long garment, yellow in color, cut to my size. They made a pact with me, inscribing it in my thought so that I would not forget it[^108-1-5]. They said, ‘Should you descend to Egypt and fetch from there the one pearl that lies near the swallowing dragon, you will put on again the gem-set robe and the garment on which it rests, and together with your brother, the well-remembered one, you will become a herald[^108-1-6] to our realm.’”

## Chapter 109

<sup>1</sup> “I left the East by a road both hard and frightening, with two guides, and I had never traveled such a road. I went by the frontier of the Mosani[^109-1-1], the place where eastern merchants find lodging, and came to the land of the Babylonians. When I entered Egypt, the guides who had journeyed with me left me. I hurried by the shortest way to the dragon and settled near its den, waiting for it to grow drowsy and fall asleep so that I might take away my pearl. Since I was alone, I was a stranger in my appearance, and I seemed foreign to my own people. There I saw a relative of mine from the East, a free man, a pleasant and handsome young man, a son of nobles[^109-1-2]. He came up to me and joined me, and I took him as a companion and made him my friend and a partner in my journey. I warned him to beware of the Egyptians and to avoid association with those impure people. I put on their clothes, lest I seem an outsider come to take the pearl, and lest the Egyptians rouse the dragon against me. But I do not know by what pretext they learned that I was not from their land. With deceit they mixed up a trick for me, and I ate some of their food. I was unaware any longer that I was a king’s son, and I became a slave to their king. I even forgot the pearl my fathers had sent me after, and from the heaviness of their food I sank into a deep sleep.”

## Chapter 110

<sup>1</sup> “While this was happening to me, my fathers found out and grieved over me. A proclamation was made in our kingdom that everyone should gather at our gates. Then the kings of Parthia, the officials, and the leading men of the East reached a decision about me, that I should not stay in Egypt. The rulers also wrote to me, indicating it like this: ‘This comes from the father, the king over kings, and from the mother who holds the East, and from their brothers, second only to us, to our son in Egypt: peace. Arise and sober up from sleep, and hear the words of the letter. Remember that you were born the son of kings. You have slipped under a slave’s yoke. Remember your robe sprinkled with gold. Remember the pearl, the reason you were sent down to Egypt. Your name was summoned out of the book of life[^110-1-1], along with the name of your brother, whom you took along in our kingdom[^110-1-2].’”

## Chapter 111

<sup>1</sup> “As an envoy, the king sealed the letter[^111-1-1] because of the wicked Babylonian children and the tyrannical demons of the Labyrinths. At its voice and its touch I started up out of sleep. I took it up, kissed it, and read it. What it said was about the very thing written in my heart. Immediately it came back to me that I was born to kings, and that my liberty was seeking its kin. I remembered also the pearl that was the reason I was dispatched down to Egypt. I went out on chariots[^111-1-2] against the fearsome dragon, and I wore it down by calling on it the name of my father. Seizing the pearl, I turned back to carry it home to my fathers. I took off the filthy garment and left it in their country, and I set both it and my road straight toward the light of my homeland in the east. Along the way I came upon the one who had roused me[^111-1-3]. As though it had a voice, it raised me from my sleep, and it led me by its light. For at times the royal garment of silk was there before my eyes. As affection led me and drew me on, I passed the Babyrinth[^111-1-4], and leaving Babylon on my left, I came to Mesos[^111-1-5], the great coastland.”

## Chapter 112

<sup>1</sup> “I did not remember my splendor[^112-1-1], since I had been a boy and very young when I left it in the royal house of my father. But suddenly I saw the garment, and it had become like me, as if reflected in a mirror. I saw my whole self in it, and I recognized and perceived myself by means of it, that we had been divided into parts although we came from the same source, and that we are one again through a single form. Moreover, I saw that the very treasurers that carried the garment were two, yet there was one form on both of them, and one royal emblem lay on both. They had money and riches held in their hands, and they were rendering me honor[^112-1-2]. The garment was most splendid, embroidered in bright colors, using gold, valuable stones, and pearls of becoming color, and they had been fastened on high. The likeness of the king over kings was all through it from one end to the other[^112-1-3], and sapphire stones had been set into it on high, fitted exactly.”

## Chapter 113

<sup>1</sup> “I saw again that movements of knowledge were being sent out all through it, and it was ready to speak. I heard it saying, ‘I belong to the man who is the bravest of all people, for whose sake I was recorded[^113-1-1] by the father in person.’ And I myself sensed his stature. All the royal movements came to rest on me as it grew, in its surging eagerness[^113-1-2]. It hurried from his hand, reaching toward the one who would receive it, and longing roused me too to rush to meet it and take it. I stretched out and received it, adorned with its colors[^113-1-3], and I clothed myself completely in my royal robe, which was far above the rest. When I had put it on, I was lifted up into a land of peace and reverence. I bowed my head and did homage to the radiance of my father, who had dispatched this to me, because I had done what I was commanded, and he in the same way had done what he promised. At the doors of his royal court, which was his from the beginning, I mixed with the others. He was glad of me and welcomed me to be with him in the palace, and all his subjects praise him with auspicious voices. He also promised that I would be sent with him to the king’s doors, so that I would be seen along with him before the king, bearing my gifts and the pearl.”

## Chapter 114

<sup>1</sup> Charisius went off home in good spirits, thinking his wife had come round and would be once more as she had been before she listened to the divine message and put her trust in Jesus. On entering, though, he found her hair chopped short and her garments ripped to shreds. Seeing it, he addressed her: “Mygdonia, my lady, what is this cruel disease that grips you? And for what reason have you done these things? I am the man you have had as husband from your maidenhood, and the gods and the laws alike grant me the authority to govern you. Why this madness of yours? Throughout the nation you have turned into an object of mockery. Lay aside your fretting over that sorcerer there. I will take his face out of your sight, so that you can never look on him again.”

## Chapter 115

<sup>1</sup> Hearing this, Mygdonia sank into sorrow, groaning and lamenting. Charisius went on: “Have I done the gods such great harm that they have draped so grievous a disease about me? In what have I offended so much that they have cast me into this degradation? I implore you, Mygdonia, do not strangle my soul with this pathetic, lowly appearance of yours, and do not exhaust my heart with the worries I carry for you. I am Charisius, your husband, honored and feared by the entire nation. What must I do? I cannot tell how to turn. And what shall I conclude? Am I to stay quiet and put up with it? Who will endure it when strangers seize his treasure? Who could tolerate the loss of your good ways[^115-1-1]? For what is it to me? The sweet scent of you fills my nostrils, and your bright face sits within my eyes. They are tearing out my soul, ruining the lovely body I took joy in gazing at, blinding my sharpest eye, and chopping away my right hand. Joy turns to sorrow, my life to death, and the light goes into darkness. Let none of my kin look on me from now on, for no help has ever come to me from them. Nor will I worship the eastern gods after they surrounded me with such evils, nor pray to them, nor offer them sacrifice, deprived of my companion. What else would I ask from them? My whole glory has been stripped away. I am a ruler, next after the royal authority, and Mygdonia has rejected me and removed all this. If only you would give me your attention as before[^115-1-2].”

## Chapter 116

<sup>1</sup> As Charisius said these things in tears, Mygdonia sat quiet, her eyes on the ground. He approached again: “Mygdonia, my dearest lady, call to mind that out of every woman in India I chose you as the loveliest and married you, even though I could have wed another far more beautiful. No, I am lying, Mygdonia: by the gods, there could not be found in the Indian land another woman like you. Alas for me forever, since you refuse to reply even with a word. Insult me, should it please you, with the sweetest of words, so I may be thought deserving of hearing it. Say only one word[^116-1-1]; just glance at me, and I shall be greatly honored. Glance at me: I am better than that sorcerer, and handsomer than he. You are my wealth and honor, and all know there is nobody like me. You are my race and kin, and see, he is taking you away from me.”

## Chapter 117

<sup>1</sup> After Charisius had spoken, Mygdonia told him: “The one I love is greater than you and your possessions. Your possessions, being of earth, return to earth[^117-1-1], but the one I love belongs to heaven and will lead me with him to heaven. Your riches will pass by, your good looks will disappear, along with your fine robes and all your deeds. You alone will remain, stripped bare with your wrongdoings. Do not bring up your doings to me. I ask the Lord to make you forget, so that you never again think of those earlier pleasures and the physical closeness, which will go by like a shade. Only Jesus stays forever, with the souls who set their hope on him. Jesus himself will release me from the disgraceful acts I did with you.” On hearing it, Charisius, his spirit slackened, turned to sleep, telling her: “Reason it out alone, taking all night over it. If you agree to be with me just as you were once, in the past, and will not look at that sorcerer, I will do whatever you wish. And if you remove your attachment from him, I will take him out of prison and release him, and he will be moved to a different land, and I will not trouble you. I know you hold on hard to the stranger. This did not start with you; he has deceived many other women, who sobered up and returned to themselves. So do not count my words as nothing, or turn me into a reproach among the Indians.”

## Chapter 118

<sup>1</sup> With these words Charisius slept. The woman took ten denarii and slipped away to hand them to the prison guards, to gain entry to the apostle. Along the road Judas Thomas was coming, and he met her. At the sight she was frightened; she thought he was one of the officials, since a bright light moved before him. As she fled she said to herself, “I have destroyed you, wretched soul! While this man lives[^118-1-1] you will never again see the apostle Judas, and so far you have not accepted the holy seal.” Fleeing, she ran to a confined spot and hid there, saying, “Better to be finished off by lesser people, who can be talked round, than to fall to this mighty ruler, who looks down on gifts.”

## Chapter 119

<sup>1</sup> While Mygdonia was pondering this, Judas entered and stood before her. She saw him, was afraid, collapsed in terror, and went limp as a corpse. He stepped up, grasped her hand, and told her, “Have no fear, Mygdonia. Jesus will not let you go, and the Lord to whom you committed your soul will not look past you. His compassionate repose will not desert you. The kind one will not desert you, for his great kindness, nor the good one, for his goodness. Rise up from the ground, therefore, you who have become wholly superior to it. Look at the light: the Lord does not let people who love him walk about in the dark[^119-1-1]. Observe the fellow traveler of his slaves: in perils he is their ally.” Mygdonia stood up and fixed her eyes on him, saying, “Where were you going, my lord? And who led you out of jail to look at the sun?” Judas Thomas answered her, “Jesus my Lord is mightier than every power, every king, and every ruler.”

## Chapter 120

<sup>1</sup> Mygdonia said, “Grant me the seal of Jesus Christ, and from your hands I will accept the free gift before you depart this life.” She led him into the courtyard, went in, and roused her nurse, saying, “Mother and nurse Marcia, every service and comfort you did for me from childhood up to my present age were futile, and for them I owe you only a temporary gratitude. Do one more kindness for me now, so you may get your repayment forever from the one who bestows great gifts.” Marcia answered, “What is it you want, my daughter Mygdonia? What would you like done? The honors you promised me earlier were never completed, because the stranger would not allow it, and you have turned me into a byword for shame throughout the nation. So what is this novelty you are telling me to do?” Mygdonia said, “Share with me in eternal life, so that I can obtain perfect food from you. Pick up bread and bring it, along with a mixture of water[^120-1-1], taking care of my liberty[^120-1-2].” The nurse said, “I shall fetch plenty of loaves and, in place of water, measures of wine, and satisfy your wish.” But she told the nurse, “I have no need of measures, nor all those loaves. Just this: bring a water mixture, a single loaf, and oil.”

## Chapter 121

<sup>1</sup> Marcia brought these, and Mygdonia took her place in front of the apostle with her head bare. He lifted the oil and poured it over her head, saying, “Holy oil granted to us for consecration, secret mystery through which the cross was revealed to us, you unfold the limbs that were shut away[^121-1-1]; you bring low the hard works; you point out hidden treasures; you are the sprout of goodness. May your power come; may it be established on your slave Mygdonia, and cure her by means of this freedom.” Once the oil was poured out, he told her nurse to strip her and tie a linen sheet about her. A spring of water was there, and the apostle climbed to it and baptized Mygdonia into the Father’s name, and the Son’s, and the Holy Spirit’s[^121-1-2]. When she had been baptized and clothed, he broke a loaf, took a cup of water, and gave her a share in Christ’s body and in the cup of God’s Son. He said, “You have taken your seal; make eternal life for yourself.” Immediately a voice sounded from above: “Yes, amen.” Marcia, on hearing the voice, was shaken, and pleaded with the apostle that she too might get the seal. He gave it to her and said, “May the Lord’s zeal be upon you as for the rest.”

## Chapter 122

<sup>1</sup> The apostle, having done this, went back to the prison, where he found the gates unlocked and the guards still asleep. Thomas said, “Which god is like you, who keep your tenderness and care back from nobody? Who is as merciful as you, who snatched your own property out of its troubles? You are the life that overcame death, the relief that cut off labor. Glory to the one begotten alone from the Father; glory to the merciful one, sent from mercy.” As he spoke, the guards roused themselves and saw every door open and those shut in still there. They said among themselves, “Did we not bolt these doors? How is it that they stand open now, with the prisoners inside?”

## Chapter 123

<sup>1</sup> At dawn Charisius came toward Mygdonia and found them in prayer, saying, “New God, you who came here among us by way of the stranger; God concealed from those who dwell in India; God who revealed your glory by means of your apostle Thomas; God whose report we heard, and we trusted in you; God toward whom we have come for rescue; God who from love of humanity and pity descended to our littleness; God who sought us out while we did not know him; God who possesses the heights, and from whom the depths are not concealed: turn the madness of Charisius away from us.” Charisius heard this and said to Mygdonia, “You have good reason to call me evil, raving, and ugly. If I had not tolerated your unruliness and granted you liberty, you would not have appealed against me[^123-1-1] or named me in God’s presence. Trust me, Mygdonia: that sorcerer is of no benefit, and he cannot carry out what he announces. But I will do under your own eyes all that I promise, so that you may believe, put up with what I say, and become to me what you were before.”

## Chapter 124

<sup>1</sup> Approaching, he implored her again: “If you will obey me, I will have no sorrow afterward. Remember the day you first met me. Say what is true: was I lovelier to you at that time, or is Jesus now?” Mygdonia replied, “That season demanded what was its own, and this one asks what is its own. That season belonged to the beginning, this one to the end; that to a short life, this to an everlasting one; that to a pleasure that goes by, this to one that stays continually; that to day and night, this to day without night. You saw that wedding, which passed and was only here, but this wedding endures forever. That fellowship was of ruin, this of life without end. Those attendants of the bride are men and women for a season, but those who are here now stay until the end. That marriage on earth establishes human affection, scattering it like dew[^124-1-1]. That bridal chamber is dismantled again, this one stays throughout. That couch is laid out for relaxation, this one for tenderness and trust. You are a groom who goes by and is undone, but Jesus is the real bridegroom, abiding forever, deathless. That gift of unveiling was wealth and robes growing old; this is living words that never go by.”

## Chapter 125

<sup>1</sup> On hearing this, Charisius left for the king and told him everything. The king commanded that Judas be fetched, in order to try him and finish him off. Charisius said, “Bear with it for now, king, and first win the man over with words, frightening him, so that he persuades Mygdonia to become to me what she was lately.” Misdaeus summoned the apostle of Christ and brought him. All the prisoners were saddened that the apostle was departing from them, for they longed for him, saying, “Even this consolation we had has been taken from us.”

## Chapter 126

<sup>1</sup> Misdaeus asked Judas, “What is the point of this strange new doctrine you teach, which both gods and people detest, and which offers nothing of use?” Judas asked, “What harm am I teaching?” Misdaeus replied, “You teach that no one can live well in the presence of the God you proclaim[^126-1-1].” Judas said, “You are right, king; that is what I teach. Answer me this: do you not get angry when the soldiers stand guard over you in dirty clothes? So if you, a king of earth who is going back to earth, demand that those under you keep up a dignified bearing in their duties, are you angry, and do you say I teach badly, when I say that the servants of my king must be dignified and pure, free of every sorrow and worry, of children and worthless wealth and empty turmoil? You want your subjects to adopt your way of life and your customs, and you punish them if they ignore your orders. How much more must those who trust my God serve him with great dignity, purity, and steadiness, free of every pleasure of the body: adultery, debauchery, theft, drunkenness, slavery to the stomach, and shameful acts?”

## Chapter 127

<sup>1</sup> When Misdaeus heard this, he said, “Look, I am letting you go. Now go and persuade Mygdonia, Charisius’s wife, not to leave him.” Judas answered, “Do not hesitate, if you have something to do. If she has rightly taken in what she learned, then no iron, no fire, and nothing else of that kind is strong enough to harm her or to cut out the one who has hold of her soul[^127-1-1].” Misdaeus answered, “One drug dissolves another, and a theriac[^127-1-2] cures a viper’s bite. You, if you want to, are able to supply the antidote to those drugs and make peace and harmony in the household. Doing this would spare you, since you have not yet had enough of life. And understand that unless you persuade her, I will snatch you away from the life that everyone longs for.” Judas replied, “This life is given only for use, and this season of time passes. The life I teach does not decay, while beauty and youth, which are visible now, will be gone in a little while.” The king said, “I have given you the advice that serves you best; you know your own affairs.”

## Chapter 128

<sup>1</sup> When the apostle left the king’s presence, Charisius approached and begged him: “I beg you, sir, I have never wronged you, or anyone else, or the gods. Why have you hit me with such a disaster? Why bring this turmoil into my house? What do you gain from it? If you think there is some profit in it, name it, and I will see that you get it without any trouble. Why are you driving me out of my mind and throwing yourself into ruin? If you do not persuade her, I will kill you and finally take myself out of life. But if there really is, as you claim, life and death beyond our departure from here, and also condemnation, victory, and a court of judgment, then I will go there and be judged alongside you. And if the God you proclaim is just and imposes his penalties justly, I know I will win my case against you, because you harmed me when I had done you no wrong. Even here I am able to repay you, and you know how much you have done against me. So listen to me and come home with me, and persuade Mygdonia to be with me as she was before she set eyes on you.” Judas answered him, “Trust me, child: if people loved God as much as they love each other, they would receive everything they asked from him, with no one forcing him.”

## Chapter 129

<sup>1</sup> While Thomas was speaking, they went into Charisius’s house and found Mygdonia sitting down and Marcia standing near her, her hand propped under her cheek[^129-1-1]. Mygdonia was saying, “Mother, let the rest of my days be cut short, and let all the hours become a single hour, and let me pass out of this life, so that I can hurry away and look on that beautiful one, whose fame I have heard, the living one who grants life to everyone who trusts in him, a place with neither day nor night, neither light nor darkness, neither good nor bad, neither poor nor rich, neither male nor female[^129-1-2], neither free nor slave, and no proud person who forces the humble down.” While she was saying this, the apostle stood beside her, and she rose at once and bowed to him. Then Charisius remarked to him, “Can you see how afraid of you she is, how she respects you, and how gladly she does whatever you order?”

## Chapter 130

<sup>1</sup> While he spoke, Judas turned to Mygdonia: “Mygdonia, my daughter, do as your brother Charisius[^130-1-1] says.” Mygdonia answered, “You could not manage this by argument, so are you forcing me to endure it in deed? For I heard from you that this life is of no use, that this comfort lasts only a moment, and that these possessions do not stay. You also said that whoever turns away from this life will receive eternal life, and that anyone who despises the light of day and night will see the light that cannot be grasped, and that whoever disregards this money will find other, everlasting wealth. Now, out of fear, who changes course after doing something and winning glory for it? Who tears it down again to the foundations? Who, having dug a spring of water in a dry and generous place, fills it in again? Who, having found a treasure, does not use it?” When Charisius heard this, he said, “I will not copy you or hurry to destroy you. Even though I am permitted, I will not put you in chains, but I will not let you talk with this sorcerer. If you do as I say, I know what I need to do[^130-1-2].”

## Chapter 131

<sup>1</sup> Judas left Charisius’s house and went off to Siphor’s house, where he lodged with him. Siphor said, “I will prepare the dining room[^131-1-1] for Judas, where he can teach.” He did so, and Siphor said, “My wife, my daughter, and I will live from now on in holiness and purity, of one mind. I ask you to let us receive the seal[^131-1-2] from you, so that we may become worshipers of the true God and be numbered with his lambs and sheep.” Judas said, “I am afraid to say what I have in mind. I do know something, but I am not able to state what I know.”

## Chapter 132

<sup>1</sup> He began to speak about baptism: “This baptism means the forgiveness of sins. It brings to new birth the light that is poured around us. It brings to new birth the new human being, mixing the Spirit with the soul and renewing it threefold, raising up a new human being who shares in the forgiveness of sins and in the Holy Spirit.” And he said, “Glory to you, whose power is hidden and yet shared in baptism. Glory to you, unseen power at work in baptism. Glory to you, renewal, through whom those who are baptized are renewed[^132-1-1], and who are taken hold of by those who come to you with devotion.” After saying this, he poured oil over their heads and said, “Glory to you, love from the depths of compassion[^132-1-2]. Glory to you, the name of Christ. Glory to you, power established in Christ.” He ordered a basin to be brought and baptized them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

## Chapter 133

<sup>1</sup> When they had been baptized and dressed, he set bread out on the table, blessed it, and said, “Bread of life, may those who eat you remain free of decay. Bread that fills hungry souls with its own blessedness, you are the one who has judged it right to receive the gift, so that you might be our forgiveness of sins, and that all who eat you might become deathless. We name you with the name of the mother[^133-1-1], of the unspeakable mystery of rulers and powers kept hidden. We name you with your own name, Jesus.” Then he said, “May the power of blessing come and settle in this bread, so that every soul that shares in it may be washed clean of its sins.” After breaking it he handed it to Siphor, his wife, and his daughter.

## Chapter 134

<sup>1</sup> King Misdaeus let Judas go, ate his dinner, and went home. He described to his wife what had befallen their relative Charisius: “Look at what has happened to that unfortunate man. You know yourself, my sister Tertia[^134-1-1], that nothing is better for a man than his own wife, in whom he finds rest. Yet it turned out that his wife went off to that sorcerer you have heard about, who has arrived in the land of the Indians; she fell under his drugs and was torn away from her own husband, and he has no idea what to do. When I wanted to put the criminal to death, he would not agree. Go to her yourself, then, and urge her to return to her own husband and abstain from the sorcerer’s empty talk.”

## Chapter 135

<sup>1</sup> As soon as she got up, Tertia went to the house of Charisius, her husband’s relative, and found Mygdonia lying on the ground in lowliness. Ashes and sackcloth had been spread under her, and she was praying that the Lord would forgive her earlier sins and that she might quickly leave this life. Tertia spoke to her: “Mygdonia, my longed-for sister and friend, what is this behavior[^135-1-1]? What illness has come over you? Why are you acting like someone out of her mind? Come to your senses and go back to your own way. Draw close to your large family, spare your true husband Charisius, and stop doing things that do not suit your freedom.” Mygdonia replied, “Tertia, the herald of life is someone you have not heard yet. He has not yet reached your ears; you have not yet tasted the medicine of life or been freed from the groans of what decays. You stand in a life that lasts only a moment, and you do not know life and salvation, the saving and everlasting kind, nor do you sense the fellowship that does not decay. You stand dressed in robes that wear out and long for none of the eternal ones. You think highly of this beauty that fades, and you give no thought to the ugliness of your soul. You are wealthy in a crowd of servants and take pride in the glory that comes from the many, but you do not buy yourself out of the sentence of death.”

## Chapter 136

<sup>1</sup> When Tertia heard this from Mygdonia, she said, “I beg you, sister, take me to the stranger who is teaching such great matters, so that I too may go and listen to him, be taught to revere the God he proclaims, join in his prayers, and share in everything you have told me.” Mygdonia answered, “He is staying at the house of Siphor the commander, for Siphor himself has become the means of salvation for everyone being saved in India.” When Tertia heard this, she ran to Siphor’s house in order to see the newly arrived apostle. ## Chapter 137

Seeing her, Misdaeus asked, “Why is your entrance lovelier than usual today? And what made you come back from a stroll? When I went, I heard about a new life, and I saw the new apostle of God, who gives life to people who put their trust in him and fulfil his commands. ## Chapter 138

## Chapter 139

You pride yourself on property, slaves, fine robes, luxury, and defiled beds, but I take pride in poverty, in a disciplined life, in humility[^139-1-3], fasting, and prayer, in partnership with the Holy Spirit, and in the company of my brothers and sisters who are worthy of God. But show that you are a worthy servant in these ways: first in holiness, which is the head of all good things; next in partnership with this God whom I proclaim, in a disciplined life, simplicity, love, and faith, in the good news that relates to him, and in the unity of pure food.”

## Chapter 140

<sup>1</sup> Persuaded through the Lord, the young man looked for a pretext to help Judas get away. While he was still weighing it, the king arrived. He ordered iron plates to be heated until they glowed and had Judas stand on them barefoot. As the soldiers were taking off his sandals, he said, “The wisdom of God is better than human wisdom[^140-1-1]. ## Chapter 141

<sup>1</sup> Seeing the vast amount of water, the king told Judas, “Ask your God to rescue me from this death, so that the flood does not destroy me.” The apostle prayed: “You who bound this element and gathered it into one place, and who send it out into different lands; who changed disorder into order; who give great and wonderful works through the hands of your slave Judas; who show mercy to my soul, so that I may take in your light at all times; who give wages to those who have labored; savior of my soul, who restore it to its own nature so that it has no share with what causes harm; who are always the source of life: stop this element, so that it does not turn back and destroy[^141-1-1]. Misdaeus watched this and gave orders for Judas to be led to the prison, adding, “Until I have decided how to deal with him.”

## Chapter 142

<sup>1</sup> As Judas was led away to prison, everyone followed him. Vazanes, the king’s son, walked on his right and Siphor on his left. ## Chapter 143

And what country is he from?” “My lord is also my master and yours,” said Thomas[^163-1-1], “and he is lord of heaven and earth.” Misdaeus said, “What is he called?” Judas said, “You cannot hear his true name at this time. But I will tell you the name given to him for the present: Jesus the Christ.” Misdaeus said, “I was in no hurry to destroy you. I held back. But you have gone on increasing in your works, so that your sorceries have been heard of in every land. Now I will do this, so that your sorceries die with you and our nation is cleansed of them.” Judas said, “What you call sorceries will stay with me, and they will never leave the people of this place.”

## Chapter 164

<sup>1</sup> While this was being said, Misdaeus was thinking about how to kill him, for he feared the crowd standing around, since many had believed in him and some of them were prominent people. He got up and took Judas outside the city, and a few armed soldiers went with him. The rest of the crowd supposed that the king wanted to learn something from him, and stood still, paying attention. When they had gone three stades[^164-1-1], he handed him over to four soldiers and one of the officers, ordering them to take him up the mountain and run him through with spears. He himself went back to the city.

## Chapter 165

<sup>1</sup> The people who were there ran to Judas, eager to snatch him away. But he was led off with the soldiers walking beside him, two on each side, with their spears in hand, while the officer held him by the hand and led him with respect. As he walked, Judas said, “Oh, your hidden mysteries, which are being completed in us right up to the end of life! Oh, the wealth of your grace, which does not let us be overcome by the suffering of the body! Look how four have taken hold of me, since I came to be from the four elements[^165-1-1]; and one leads me, since I belong to one, to whom I am going, always unseen. Now I learn that my Lord, who was from one, to whom I am going, and who is always with me unseen, was struck by one; but I, since I am from four, am struck by four[^165-1-2].”

## Chapter 166

<sup>1</sup> When they came to the place where they were going to run him through, Judas said to those holding him, “Listen to me, at least now, for I am standing at my departure from the body. Do not let the eyes of your minds be darkened, and do not let your ears be blocked against hearing what I say. Believe in the God whom I proclaim, and give up the recklessness of your souls. Live as citizens in the way that suits free people, with the glory that people give and the life that is lived toward God.”

## Chapter 167

<sup>1</sup> He said to Vazanes, “Son of the king of this land, servant of Jesus Christ, pay King Misdaeus’ attendants what they ask, so that they let me go aside and pray.” When Vazanes had persuaded the soldiers, Judas turned to prayer, which was this: “My Lord and my God, my hope and redeemer, leader and guide in every place, be with all who serve you, and guide me today as I come to you. Let no one take my soul, which I handed over to you. Let the toll collectors[^167-1-1] not see me, and let the debt collectors not slander me. Let the serpent[^167-1-2] not see me, and let the children of the dragon not hiss at me. See, Lord, I have done your work and finished what you commanded. I became a slave, and for that reason today I receive freedom. So give it to me, and bring it to completion. I say this not because I doubt, but so that those who should hear it may hear.”

## Chapter 168

<sup>1</sup> After he had prayed, he said to the soldiers, “Come and carry out the task of the one who sent you.” At once the four struck him and killed him. All the brothers and sisters[^168-1-1] wept. They wrapped him in fine robes and many linen cloths and laid him in the tomb where the kings of long ago were buried.

## Chapter 169

<sup>1</sup> Siphor and Vazanes did not want to go down to the city, but spent the whole day there and stayed the night. Judas appeared to them and said, “I am not here. Why do you sit guarding me? I have gone up and received what I hoped for. Get up and walk about, and before long you will be gathered to me.” Misdaeus and Charisius put heavy pressure on Tertia and Mygdonia, but could not make them give up their purpose. Judas appeared and said to them, “Do not forget what came before. Jesus, the holy and living one, will himself help you.” So Misdaeus and Charisius, unable to persuade them, let them follow their own purpose. All the brothers and sisters[^169-1-2] there gathered together, for Judas had made Siphor an elder[^169-1-1] and Vazanes a deacon on the mountain, when he was led away to be put to death. The Lord helped them, and through them the faith grew.

## Chapter 170

<sup>1</sup> After a long time had passed, one of Misdaeus’ sons became possessed by a demon. The demon was so fierce that no one could heal him. Misdaeus thought to himself, “I will go and open the tomb, take a bone from the apostle of God and fasten it to my son, and I know he will be healed.” So he went off to do what he had planned. Judas appeared to him and said, “You did not trust a living man; how can you want to trust a dead one? But do not be afraid; Jesus the Christ shows you kindness out of his great goodness.” Misdaeus did not find the bones, for one of the brothers had stolen them and carried them to the western regions[^170-1-1]. Instead he took dust from the place where the apostle’s bones had lain, fastened it to his son and said, “I believe in you, Jesus, now that the one who always troubles people, so that they will not look to your light of the mind, has left me.” When his son was healed in this way, Misdaeus joined the rest of the brothers and sisters[^170-1-3], taking his place beneath Siphor, and asked them all to pray for him, so that he might obtain mercy from our Lord Jesus Christ. The acts of Judas Thomas[^170-1-2] the apostle, which he performed in India, carrying out the command of the one who sent him, are finished. To him be glory for ever and ever. Amen.
