# Infancy Gospel of Thomas

A collection of stories about Jesus as a boy between the ages of five and twelve, probably from the second century and later ascribed to Thomas. The child Jesus works wonders, confounds his teachers and, in the earliest stories, strikes down those who cross him. It was translated and retold across the ancient and medieval world. It is not the Gospel of Thomas found at Nag Hammadi. The translation follows the shorter Greek form of the text.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> I, Thomas the Israelite[^1-1-1], am writing to inform[^1-1-2] all of you, my brothers and sisters[^1-1-3] among the gentiles, about the childhood deeds and the great works of our Lord Jesus Christ, which he did after he was born in our country. This is how it began.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> At the age of five, this child Jesus was playing beside a stream where people crossed[^2-1-1]. He channeled the flowing water into pools and made it clear straightaway, ordering it by his word alone.

<sup>2</sup> He made soft clay and shaped twelve sparrows out of it. This was on a sabbath. Many other children were playing with him.

<sup>3</sup> A Jew noticed what Jesus was doing while playing on the sabbath. He went off at once and told Jesus’ father Joseph, “Your child is down by the stream. He took clay and shaped twelve little birds[^2-3-1], and so he has profaned the sabbath.”

<sup>4</sup> Joseph went there, saw it, and called out, “Why are you doing what is not permitted on the sabbath?” Jesus, however, struck his hands together and shouted to the sparrows, “Off with you[^2-4-1]!” The sparrows took wing and flew off, chirping.

<sup>5</sup> The Jews who witnessed this were astonished. They left and reported to their leaders what they had seen Jesus do.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> The son of Annas[^3-1-1] the scribe was standing there with Joseph. He took a willow branch[^3-1-2] and drained the water that Jesus had collected.

<sup>2</sup> When Jesus saw what had been done, he was angry and said to him, “You wicked, godless fool[^3-2-1], what harm did the pools and the water do to you? Look, you too will wither like a tree, and you will bear no leaves, no root, and no fruit.”

<sup>3</sup> Immediately that boy was completely withered. Jesus left and went to Joseph’s house. The parents of the withered boy picked him up, grieving over his lost youth[^3-3-1], and carried him to Joseph. They blamed him, saying, “You have a child who does such things.”

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> Later he was walking through the village again, and a child ran up and struck him on the shoulder. Jesus was provoked and said to him, “You will not go any farther on your way.” The child dropped dead on the spot. Some who saw it said, “Where does this child come from? Every word he says is done at once.”

<sup>2</sup> The parents of the dead child came to Joseph and reproached him: “With a child like that you cannot live with us in the village. Either teach him to bless and not to curse, or leave, because our children are dying.”

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Joseph took the child aside and cautioned him: “Why do you do such things? These people are suffering because of it, and they hate us and persecute us.” Jesus answered, “I know that these words did not come from you. Still, for your sake I will be silent. They, though, will bear their punishment.” And in that instant his accusers lost their sight.

<sup>2</sup> Those who saw it were frightened and perplexed. They said about him that whatever he said, good or evil, came to pass and even turned into a marvel. Seeing that Jesus had done such a thing, Joseph rose, grabbed his ear, and tugged hard.

<sup>3</sup> The child grew angry and told him, “It should be enough for you to look for me without finding me, and you have behaved very unwisely. Do you not know that I am yours[^5-3-1]? Do not upset me.”

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> A teacher called Zacchaeus[^6-1-1] happened to be standing nearby. He listened as Jesus said these things to his father, and he was astonished that a mere child could talk like this.

<sup>2</sup> After a few days Zacchaeus approached Joseph. “Your child is bright and has a good mind. Give him to me, and he can study the alphabet. Along with writing I will teach him every branch of learning, and to address all the elders respectfully, honoring them like grandfathers and fathers, and to be fond of children his own age.”

<sup>3</sup> Zacchaeus went through every letter with Jesus, from alpha to omega, questioning him thoroughly. Jesus looked at the teacher Zacchaeus and said, “You do not understand the nature of the alpha. How, then, can you teach another person the beta? Hypocrite[^6-3-1]! If you know it, teach me the alpha first, and afterward I will trust you with the beta.” Next he started to question the teacher about the first letter, and Zacchaeus had no reply for him.

<sup>4</sup> Many people were listening when the child said to Zacchaeus, “Teacher, consider the structure[^6-4-1] of the first letter. See how it has lines and a middle stroke that you can see crossing, drawing together, climbing as they go and then gathering at the top again: three marks of a kind, a leading one and a supporting one, of equal measure. These are the lines of the alpha.”

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> When the teacher Zacchaeus heard the child speak such riddles[^7-1-1] about the first letter, he was at a loss at such a defense and such teaching. He said to those present, “Alas, I am bewildered, wretch that I am. I have brought shame on myself by drawing this child to me.”

<sup>2</sup> “Take him away from me, I beg you, brother Joseph[^7-2-1]. I cannot bear the severity of his look, and I cannot follow his words at all. This child is not of this earth. He can even tame fire. Perhaps he was born before the creation of the world. What womb carried him, what mother nursed him? I do not know. Alas, my friend, he has left me out of my mind. I cannot follow his reasoning. I have deceived myself, three times wretched. I set out to get a pupil, and I find that I have a teacher.”

<sup>3</sup> “I am ashamed, my friends, that I, an old man, have been beaten by a child. All that is left for me is to lose heart and die because of this child, for I cannot look him in the face at this hour. When everyone says that a small child has beaten me, what can I answer? And what can I tell them about the things he said to me about the first letter? I am at a loss, my friends, for I do not know its beginning or its end.”

<sup>4</sup> “So I ask you, brother Joseph[^7-4-1], to take him back to your house. He is someone great, a god or an angel, or what I should call him I do not know.”

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> While the Jews were consoling Zacchaeus, the child laughed loudly and said, “Now let what is yours bear fruit, and let those who are blind in heart see. I have come from above to curse them and call them to the things above, as the one who sent me for your sake commanded.”

<sup>2</sup> When the child had finished speaking, everyone who had fallen under his curse was saved at once. And from then on no one dared to anger him, in case he should curse them and they be crippled.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> A few days later Jesus was playing in a house, up on the roof, and one of the children playing with him fell from the house to the ground and died. When the other children saw it, they ran away, and Jesus stayed there alone.

<sup>2</sup> The dead child’s parents came and accused him of throwing the child down. Jesus said, “I never threw him down.”[^9-2-1] But they kept abusing him.

<sup>3</sup> Jesus jumped down from the roof and stood beside the child’s body. He cried out in a loud voice, “Zeno[^9-3-1]” (that was the boy’s name), “get up and tell me, did I throw you down?” The boy got up at once and said, “No, Lord, you did not throw me down. You raised me up.” The people who saw this were amazed. The child’s parents praised God for the sign that had happened, and they worshipped Jesus.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> A few days later someone was splitting wood in a corner[^10-1-1]. The axe slipped and cut open the sole of his foot, and he was bleeding so badly that he was dying.

<sup>2</sup> There was an uproar, and people ran together, and the child Jesus ran there too. He pushed through the crowd, took hold of the injured foot, and the man was healed at once. He said to the young man, “Get up, split your wood, and remember me.” When the crowd saw what had happened, they worshipped the child, saying, “Truly the Spirit of God lives in this child.”

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> When he was six years old, his mother sent him to draw water and bring it home, and she gave him a jar. In the crush of the crowd the jar struck something and broke.

<sup>2</sup> Jesus spread out the cloak[^11-2-1] he was wearing, filled it with water, and carried it to his mother. When his mother saw the sign that had happened, she kissed him, and she kept to herself the mysteries[^11-2-2] she saw him performing.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> Again, at sowing time, the child went out with his father to sow wheat in their field. While his father was sowing, the child Jesus also sowed one grain of wheat.

<sup>2</sup> When the time came, he harvested and threshed it, and it yielded a hundred measures[^12-2-1]. He called all the poor of the village to the threshing floor and gave them the grain, and Joseph took away what was left of it. Jesus was eight years old when he did this sign.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> His father was a carpenter, and in those days he made ploughs and yokes. A rich man ordered a bed from him. One of the crossbeams, the one called the shorter, had been cut too short, and Joseph did not know what to do. The child Jesus said to his father Joseph, “Put the two pieces of wood on the ground, and make them equal, starting from the middle[^13-1-1].”

<sup>2</sup> Joseph did as the child told him. Jesus stood at the other end, took hold of the shorter piece of wood, and stretched it until it was the same length as the other. His father Joseph saw this and was amazed. He put his arms around the child and kissed him, saying, “I am blessed that God has given me this child.”

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> Joseph noticed the child’s intelligence and his growth, and again resolved that he should not go without the skill of reading. He brought him to a different teacher. The teacher told Joseph, “First I will give him Greek, then Hebrew.” This teacher knew what the child was capable of, and he feared him. Still, he wrote the alphabet out and spent a long time on it, but Jesus gave no response.

<sup>2</sup> Jesus said, “If you truly are a teacher, and know your letters well, then tell me what the alpha means, and I will tell you what the beta means.” The teacher lost his temper and hit him on the head. The boy, stung by the blow, cursed him, and the teacher immediately fainted and fell on the ground, face down.

<sup>3</sup> The boy went home to Joseph. Joseph was upset and ordered the boy’s mother, “Never let him leave the house, because anyone who angers him dies.”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> Some time afterward another teacher, who was a good friend of Joseph’s, said, “Send the child to my school. Perhaps I can use gentle persuasion to teach him his letters.” Joseph answered, “If you dare, my friend[^15-1-1], take him along.” The teacher took Jesus with fear and great worry, but the child went willingly.

<sup>2</sup> Confidently he entered the school and saw a book lying on a reading stand[^15-2-1]. He picked it up, but instead of reading the letters in it he opened his mouth and spoke in the Holy Spirit, teaching the law to the people around him. A big crowd came together and listened, amazed at the beauty of his teaching and the readiness of his words, since a child so young spoke this way.

<sup>3</sup> Joseph heard of it and became frightened. He ran to the school, fearing that this teacher also had no experience. The teacher told Joseph, “You should know, my friend[^15-3-1], that I accepted the child as a student, and he is full of grace and wisdom. I beg you, take him home.”

<sup>4</sup> When the child heard this he smiled at the man and said, “Since you have spoken and testified correctly, the other teacher, who was struck down, will be cured because of you.” And right away the other teacher was cured. Joseph took the child and went back home.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> Joseph sent his son James to tie up some wood and bring it to the house, and the child Jesus followed him. While James was gathering the sticks, a viper bit James on the hand.

<sup>2</sup> As James lay wasting away and dying, Jesus came up and blew on the bite. The pain stopped at once, the creature burst, and James was well again.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> After this, a small infant in Joseph’s neighborhood died, and its mother wept bitterly. Jesus heard that there was great mourning and commotion, and he ran there quickly. He found the child dead, touched its chest, and said, “I tell you, baby, do not die, but live and be with your mother.” And at once the child looked up and laughed. He said to the woman, “Take him, give him your milk, and remember me.”

<sup>2</sup> The crowd that stood by was astonished and said, “Truly this child is either a god or an angel of God, for every word of his is a ready deed.” And Jesus left and went on playing with other children.

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> Some time later a house was being built and there was a great commotion. Jesus stood up and went over to it. He saw a man lying dead, took him by the hand, and said, “I tell you, man, get up and do your work.” And at once the man got up and worshipped him.

<sup>2</sup> When the crowd saw this, they marveled and said, “This child is from heaven, for he has saved many souls from death, and he has power to save them all their lives.”

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> When Jesus was twelve, his parents went to Jerusalem for the feast of Passover[^19-1-1] as usual, in a company of pilgrims. When the festival was over they set out for home. On the way, the child Jesus went back up to Jerusalem, but his parents believed he was with the travelers.

<sup>2</sup> After a day on the road they started looking for him among their kin. They did not find him, so they became distraught and returned to the city to look. Three days later they found him in the temple, seated among the teachers[^19-2-1]. He was listening to them and putting questions to them. Everyone gave him their attention and wondered how a child could silence the elders and the teachers of the people, working through the key points of the law and the parables of the prophets.

<sup>3</sup> His mother Mary stepped forward and said, “My child, why did you treat us like this? Look, we have been sick with worry, searching for you.” Jesus replied, “Why were you searching? Do you not know that it is right for me to be in my Father’s house[^19-3-1]?”

<sup>4</sup> The scribes and Pharisees asked, “Are you this child’s mother?” “I am,” she said. They told her, “You are blessed among women[^19-4-1], since God has blessed your womb’s fruit. Such splendor, such goodness and wisdom, we have never seen nor heard of.”

<sup>5</sup> Jesus rose and went after his mother, and he obeyed his parents. His mother treasured all that had happened. Jesus advanced in wisdom, in age, and in favor[^19-5-1]. Glory belongs to him for ever and ever. Amen.
