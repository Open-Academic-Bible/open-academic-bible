# Protevangelium of James

A narrative of the birth and childhood of Mary and the birth of Jesus, written in the second half of the second century and presented as the work of James, the brother of Jesus. It is the source of the names of Mary's parents, Joachim and Anna, and of many later traditions about Mary. More than 140 Greek manuscripts survive, along with early translations into many languages, a sign of how widely it was read, especially in the Eastern churches.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> In the records[^1-1-1] of the twelve tribes of Israel there was a man named Joachim, who was very rich. He used to bring his offerings twice over, telling himself, “What I have in excess will go to all the people, and what I offer for forgiveness will go to the Lord, as atonement for me[^1-1-2].”

<sup>2</sup> The great day of the Lord drew near, and the people of Israel were bringing their offerings. Reuben stood in front of Joachim and said, “You are not permitted to bring your offerings first, because you have produced no offspring in Israel.”

<sup>3</sup> Joachim was deeply distressed, and he went to the register of the twelve tribes of the people. He said, “I will look at the register of the twelve tribes of Israel, to see whether I alone have produced no offspring in Israel.” He searched and found that all the righteous had raised up offspring in Israel. Then he remembered the patriarch Abraham, to whom God gave a son, Isaac, in his last days.

<sup>4</sup> Joachim was deeply distressed. He did not show himself to his wife but went off into the wilderness, pitched his tent there, and fasted forty days and forty nights. He said to himself, “I will not go down for food or drink until the Lord my God visits me. Prayer will be my food and drink.”

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> His wife Anna meanwhile sang two dirges and gave voice to two laments. She said, “I will mourn my widowhood, and I will mourn my childlessness.”

<sup>2</sup> The great day of the Lord drew near, and her slave Judith said, “How long will you humble your soul? Look, the great day of the Lord has come, and you are not allowed to mourn. Take this headband[^2-2-1], which the woman in charge of the work[^2-2-2] gave me. I am not allowed to wear it, because I am a slave and it carries a royal mark.”

<sup>3</sup> Anna said, “Get away from me! I have done nothing wrong, and the Lord has humbled me greatly. Perhaps some trickster[^2-3-1] gave you this, and you have come to make me share in your sin.” Judith answered, “What curse could I put on you, when you will not listen to me? The Lord has closed your womb, so that you would not bear fruit in Israel.”

<sup>4</sup> Anna was deeply distressed. She took off her mourning clothes, washed her head, put on her wedding clothes, and about the ninth hour[^2-4-2] went down into the garden to walk. She saw a laurel tree[^2-4-1] and sat beneath it, and she begged the Master, saying, “God of our fathers, bless me and hear my prayer, just as you blessed the womb of Sarah and gave her a son, Isaac.”

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Anna looked up intently at the sky and saw a sparrow’s nest in the laurel tree. She broke into lament, saying, “Woe to me! Who gave me birth, and what womb brought me forth? I was born a curse in the eyes of the people of Israel. They mocked me, and they drove me with scorn out of the Lord’s temple.

<sup>2</sup> Woe to me! What am I like? I am not like the birds of the sky, because even the birds of the sky are fruitful in your sight, Lord. Woe to me! What am I like? I am not like the animals of the earth, because even the animals of the earth are fruitful in your sight, Lord.

<sup>3</sup> Woe to me! What am I like? I am not like these waters, because even these waters are fruitful in your sight, Lord. Woe to me! What am I like? I am not like this earth, because even this earth bears its crops in their season and blesses you, Lord.”

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> An angel of the Lord appeared at her side and said, “Anna, Anna, the Lord has listened to your prayer. You will conceive and bear a child, and people throughout the world[^4-1-1] will speak of your offspring.” Anna replied, “By the life of the Lord my God, whether I bear a boy or a girl, I will present it as a gift to the Lord my God, and it will serve him for its whole life.”

<sup>2</sup> Then two angels came and told her, “Look, Joachim your husband is on his way with his flocks. An angel of the Lord went down to him and said, ‘Joachim, Joachim, the Lord God has listened to your prayer. Leave that place, because your wife Anna will conceive.’”

<sup>3</sup> Joachim came down and summoned his shepherds. “Bring me ten ewe lambs[^4-3-2] without spot or blemish,” he said, “to be the Lord my God’s. Bring me twelve tender calves, for the priests and the council of elders[^4-3-1], and a hundred kids for all the people.”

<sup>4</sup> Joachim arrived with his flocks just as Anna was standing at the gate. When she saw him coming, she ran and clung to his neck. “Now I know,” she said, “that the Lord God has greatly blessed me. Look, a widow no longer, I who was childless will conceive.” Joachim stayed home and rested that first day.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> The next day he brought his offerings, saying to himself, “If the Lord God has been gracious to me, the priest’s plate will make[^5-1-1] it clear to me.” Joachim brought his offerings and kept his eyes on the priest’s plate as he approached the Lord’s altar, and he saw no sin in himself. Joachim said, “Now I know that the Lord has been gracious to me and has forgiven me all my sins.” He came down from the Lord’s temple acquitted and went away to his house.

<sup>2</sup> Her months were completed, and in the ninth month Anna gave birth. She asked the midwife, “What have I borne?” The midwife said, “A girl.” Anna said, “My soul has been lifted up this day[^5-2-1],” and she lay down. When the days were over, Anna washed herself clean, gave the child her breast, and named her Mariam[^5-2-2].

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> The child grew stronger with each day. At six months her mother set her on the ground to test whether she could stand. Seven steps she took, and then she came into her mother’s lap. Her mother lifted her up and said, “By the life of the Lord my God, you will walk no more on this ground until I bring you to the Lord’s temple.” So she turned the child’s bedroom into a sanctuary, and let nothing common or unclean cross its floor. She summoned the undefiled daughters of the Hebrews to attend to the child.

<sup>2</sup> At the child’s first birthday Joachim put on a great feast. He asked the priests, the scribes, the council of elders[^6-2-1], and all Israel’s people. Joachim presented the child to the priests, who blessed her: “God of our fathers, bless this girl and grant her a name that lasts for ever, known in every generation.” The whole people said, “So be it, so be it. Amen.” Next he presented her to the chief priests, who blessed her: “God of the heights, look upon this girl and give her the highest blessing, which has no one to follow it.”

<sup>3</sup> Her mother carried her into the sanctuary of her bedroom and nursed her. Then Anna composed a song for the Lord God: “To the Lord my God I will sing, for he has visited me and lifted from me the shame my enemies put on me. The Lord has given me the fruit of his own righteousness, one child, yet worth many[^6-3-1], before him. Who will tell the sons of Reuben that Anna nurses a child[^6-3-2]? Hear this, hear this, all twelve tribes of Israel: Anna is nursing!” She put the child to rest in the sanctuary of her bedroom, went out, and attended to her guests. When the feast was done, they went down glad, glorifying the God of Israel.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> The child grew with each passing month. When she was two years old, Joachim said, “Let us take her up to the Lord’s temple, so that we may pay the vow we made, in case the Master sends something against us and our offering is not accepted.” Anna said, “Let us wait for the third year, so that the child does not look for a father or mother.” Joachim said, “Let us wait.”

<sup>2</sup> When the child was three, Joachim said, “Call the undefiled daughters of the Hebrews, and let each take a lamp[^7-2-1] and stand there with it lit, so that the child does not turn back and her heart is not drawn away from the Lord’s temple.” They did this until they had gone up into the Lord’s temple. The priest received her, kissed her and blessed her, saying, “The Lord has made your name great in all generations. At the end of the days the Lord will reveal his ransom through you to the people of Israel.”

<sup>3</sup> He sat her down on the third step of the altar, and the Lord God put grace upon her. She danced on her feet, and the whole house of Israel loved her.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Her parents went down marveling and praising the Master God, because the child had not turned back. Mary stayed in the Lord’s temple like a dove being raised there, and she received her food from an angel’s hand.

<sup>2</sup> When she was twelve, the priests held a council. They said, “Look, Mary has reached the age of twelve in the Lord’s temple. What should we do with her, so that she does not defile the Lord’s sanctuary?” They said to the high priest, “You stand at the Lord’s altar. Go in and pray about her, and whatever the Lord reveals to you, that is what we will do.”

<sup>3</sup> The high priest took the robe with the twelve bells[^8-3-1], went into the holy of holies[^8-3-2], and prayed about her. Suddenly an angel of the Lord stood beside him and said, “Zacharias, Zacharias, go out and gather the widowers[^8-3-3] of the people. Let each of them bring a rod, and the one to whom the Lord shows a sign will be her husband.” The heralds went out through the whole surrounding region of Judea, the Lord’s trumpet sounded, and everyone came running.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> Joseph dropped his axe[^9-1-1] and hurried out to the assembly. Once everyone had gathered, they went to the high priest with their rods. The high priest collected them, entered the temple, and prayed. After finishing his prayer he picked the rods up again, came out, and returned them to the men. None of them bore any sign. Joseph was given the final rod, and look: a dove flew out of it and settled on Joseph’s head. “Joseph,” said the priest, “the lot has fallen on you. You are to take the Lord’s virgin and keep her.”

<sup>2</sup> Joseph protested. “I already have sons, and I am old, and she is only a girl. I would not want the people of Israel to laugh at me.” The priest answered, “Fear the Lord your God, and call to mind what God did to Dathan, Abiram and Korah[^9-2-1]: the ground was cleft apart, and they were swallowed because they had opposed him. Be afraid now, Joseph, that the same might come upon your house.”

<sup>3</sup> In fear Joseph took her to be under his care. He told Mariam, “Look, I took you from the Lord’s temple, and now I leave you at my house. I must go off to my building projects, and then I will return to you. The Lord will protect you.”

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> The priests held a council and said, “Let us make a curtain[^10-1-1] for the Lord’s temple.” The priest said, “Call me undefiled young women from the tribe of David.” The attendants went and searched and found seven young women[^10-1-3]. The priest then remembered the girl Mariam, that she was from the tribe of David and was undefiled before God. The attendants went and brought her, and they brought the young women into the Lord’s temple. The priest said, “Draw lots for me to decide who will spin the gold, the amiantos[^10-1-2], the fine linen, the silk, the blue[^10-1-4], the scarlet, and the true purple.”

<sup>2</sup> The true purple and the scarlet fell by lot to Mariam. She took them and went to her house. At that time Zacharias became mute, and Samuel took his place until Zacharias spoke again. Mariam took the scarlet and spun it.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> She picked up the pitcher[^11-1-2] and set off to draw water. And look, a voice spoke: “Greetings, favored one[^11-1-1]! The Lord is with you; among women you are blessed.” She glanced right and left, looking for the source of the voice. Shaking, she went back into her house, set the pitcher down, took up the purple, sat in her chair, and began to draw out the thread.

<sup>2</sup> An angel of the Lord was suddenly standing in front of her. “Mariam, do not fear[^11-2-1],” he said. “You have won the favor of the Master of all, and by his word you will conceive.” She heard this and was uncertain what to think. “Shall I conceive from the Lord, the living God,” she asked herself, “and bear a child the way any woman does?”

<sup>3</sup> The angel replied, “No, Mariam, not like that. The Lord’s power will overshadow you[^11-3-1], and so the holy child to be born of you will be called Son of the Most High. You are to give him the name Jesus, since he will rescue his people from their sins[^11-3-2].” Mariam said, “See, I am the Lord’s slave girl before him. May it be done to me as you have spoken.”

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> She finished the purple and the scarlet and took them to the priest. The priest blessed her and said, “Mariam, the Lord God has made your name great, and you will be blessed in all the generations of the earth.”

<sup>2</sup> Mariam rejoiced and went to her relative Elisabeth and knocked at the door. When Elisabeth heard it, she threw down the scarlet wool, ran to the door and opened it. She saw Mariam and blessed her and said, “Why has this happened to me[^12-2-1], that the mother of my Lord comes to me? For look, the child in me leapt and blessed you.” But Mariam had forgotten the mysteries that the archangel Gabriel had told her. She looked up at the sky and said, “Who am I, Lord, that all the generations of the earth call me blessed[^12-2-2]?”

<sup>3</sup> She stayed with Elisabeth for three months. Day by day her womb grew larger. Mariam was afraid, so she went home and hid herself from the people of Israel. She was sixteen[^12-3-1] years old when these mysteries took place.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> In her sixth month, look, Joseph came back from his building work. When he went into the house he found her pregnant. He struck his face, threw himself on the ground on sackcloth, and wept bitterly, saying, “With what face can I look at the Lord my God? What prayer can I pray for this young woman? I received her as a virgin from the Lord God’s temple, and I did not protect her. Who has hunted me down[^13-1-1]? Who has done this evil thing in my house and defiled the virgin? Has the story of Adam been repeated in me[^13-1-2]? Just as the serpent came in the hour of his praise[^13-1-3], found Eve alone, and deceived her, so it has happened to me.”

<sup>2</sup> Joseph got up from the sackcloth and called Mariam. He said to her, “You were in God’s care. Why have you done this? Have you forgotten the Lord your God? Why have you humbled your soul, you who were raised in the holy of holies and received food from the hand of an angel?”

<sup>3</sup> She wept bitterly and said, “I am pure, and I have not known any man[^13-3-1].” Joseph said to her, “Then where did what is in your womb come from?” She answered, “As the Lord my God lives, I do not know where it came from.”

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> Joseph was very afraid. He withdrew from her in silence and wondered what to do about her. He said, “If I hide her sin, I will be found fighting against the Lord’s law. If I reveal her to the people of Israel, I am afraid that what is in her is from an angel[^14-1-1], and I will be found handing innocent blood over to a sentence of death. What then shall I do with her? I will divorce her secretly.” Night came over him.

<sup>2</sup> And look, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and said, “Do not be afraid[^14-2-1] of this young woman, for what is in her comes from the Holy Spirit. She will bear a son, and you will name him Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.” Joseph woke from sleep and praised the God of Israel who had given him this grace, and he kept her safe.

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> Annas the scribe[^15-1-1] came to Joseph and asked, “Why did you not come to our assembly?” Joseph replied, “I was tired after my trip, and I took the first day to rest.” Annas turned and noticed that Mariam was pregnant.

<sup>2</sup> He hurried off to the priest and told him, “Joseph, the man you speak up for, has done a terrible thing.” “What do you mean?” asked the priest. Annas answered, “He has defiled the virgin he took from the Lord’s temple. He took her in marriage by stealth[^15-2-1] and kept it from the people of Israel.” “Joseph did this?” said the priest. The scribe Annas replied, “Send some attendants, and they will find the virgin with child.” The attendants went, found things as he had described, and brought her and Joseph to the court.

<sup>3</sup> The priest said, “Mariam, what have you done? You humbled your soul and forgot the Lord your God, you who grew up in the holy of holies and took your food from an angel’s hand. You listened to the hymns and danced in his presence. What is this that you have done?” Weeping bitterly, she answered, “By the life of the Lord my God, I stand pure in his sight, and no man has ever been with me[^15-3-1].”

<sup>4</sup> Then the priest said to Joseph, “What have you done?” Joseph answered, “By the life of the Lord my God, I am clean where she is concerned.” The priest said, “Stop bearing false witness and tell the truth. You took her in marriage by stealth, you hid it from Israel, and you did not bend your head beneath the strong hand[^15-4-1] to have your seed blessed.” Joseph stayed silent.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> The priest said, “Give back the virgin you took from the Lord’s temple.” Joseph burst into tears. The priest said, “I will give you both the water of the Lord’s test[^16-1-1] to drink, and it will make your sins plain before your eyes.”

<sup>2</sup> The priest took the water and gave it to Joseph to drink, and sent him into the hill country, and he came back unharmed. He also gave it to Mariam to drink and sent her into the hill country, and she came back unharmed. All the people were amazed that sin did not show in them.

<sup>3</sup> The priest said, “If the Lord God has not made your sins plain, then I do not condemn you either.” He let them go. Joseph took Mariam and went home to his house, rejoicing and praising the God of Israel.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> A decree was issued by the emperor Augustus[^17-1-1]: everyone in Bethlehem of Judea was to be enrolled. Joseph thought, “My sons I can enroll. But this young woman, what am I to do about her? In what capacity should I enter her name? As my wife? I would be ashamed. As my daughter? The whole of Israel knows she is no daughter of mine. Whatever the Lord wants, this day of the Lord will bring.”

<sup>2</sup> He put a saddle on the donkey and seated her on it. His son led the animal and Joseph walked behind. When they were about three miles along, Joseph looked back and saw her gloomy face. “Maybe her baby is distressing her,” he said to himself. He looked back again and saw her laughing. “Mariam,” he said, “what is going on with you? Your face is cheerful at one moment and sad the next.” Mariam answered, “I can see two peoples with my eyes. One is weeping and lamenting, the other is rejoicing and glad.”

<sup>3</sup> Halfway there Mariam said to him, “Help me down from the donkey, because the baby inside me is pushing to be born.” He lifted her down and asked, “Where can I take you to cover your shame? This region is empty of people.”

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> He found a cave there and brought her into it. He left his sons with her and went out to look for a Hebrew midwife in the region of Bethlehem.

<sup>2</sup> But I, Joseph, was walking, and yet I was not walking. I looked up at the air and saw that the air was stunned[^18-2-1]. I looked up at the vault of heaven and saw it standing still, and the birds of the sky were motionless. I looked at the ground and saw a bowl lying there, with workers reclining beside it, their hands in the bowl. Those who were chewing were not chewing, those who were lifting food did not lift it, and those who were bringing it to their mouths did not bring it, but the faces of all of them were looking upward. And look, sheep were being driven along, but they did not move forward and stood still. The shepherd raised his hand to strike them with his staff, and his hand stayed raised. I looked at the flowing river and saw the kids with their mouths resting on the water, not drinking. Then everything suddenly went on again in its course[^18-2-2].

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> And look, a woman coming down from the hill country said to me, “Where are you going, sir?” I said, “I am looking for a Hebrew midwife.” She answered, “Are you from Israel?” I said, “Yes.” She said, “And who is the woman giving birth in the cave?” I said, “My betrothed[^19-1-1].” She said, “Is she not your wife?” I said, “She is Mariam, who was raised in the Lord’s temple. I received her by lot as my wife, and yet she is not my wife, but has a child conceived by the Holy Spirit.” The midwife said, “Is this true?” Joseph said to her, “Come and see.” And the midwife went with him.

<sup>2</sup> They stood at the mouth of the cave, and look, a bright cloud was overshadowing the cave. The midwife said, “My soul has been lifted up[^19-2-1] today, because my eyes have seen wonders: salvation has been born for Israel[^19-2-2].” At once the cloud drew back from the cave, and a great light appeared in the cave, so that our eyes could not bear it. Little by little the light withdrew, until the baby appeared and took the breast from his mother Mary.

<sup>3</sup> The midwife cried out, “This is a great day for me, because I have seen this extraordinary sight!” She went out of the cave, and Salome met her. She said, “Salome, Salome, I have an extraordinary sight to tell you about: a virgin has given birth, which her nature does not allow.” Salome said, “As the Lord my God lives, unless I put in my finger and examine her condition[^19-3-1], I will not believe that a virgin has given birth.”

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> The midwife entered and said to Mariam, “Get into position[^20-1-1], because a serious trial[^20-1-2] awaits you.” Salome inserted her finger. She cried out, “Alas for my lawless disbelief! I have put the living God to the test, and look, my hand is dropping off, consumed by fire.”

<sup>2</sup> Salome knelt before the Master and prayed, “God of my ancestors, remember me. I come from the line of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Do not hold me up as a warning to the people of Israel; restore me to the poor. You know, Master, that I carried out my healings in your name and took my pay from you.”

<sup>3</sup> At once an angel of the Lord was at her side. “Salome, Salome,” he said, “the Lord has listened to you. Reach out your hand to the child and lift him, and you will find salvation and joy.”

<sup>4</sup> Salome went up to the child and lifted him. “I will bow before him,” she said, “for a great king has been born to Israel.” Immediately she was cured, and she left the cave acquitted[^20-4-1]. Then a voice said, “Salome, Salome, tell no one the amazing things you have seen, until the child has entered Jerusalem.”

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> Joseph was making ready to leave for Judea. A great stir arose in Bethlehem of Judea, because magi[^21-1-1] had arrived and were asking, “Where is the newborn king of the Jews? We saw his star rising[^21-1-2] and have come to bow down before him.”

<sup>2</sup> Herod was troubled when he heard it. He sent attendants to the magi, and he called in the chief priests and cross-examined them: “What do the writings say about the Christ[^21-2-1][^21-2-2]? Where is he to be born?” “In Bethlehem of Judea,” they told him, “since that is what has been written.” He let them go. Next he put his question to the magi: “What sign did you see concerning the king who was born?” They answered, “A huge star blazed among these stars and made them dim, until the stars could no longer be seen. From that we understood that a king had been born to Israel, and we came to bow down before him.” Herod told them, “Go and search. When you find him, let me know, so that I also can come and worship him.”

<sup>3</sup> The magi set out. And look, the star they had seen rising[^21-3-1] went ahead of them until they entered the cave, and it came to rest directly above it. There the magi saw the child with Mariam his mother, and they brought out gifts from their bags: gold, frankincense and myrrh.

<sup>4</sup> An angel warned them[^21-4-1] not to go into Judea, so they returned to their homeland by a different route.

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> When Herod realized that the magi had tricked him, he was furious. He sent out killers with the order, “Kill the infants[^22-1-1] from two years old and under.”

<sup>2</sup> When Mariam heard that the infants were being killed, she was frightened. She took the child, wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a feeding trough for oxen[^22-2-1].

<sup>3</sup> Elisabeth heard that John[^22-3-1] was being searched for. She took him and went up into the hill country, and she looked around for a place to hide him, but there was no hiding place. Elisabeth groaned and said aloud, “Mountain of God, take in a mother with her child.” She was not able to climb any higher. At once the mountain split open and received her. A light shone for them there, for an angel of the Lord was with them and kept them safe.

## Chapter 23

<sup>1</sup> Herod was searching for John. He sent attendants to Zacharias with the question, “Where have you hidden your son?” Zacharias answered, “I am a servant[^23-1-1] of God, and I stay constantly in the Lord’s temple. I do not know where my son is.”

<sup>2</sup> The attendants left and reported all this to Herod. Herod was furious and said, “His son is going to be king of Israel.” He sent to Zacharias again with the message, “Tell the truth. Where is your son? You know that your blood is in my hand.” The attendants went and reported this to him.

<sup>3</sup> Zacharias said, “I am a witness to God. If you shed my blood, the Lord will receive my spirit, because you will be shedding innocent blood[^23-3-1] at the entrance of the Lord’s temple.” About daybreak Zacharias was murdered, and the people of Israel did not know that he had been killed.

## Chapter 24

<sup>1</sup> At the hour of greeting the priests went in, but Zacharias’s customary blessing did not meet them. The priests stood waiting for Zacharias, to greet him with prayer[^24-1-1] and to praise the Most High.

<sup>2</sup> When he was slow to come, they were all afraid. One of them dared to go in, and beside the altar he saw blood that had congealed and a voice saying, “Zacharias has been murdered, and his blood will not be wiped away until his avenger comes.” When he heard this, he was afraid. He went out and told the priests.

<sup>3</sup> They dared to go in and saw what had happened. The paneled ceilings of the temple wailed, and the priests tore their clothes from top to bottom. They did not find his body, but they found his blood, which had turned to stone. Terrified, they went out and announced to all the people that Zacharias had been murdered. All the tribes of the people heard it, and they mourned for him and lamented for three days and three nights.

<sup>4</sup> After the three days the priests deliberated about whom they should appoint in his place, and the lot fell on Symeon[^24-4-1]. It was he who had been told by the Holy Spirit that he would not see death until he saw the Christ[^24-4-2] in the flesh.

## Chapter 25

<sup>1</sup> I, James, who wrote this account in Jerusalem, withdrew to the wilderness when unrest broke out at the death of Herod. I stayed there until the unrest in Jerusalem came to an end, praising the Master God, who gave me the gift and the wisdom to write this account.

<sup>2</sup> Grace will be with[^25-2-1] those who fear our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen.
