# Gospel of Peter

An account of the trial, death and resurrection of Jesus told in Peter's name, probably from the first half of the second century. Around 200, Serapion, bishop of Antioch, first allowed a church in Rhossus to read it and then withdrew his permission after examining it. Only one substantial part survives, in a manuscript found at Akhmim in Egypt in 1886–87; it begins in the middle of the trial and breaks off in mid-sentence.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> No one among the Jews rinsed his hands[^1-1-1]: not Herod, and not one of his judges. They refused to wash, and Pilate rose from his seat[^1-1-2].

<sup>2</sup> At that point King Herod ordered the Lord to be seized. “Carry out on him everything I told you to do,” he said.

## Chapter 2

<sup>3</sup> Standing nearby was Joseph[^2-3-1], who was a friend both to Pilate and to the Lord. Realizing that they were about to have him crucified, he approached Pilate and requested the Lord’s body for burial.

<sup>4</sup> Pilate sent a message to Herod, asking for the body.

<sup>5</sup> Herod replied, “Brother Pilate, we would have buried him in any case, even if nobody had requested it, because the sabbath is about to break[^2-5-1]. The law says that the sun should not go down[^2-5-2] on someone who has been executed.” Then he delivered him to the people, one day before their feast of Unleavened Bread.

## Chapter 3

<sup>6</sup> The men who had taken the Lord ran along, shoving him, and said, “Let us haul the Son of God off, since he is in our power.”

<sup>7</sup> They wrapped a purple robe[^3-7-1] around him and sat him in the seat of judgment. “Give a just verdict, King of Israel,” they said.

<sup>8</sup> A man among them fetched a wreath of thorns and placed it on the Lord’s head.

<sup>9</sup> Some of the bystanders spat in his face and others slapped his cheeks, while still others prodded him with a reed[^3-9-1]. Some scourged him, saying, “This is the honor we will pay to the Son of God.”

## Chapter 4

<sup>10</sup> Two criminals[^4-10-1] were led in, and they crucified the Lord in the middle, between the two. He said nothing, as if he suffered no pain.

<sup>11</sup> After raising the cross, they put an inscription on it: “This is the King of Israel.”

<sup>12</sup> They placed his garments in front of him, shared them out, and threw dice[^4-12-1] for them.

<sup>13</sup> Then one of those criminals rebuked them. “We are being punished this way for the wrongs we committed,” he said, “but this man has become the savior of humankind[^4-13-1]. What injustice has he done you?”

<sup>14</sup> Angry with him, they gave orders not to break his legs[^4-14-1], so that his death would come with torture.

## Chapter 5

<sup>15</sup> Midday came, and a darkness fell over the whole of Judea[^5-15-1]. People were troubled and uneasy that the sun might already have gone down while he still lived. The law, for them, says that the sun must not go down[^5-15-2] on a man who has been put to death.

<sup>16</sup> Someone among them said, “Make him drink gall[^5-16-1] mixed with vinegar.” They blended the drink and gave it to him.

<sup>17</sup> In this way they carried everything through to the end and filled up the full measure of their sins[^5-17-1].

<sup>18</sup> Many were going about with lamps, in the belief that it was night, and they stumbled[^5-18-1].

<sup>19</sup> The Lord shouted, “My power, my power, you have abandoned me[^5-19-1]!” With these words he was lifted up[^5-19-2].

<sup>20</sup> In that very hour the veil of the Jerusalem temple[^5-20-1] split down the middle.

## Chapter 6

<sup>21</sup> Next the nails were drawn[^6-21-1] out of the Lord’s hands, and he was laid on the earth. The entire earth quaked[^6-21-2], and a great fear arose.

<sup>22</sup> Then the sun came out, and the hour was found to be the ninth[^6-22-1].

<sup>23</sup> The Jews were delighted. They released his body to Joseph for burial, because Joseph had witnessed the good that he had done.

<sup>24</sup> Joseph received the Lord, bathed his body, bound it in a linen sheet[^6-24-1], and had it carried into his private tomb, the one known as Joseph’s Garden.

## Chapter 7

<sup>25</sup> Then the Jews, along with the elders and priests, understood how great a wrong they had done to themselves. They started to strike their chests[^7-25-1] and wail: “Our sins bring doom! The judgment and Jerusalem’s end have drawn close!”

<sup>26</sup> I was grieving too[^7-26-1], along with my companions. We were hurt in our minds[^7-26-2] and stayed out of sight, because they were hunting for us as if we were lawbreakers who meant to set the temple ablaze.

<sup>27</sup> Through all this we fasted, and we stayed in seclusion, mourning and crying day and night until the sabbath.

## Chapter 8

<sup>28</sup> The scribes, Pharisees and elders met up. They had been told that the whole people were muttering and striking their chests, saying, “If such tremendous signs came at his death, look how righteous he must have been!”

<sup>29</sup> Fear took hold of the elders. They went to Pilate and pleaded with him:

<sup>30</sup> “Hand soldiers over to us to watch his tomb for three days. Otherwise his disciples may come and steal him away, the people may conclude[^8-30-1] that he was raised from the dead, and they may do us harm.”

<sup>31</sup> Pilate assigned them the centurion Petronius[^8-31-1], together with some soldiers, to guard the grave. Elders and scribes accompanied them to the tomb,

<sup>32</sup> and along with the centurion and his soldiers, all those present rolled a huge stone across the entrance of the tomb.

<sup>33</sup> They also plastered seven seals[^8-33-1] over it, then set up a tent on the spot and stood guard.

## Chapter 9

<sup>34</sup> As the sabbath began to break the next morning, a throng arrived from Jerusalem and its surroundings, eager to look at the sealed tomb.

<sup>35</sup> In the night when the Lord’s day[^9-35-1] was about to dawn, the soldiers were on watch, standing guard in pairs, shift by shift[^9-35-2]. A great sound rang out from heaven,

<sup>36</sup> and they saw the sky torn open and two men[^9-36-1] descending from it, glowing brightly, who came near to the tomb.

<sup>37</sup> By itself, the stone that had been set against the entrance rolled off and shifted aside. The tomb stood open, and the two young men[^9-37-1] entered it.

## Chapter 10

<sup>38</sup> Seeing it, those soldiers roused the centurion and the elders, who were also keeping watch there.

<sup>39</sup> As they were telling what they had witnessed, they noticed three men leaving the tomb again. Two of them held up[^10-39-1] the third, and a cross trailed after them.

<sup>40</sup> Where the heads of the two reached as far as the sky, the head of the one whom they led by the hand[^10-40-1] rose higher than the heavens.

<sup>41</sup> A voice from the heavens could be heard asking, “Have you made your proclamation to the sleepers[^10-41-1]?”

<sup>42</sup> From the cross came the reply, “Yes[^10-42-1].”

## Chapter 11

<sup>43</sup> They talked it over among themselves, and decided to go and give Pilate an account of this.

<sup>44</sup> They were still thinking about it when the heavens seemed to open a second time, and some person[^11-44-1] descended and went into the grave.

<sup>45</sup> After witnessing this, the centurion’s party left the tomb they had been watching and rushed to Pilate while it was still night. They told him all they had seen, in great agony of mind: “He really was God’s Son[^11-45-1].”

<sup>46</sup> Pilate replied, “The blood of God’s Son is not on my hands[^11-46-1]. You chose this.”

<sup>47</sup> Then all of them crowded around him, begging and pressing him to tell the centurion and the soldiers to keep silent about what they had seen.

<sup>48</sup> “It is to our advantage,” they said, “to bear the heaviest sin in the sight of God, rather than to end up in the hands of the Jewish crowd and be stoned.”

<sup>49</sup> Pilate accordingly instructed the centurion and the soldiers to tell no one.

## Chapter 12

<sup>50</sup> Very early on the Lord’s day, Mary Magdalene[^12-50-1], a woman disciple of the Lord, had been afraid because of the Jews, whose anger was blazing. For that reason she had not performed at the Lord’s tomb the rites that women usually perform for the dead and for those dear to them[^12-50-2].

<sup>51</sup> Now she brought her women friends[^12-51-1] along and went to the place where he had been put.

<sup>52</sup> They feared being seen by the Jews, and they said to each other, “If we could not weep and mourn on the day when he was crucified, let us at least do so now, at his grave.

<sup>53</sup> But who will move the stone aside for us, the one placed in front of the tomb, so we can get inside, sit with him, and carry out our duty[^12-53-1]?

<sup>54</sup> The stone was enormous, and we worry that someone will spot us. If we cannot get in, let us put down by the entrance the things we have brought in his memory. We will cry and beat our chests until we are back at home.”

## Chapter 13

<sup>55</sup> They set out and found the grave already opened. Approaching, they stooped to peer inside, and there they saw a good-looking young man[^13-55-1] seated in the middle of the tomb, dressed in a brilliantly shining garment. He addressed them:

<sup>56</sup> “What brings you here? Who is it you are seeking? Surely not the one who was crucified? He has been raised[^13-56-1] and has left. If you cannot believe it, stoop down and look at the spot where he lay: he is not there. He was raised, and he went back to the one who had sent him.”

<sup>57</sup> Terrified, the women fled[^13-57-1].

## Chapter 14

<sup>58</sup> It was the final day of Unleavened Bread[^14-58-1]. The feast was coming to its end, and a good many people were setting off for their homes.

<sup>59</sup> We, the twelve disciples[^14-59-1] of the Lord, were crying and mourning, and each of us, sad at what had taken place, left for home.
