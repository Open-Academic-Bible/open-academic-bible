# Martyrdom of Polycarp

A letter from the church at Smyrna describing the arrest and execution of its bishop Polycarp, probably in 155 or 156. It is the earliest Christian account written to describe a martyrdom, and it already speaks of keeping the anniversary of his death. Like other martyr accounts of its time, it portrays the Jews of the city as hostile; the text is translated as it stands.

<sup>1</sup> The church of God residing in Smyrna[^0-1-1], to the church of God residing at Philomelium, and to all the communities of the holy and universal church everywhere: may the mercy, peace, and love of God the Father and of our Lord Jesus Christ increase for you.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> We have written to you, brothers and sisters[^1-1-1], about the martyrs and about the blessed Polycarp, who, as though sealing it by his own witness, brought the persecution to an end. Almost everything that came before happened so that the Lord might show us from above a martyrdom in keeping with the gospel.

<sup>2</sup> He waited to be betrayed, as the Lord did, so that we also would copy him, keeping an eye not only on our own situation[^1-2-1] but on that of those near us. True, unshakable love wants salvation not for oneself alone but for all the brothers and sisters.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> Blessed and noble, then, are all the martyrdoms that have taken place according to God’s will. For we must show reverence and credit God with authority over all things.

<sup>2</sup> Who could fail to admire their nobility, endurance, and devotion to their Master? They were lashed until the very arrangement of their flesh, down to the veins and arteries inside, was visible, and they bore it, so that onlookers pitied them and wept. Some reached such a height of nobility that not one of them cried out or groaned, showing us all that at that hour the noble martyrs of Christ, under torture, were away from the flesh, or rather that the Lord was standing beside them and conversing with them.

<sup>3</sup> Giving their minds to Christ’s grace, they scorned worldly torments, purchasing eternal life with one hour. The fire of their cruel torturers felt cold to them, since they kept in view their escape from the fire that is eternal and never goes out, and with the eyes of their hearts they looked up at the good things stored up for those who endure, things no ear has heard[^2-3-1], no eye has seen, and no human heart has imagined. The Lord showed these to them, who were no longer human but already angels.

<sup>4</sup> Likewise those condemned to the wild animals endured terrible punishments: they were laid on sharp shells and tormented with other kinds of varied tortures, in the hope that the tyrant could turn them to denial by relentless punishment. For the devil devised many schemes against them.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Thanks be to God: the devil did not prevail over all. The most noble Germanicus put heart into the fearful by the endurance he showed, and he fought the beasts in a remarkable way. When the proconsul tried to persuade him and urged him to pity his youth, he pulled the beast onto himself by force, wanting to leave their unjust and lawless life sooner.

<sup>2</sup> At this, the whole crowd, amazed at the nobility of the God-loving and God-fearing race of Christians, shouted: “Away with the atheists[^3-2-1]! Let Polycarp be found!”

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> One man, named Quintus, a Phrygian who had recently come from Phrygia, lost his nerve when he saw the beasts. He was the one who had forced himself and some others to come forward voluntarily. The proconsul pleaded with him at length and persuaded him to swear and to sacrifice. For this reason, brothers and sisters, we do not approve of those who hand themselves over, since the gospel does not teach this.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> The truly admirable Polycarp, when he first heard, was not alarmed, but wanted to stay in the city. Most of the others persuaded him to slip away. He left for a small farm not far from the city and stayed there with a few companions, doing nothing night and day but praying for everyone and for the churches throughout the world, as was his habit.

<sup>2</sup> While he was praying, three days before his arrest, he had a vision: he saw his pillow being burned up by fire. Turning to those with him he said: “I must be burned alive.”

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> As those searching for him kept at it, he moved to another small farm, and the searchers immediately arrived. When they did not find him, they seized two young slaves, one of whom confessed under torture.

<sup>2</sup> He could not have stayed hidden, because those who betrayed him were from his own household. The police chief[^6-2-1], who had received the same name, Herod as he was called, hurried to bring him into the stadium, so that Polycarp might complete his appointed lot as a partner of Christ, and his betrayers might receive the same penalty as Judas.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> With the young slave in custody, on the Day of Preparation[^7-1-1], around dinner time, the pursuers went out, police and horsemen with their usual weapons, rushing as though after a bandit. Late in the evening they converged on the place and found him lying in an upstairs room. He could have gone to another place from there, but he refused, saying, “God’s will be done[^7-1-2].”

<sup>2</sup> When he heard that they had come, he went down and spoke with them. Those present marveled at his age and calm, and wondered why there was such an effort to arrest a man so old. He immediately ordered food and drink to be served to them at that hour, as much as they wanted, and he asked them to give him an hour to pray undisturbed.

<sup>3</sup> They permitted it, so he stood up and prayed, so filled with God’s grace that for two hours he could not fall silent, and his hearers were stunned. Many were sorry that they had come for such a godlike old man.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> When at last he finished praying, after remembering everyone who had ever come across him, small and great, famous and unknown, and the entire universal church[^8-1-1] throughout the world, the time came to leave. They put him on a donkey and led him toward the city. It was a great sabbath.

<sup>2</sup> The police chief Herod and his father Nicetes came out to meet him; they moved him into their carriage and, sitting alongside, they tried to talk him round: “What harm is there in saying ‘Caesar is lord,’ and offering incense, and doing what goes with that, and being saved?” At first he gave them no answer, but when they kept on he said, “I will not do what you advise.”

<sup>3</sup> When they failed to persuade him, they spoke terrible words to him and hurried to shove him down, so that as he came down from the carriage he scraped his shin. Without turning around, as if he had suffered nothing, he walked quickly and eagerly as he was led to the stadium. The uproar in the stadium was so great that no one could be heard.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> As Polycarp entered the stadium, a voice came from heaven: “Be strong[^9-1-1], Polycarp, and act like a man.” No one saw who spoke, but those of our people who were there heard the voice. As he was brought in, there was a great uproar when people heard that Polycarp had been arrested.

<sup>2</sup> When he was brought forward, the proconsul asked whether he was Polycarp. When he said yes, the proconsul tried to persuade him to deny, saying, “Have respect for your age,” and other things of this kind, as they are accustomed to say: “Swear by the fortune of Caesar; repent; say, ‘Away with the atheists.’” Polycarp, with a stern face, looked at the whole crowd of lawless gentiles in the stadium, waved his hand at them, groaned, looked up to heaven, and said, “Away with the atheists.”

<sup>3</sup> When the proconsul pressed him and said, “Swear, and I will release you; curse Christ,” Polycarp said, “For eighty-six years[^9-3-1] I have been his slave, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my king who saved me?”

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> When the proconsul insisted again and said, “Swear by the fortune of Caesar,” Polycarp answered, “If you are vainly hoping that I will swear by Caesar’s fortune, as you say, and pretend not to know who I am, listen plainly: I am a Christian. And if you want to learn the teaching of Christianity, set a day and listen.”

<sup>2</sup> The proconsul said, “Persuade the people.” Polycarp said, “You I considered worthy of an explanation. We have been taught to give to rulers and authorities appointed by God[^10-2-1] the honor due them, so long as it does not harm us. But those people I do not think worthy of a defense before them.”

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> The proconsul said, “I have wild animals; I will throw you to them if you do not change your mind.” Polycarp said, “Call them. For us, a change of mind from better to worse is not possible; but it is good to change from what is harsh to what is right.”

<sup>2</sup> The proconsul replied: “I will have fire consume you, if you look down on the animals, unless you repent.” Polycarp said: “The fire you threaten burns for a moment and is quickly put out. You are ignorant of the fire of the judgment to come, the eternal punishment reserved for the ungodly. But why wait? Bring what you will.”

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> Saying these things and more besides, he was filled with boldness and joy, and his face was full of grace; so he did not fall apart, shaken by what was said to him. The proconsul, on the contrary, was dumbfounded, and sent his own herald to the middle of the stadium to announce three times: “Polycarp has declared himself a Christian.”

<sup>2</sup> When the herald said this, the whole crowd of gentiles and Jews living in Smyrna shouted in uncontrollable rage and with a loud voice: “This is the teacher of Asia, the father of the Christians, the destroyer of our gods, who teaches many not to sacrifice or worship.” As they said this, they shouted and asked the Asiarch[^12-2-1] Philip to let a lion loose on Polycarp. He said that he could not, because he had already closed the animal hunts.

<sup>3</sup> Then they decided to shout together that Polycarp should be burned alive. For the vision that had appeared to him on his pillow had to be fulfilled, when he saw it burning as he prayed, and turned and said prophetically to the faithful with him, “I must be burned alive.”

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> This happened with great speed, faster than it can be told, as the crowds immediately gathered wood and kindling from the workshops and the baths, with the Jews especially eager to help, as is their custom.

<sup>2</sup> When the pyre was ready, he took off all his outer clothing, loosened his belt, and tried to take off his sandals too, something he had not done before, because each of the faithful always eagerly competed to be the first to touch his skin; for even before his martyrdom he had been adorned with every good thing because of his good conduct.

<sup>3</sup> At once the tools fitted for the pyre were put around him. When they were about to nail him as well, he said: “Let me be as I am. The one who gave me the power to endure the fire will also give me the power to remain on the pyre without flinching, even without the safeguard of your nails.”

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> They did not nail him, but only tied him. Placing his hands behind his back and bound there, he was like a splendid ram chosen from a great flock for sacrifice, a whole burnt offering made ready for God. Looking up to heaven, he said: “Lord God, Almighty, Father of your beloved and blessed servant[^14-1-1] Jesus Christ, through whom we have received knowledge of you; God of angels, of powers, of all creation, and of the whole family of the righteous who live in your presence:

<sup>2</sup> I bless you for considering me worthy of this day and this hour, so that I would have a place among the martyrs in the cup of your Christ[^14-2-1], bound for the rising to everlasting life of both soul and body, in the imperishability that the Holy Spirit gives. Let me be received among them in your sight today as a sacrifice that is rich and welcome, just as you made ready beforehand, showed beforehand, and have brought to completion, the truthful God who never deceives.

<sup>3</sup> On account of this, and for everything else, I praise you, I bless you, I glorify you through the everlasting heavenly high priest[^14-3-1], Jesus Christ, your beloved servant. Through him, with him and the Holy Spirit, glory be yours now and in the ages that are coming. Amen.”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> After he had sent up his Amen and finished the prayer, the men tending the fire set it alight. As a great flame shot up, we saw a wonder, we to whom it was given to see, and who were preserved to tell the rest what had happened.

<sup>2</sup> The fire formed itself into a vault, like the sail of a ship swelled by the wind, and ringed the martyr’s body about. He stood in the middle, not as flesh being burned, but as bread being baked, or as gold and silver being refined in a furnace. We also perceived a fragrance so sweet, like frankincense or some other costly spice.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> At last the lawless ones, when they saw that fire could not consume his body, ordered an executioner to step up and drive a short sword into him. When he did, a dove[^16-1-1] flew out along with so much blood that the fire was quenched, and the entire crowd was amazed at how great a difference there is between unbelievers and the chosen.

<sup>2</sup> The most admirable martyr Polycarp was one of these, a teacher in our own times, apostolic and prophetic, bishop of the universal church in Smyrna. Every word that he uttered from his mouth was fulfilled and will be fulfilled.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> But the jealous, envious, and evil one, the adversary of the race of the righteous, saw the greatness of his witness and his blameless life from the beginning, saw that he was crowned with the crown of incorruptibility and had carried off an undeniable prize, and he made sure that we could not take even his poor body, though many wished to do so and to have a share in his holy flesh.

<sup>2</sup> So he prodded Nicetes, Herod’s father and Alce’s brother, to approach the governor and ask him not to release the body. “Or else,” he said, “they will give up the crucified man and start to worship this one.” This was said at the urging and insistence of the Jews, who also stood watch when we were about to take the body from the fire. They failed to understand that we could never abandon Christ, who suffered, faultless, on behalf of sinners for the salvation of everyone in the world who is being saved, nor worship anyone else.

<sup>3</sup> Him, the Son of God, we worship; the martyrs we love, as the Lord’s disciples and imitators, as they deserve, because of their matchless loyalty to their own king and teacher. May we be allowed to share with them and to be their fellow students.

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> When the centurion saw the contentiousness of the Jews, he placed the body in the middle and burned it, as is their custom.

<sup>2</sup> So we later gathered up his bones, which are more valuable than precious stones and more tested than gold, and laid them where it was fitting.

<sup>3</sup> Where it is possible, the Lord will let us assemble in that place with rejoicing and gladness to keep the anniversary of his martyrdom as his birthday, as a remembrance of those who have already contended and as training and preparation for those yet to come.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> That is the story of the blessed Polycarp, who was the twelfth martyr at Smyrna counting those from Philadelphia, yet he alone is remembered by everyone above the rest, to the extent that even the gentiles talk about him everywhere. He was not only a famous teacher but also a martyr beyond compare, and everyone longs to imitate his martyrdom, which came about in keeping with the gospel of Christ.

<sup>2</sup> By endurance he defeated the unjust ruler and so won the crown of incorruptibility. Now he rejoices with the apostles and all the righteous as he glorifies God the almighty Father and blesses our Lord Jesus Christ, savior of our souls, steersman of our bodies, and shepherd of the universal church all over the world.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> You asked that the events be set out for you at greater length. For now we have reported them in outline through our brother Marcion. When you have learned of them, forward the letter to the brothers and sisters beyond you, so that they too may glorify the Lord, who chooses from among his own slaves.

<sup>2</sup> To the one who can bring all of us, by his grace and gift, into his heavenly kingdom through his only Son[^20-2-1] Jesus Christ: glory, honor, power, and greatness forever. Greet all the saints. Those with us send you greetings, and so does Evarestus, who has written this with his entire household.

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> The blessed Polycarp bore witness on the second of the month Xanthicus, the seventh day before the Kalends of March[^21-1-1], on a great sabbath, at the eighth hour. Herod arrested him while Philip of Tralles was high priest and Statius Quadratus was proconsul, and while Jesus Christ was reigning for ever. To him belong glory, honor, majesty, and an everlasting throne, from one generation to the next. Amen.

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> We pray, brothers and sisters, that you fare well, living by the word of Jesus Christ in line with the gospel, with whom be glory to God the Father and the Holy Spirit, for the salvation of the holy elect, as the blessed Polycarp bore witness. May we be found in his footsteps in the kingdom of Jesus Christ.

<sup>2</sup> Gaius copied this from the papers of Irenaeus, a disciple of Polycarp, who lived in the same circles as Irenaeus. I, Socrates, in Corinth, wrote it down from the copies of Gaius. Grace be with all.

<sup>3</sup> And I, Pionius[^22-3-1], wrote it again from the copy mentioned above, after searching for it, because the blessed Polycarp revealed it to me in a vision, as I will explain in what follows, gathering it when it was already nearly worn out with time, so that the Lord Jesus Christ may gather me also with his elect into his heavenly kingdom, to whom be glory with the Father and the Holy Spirit forever and ever. Amen.
