# Epistle to Diognetus

An anonymous defense of Christianity addressed to an otherwise unknown Diognetus, probably from the second or early third century. Its description of Christians as "the soul of the world" is among the best-known passages of early Christian writing. It was known from a single manuscript, which was destroyed in 1870; the text has gaps, and chapters 11–12 may come from another author.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Most excellent[^1-1-1] Diognetus, I can see how very much you want to learn the Christians’ way of honoring God. You ask your questions with such care and clarity: Which God have they put their trust in, and how do they serve him, that all of them look down on the world and make light of death, neither regarding as gods the ones the Greeks think are gods nor keeping the superstitions of the Jews? What tender affection do they have for each other? And why has this new people, this new practice, entered life only now and not before? I am glad of your zeal, and I ask God, who gives us the ability both to speak and to listen, that I may speak in the way that will most improve you as you listen, and that you may listen in a way that does not hurt the speaker.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> Come now. Cleanse yourself of every thought that has taken possession of your mind, throw off the habit that is deceiving you, and become a new person from the beginning, as someone who is about to hear a new word, just as you said yourself. Consider, not only with your eyes but with your intelligence, what material and what shape belong to the things that you name and regard as gods.

<sup>2</sup> Is not one of them a stone[^2-2-1], like the stone we tread on, and another bronze, no better than the vessels forged for our use, another wood, already rotting, another silver, needing a person to guard it so that it will not be stolen, another iron, eaten by rust, another clay, no better than the pot made for the lowliest service?

<sup>3</sup> Are not all of these of perishable material? Were they not forged by iron and fire? Did not a stonecutter shape one, a bronze smith another, a silversmith another, a potter another? Before the skill of these craftsmen gave them their present shapes, could not each of them have been given a different form, even now? And could not the vessels that are made today of the same material become like these, if they happened on the same craftsmen?

<sup>4</sup> And might not these very objects that you now worship be turned by human hands into vessels like the others? Are they not all deaf, all blind, all lifeless, all without sensation, all unable to move, all rotting, all decaying?

<sup>5</sup> Do you call these things gods? Do you serve them? Do you bow down to them? And in the end you become just like them[^2-5-1].

<sup>6</sup> Is that why you hate the Christians: because they do not take these to be gods?

<sup>7</sup> For you who think you praise and honor them, do you not in fact despise them far more? Do you not mock and insult them far more, worshiping the stone and clay ones without guarding them, while locking up the silver and gold ones at night and posting guards beside them by day so that they will not be stolen?

<sup>8</sup> As for the honors you think you offer: if the idols have feelings, you are punishing them instead; if they have none, you are proving it, worshiping them with blood and the smoke of fat.

<sup>9</sup> Let one of you put up with this; let one of you allow it to be done to himself. But no human being will willingly endure this punishment, for a person has feeling and reason. The stone endures it, because it feels nothing. So are you not proving its lack of feeling?

<sup>10</sup> There is a great deal more I could say about why Christians have not become slaves to gods like these; but if someone is not satisfied even with this, I think it pointless to say more.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Next, I think you most want to hear why they do not worship in the same way as the Jews.

<sup>2</sup> The Jews, then, in so far as they abstain from the service described above, are right to worship one God of all and to regard him as Master. But if they offer him this worship in the same manner as those just mentioned, they are mistaken.

<sup>3</sup> What the Greeks offer to senseless and deaf things shows their folly. What these people offer, supposing that they are giving it to a God who needs it, they would more likely count as folly than as reverence.

<sup>4</sup> For the one who made heaven and earth[^3-4-1] and everything in them, and who provides us all with what we need, would himself need none of those things that he supplies to those who think they are giving them.

<sup>5</sup> Those who think they are performing sacrifices to him with blood and the smoke of fat and whole burnt offerings, and honoring him with these gifts, seem to me no different from those who show the same eagerness toward deaf idols. The latter offer to things that cannot share in the honor, the former to one who needs nothing.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> As for their timidity about foods, their superstition about sabbaths, their boasting about circumcision, and their pretense about fasting and new moons, which are ridiculous and not worth discussing, I do not think you need to learn from me.

<sup>2</sup> For to accept some of the things God created for human use as well made, and to refuse others as useless and superfluous: how is that not lawless?

<sup>3</sup> And to lie about God, as if he forbade doing anything good on the day of the sabbaths[^4-3-1]: how is that not impious?

<sup>4</sup> And to boast about the cutting of the flesh as proof of election, as if for this reason they were especially loved by God: how is that not worthy of mockery?

<sup>5</sup> And their keeping watch on stars and moon to observe months and days, and dividing God’s arrangements and the changes of the seasons to suit their own impulses, some for feasts, some for mourning: who could consider this a sign of reverence rather than far more of folly?

<sup>6</sup> As for the common frivolity and deception, and the meddling and boasting of the Jews, I think you have learned enough about why Christians rightly abstain from them. But as for the mystery of their own religion, do not expect to be able to learn it from a human being.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Christians are not distinguished from other people by country, language, or customs.

<sup>2</sup> They do not live in cities of their own, or use some strange dialect, or practice a life that is out of the ordinary.

<sup>3</sup> This teaching of theirs was not discovered through the thought and reflection of inquisitive people, and they do not champion a human doctrine, as some do.

<sup>4</sup> They dwell in Greek and in foreign cities, wherever each one’s lot has fallen, and they conform to local customs in dress, food, and the rest of life; and yet the character of their own citizenship that they exhibit is extraordinary and, by everyone’s admission, contrary to expectation.

<sup>5</sup> They live in their own countries, but as resident aliens[^5-5-1]; they take part in everything as citizens, and endure everything as foreigners. Every foreign land is their homeland, and every homeland is a foreign land.

<sup>6</sup> They marry like everyone else, and they have children, but they do not abandon their newborn.

<sup>7</sup> They share a table, but not a bed.

<sup>8</sup> They exist in flesh, but they do not live by the flesh[^5-8-1].

<sup>9</sup> They spend their days on earth, but they are citizens of heaven.

<sup>10</sup> They obey the established laws, and in their own lives they surpass the laws.

<sup>11</sup> They love everyone, and are persecuted by everyone.

<sup>12</sup> They are unknown, and yet condemned; they are put to death, and yet made alive[^5-12-1].

<sup>13</sup> They are poor, and make many rich; they lack everything, and have plenty of everything.

<sup>14</sup> They are dishonored, and in their dishonor are glorified; they are slandered, and yet vindicated.

<sup>15</sup> They are insulted, and they bless[^5-15-1]; they are mistreated, and they show respect.

<sup>16</sup> When they do good they are punished as evildoers; when punished they rejoice as though given life.

<sup>17</sup> They are attacked by Jews as foreigners and persecuted by Greeks, and those who hate them cannot state the reason for their hostility.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> Put simply: Christians are to the world what the soul is to the body.

<sup>2</sup> The soul is spread through all the parts of the body, and Christians through the cities of the world.

<sup>3</sup> The soul resides in the body but does not come from the body; Christians reside in the world but do not come from the world[^6-3-1].

<sup>4</sup> The soul is unseen, held in a seen body; Christians are recognized as living in the world, but their reverence for God stays unseen.

<sup>5</sup> The flesh hates the soul and wars against it, though wronged in nothing, because it is kept from indulging its pleasures; the world hates Christians, though wronged in nothing, because they oppose its pleasures.

<sup>6</sup> The soul loves the flesh and its limbs even though the flesh hates it; Christians love those who hate them[^6-6-1].

<sup>7</sup> The soul is shut up in the body, yet itself holds the body together; Christians are held in the world as in a prison, yet they themselves hold the world together.

<sup>8</sup> The immortal soul lives in a mortal tent[^6-8-1]; Christians live as aliens among perishable things, awaiting the imperishability in heaven.

<sup>9</sup> The soul, when ill-treated in food and drink, grows better; Christians, when punished day by day, increase all the more.

<sup>10</sup> God has set them in so important a post that it is not permitted for them to desert it.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> This, as I said, is no earthly invention that was handed over to them, nor do they think a mortal idea worth guarding so carefully, nor have they been entrusted with the management of human mysteries.

<sup>2</sup> No: the truly almighty, all-making, unseen God himself established the truth and the holy, unfathomable Word from heaven among people and fastened it in their hearts. It was not, as one might imagine, a servant he dispatched to them, or an angel, or a ruler, or any of those who run earthly affairs or are entrusted with the governance of the heavens. It was the very designer and maker of the universe[^7-2-1], through whom he made the heavens, through whom he shut the sea inside its own limits; whose mysteries every element keeps faithfully; from whom the sun received the measure of its daily journeys to observe; whom the moon obeys when he commands it to shine at night; whom the stars obey as they follow the course of the moon; by whom everything has been set in order, marked out, and put in subjection: sky and everything in the sky, earth and everything on earth, sea and everything in the sea, fire, air, the deep, things on high, things below, things in between. This is the one he sent to them.

<sup>3</sup> Did he send him, as a human being might reckon, to rule by tyranny, fear, and terror?

<sup>4</sup> Not at all. He sent him with fairness and gentleness, like a king who sends a king, his son. He sent him as God, he sent him as a human being to human beings, he sent him to save and to persuade, not to compel, because compulsion is foreign to God.

<sup>5</sup> He sent him as one calling, not pursuing; he sent him as one loving, not judging.

<sup>6</sup> He will send him as judge, in fact. And who will be able to stand at his coming? ...[^7-6-1]

<sup>7</sup> ... Do you not see[^7-7-1] them thrown to wild animals so that they would deny the Lord, and not being overcome?

<sup>8</sup> Do you not see that the more of them are punished, the more the others multiply?

<sup>9</sup> These are not deeds that look human. They are God’s power. They are evidence that he is present.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> For what human being at all knew what God is before he came?

<sup>2</sup> Or do you take seriously the vain and silly talk of those supposedly reliable philosophers? Some of them said that God is fire (the very place they are going to, they call God[^8-2-1]), others that God is water, and others some other element that God created.

<sup>3</sup> Yet if any of these statements is acceptable, one could equally declare each of the other created things to be God.

<sup>4</sup> But these are the tricks and deceptions of conjurers.

<sup>5</sup> No human being has either seen him or known him; he himself revealed himself.

<sup>6</sup> He revealed himself through faith, by which alone it is permitted to see God.

<sup>7</sup> For the master and maker of all, God, who made all things and ordered them in sequence, was not only kind to humanity but also patient.

<sup>8</sup> He has always been so, and is, and will be: kind, good, free from anger, and true; indeed he alone is good[^8-8-1].

<sup>9</sup> Having conceived a great and inexpressible plan, he shared it with his servant[^8-9-1] alone.

<sup>10</sup> So as long as he kept it in mystery and guarded his wise plan, he seemed to neglect us and not care about us.

<sup>11</sup> But when he opened it up through his beloved servant and made plain what had been planned from the start, he gave us everything together: sharing in his gifts, seeing, and understanding, things which none of us would ever have looked for.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> Having already planned everything with his servant, he allowed us, until the previous period, to be swept along as we wished by unruly impulses, drawn off by pleasures and desires. He did not at all take pleasure in our sins; he put up with them. He did not approve of that season of wrongdoing; he was building the present season of righteousness. So, after proving by our own deeds in that earlier time that we were unworthy of life, we would by God’s goodness now be counted worthy, and, having demonstrated that we ourselves could not enter God’s kingdom, we would be made able by God’s power.

<sup>2</sup> But when our injustice was complete, and it had been fully shown that punishment and death, its wages, were to be expected, and the time arrived which God had appointed to reveal at last his goodness and power (oh, the overflowing kindness and love of God!), he did not hate us, or reject us, or remember our wrongs against us. He was patient, he endured, and in mercy he himself took on our sins. He himself handed over his own Son as the ransom price[^9-2-1] for us: the holy for the lawless, the blameless for the wicked, the just for the unjust, the incorruptible for the corruptible, the immortal for the mortal.

<sup>3</sup> What else could cover our sins except his righteousness?

<sup>4</sup> In whom could we, the lawless and ungodly, be made righteous, except in the Son of God alone?

<sup>5</sup> O sweet exchange! O unsearchable working! O unexpected benefits! The lawlessness of many is hidden in one righteous one, and the righteousness of one makes many lawless ones righteous[^9-5-1].

<sup>6</sup> Having shown in the earlier time the impossibility of our nature gaining life, and now having revealed a savior able to save even what is impossible, he wanted us in both cases to trust his kindness, to regard him as nurse, father, teacher, counselor, physician, mind, light, honor, glory, strength, and life, and not to worry about clothing and food[^9-6-1].

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> If you too long for this faith, you will first receive knowledge of the Father ...[^10-1-1]

<sup>2</sup> For God loved human beings, for whose sake he made the world, to whom he subjected everything on earth, to whom he gave reason and mind, to whom alone he allowed to look upward to him, whom he formed from his own image[^10-2-1], to whom he sent his only Son, to whom he promised the kingdom in heaven, and will give it to those who love him.

<sup>3</sup> Once you have come to know him, what joy do you expect to be filled with? And how will you love the one who loved you so much ahead of you[^10-3-1]?

<sup>4</sup> If you love him, you will imitate his goodness. Do not be surprised that a human being can imitate God: one can, if God is willing.

<sup>5</sup> For happiness does not consist in oppressing one’s neighbors, nor in wishing to have more than the weaker, nor in being rich and forcing those below you; no one can imitate God in these things, but they are far from his greatness.

<sup>6</sup> But whoever takes up a neighbor’s burden[^10-6-1], whoever wishes to benefit another who is worse off where he himself is better off, whoever gives what he has received from God to those in need and so becomes a god to those who receive it: this one is an imitator of God.

<sup>7</sup> Then, though you are on earth, you will see that God governs in heaven; then you will begin to speak God’s mysteries; then you will love and admire those who are punished for refusing to deny God; then you will condemn the deceit and error of the world, when you truly know what it is to live in heaven, when you despise what seems to be death here, when you fear the real death, which is reserved for those who will be condemned to the eternal fire[^10-7-1] that will punish to the end those handed over to it.

<sup>8</sup> Then you will marvel at those who endure the temporary fire for the sake of righteousness[^10-8-1], and call them blessed, when you know that other fire.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> What I say is not foreign, and my inquiry is not unreasonable. I was a student of the apostles, and now I am a teacher of the gentiles[^11-1-1]. I serve worthily the things handed down to those who are becoming students of truth.

<sup>2</sup> For who, rightly taught and made a friend of the Word, would not seek to learn clearly what the Word plainly showed to disciples? To them the Word, when he appeared, made it clear, speaking with boldness, not understood by unbelievers but explaining to disciples, who, being reckoned faithful by him, knew the mysteries of the Father.

<sup>3</sup> For this reason he sent the Word, to appear to the world: dishonored by the people, preached by the apostles, believed by the nations.

<sup>4</sup> This is the one who is from the beginning, who appeared as new and was found to be ancient, and who is ever young, being born in the hearts of the saints.

<sup>5</sup> This is the one who always is, who today is counted a son, through whom the church is enriched and grace is unfolded and multiplied among the saints, providing understanding, revealing mysteries, proclaiming seasons, rejoicing over the faithful, giving to those who seek, those by whom the pledges of faith are not broken and the boundaries of the fathers are not overstepped.

<sup>6</sup> After that the fear of the law is sung, the grace of the prophets is acknowledged, the faith of the gospels is established, the tradition of the apostles is guarded, and the grace of the church bounds with joy.

<sup>7</sup> If you do not grieve this grace, you will know what the Word speaks, through whom he wishes, when he wishes.

<sup>8</sup> For whatever we have been moved, by the will of the commanding Word, to utter with labor, from love of the things revealed to us we share with you.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> When you read and listen eagerly to these things, you will know what God provides to those who love rightly, who have become a paradise of delight, having caused to grow in themselves a flourishing tree bearing every fruit, adorned with various fruits.

<sup>2</sup> In this garden stand a tree of knowledge and a tree of life[^12-2-1]. It is not the tree of knowledge that kills, but disobedience that kills.

<sup>3</sup> The things written are not without meaning: God planted a tree of knowledge and a tree of life in the middle of paradise from the beginning, showing life through knowledge; and because the first people did not use it purely, they were stripped bare by the serpent’s deception.

<sup>4</sup> For there is no life without knowledge, nor secure knowledge without true life; therefore each has been planted near the other.

<sup>5</sup> The apostle, seeing this power, and blaming knowledge exercised toward life without the command of truth, says: “Knowledge puffs up[^12-5-1], but love builds up.”

<sup>6</sup> Whoever thinks they know something without true knowledge, witnessed to by life, has not known; they are deceived by the serpent, having not loved life. But whoever has come to know with fear and seeks life plants in hope, expecting fruit.

<sup>7</sup> Let your heart be knowledge, and let your life be the true Word, received.

<sup>8</sup> Bearing its tree and picking its fruit, you will always harvest what is desired from God, things the serpent does not touch and deception does not mix with. Eve is not corrupted[^12-8-1], but is trusted as a virgin,

<sup>9</sup> and salvation is displayed, and apostles are made wise, and the Lord’s Passover[^12-9-1] advances, and seasons are gathered and harmonized with the world, and the Word, teaching the saints, rejoices; through him the Father is glorified. To him be glory forever. Amen.
