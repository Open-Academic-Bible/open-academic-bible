# Ignatius to Polycarp

Chapters: [1](#chapter-1) · [2](#chapter-2) · [3](#chapter-3) · [4](#chapter-4) · [5](#chapter-5) · [6](#chapter-6) · [7](#chapter-7) · [8](#chapter-8)

<sup>1</sup> Ignatius, who is also called Theophorus[^0-1-1], writes to Polycarp, bishop of the Smyrnaean church, but even more one who is watched over[^0-1-2] by God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ: warmest greetings.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> I welcome your mind in God, set firm as if on an immovable rock, and I give thanks all the more because I was counted worthy to see your blameless face. May I have joy of it in God.

<sup>2</sup> By the grace with which you are clothed, I call on you to lengthen your stride and to urge everyone to be saved. Defend your office with complete care for body and spirit. Be concerned about unity; nothing is better. Carry them all, as the Lord carries you; tolerate every person in love, as you are already doing.

<sup>3</sup> Devote yourself to constant prayer. Ask for more understanding than you have. Stay awake, with an unsleeping spirit. Speak to each person in the way of God’s own character. Carry everyone’s diseases[^1-3-1], like a perfect athlete. Where the labor is greater, the gain is great.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> If you love good disciples, you deserve no thanks; rather, bring the more troublesome ones into line through gentleness. Not every wound is healed with the same salve. Soothe fevers with warm compresses.

<sup>2</sup> Be shrewd as a snake[^2-2-1] in everything, and innocent as a dove always. That is why you are both flesh and spirit: you handle what you see in front of you with coaxing, and for the things you cannot see, you ask that they be shown to you, so that you are short of nothing and overflowing with every gift. The present moment needs you, as sailors need winds and the storm-tossed need a harbor, in order to reach God. Stay sober, as God’s athlete: the prize is incorruptibility and eternal life, about which you are already convinced. In everything I give my life for you[^2-2-2], I and my chains, which you loved.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Those who look reliable yet teach otherwise must not scare you. Hold your ground like an anvil under the hammer. A great athlete takes a beating and still wins. Most of all, we have to put up with everything for God’s sake, so that he in turn will put up with us.

<sup>2</sup> Be keener than you are. Learn to read the times. Look for the one above time, who has no time, who is unseen, yet became seen for us; who cannot be touched, who cannot suffer, yet for us became capable of suffering, and who in every way endured on our account.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> Widows must not be neglected. After the Lord, you must be their guardian. Nothing should happen without your approval, and you should do nothing without God, which indeed you do not. Stand firm.

<sup>2</sup> Gatherings should happen more frequently. Look for each person by name.

<sup>3</sup> Do not treat slaves, men or women, with contempt; but neither should they get conceited. Rather, let them be better slaves, for God’s glory, to win a finer freedom from God. They must not crave to be bought free out of the common fund, or they will turn out to be slaves of their own cravings.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Run away from wicked schemes, or better, preach against them. Speak to my sisters: they should love the Lord and be satisfied with their husbands in body and spirit. In the same way, command my brothers, in the name of Jesus Christ, to show their wives the love the Lord shows the church[^5-1-1].

<sup>2</sup> Should anyone have the strength to live in purity, honoring the Lord’s flesh, let them do so without boasting. If they boast, they are lost; if they become better known than the bishop, they are corrupted. Those who marry, men and women both, should form their union with the bishop’s consent, so that the marriage is in line with the Lord and not with passion. Let it all be done to honor God.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> Give your attention to the bishop, so that God may give his to you. I give my life for those who submit to the bishop, the presbyters, and the deacons, and may my portion in God be among theirs. Toil alongside each other, struggle together, race together, suffer together, rest together, rise together, as stewards, companions, and servants of God.

<sup>2</sup> Satisfy the commander you serve under, who also pays your wages. None of you should be caught deserting. Keep your baptism as your shield, faith as your helmet[^6-2-1], love as your lance, patience as your complete armor. Let your good works be the money you deposit, so that you may collect your full due. So be forbearing with each other in gentleness, as God is with you. I would like always to have joy in you.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> Since the church of Antioch in Syria has peace, as news has reached me, thanks to your prayer, I myself have grown more confident in God’s freedom from anxiety, as long as I get to God through suffering and am found a disciple at your resurrection.

<sup>2</sup> Polycarp, most blessed by God, it is fitting that you convene a council most suited to God and select someone who is very dear to you and does not tire easily, who may be called God’s runner. Consider him worthy to go to Syria and bring glory to your tireless love, for God’s glory.

<sup>3</sup> A Christian does not own their own time but devotes it to God. This is God’s work, and yours also, when you have finished it. I trust in grace that you are ready for a good deed that suits God. Knowing how intense you are for the truth, I have appealed to you in just a few lines.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Since I could not write to every church, because I am sailing suddenly from Troas to Neapolis, as orders require, please write to the churches nearby, since you share the mind of God, so that they do likewise. Those who can should send people on foot; others can send letters with your messengers, so that you will all be glorified by a deed that lasts, as you deserve.

<sup>2</sup> My greetings to everyone by name, and to the wife of Epitropus[^8-2-1] with all her household and children. Greetings to my dear Attalus. Greetings to the person who will be thought worthy to travel to Syria. Grace will always be with him, and with Polycarp, who sends him.

<sup>3</sup> I pray that you always do well in our God Jesus Christ, in whom may you remain in the unity of God and under his oversight. I greet Alke, a name dear to me. Farewell in the Lord.
