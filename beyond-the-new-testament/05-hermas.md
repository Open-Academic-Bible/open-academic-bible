# Shepherd of Hermas

A long book of visions, commandments and parables written in Rome in the first half of the second century, centred on repentance and a second chance for Christians who sin after baptism. It was among the most widely copied Christian books of the second and third centuries. Irenaeus quotes it as scripture; it is copied after Barnabas in Codex Sinaiticus (which preserves the first part) and appears in the Claromontanus list. The Muratorian list says it may be read privately but not publicly in church, and Athanasius recommends it to new converts. The Greek text breaks off near the end; the last chapters survive in Latin and Ethiopic translations.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> The man who had raised me sold me, in Rome, to a woman called Rhoda. Many years passed, and then I recognized her[^1-1-1] and came to love her as a sister.

<sup>2</sup> A while afterward I noticed her bathing in the Tiber. I reached out a hand and helped her up out of the water. As I took in her beauty I began to reflect: “I would be a happy man to have a wife like this, so lovely in appearance and so fine in character.” That was the whole of my plan, and nothing else, not a single thing.

<sup>3</sup> Later still I was making my way toward the villages[^1-3-1], marveling as I went at God’s creatures and how grand, splendid, and mighty they are, and I fell asleep as I walked. A spirit seized me and bore me away over a trackless stretch that no human being could cross, a steep place split by streams of water. After crossing that river I reached flat ground, dropped to my knees, and started to pray to the Lord and to admit my sins.

<sup>4</sup> As I prayed, the sky opened, and there I saw the woman I had longed for, greeting me from heaven and calling, “Hermas, hello!”

<sup>5</sup> I looked up at her and asked, “Lady, what are you doing here?” “I have been taken up,” she replied, “to bring your sins to the Lord’s attention as an accusation.”

<sup>6</sup> “Are you accusing me now?” I asked. “No,” she said, “but hear the words I have to tell you. The God who lives in the heavens, who brought into being what exists out of what did not, and who gave it increase and growth[^1-6-1] for the sake of his holy church, is angry with you for sinning against me.”

<sup>7</sup> I replied, “I, sin against you? In what place? When did I ever say anything shameful to you? Have I not always taken you for a goddess? Have I not always held you in respect as a sister? Why do you slander me, madam, with these wicked and filthy charges?”

<sup>8</sup> Laughing, she told me, “Desire for what is wicked rose up in your heart. Or does it seem to you that an evil desire rising up in the heart of a righteous man is no wrong? It is a sin, and a great one,” she said. “A righteous man has righteous aims. While his aims are righteous, his standing in heaven is secure and he finds the Lord easily appeased in all he does. But those who set their hearts on evil draw death and captivity down on themselves—especially those who make this age[^1-8-1] their own possession, pride themselves on their riches, and do not cling to the good things that are coming.

<sup>9</sup> The souls of those who have no hope, because they have given up on themselves and on life, will repent[^1-9-1]. But as for you, pray to God, and he will cure your sins[^1-9-2], and those of your entire household and of all the saints.”

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> When she had said this, the heavens shut. I was shaking from head to foot and sick with grief, and I thought to myself, “If this sin stands recorded against me, how can I be saved? How can I satisfy God for my sins, which are so thorough[^2-1-1]? What words can I find to plead with the Lord for his favor?”

<sup>2</sup> As I was deliberating over this, weighing it in my heart, I saw in front of me a big white seat of snowy wool, and an old woman came, wearing brilliant clothes and carrying a book in her hands. She sat down by herself and greeted me: “Hermas, hello!” Sad and in tears, I answered, “Hello, Lady.”

<sup>3</sup> “Why are you so sullen, Hermas?” she asked. “You are the patient one, the one who never loses his temper, who is always laughing. Why do you look so dejected, instead of cheerful?” I replied, “A most worthy woman says I have sinned against her.”

<sup>4</sup> “That is not how it is with a slave of God,” she said. “But the idea certainly did rise up in your heart concerning her. For God’s slaves, a plan like that does bring sin. It is a wicked and appalling plan to hold against a spirit so deeply venerable and already tested, when a man desires an evil deed—and above all Hermas, the self-controlled man, who stays away from every evil desire and is full of complete sincerity and great innocence.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> “No, God is not angry with you over that. His purpose is that you bring your household back to the Lord—they have broken the law against him and against you, their parents. Out of love for your children you never corrected your household, and let it go to ruin. That is why the Lord is angry with you. Yet he will heal all the harm that has been done in your house; it was because of their sins and lawless deeds that you yourself were corrupted by the affairs of everyday life.

<sup>2</sup> “But the abundant compassion of the Lord has taken pity on you and your house, and he will make you strong and set you on a firm footing in his glory. Just do not grow slack. Be of good courage, and strengthen your household. A smith who keeps pounding his work gets his way with whatever he is making; in the same way, daily righteous speech wins out over every kind of wickedness. So do not stop admonishing your children. For I know that if they repent with all their heart, their names will be entered in the books of life[^3-2-1] along with the saints.”

<sup>3</sup> When she had spoken these words she asked, “Would you like to hear me read?” “I would, Lady,” I said. “Then become a listener,” she told me, “and hear the glories of God.” What I heard was vast and astonishing, and I was unable to retain it; every word was dreadful, beyond what a human being can carry. The closing words, though, I did remember, because they were useful to us and mild:

<sup>4</sup> “Look: the God of the powers[^3-4-1], whom I love, who made the world by his mighty strength and great understanding, who covered his creation with beauty by his glorious plan, who fixed the heaven by a powerful word and set the earth on the waters[^3-4-2], who by his own wisdom and providence created his holy church and blessed it—look, he is relocating the heavens, the mountains, the hills, and the seas, and everything is being made level[^3-4-3] for his chosen ones, so that he can give them the promised reward with abundant glory and joy, as long as they hold to the decrees of God that they received in great faith.”

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> When she had finished her reading and risen from her seat, four young men came, lifted the seat, and carried it off to the east.

<sup>2</sup> She called me to her, laid a hand on my chest, and asked, “Did you like my reading?” “Lady,” I answered, “I like these last parts, but the first parts are difficult and harsh.” “These last parts,” she told me, “are for the righteous; the first are for the gentiles and the apostates.”

<sup>3</sup> As she was talking with me, two men appeared, took her under the arms, and went away eastward, to where the seat had gone. She left in good spirits, and on her way called out to me, “Be a man[^4-3-1], Hermas.”

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> At the same time of year as the year before, I was on my way to the villages, and while walking I recalled the previous year’s vision. Again a spirit took me up and carried me to the same place as last year.

<sup>2</sup> Reaching the place, I went down on my knees and began praying to the Lord and glorifying his name, because he had counted me worthy and had shown me my earlier sins.

<sup>3</sup> After I rose from prayer, I saw before me the old woman I had seen last year. She was walking about and reading a small scroll. “Can you tell God’s elect these things?” she asked. “Lady,” I said, “I cannot hold so much in my memory. Give me the little book so I can transcribe it.” “Take it,” she said, “and give it back.”

<sup>4</sup> I took it, withdrew to a place in the fields, and transcribed all of it letter by letter, for I could not tell the syllables apart. When I had come to the end of the letters of the little book, it was suddenly snatched out of my hand—I did not see by whom.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> After fifteen days of fasting and of putting many questions to the Lord, the meaning of the writing was disclosed to me. This is what had been written:

<sup>2</sup> “Your offspring, Hermas, have defied God, blasphemed the Lord, and betrayed their parents in great wickedness. They are known as traitors to their parents, and treachery brought them no benefit. On top of their sins they have piled debauchery and heaps of evil, and so the measure of their lawless deeds is full.

<sup>3</sup> Make these words known to all your children and to your wife, who is going to be your sister. She too fails to restrain the tongue she uses for doing wrong. But when she hears these words she will restrain it, and she will find mercy.

<sup>4</sup> Once you have made them aware of the words that the Master ordered me to disclose to you, all the sins they committed before will be forgiven them, and those of every saint who has sinned until now, if they repent from the heart and rid their hearts of doubt[^6-4-1].

<sup>5</sup> For the Master has taken an oath by his glory about his elect: if there is any further sin after the appointed day, they will have no salvation. For the righteous, repentance has a limit; the days of repentance are over for all the saints. For the gentiles, however, repentance remains open to the last day.

<sup>6</sup> Tell the leaders of the church, then, to put their ways in order with righteousness, so that they may receive the promises in full, with great glory.

<sup>7</sup> You who do what is right, stand firm and do not waver, so that your passage may be in the company of the holy angels. How fortunate you are who endure the great tribulation[^6-7-1] that is coming, and who will not deny their own life.

<sup>8</sup> The Lord has sworn by his Son that people who denied their Christ have been cut off from their life; that applies to those who will deny him in the coming days. As for those who denied him before, he showed them mercy out of his abundant compassion.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> “As for you, Hermas, stop holding grudges against your children, and do not let your sister go[^7-1-1], so that they may be purified of their former sins. They will be disciplined with a just discipline, provided you hold no grudge against them. Grudge-bearing produces death. You yourself, Hermas, have suffered great private troubles because of your household’s transgressions, since you paid no heed to them. You overlooked them and tangled yourself up in your own wicked dealings.

<sup>2</sup> What saves you is that you did not fall away from the living God[^7-2-1], together with your sincerity and your considerable self-control. These have saved you, if you hold to them; and they save all who practice them and walk in innocence and sincerity. Such people will overpower every kind of wickedness and will last on to eternal life.

<sup>3</sup> Fortunate are all who work righteousness: they will not be destroyed for all time.

<sup>4</sup> Tell Maximus: ‘See, persecution is coming; if it seems right to you, deny once more.’ ‘The Lord is close to those who turn back,’ as is written in the book of Eldad and Modat[^7-4-1], who prophesied to the people in the desert.”

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Brothers and sisters, a revelation came to me in my sleep. A young man of remarkable beauty asked me, “Who do you take that old woman to be, the one who gave you the little book?” “The Sibyl,” I answered. “You are mistaken,” he said; “she is not.” “Who is she, then?” “The church.” I asked him, “But why is she old?” “Because,” he replied, “she was created before everything. That is why she is old, and it was for her that the world was put in order.”

<sup>2</sup> Later I saw a vision in my house. The old woman came and asked whether I had yet given the book to the elders. I told her I had not. “That is good,” she said, “for I have more words to add. When I have finished them all, they will be made known to all the elect through you.

<sup>3</sup> You are to write out two little books, one to send to Clement and one to Grapte. Clement will forward his to the cities abroad, since that is his responsibility; Grapte will instruct the widows and orphans. You yourself will read yours in this city, together with the elders who lead the church.”

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> Brothers and sisters, the third vision that I saw was as follows.

<sup>2</sup> For many days I had fasted and begged the Lord to make plain the revelation he had promised to show me through that old woman. That same night she appeared to me and said, “Since you are so desperate and so keen to learn everything, go out to the field where you work the grain[^9-2-1]; at about the fifth hour[^9-2-2] I will show myself to you and point out what you are to see.”

<sup>3</sup> “Where in the field, Lady?” I asked. “Wherever you like,” she said. I picked out a pleasant, out-of-the-way place. Before I could speak to her and tell her which place it was, she told me, “I will be there, wherever you choose.”

<sup>4</sup> So, brothers and sisters, I went out to the field, reckoned up the hours, and arrived at the spot where I had told her to come. There I saw an ivory couch with a linen pillow on it, and over the pillow a cover of fine linen was stretched out.

<sup>5</sup> Seeing these things laid out and nobody there, I was thunderstruck. Something like trembling seized me, my hair stood straight up, and a sort of dread came over me, alone as I was. Collecting myself, recalling God’s glory and finding courage, I knelt and made my confession to the Lord all over again, as I had done earlier.

<sup>6</sup> She arrived with six young men, the same ones I had seen before. She took her stand near me and overheard me as I prayed and admitted my sins to the Lord. Touching me, she said, “Hermas, enough of asking about nothing but your sins. Ask about righteousness too, so that you can carry some part of it home to your household.”

<sup>7</sup> Taking me by the hand she lifted me up, brought me to the couch, and told the young men, “Off you go, and build.”

<sup>8</sup> When the young men had left and the two of us were alone, she said, “Sit down here.” “Lady,” I said, “let the elders[^9-8-1] sit first.” “Do as I say,” she answered. “Sit.”

<sup>9</sup> I started toward a seat on the right-hand side, but she held me back, signaling with her hand that I was to sit at her left. I was mulling this over, troubled that she would not let me take the right-hand place, when she said, “Does this trouble you, Hermas? That right-hand position belongs to others, who have already pleased God and who suffered for the name’s sake. You are a long way from qualifying to sit with them. But hold on to your sincerity as you are doing, and you will take a seat among them—you and everyone who matches their deeds and bears what they bore.”

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> “What did they put up with?” I asked. “Hear it,” she said. “Floggings, jails, great afflictions, crosses, wild animals—all for the name. For that reason the right-hand side of the holy place[^10-1-1] is theirs, along with anyone who suffers for the name; the left-hand side belongs to all the rest. But both groups, those seated on the right and those on the left, receive the same gifts and the same promises. It is just that the ones on the right sit there and enjoy a certain glory.

<sup>2</sup> You are eager to sit on the right with them, but your shortcomings are numerous. Even so, you will be cleansed of your shortcomings, as will everyone who does not doubt, cleansed of every sin up to this day.”

<sup>3</sup> With that she meant to go. But I threw myself at her feet and implored her, in the Lord’s name, to show me the vision she had promised.

<sup>4</sup> Again she took my hand, raised me, and seated me on the couch on the left side, while she herself sat on the right. Lifting a bright rod, she said, “Do you see anything big?” “Lady, I see nothing,” I said. “Look again,” she said. “Right in front of you, do you not see a huge tower being built on the water, of bright square stones?”

<sup>5</sup> The tower was going up as a square, built by the six young men who had come with her. Countless thousands of other men were hauling stones—some from the depths, some from the land—and passing them to the six, who took them and built.

<sup>6</sup> Every stone pulled up from the depths went into the building exactly as it came, for they were already shaped and fit the joints with the others. They bonded to each other so tightly that no seam showed, and the tower looked as if it had been built from one single stone.

<sup>7</sup> Of the other stones, the ones carried in from dry ground, some they threw out and some they set in the building; still others they smashed into pieces and flung far away from the tower.

<sup>8</sup> Plenty of other stones were lying around the tower, and no one used them for the building. Some of them were scabby, some had splits, some were chipped, and some were white and round and could not be fitted into the building.

<sup>9</sup> I saw further stones tossed far from the tower. They landed on the road but did not stay on it; they rolled off the road into the wilds. Others dropped into a fire and burned. Still others dropped beside water and could not roll into it, though they wanted to roll and reach the water.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> After showing me this she was about to hurry away. “Lady,” I said, “what good does it do me to have seen this if I do not know what it means?” She answered, “You are a sly one[^11-1-1], wanting to know about the tower.” “Yes, Lady,” I said, “so that I can tell my brothers and sisters and cheer them up, and so that, once they hear, they may know the Lord in all his glory.”

<sup>2</sup> “Many will hear,” she said. “Some of those who hear will be glad, and some will weep; but even the weepers, if they listen and repent, will be glad as well. Now listen to the parables of the tower, for I will unveil everything to you. And do not weary me any longer about revelations; these revelations are finished, for they have run their course. But you will not give up asking for revelations, because you are shameless.

<sup>3</sup> The tower you see under construction is I, the church, whom you have seen both now and earlier. So ask whatever you like about the tower, and I will reveal it, that you may rejoice with the saints.”

<sup>4</sup> “Lady,” I said, “since you have judged me worthy, just this once, to have everything revealed, please reveal it.” “Whatever may be revealed to you will be,” she said. “Only keep your heart fixed on God, and have no doubts about what you see.”

<sup>5</sup> “Why is the tower built over water, Lady?” I asked. “I explained to you earlier,” she said, “and you keep probing; by probing you discover the truth. Hear, then, the reason the tower is built over water: your life was saved through water[^11-5-1] and will be. And the tower is founded on the word of the almighty, glorious name, and is held up by the Master’s unseen power.”

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> “Lady,” I answered, “this is a great and wonderful matter. But the six young men who are building—who are they?” “They are the holy angels of God, the first ones made, to whom the Lord entrusted all his creation to enlarge it, build it, and have lordship over all that was made. Through them, then, the building of the tower will be finished.”

<sup>2</sup> “And the others who are bringing the stones—who are they?” “They too are holy angels of God, but the six outrank them. When the tower’s construction is complete, everyone together will celebrate around the tower and give glory to God that the building of the tower is done.”

<sup>3</sup> I asked, “Lady, I should like to know what becomes of the stones[^12-3-1], and what sort of power they have.” She answered, “It is not that you are more worthy than all the rest to have this uncovered for you. Others were before you and are better than you, and these visions should have been revealed to them. But so that God’s name may be glorified, it has been revealed to you, and will be, on account of the double-minded[^12-3-2], who ask in their hearts whether these things are real or not. Tell them that all this is true, that nothing is outside the truth, and that everything is sound, firm, and well founded.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> “Now hear about the stones that go into the building. The square white stones that match at their joints are the apostles, overseers[^13-1-1], teachers, and deacons, who lived by the holiness of God and served God’s elect as overseers, teachers, and deacons, in purity and dignity. Some have fallen asleep, and some are still alive. They were always in harmony with one another, kept peace among themselves, and listened to one another. That is why their joints match in the structure of the tower.”

<sup>2</sup> “And those hauled up from the deep and laid in the building, matching their joints with the other stones already built in—who are they?” “They are the ones who suffered for the name of the Lord.”

<sup>3</sup> “And the other stones, brought from dry land—I want to know who they are, Lady.” She said, “The ones that go into the building without being cut, the Lord has approved, because they walked in the Lord’s uprightness and carried out his commandments rightly.”

<sup>4</sup> “And those that are brought along and set in the building—who are they?” “They are young in the faith, and faithful. The angels urge them to do good, because wickedness was found in them.”

<sup>5</sup> “And those that were thrown away and cast off—who are they?” “People who have sinned and want to repent. They were not hurled far from the tower for just that reason: they can still serve the building, if they repent. Those who mean to repent will therefore be strong in faith if they do so now, while the tower is under construction. Once the building is complete, they will have no place left, but will be castaways; all they will have is a spot lying next to the tower.”

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> “Would you like to hear about the ones that were chopped to bits and flung away from the tower? Those are the sons of lawlessness. Their belief was hypocrisy, and not a bit of wickedness left them. For that reason they have no salvation: their evil ways make them unfit for the building. That is why they were hacked up and hurled far off, on account of the Lord’s anger—they had provoked him.

<sup>2</sup> As for the others, the many you saw lying about and not going into the building, the scabby ones are those who knew the truth but did not remain in it.”

<sup>3</sup> “And those with the cracks—who are they?” “These are people who hold grudges against one another in their hearts and are not at peace among themselves. They wear a face of peace, but when they part from each other, their wickedness stays in their hearts. These are the cracks the stones have.

<sup>4</sup> The chipped ones are those who have believed and for the most part live in righteousness, but have certain parts of lawlessness. That is why they are chipped and not whole.”

<sup>5</sup> “And the white round stones that cannot be fitted into the building—who are they, Lady?” She replied, “How long will you go on being foolish and thick-headed, questioning everything and grasping nothing? These are people who have faith, and also the riches of this age. Whenever tribulation comes, they disown their Lord for the sake of their riches and their business affairs.”

<sup>6</sup> I said in reply, “Then when will they become useful for the building, Lady?” “When the riches that draw their souls away have been trimmed off them,” she said, “only then will they be of use to God. A round stone cannot become a square one unless some of it is cut away and thrown off; in the same way, people who are rich in this age cannot be of use to the Lord until their riches are trimmed away.

<sup>7</sup> Judge it first by your own case. When you were rich you were of no use, but now you are of use, and a benefit to life. Make yourselves useful to God, since you yourself are taken from those very stones[^14-7-1].

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> “The other stones you saw thrown far from the tower, landing on the road and rolling off it into the pathless country, are people who believed but, because of their doubt, leave their true road. They imagine they can find a better one, so they wander off course and trudge miserably through the pathless country.

<sup>2</sup> Those that fall into the fire and burn are people who have finally departed from the living God, and it no longer enters their hearts to repent, because of their licentious desires and the wicked things they have done.

<sup>3</sup> And the ones that fall close to the water but cannot roll into it—would you like to know who they are? They are people who heard the word and wanted to be baptized into the name of the Lord. But then, when the purity of the truth comes back to their minds, they reverse their decision[^15-3-1] and go back to their evil desires.”

<sup>4</sup> That ended her explanation of the tower.

<sup>5</sup> Brazenly, I asked her yet another question: whether all these rejected stones that did not fit into the tower’s structure had any chance of repentance and a place in this tower. “They do have repentance,” she said, “but they cannot be fitted into this tower.

<sup>6</sup> They will be fitted into another place, a far lesser one, and only after they have been tormented and have served out the days of their sins. The reason they will be moved is that they had a share in the righteous word. Then, because of the wicked things they did, it will happen that they are moved out of their torments—if those deeds come up in their hearts[^15-6-1]. If they do not, they are not saved, since their hearts are hard.”

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> When I had finished asking her about all this, she said to me, “Do you want to see something else?” Since I was eager to look, I was overjoyed at the chance.

<sup>2</sup> She looked at me and smiled. “Do you see seven women around the tower?” “I do, Lady,” I said. “It is by them that this tower is held up, at the Lord’s command.

<sup>3</sup> Now hear what they do. The first of them, the one clasping her hands, is called Faith. Through her God’s elect are saved.

<sup>4</sup> The second, who is belted and acts like a man, is called Self-Control. She is the daughter of Faith. Whoever follows her becomes blessed in his life, because he will keep away from all wicked deeds, believing that if he keeps away from every evil desire he will inherit eternal life.”

<sup>5</sup> “And the others, Lady—who are they?” “They are one another’s daughters. Their names are Sincerity, Knowledge, Innocence, Dignity, and Love. When you do all the works of their mother, you can live.”

<sup>6</sup> “I wanted to know, Lady,” I said, “which of them has what power.” “Listen,” she said, “to the powers they have.

<sup>7</sup> The powers of these women are linked to one another and follow one another in the order of their birth. Faith gives birth to Self-Control, Self-Control to Sincerity, Sincerity to Innocence, Innocence to Dignity, Dignity to Knowledge, and Knowledge to Love. The works of these women are therefore pure, dignified, and divine.

<sup>8</sup> Whoever serves these women and has the strength to hold on to their works will have a home in the tower with God’s saints.”

<sup>9</sup> I asked her about the times, whether the end was already here. She shouted at the top of her voice: “Senseless man, do you not see that the tower is still going up? Once the tower being built is complete, the end arrives. But it will rise quickly. Ask me nothing more. This reminder is enough for you and the saints, along with the renewal of your spirits.

<sup>10</sup> But this was not revealed to you alone; it is so that you may make it known to everyone,

<sup>11</sup> three days from now. You must grasp it yourself first. And I give you this charge first, Hermas: tell all these words I am about to speak to you into the ears of the saints, so that when they have heard them and done them, they may be cleansed from their wicked ways, and you with them.”

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> “Listen to me, children. I brought you up in great sincerity, innocence, and dignity, through the mercy of the Lord, who let righteousness drip upon you so that you would be made righteous and holy from all wickedness and all crookedness. But you do not want to stop your wickedness.

<sup>2</sup> Now, then, listen to me. Be at peace among yourselves, look in on one another, and help one another; and do not enjoy God’s creations by yourselves, in excess[^17-2-1], but give a share to those in need as well.

<sup>3</sup> Some people draw weakness on their flesh from their many foods and damage their flesh, while the flesh of those who have no food is damaged because they do not have enough nourishment, and their body wastes away.

<sup>4</sup> This imbalance, then, is harmful to you who have and do not share with those in need.

<sup>5</sup> Watch for the judgment that is coming. You who are in high places, seek out the hungry while the tower is not yet finished. For after the tower is finished, you will want to do good and will have no opportunity.

<sup>6</sup> So watch out, you who boast of your wealth, lest the needy groan and their groaning rise to the Lord, and you be shut outside the door of the tower along with your goods.

<sup>7</sup> I say this to you who preside over the church and take the places of honor: do not be like the poisoners[^17-7-1]. Poisoners keep their drugs in boxes, but you keep your drug and your venom in your heart.

<sup>8</sup> You are hardened, and you do not want to cleanse your hearts and blend your minds together in a pure heart, so that you may receive mercy from the great King.

<sup>9</sup> Watch out, then, children, lest these divisions rob you of your life.

<sup>10</sup> How can you want to discipline the Lord’s elect when you yourselves have no discipline? So discipline one another and be at peace among yourselves, so that I too may stand cheerful before the Father and give an account to the Lord on behalf of you all.”

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> When she had finished talking with me, the six young builders came and took her off to the tower, and four others picked up the couch and took it to the tower as well. I did not see their faces, since they had their backs turned.

<sup>2</sup> As she left, I begged her to reveal to me the meaning of the three forms in which she had appeared to me. She answered, “You must ask someone else about these, so that it may be revealed to you.”

<sup>3</sup> In the first vision, last year, brothers and sisters, she had appeared to me very old and seated in a chair.

<sup>4</sup> In the second vision she had a younger face, though her flesh and hair were old, and she spoke to me standing. She was more cheerful than before.

<sup>5</sup> In the third vision she was completely younger and of surpassing beauty, with only her hair old. She was cheerful throughout, and sat on a couch.

<sup>6</sup> I was deeply distressed about these things and longed to understand this revelation. Then in a vision at night I saw the old woman, and she said to me, “Every question requires humility. So fast, and you will receive from the Lord what you ask.”

<sup>7</sup> So I fasted for one day, and that very night a young man appeared to me and said, “Why do you keep asking for revelations as you pray? Take care that by asking for so much you do not harm your flesh.

<sup>8</sup> These revelations are enough for you. Can you see revelations stronger than the ones you have seen?”

<sup>9</sup> I answered him, “Sir, I ask only this: that the revelation about the three forms of the old woman be made complete.” He answered me, “How long will you be senseless? It is your doubts that make you senseless, and the fact that your heart is not set on the Lord.”

<sup>10</sup> I answered him again, “But from you, sir, we will learn about them more exactly.”

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> “Listen,” he said, “about the forms you are asking about.

<sup>2</sup> In the first vision, why did she appear to you old and seated in a chair? Because your spirit is old and already withered, and has no strength because of your softness and your doubts.

<sup>3</sup> Just as the old, having no more hope of becoming young again, expect nothing but their falling asleep, so you too, made soft by the cares of this life, have given yourselves up to apathy and have not cast your anxieties on the Lord[^19-3-1]. Your mind has been broken, and you have grown old in your sorrows.”

<sup>4</sup> “Then why was she sitting in a chair, sir? I wanted to know.” “Because every sick person sits in a chair on account of his sickness, so that the weakness of his body may be supported. There you have the meaning of the first vision.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> In the second vision you saw her standing, with a younger and more cheerful face than before, but with old flesh and hair. Listen,” he said, “to this parable as well.

<sup>2</sup> Suppose an old man has already given up all hope for himself because of his frailty and poverty, expecting nothing but the final day of his life. Then, out of nowhere, an inheritance is left to him. He hears of it, rouses himself, is delighted, and puts on strength. He no longer lies down but stands; his spirit, already ruined by his former way of life, is made new. He no longer sits about but takes courage. It is the same with you, now that you have heard the revelation the Lord gave you,

<sup>3</sup> because he took pity on you, renewed your spirits, and you laid aside your softness. Strength came to you and you were made mighty in faith; and the Lord, seeing how you had been strengthened, was glad. That is why he showed you the tower’s construction, and he will show you more, if you keep peace among yourselves with your whole heart.

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> In the third vision you saw her younger, beautiful, cheerful, and lovely in form.

<sup>2</sup> Suppose someone who is grieving receives some good news. At once he forgets his former griefs and expects nothing but the news he has heard; from then on he is strengthened for the good, and his spirit is renewed by the joy he has received. In the same way, you too have received a renewal of your spirits by seeing these good things.

<sup>3</sup> And as for your seeing her seated on a couch, the position is strong, because a couch has four feet and stands firmly. For the world, too, is held together by four elements.

<sup>4</sup> Those who have repented, then, will be completely new and firmly founded—those who have repented with their whole heart. You now have the revelation in full. Ask for nothing more about revelations; if anything more is needed, it will be revealed to you.”

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> The vision I saw twenty days after the previous vision, brothers and sisters, was a symbol of the tribulation that is coming.

<sup>2</sup> I was on my way to a field along the Campanian Way. It lies about ten stades[^22-2-1] from the public road, and the place is easy to reach.

<sup>3</sup> Walking by myself, I begged the Lord to finish the revelations and visions he had shown me by means of his holy church, to give me strength, and to grant repentance to his slaves who had fallen, so that his great and glorious name might be glorified for considering me worthy to be shown his wonders.

<sup>4</sup> While I was giving him glory and thanks, something like the sound of a voice answered me: “Do not doubt, Hermas.” I began to reason within myself: “What have I to doubt about, when I am so firmly grounded by the Lord and have seen glorious things?”

<sup>5</sup> I walked on a little, brothers and sisters, and look, I saw a cloud of dust that seemed to reach the sky, and I began to say to myself, “Surely it is not cattle coming and stirring up dust?” It was about a stade away from me.

<sup>6</sup> The dust kept growing, and I suspected it was something from God. The sun shone out a little, and look, I saw an enormous beast like some sea monster, with fiery locusts coming out of its mouth. The beast was about a hundred feet[^22-6-1] long, and its head looked like a clay jar.

<sup>7</sup> I began to weep and to ask the Lord to rescue me from it, and I remembered the word I had heard: “Do not doubt, Hermas.”

<sup>8</sup> So, brothers and sisters, I put on the faith of the Lord, remembered the great things he had taught me, took courage, and gave myself up to the beast. It came on with such a rush that it could have wrecked a city.

<sup>9</sup> I came near it, and that huge monster stretched itself out on the ground and put out nothing but its tongue. It did not move at all until I had passed it.

<sup>10</sup> On its head the beast had four colors: black; then red like fire and blood; then gold; then white.

## Chapter 23

<sup>1</sup> After I had gone past the beast and walked on about thirty feet, look, a virgin came to meet me, dressed up as if coming from a bridal chamber, all in white, with white sandals, veiled as far as the forehead, with a headband for her veil. Her hair was white.

<sup>2</sup> From the earlier visions I knew she was the church, and I became more cheerful. She greeted me, saying, “Greetings, my good man.” I greeted her in return: “Greetings, Lady.”

<sup>3</sup> She answered, “Did nothing meet you?” “Lady,” I said to her, “such a beast, able to destroy whole peoples! But by the power of the Lord and his great compassion I escaped it.”

<sup>4</sup> “You were right to escape it,” she said, “since you cast your cares on God[^23-4-1] and opened your heart to the Lord, convinced that you can be saved by nothing but the great, glorious name. That is why the Lord dispatched his angel who has charge of the beasts—Thegri is his name—and he blocked the creature’s mouth so it would not hurt you. You have come through a great tribulation by your faith, because you did not waver at the sight of so huge a beast.

<sup>5</sup> So go and report his great deeds to God’s elect, and tell them that this beast foreshadows the great tribulation to come. If you prepare ahead of time and repent to the Lord with all your heart, you will be able to escape it—as long as your heart is made clean and spotless and you serve the Lord blamelessly all your remaining days. Hand your anxieties over to the Lord, and he will put them right.

<sup>6</sup> Believe in the Lord, you who are double-minded: he can do all things! He both turns his anger away from you and sends scourges on you double-minded ones. Alas for those who hear these words and pay no attention; better for them never to have been born.”

## Chapter 24

<sup>1</sup> I asked her about the four colors that the beast had on its head. She answered, “You are being inquisitive again about this sort of thing.” “Yes, Lady,” I said, “tell me what they mean.”

<sup>2</sup> “Listen,” she said: “the black stands for this world, where you live;

<sup>3</sup> the red like fire and blood means that this world must perish through blood and fire.

<sup>4</sup> The gold part is you, who have escaped from this world. For just as gold is tested by fire and becomes useful, so you who live in the world are being tested. Those of you who stay and are put through the fire will be purified. As gold throws off its dross, so you too will throw off all grief and distress, and you will be purified and be of use in building the tower.

<sup>5</sup> The white part is the coming age, where God’s elect will make their home; for those chosen by God for eternal life will be unstained and clean.

<sup>6</sup> So do not stop speaking into the ears of the saints. You also have the symbol of the great tribulation to come. But if you are willing, it will be nothing. Remember what has been written before.”

<sup>7</sup> After saying this she left, and I did not see where she went, because a cloud came up. I turned back in fear, thinking the beast was coming.

## Chapter 25

<sup>1</sup> While I was praying in my house, seated on my bed, a man entered with a glorious look about him, dressed as a shepherd, wearing a white goatskin, a bag slung over his shoulders, and a staff in his hand. He greeted me, and I returned his greeting.

<sup>2</sup> At once he sat down beside me and said, “I was sent by the most venerable angel to live with you for the rest of your life.”

<sup>3</sup> I thought he had come to test me, and I said to him, “Who are you? For I know the one to whom I was handed over.” He said, “Do you not recognize me?” “No,” I said. “I am the shepherd,” he said, “to whom you were handed over.”

<sup>4</sup> While he was still speaking, his appearance changed, and I recognized that he was the one to whom I had been handed over. At once I was thrown into confusion, fear seized me, and I was completely shattered with grief that I had answered him so badly and foolishly.

<sup>5</sup> He replied, “Do not be thrown off; take strength instead from the commandments I am about to give you. I was sent,” he said, “to show you again everything you saw earlier, just the main points that are good for you. Write down my commandments and parables before anything else; the rest you will record as I show it to you. For that reason,” he said, “I direct you to put the commandments and parables in writing first, so that you can go over them regularly and be able to keep them.”

<sup>6</sup> So I wrote the commandments and parables as he had commanded me.

<sup>7</sup> If you listen and keep these things, then, and live by them and carry them out with a clean heart, you will get from the Lord everything he promised you. But if you listen and do not repent, and instead pile more onto your sins, you will get the reverse from the Lord. The shepherd, the angel of repentance, ordered me to write all of this in exactly this way.

## Chapter 26

<sup>1</sup> Above all else, believe that God is one, who created everything and put it in order, who brought all things into being out of nothing[^26-1-1], who holds everything within himself while he alone is beyond holding.

<sup>2</sup> So believe in him and fear him, and out of that fear be self-controlled. Keep this up, and you will throw off every kind of wickedness and put on every virtue of righteousness, and you will live for God, if you keep this commandment.

## Chapter 27

<sup>1</sup> He said to me, “Be sincere and innocent, and you will be like little children who know nothing of the wickedness that destroys human life.

<sup>2</sup> First, do not speak ill of anyone, and take no pleasure in hearing it done. Otherwise you who listen become answerable for the slanderer’s sin, if you believe the slander you hear; by believing it, you too will come to hold something against your brother[^27-2-1]. That is how you will share the guilt of the slanderer’s sin.

<sup>3</sup> Slander is evil. It is a restless demon, never at peace, always making its home among quarrels. Keep away from it, and you will always be prosperous with everyone.

<sup>4</sup> Put on dignity, in which there is no evil stumbling block, but everything is smooth and glad. Do good, and give to everyone who lacks, simply and without hesitation, out of the earnings God gives you; do not agonize over whom to give to and whom not to give to. Give to all, for God wants to give to everyone out of his own gifts.

<sup>5</sup> Those who receive will have to account to God for why they received and for what. Those who receive because they are in distress will not be judged, but those who receive by pretending will pay the penalty.

<sup>6</sup> The giver, then, is blameless. As he received from the Lord a service to carry out, he carried it out simply, without making distinctions about whom to give to and whom not. So this service, simply carried out, became glorious in God’s eyes. Whoever serves God in this simple way will live.

<sup>7</sup> Keep this commandment, then, as I have told you, so that your repentance and your household’s may be found sincere, and your innocence pure and undefiled.”

## Chapter 28

<sup>1</sup> Again he said to me, “Love truth, and let nothing but truth come out of your mouth, so that the spirit God made to live in this flesh may be found true by all people. In this way the Lord who lives in you will be glorified, because the Lord is true in every word, and with him there is no falsehood.

<sup>2</sup> Liars, then, set the Lord aside and become cheats against the Lord, because they do not give back the deposit they received. For they received from him a spirit that does not lie. If they hand it back as a lie, they have defiled the Lord’s commandment and become cheats.”

<sup>3</sup> When I heard this I wept bitterly. Seeing me weep, he said, “Why are you weeping?” “Because, sir,” I said, “I do not know whether I can be saved.” “Why not?” he asked. “Because, sir, never in my life have I spoken a true word. I have always spoken craftily with everyone, and made my lie look like truth to all; and no one ever contradicted me, but my word was believed. So how, sir, can I live, after doing these things?”

<sup>4</sup> “You are thinking rightly and truly,” he said. “For as a slave of God you ought to walk in truth, and not let an evil conscience live beside the spirit of truth or bring grief to that dignified and true spirit.” “Sir,” I said, “I have never heard words like these set out so exactly.”

<sup>5</sup> “Now you do hear them,” he said. “Observe them, so that the false things you said earlier in your business dealings, once these new words prove true, may themselves gain credence—for even those can become believable. If you observe these words and from now on tell only the truth, you will be able to obtain life for yourself. Whoever hears this commandment and avoids that most wicked lie will live for God.”

## Chapter 29

<sup>1</sup> “I command you,” he said, “to preserve purity. Do not let your heart dwell on another man’s wife, on any sexual immorality[^29-1-1], or on any similar evil practices. For if you do so, you commit a great sin. If you always remember your own wife, you will never go wrong.

<sup>2</sup> For if that thought enters your heart, you will go wrong; and if you entertain other evil thoughts like it, you commit sin. This thought is a great sin in a slave of God; and if anyone carries out the evil deed, he is working death for himself.

<sup>3</sup> So take care. Keep away from this thought; for where dignity lives, lawlessness ought not to enter the heart of a righteous man.”

<sup>4</sup> I said to him, “Sir, allow me to ask a few questions.” “Ask,” he said. “Sir,” I said, “if someone has a wife who is faithful in the Lord, and finds her in some act of adultery, does the husband sin by living with her?”

<sup>5</sup> “As long as he does not know,” he said, “he does not sin. But if the husband learns of her sin, and the woman does not repent but stays with her sexual immorality, and the husband goes on living with her, he becomes guilty of her sin and a partner in her adultery.”

<sup>6</sup> “What should the husband do, sir,” I asked, “if the woman persists in this passion?” “Let him divorce her,” he said, “and let the husband stay by himself. But if he divorces his wife and marries another, he too commits adultery.”

<sup>7</sup> “And if, sir,” I said, “after the wife has been divorced, she repents and wants to return to her own husband, will she not be taken back?”

<sup>8</sup> “Of course,” he said. “A husband who refuses to take her back sins, and loads a heavy sin on himself. Whoever has sinned and repents has to be received—though not again and again, since God’s slaves have just one repentance. Because she might repent, then, the husband must not remarry. The same course holds for a wife and for a husband.

<sup>9</sup> It is not only adultery,” he said, “when someone defiles his flesh; whoever behaves like the gentiles also commits adultery. So if someone persists in such deeds and does not repent, keep away from him and do not live with him; otherwise you too share in his sin.

<sup>10</sup> This is why you were told to remain single, whether husband or wife, for repentance is possible in such cases.

<sup>11</sup> I am not,” he said, “offering an opening for this matter to be handled that way. The aim is that the one who has sinned should not sin again. As for his earlier sin, there is One who can give healing; for he is the one who has authority over all things.”

## Chapter 30

<sup>1</sup> I asked him again, “Since the Lord has judged me worthy to have you live with me always, put up with a few more words from me: I understand nothing, and my heart has been hardened by what I did before. Make me understand, for I am very foolish and grasp nothing at all.”

<sup>2</sup> He answered, “I am the one set over repentance, and I give understanding to everyone who repents. Does it not seem to you that repenting is understanding in itself? Repenting is great understanding,” he said. “For the sinner comes to see that he has done evil before the Lord; the deed he committed rises in his heart, and he repents. He no longer does evil but pours himself into doing good, humbles his own soul, and torments it for having sinned. You see, then, that repentance is great understanding.”

<sup>3</sup> “That, sir,” I said, “is why I question you about everything in detail: first, because I am a sinner, so that I may know what works I must do in order to live, since my sins are many and of every kind.”

<sup>4</sup> “You will live,” he said, “if you keep my commandments and walk in them; and whoever hears these commandments and keeps them will live for God.”

## Chapter 31

<sup>1</sup> “Sir,” I said, “I will add one more question.” “Ask,” he said. “Sir,” I said, “I have heard from certain teachers that there is no other repentance except the one when we went down into the water[^31-1-1] and received forgiveness of our earlier sins.”

<sup>2</sup> He replied, “You were told correctly; that is how it stands. A person who has received forgiveness of sins should never sin again but live in purity.

<sup>3</sup> But since you inquire into everything exactly, I will explain this to you as well, without giving an excuse to those who will believe in the future or to those who have now believed in the Lord. For those who have now believed or are about to believe do not have repentance for sins; they have forgiveness of their earlier sins.

<sup>4</sup> For those who were called before these days the Lord established repentance. The Lord knows hearts and knows all things beforehand; he knew human weakness and the devil’s many-sided cunning, and that the devil would do some harm to God’s slaves and deal wickedly with them.

<sup>5</sup> So the Lord, being full of compassion, had pity on what he had made and appointed this repentance, and authority over this repentance was given to me.

<sup>6</sup> But I tell you,” he said, “that after that great and holy calling, if someone is tempted by the devil and sins, he has one repentance. But if he keeps on sinning and repenting, it does such a person no good; he will hardly live.”

<sup>7</sup> I said to him, “Hearing this from you so precisely has given me life; for I know that if I stop adding to my sins, I will be saved.” “You will be saved,” he said, “and so will all who do this.”

## Chapter 32

<sup>1</sup> I asked him again, “Sir, since you have been patient with me, explain this to me as well.” “Ask,” he said. “Sir,” I said, “if a wife, or again a husband, dies and one of them remarries, does the one who marries sin?”

<sup>2</sup> “He does not sin,” he said. “But if someone stays single, he gains for himself greater honor and great glory with the Lord. Yet if he does remarry, he does not sin.

<sup>3</sup> So keep purity and dignity, and you will live for God. Everything I tell you and am going to tell you, keep from now on, from the day you were handed over to me, and I will live in your house.

<sup>4</sup> Your earlier transgressions will be forgiven if you keep my commandments; and everyone will be forgiven, if they keep these commandments of mine and walk in this purity.”

## Chapter 33

<sup>1</sup> “Be patient and understanding,” he said, “and you will master all wicked deeds and do all that is right.

<sup>2</sup> If you are patient, the Holy Spirit who lives in you will remain pure, not overshadowed by some other, evil spirit. Dwelling in a roomy place, it will celebrate and rejoice along with the vessel[^33-2-1] it lives in, serving God with abundant cheer and enjoying well-being within itself.

<sup>3</sup> But if irritability sets in, the Holy Spirit, being tender, is immediately squeezed, since its place is no longer clean, and it tries to depart from there; for it is smothered by the evil spirit and has no space to serve the Lord as it chooses, defiled by the irritability. The Lord lives in patience, but the devil in a short temper[^33-3-1].

<sup>4</sup> So when both spirits live in the same place, it is harmful and bad for the person in whom they live.

<sup>5</sup> Suppose you take a very small amount of wormwood and pour it into a jar of honey. Is not all the honey ruined? So much honey is spoiled by the tiniest bit of wormwood, which destroys the sweetness of the honey, and the owner no longer values it, because it has turned bitter and lost its use. But if no wormwood is dropped into the honey, the honey stays sweet and is useful to its owner.

<sup>6</sup> You see that patience is sweeter than honey and useful to the Lord, who lives in it. A short temper is bitter and useless. If a short temper is mixed with patience, the patience is defiled, and its prayer is no longer of use to God.”

<sup>7</sup> “Sir,” I said, “I would like to know how a short temper works, so that I can guard against it.” “Indeed,” he said, “if you and your household do not guard against it, you have lost all your hope. But guard against it, for I am with you. And everyone will keep away from it who repents with their whole heart. I will be with them and protect them, for they have all been made righteous by the most venerable angel.

## Chapter 34

<sup>1</sup> Now hear,” he said, “how a short temper operates, how wicked it is, and how by its action it overturns my slaves and pulls them off the path of righteousness. It cannot lead off course those who are full of faith, nor can it act on them, since my power is with them; it misleads the empty and the double-minded.

<sup>2</sup> When it sees such people at ease, it slips into the heart of that person, and for no reason the man or the woman turns bitter, over some matter of daily life, or about food, or some petty thing, or a friend, or giving or receiving, or other foolish matters like these. For all these things are foolish, empty, senseless, and unhelpful for slaves of God.

<sup>3</sup> Patience, by contrast, is great and strong, with a sturdy power, thriving in a wide-open space, cheerful, exultant, free from care, glorifying the Lord at every time, with nothing bitter in itself, and remaining always gentle and calm. This patience lives, then, with those whose faith is whole.

<sup>4</sup> A short temper, however, is first of all foolish, flighty, and senseless. Then from the senselessness comes bitterness, from bitterness fury, from fury anger, and from anger wrath. Then this wrath, put together from so many evils, becomes a great and incurable sin.

<sup>5</sup> For when these spirits live in one vessel where the Holy Spirit also lives, that vessel cannot hold them but overflows.

<sup>6</sup> So the delicate spirit, which is not used to living with an evil spirit or with harshness, leaves such a person and seeks to live with gentleness and calm.

<sup>7</sup> Then, when it has departed from the person where it lived, that person is emptied of the righteous spirit and, being filled from then on with the evil spirits, is unsteady in every action, dragged this way and that by the evil spirits, and utterly blinded to good thinking. That is what happens to everyone with a short temper.

<sup>8</sup> Keep away, then, from a short temper, the most evil of spirits. Clothe yourself in patience, set yourself against short temper and bitterness, and you will be found in the company of dignity, which is loved by the Lord. Take care never to pass over this commandment: if you get the better of it, you will be able to keep the others I am going to give you. Be strong in them, take power, and let everyone take power who wants to walk in them.”

## Chapter 35

<sup>1</sup> “In the first commandment,” he said, “I told you to guard faith, fear, and self-control.” “Yes, sir,” I said. “Now I want to explain their powers to you as well, so that you can understand what power and activity each one has. Their activities are twofold: they are set over what is right and what is wrong.

<sup>2</sup> You, then, put your trust in what is right, and do not trust what is wrong. For what is right has a straight road, but what is wrong has a crooked one. You should walk on the straight, level road, and leave the crooked one alone.

<sup>3</sup> For the crooked road has no paths, only trackless ground, with many stumbling blocks, and it is rough and thorny. It is harmful to those who walk on it.

<sup>4</sup> But those who walk on the straight road walk evenly and without stumbling, for it is neither rough nor thorny. You see, then, that it is better to walk on this road.”

<sup>5</sup> “It pleases me, sir,” I said, “to walk on this road.” “You will walk on it,” he said, “and so will everyone who turns to the Lord with their whole heart.”

## Chapter 36

<sup>1</sup> “Now listen,” he said, “about faith. There are two angels with a person, one of righteousness and one of wickedness.”

<sup>2</sup> “How then, sir,” I asked, “will I recognize their workings, since both angels live with me?”

<sup>3</sup> “Listen,” he said, “and you will grasp them. The angel of righteousness is tender, bashful, mild, and quiet. Whenever he rises up in your heart, he immediately talks with you about justice, purity, dignity, contentment, every righteous action, and every splendid virtue. If all these arise in your heart, you may be sure the angel of righteousness is with you. That is what the angel of righteousness does. Put your trust in him and in his works.

<sup>4</sup> Now look at the works of the angel of wickedness. First of all, he is quick-tempered, bitter, and senseless, and his works are evil, overturning the slaves of God. When he comes up into your heart, know him by his works.”

<sup>5</sup> “How will I recognize him, sir?” I asked. “I do not know.” “Listen,” he said. “When a fit of temper or bitterness comes over you, know that he is within you. Then come the craving for many activities, lavish spreads of food, drinking and carousing, all kinds of unsuitable foods, desires for women, greed, a great deal of pride and arrogance, and whatever is close or similar to these. When these come up into your heart, know that the angel of wickedness is within you.

<sup>6</sup> Once you recognize his works, keep away from him; do not trust him at all, because his works are evil and unhelpful for slaves of God. You now have the workings of both angels. Understand them, and trust the angel of righteousness,

<sup>7</sup> but keep away from the angel of wickedness, because his teaching is evil in every deed. For even if someone is a believing man, when this angel’s thought comes up into his heart, that man or woman is bound to sin in some way.

<sup>8</sup> And again, if someone is extremely wicked, man or woman, and the works of the angel of righteousness come up into his heart, he is bound to do something good.

<sup>9</sup> You see, then,” he said, “that it is good to follow the angel of righteousness, and to renounce the angel of wickedness.

<sup>10</sup> This commandment shows what concerns faith: that you should trust the works of the angel of righteousness and, by doing them, live for God. And believe that the works of the angel of wickedness are hard; by not doing them you will live for God.”

## Chapter 37

<sup>1</sup> “Fear the Lord,” he said, “and keep his commandments. By keeping God’s commandments you will be strong in all you do, and nothing you do will be rivaled. For if you fear the Lord, whatever you do will be done well. That is the kind of fear you need, in order to be saved.

<sup>2</sup> Do not be afraid of the devil, though. If you fear the Lord, you will rule over the devil, since he has no power in him. Where power is absent, fear is absent too; but where there is glorious power, fear goes with it. Everyone who has power commands fear, while the person without power is looked down on by all.

<sup>3</sup> Fear the works of the devil, though, because they are evil. If you fear the Lord, then, you will not do them, but will keep away from them.

<sup>4</sup> There are, then, two kinds of fear. When you are inclined to do evil, fear the Lord and you will refrain from it; when you are inclined to do good, fear the Lord and you will carry it out. The fear of the Lord is therefore strong, great, and splendid. Fear the Lord, then, and you will live to him; and everyone who fears him and observes his commandments will live to God.”

<sup>5</sup> “Sir,” I asked, “why did you say about those who keep his commandments, ‘They will live for God’?” “All creation fears the Lord,” he said, “but it does not keep his commandments. Life with God belongs to those who both fear him and keep his commandments; for those who do not keep his commandments there is no life in him.”

## Chapter 38

<sup>1</sup> “I told you,” he said, “that God’s creatures are twofold; for self-control too is twofold. In some matters one must hold back, but in others one must not.”

<sup>2</sup> “Tell me, sir,” I said, “in which matters one must hold back, and in which not.” “Listen,” he said. “Refrain from doing evil, but do not refrain from doing good—do it. For if you hold yourself back from doing good, you commit a great sin; if you hold yourself back from doing evil, you accomplish great righteousness. So abstain from every sort of wickedness, and do good.”

<sup>3</sup> “What kinds of wickedness, sir,” I asked, “must we hold back from?” “Listen,” he said: “from adultery and sexual immorality, from lawless drunkenness, from wicked indulgence, from rich foods, from lavish wealth, from boasting, haughtiness, and arrogance, and from lying, slander, hypocrisy, grudge-bearing, and all blasphemy.

<sup>4</sup> These are the wickedest deeds in human life. A slave of God must hold back from these deeds; for whoever does not hold back from them cannot live for God. Listen, then, to what follows from these:”

<sup>5</sup> “Sir,” I said, “are there still more evil deeds?” “Indeed, there are many,” he said, “from which a slave of God must hold back: theft, lying, fraud, false witness, greed, evil desire, deceit, vanity, boasting, and whatever is like these.

<sup>6</sup> Do these not seem evil to you?” “Very evil,” I said, “for slaves of God.” “A person who serves God must hold back from all of these. So hold back from all of them, so that you may live for God and be enrolled with those who hold back from them. Those are the things you must hold back from.

<sup>7</sup> Now listen to what you must not hold back from but must do,” he said. “Do not hold back from the good, but do it.”

<sup>8</sup> “Explain to me also, sir,” I said, “the force of the good things, so that I can walk in them and serve them, and by performing them be saved.” “Listen,” he said, “to the good works too, which you must do and must not hold back from.

<sup>9</sup> First of all: faith, fear of the Lord, love, harmony, words of righteousness, truth, endurance. Nothing is better than these in human life. If anyone keeps these and does not hold back from them, that person becomes blessed in his life.

<sup>10</sup> Then hear what follows from these: to care for widows, to look in on orphans and the needy, to ransom God’s slaves from their distress, to be hospitable (for in hospitality the doing of good is found), to oppose no one, to be quiet, to become poorer than all people, to revere the old, to practice righteousness, to preserve brotherhood[^38-10-1], to put up with insult, to be patient, to hold no grudge, to encourage those who are worn down in spirit, not to cast away those who have stumbled from the faith but to turn them back and cheer them up, to admonish sinners, not to oppress debtors and those in need, and whatever else is like these.”

<sup>11</sup> “Do these seem good to you?” he asked. “What could be better than these, sir?” I said. “Then walk in them,” he said, “and do not hold back from them, and you will live for God.

<sup>12</sup> So keep this commandment. If you do what is good and do not hold back from it, you will live for God, and so will everyone who acts this way. And again, if you do not do evil and hold back from it, you will live for God, and so will everyone who keeps these commandments and walks in them.”

## Chapter 39

<sup>1</sup> He said to me, “Remove doubt[^39-1-1] from yourself, and do not doubt at all when you ask God for something, saying to yourself, ‘How can I ask the Lord for anything and receive it, when I have sinned so much against him?’

<sup>2</sup> Do not reason like that. Turn to the Lord with your whole heart and ask him without hesitating, and you will come to know his rich compassion: he will surely not abandon you, but will fulfill the request of your soul.

<sup>3</sup> For God is not like human beings, who hold grudges. He bears no grudges, and he has compassion on what he has made.

<sup>4</sup> So cleanse your heart of all the emptiness of this age and of the things I have told you, and ask the Lord, and you will receive everything and lack nothing of all you ask, if you ask the Lord without hesitating.

<sup>5</sup> But if you hesitate in your heart, you will receive nothing at all of what you request. For those who hesitate toward God are the double-minded, and they obtain nothing at all of their requests.

<sup>6</sup> But those who are complete in faith ask for everything trusting in the Lord, and they receive, because they ask without hesitating, doubting nothing. For every double-minded person, unless he repents, will hardly be saved.

<sup>7</sup> So purge doubt from your heart, put on faith, which is strong, and trust God that you will receive all that you ask. If you ask the Lord for something and receive it a bit late, do not doubt just because you did not get what your soul asked for quickly; surely it is due to some trial or some failing of which you are unaware that your request arrives late.

<sup>8</sup> So do not stop making the request of your soul, and you will receive it. But if you lose heart and doubt as you ask, blame yourself and not the one who gives to you.

<sup>9</sup> Take care with this doubt: it is evil and foolish, and tears many up from the faith, even people who are most faithful and strong. This doubt is, in fact, a daughter of the devil and does terrible harm to God’s slaves.

<sup>10</sup> So scorn doubt and master it in everything, clothing yourself with strong and powerful faith. For faith promises all things and brings all things to completion, but doubt, having no confidence in itself, fails in everything it attempts.

<sup>11</sup> You see, then,” he said, “that faith comes from above, from the Lord, and has great power; but doubt is an earthly spirit from the devil, and has no power.

<sup>12</sup> So serve faith, which has power, and keep away from doubt, which has none, and you will live for God; and everyone will live for God who thinks this way.”

## Chapter 40

<sup>1</sup> “Remove grief from yourself,” he said, “for it is the sister of doubt and of a short temper.”

<sup>2</sup> “How, sir,” I said, “is it their sister? For a short temper seems to me one thing, doubt another, and grief another.” “You are a senseless man,” he said, “and you do not understand that grief is the most evil of all spirits and the most dreadful for slaves of God. More than any other spirit it destroys a person and wears out the Holy Spirit—and yet it also saves.”

<sup>3</sup> “I am senseless, sir,” I said, “and I do not understand these parables. I do not see how it can wear out and also save.”

<sup>4</sup> “Listen,” he said. “Those who have never searched into the truth or inquired about the divine, but have merely believed, and are tangled up in business, wealth, friendships with gentiles, and many other affairs of this age—all who are attached to these things fail to understand the parables about the divine. For they are darkened by these activities and ruined, and they become like fallow ground.

<sup>5</sup> Just as good vineyards, when neglected, are overrun with thorns and every sort of weed, so people who have believed but fall into these many activities I have mentioned are led astray from their understanding, and they grasp nothing at all about righteousness. Even when they hear about God and truth, their minds are taken up with their business, and they understand nothing at all.

<sup>6</sup> But those who have fear of God, who investigate God and truth and keep their heart turned to the Lord, grasp and understand everything said to them faster, because the fear of the Lord is in them. Where the Lord dwells, there is great understanding as well. Hold fast to the Lord, then, and you will understand and perceive everything.”

## Chapter 41

<sup>1</sup> “Now listen, you dense man,” he said, “to how grief wears out the Holy Spirit and also saves.

<sup>2</sup> When a double-minded person sets about some action and fails at it because of his doubt, this grief enters the person, grieves the Holy Spirit, and wears it out.

<sup>3</sup> Then again, when a short temper clings to a person over some matter and he becomes very bitter, grief enters the heart of the person who lost his temper; he is grieved over what he did, and repents that he did evil.

<sup>4</sup> This grief, then, seems to bring salvation, since he did evil and then repented. So both activities grieve the Spirit: doubt, because it did not succeed in its action, and a short temper, because it did what was evil. Both are distressing to the Holy Spirit, doubt and a short temper.

<sup>5</sup> So remove grief from yourself, and do not afflict the Holy Spirit that lives in you, lest it plead with God and leave you.

<sup>6</sup> For the Spirit of God that was given to this flesh cannot bear grief or distress.

## Chapter 42

<sup>1</sup> Put on cheerfulness, then, which always finds favor with God and is acceptable to him, and take delight in it. For every cheerful person does good, thinks good, and scorns grief.

<sup>2</sup> But the grieving person is always doing wrong. First he does wrong because he grieves the Holy Spirit, which was given to a person as a cheerful one. Second, by grieving the Holy Spirit he commits lawlessness, since he neither prays to the Lord nor confesses to him. For the prayer of a grieving person never has the power to go up to the altar of God.”

<sup>3</sup> “Why does the prayer of a grieving person not go up to the altar?” I asked. “Because grief has settled in his heart,” he said. “Grief mixed in with the prayer does not allow the prayer to rise pure to the altar. Vinegar and wine poured together do not taste as pleasant as each does alone; in the same way, grief mixed with the Holy Spirit does not make the same prayer.

<sup>4</sup> So cleanse yourself of this evil grief, and you will live for God; and all will live for God who throw grief off themselves and put on all cheerfulness.”

## Chapter 43

<sup>1</sup> He pointed out to me some people sitting on a bench and another man seated in a chair, and asked, “Do you see the people sitting on the bench?” “I do, sir,” I said. “They are believers,” he said, “and the one in the chair is a false prophet, who ruins the minds of God’s slaves—the minds of the double-minded, that is, not those of believers.

<sup>2</sup> These double-minded people approach him as they would a fortune-teller and ask what will become of them. And that false prophet, who has no strength of a divine spirit in himself, replies to them to fit their questions and the cravings of their wickedness, and fills their souls just as they desire.

<sup>3</sup> For he is empty himself, and gives empty answers to the empty; whatever he is asked, he answers to suit the person’s emptiness. Yet he does speak a few true words, because the devil fills him with his own spirit, hoping to break some of the righteous.

<sup>4</sup> Those who are strong in the faith of the Lord, clothed in truth, do not attach themselves to such spirits but keep away from them. But those who are double-minded and keep changing their minds consult oracles like the gentiles and bring a greater sin on themselves by their idolatry. For anyone who consults a false prophet about some course of action is an idolater, emptied of the truth, and a fool.

<sup>5</sup> No spirit given by God is ever consulted. It has the power of the divine and says everything of its own accord, because it comes from above, from the power of the divine Spirit.

<sup>6</sup> But the spirit that is consulted and answers according to people’s desires is earthly and lightweight, without power; and it does not speak at all unless it is asked.”

<sup>7</sup> “How, then, sir,” I asked, “will a person know which of them is a prophet and which a false prophet?” “Listen,” he said, “to what I tell you about both prophets, and so you will test the prophet and the false prophet. Test the person who has the divine spirit by his life.

<sup>8</sup> To begin with, whoever has the spirit from above is meek, tranquil, and humble. He stays clear of all wickedness and of the empty cravings of this age, counts himself lower than everyone, and gives nobody an answer when questioned. He does not speak by himself, nor does the Holy Spirit speak whenever someone wants to talk; it speaks when God wishes it to speak.

<sup>9</sup> So when someone who has the divine Spirit comes into an assembly[^43-9-1] of righteous people who trust in the divine Spirit, and that gathering of people offers prayer to God, the angel of the prophetic spirit who is attached to him fills him, and, full of the Holy Spirit, he addresses the crowd as the Lord pleases.

<sup>10</sup> That is how the divine Spirit will be made plain. Such, then, is the Lord’s power over the divine Spirit.

<sup>11</sup> Now listen,” he said, “about the earthly spirit, which is empty, has no power, and is foolish.

<sup>12</sup> First, the person who imagines he has a spirit exalts himself and wants the best seat; at once he is bold, shameless, and talkative, spends his time in much luxury and many other deceits, and takes pay for his prophecy; if he is not paid, he does not prophesy. Can a divine spirit take pay and prophesy? A prophet of God cannot do this; the spirit of such prophets is earthly.

<sup>13</sup> Then he never comes near an assembly of righteous men but avoids them. He attaches himself to the double-minded and empty, prophesies to them in a corner, and deceives them, saying everything emptily to match their desires. For he answers empty people: an empty vessel set beside other empty vessels is not broken; they agree with each other.

<sup>14</sup> But when he comes into an assembly full of righteous men who have the divine Spirit, and they pray, that man is emptied out. The earthly spirit flees from him in fear, and the man is struck dumb and utterly shattered, unable to say a word.

<sup>15</sup> Suppose you stack wine or oil in a storeroom and put an empty jar among them. When you later want to unstack the storeroom, the jar you put in empty you will find empty. So too the empty prophets, when they come among the spirits of the righteous, are found to be just as they came.

<sup>16</sup> You now have the life of both prophets. Test, then, by his works and his life the person who calls himself a bearer of the Spirit.

<sup>17</sup> Trust the spirit that comes from God and has power; but put no trust at all in the earthly and empty spirit, because there is no power in it, for it comes from the devil.

<sup>18</sup> Listen to a parable I am going to give you. Take a stone and throw it toward heaven; see if you can reach it. Or take a squirt of water and shoot it at heaven; see if you can pierce it.”

<sup>19</sup> “How can such things be, sir?” I said. “You have named two things that are impossible.” “Just as these are impossible,” he said, “so the earthly spirits are powerless and ineffective.

<sup>20</sup> Consider instead the power that comes from above. Hail is a tiny pellet, yet when it lands on a person’s head, how much pain it gives! Or take a drop that falls from a roof tile to the ground and bores through the stone.

<sup>21</sup> You see, then, that the tiniest things from above, when they fall on the earth, have great power. So too the divine Spirit, coming from above, is powerful. Trust this Spirit, then, and keep away from the other.”

## Chapter 44

<sup>1</sup> He said to me, “Remove every evil desire from yourself, and put on the good and dignified desire. Clothed in this desire, you will hate evil desire and rein it in as you please.

<sup>2</sup> For evil desire is wild and hard to tame. It is fearsome, and in its savagery it devours people. Above all, if a slave of God falls into it and lacks understanding, he is terribly devoured by it. It devours those who have no garment of good desire but are mixed up in this age; these it hands over to death.”

<sup>3</sup> “What kinds of deeds, sir,” I asked, “belong to evil desire, the ones that hand people over to death? Tell me, so that I may keep away from them.” “Listen,” he said, “to the deeds through which evil desire puts God’s slaves to death.”

## Chapter 45

<sup>1</sup> “Foremost of all is desire for another man’s wife, or another woman’s husband, for lavish wealth, for many useless dishes, for drunkenness, and for many other foolish luxuries. For every luxury is foolish and empty for slaves of God.

<sup>2</sup> These desires, then, are evil, and they put God’s slaves to death. This evil desire is a daughter of the devil. You must keep away from evil desires, so that by keeping away you may live for God.

<sup>3</sup> But those who are mastered by them and do not resist them will die in the end; for these desires are deadly.

<sup>4</sup> But you, put on the desire for righteousness and resist these desires with the fear of the Lord as your armor; the fear of God lives within good desire. When evil desire sees you equipped with the fear of God and resisting, it will run far away from you and not show itself to you again, terrified of your weapons.

<sup>5</sup> So when you have defeated it and been crowned for the victory, go to the desire for righteousness, hand over to it the victory you won, and serve it as it wishes. If you serve good desire and submit to it, you will be able to master evil desire and subdue it as you wish.”

## Chapter 46

<sup>1</sup> “I would like to learn, sir, how I am to serve good desire.” “Listen,” he said: “practice righteousness and virtue, truth and the fear of the Lord, faith and gentleness, and whatever other good things resemble these. By doing so you will be a slave of God who gives him pleasure, and you will live for him; and everyone who serves good desire will live for God.”

<sup>2</sup> So he finished the twelve commandments, and said to me, “You have these commandments. Walk in them, and encourage those who hear them, so that their repentance may be pure for the rest of their lives.

<sup>3</sup> Carry out this ministry that I give you with care, and you will accomplish much. For you will find favor among those who are about to repent, and they will be persuaded by your words; I will be with you, and I will compel them to be persuaded by you.”

<sup>4</sup> I said to him, “Sir, these commandments are great, beautiful, and glorious, and able to gladden the heart of anyone who can keep them. But I do not know whether a human being can keep these commandments, because they are very hard.”

<sup>5</sup> He replied, “If you resolve that they can be kept, you will keep them with ease, and they will not be hard. But once the idea has come into your heart that no human being can keep them, you will not keep them.

<sup>6</sup> And now I tell you: if you do not keep them but pay them no heed, you will have no salvation—neither you, nor your children, nor your household—since you have already decided for yourself that no human being can keep these commandments.”

## Chapter 47

<sup>1</sup> He said this to me in such great anger that I was thrown into confusion and terribly afraid of him; for his appearance changed, so that no one could bear his anger.

<sup>2</sup> When he saw me thoroughly shaken and bewildered, he began to speak more mildly and cheerfully. “Foolish, senseless, double-minded man,” he said, “do you not grasp the glory of God, how great, strong, and wonderful it is—that he made the world on humanity’s account, put the whole of his creation under humanity, and gave humanity full authority to rule everything beneath heaven?

<sup>3</sup> If, then, a human being is lord of all God’s creatures and rules over everything, can he not also master these commandments? The person who has the Lord in his heart,” he said, “can master all things and all these commandments.

<sup>4</sup> But for those who have the Lord only on their lips, while their hearts are hardened and far from the Lord, these commandments are hard and impassable.

<sup>5</sup> So you who are empty and shallow in faith, set your Lord in your heart, and you will know that nothing is easier than these commandments, or sweeter, or gentler.

<sup>6</sup> Turn around, you who walk by the devil’s commandments—difficult, bitter, savage, and licentious—and do not fear the devil, because there is no power in him against you,

<sup>7</sup> for I will be with you, I the angel of repentance, who have mastery over him. The devil has only fear, and his fear has no strength. So do not fear him, and he will flee from you[^47-7-1].”

## Chapter 48

<sup>1</sup> I said to him, “Sir, give me a hearing for a few words.” “Say what you like,” he said. “People, sir,” I said, “are eager to keep the commandments of God, and there is nobody who fails to ask the Lord for strength in his commandments and for submission to them. But the devil is ruthless and overpowers them.”

<sup>2</sup> “He cannot overpower those slaves of God who put their hope in him with all their heart,” he said. “The devil can struggle against them, but not throw them. So if you resist him, he will be defeated and run from you, humiliated. Those who are quite empty, however, fear the devil as though he held power.

<sup>3</sup> When a man fills a great many jars with good wine, and among them are a few quite empty ones, he goes to the jars and does not inspect the full ones—he knows they are full—but inspects the empty ones, fearing they have turned sour. For empty jars quickly turn sour, and the pleasantness of the wine is lost.

<sup>4</sup> So too the devil comes to all the slaves of God to tempt them. Those who are full in the faith stand firm against him, and he withdraws from them, having no place to enter. So he goes then to the empty ones and, finding a place, goes into them, and does in them whatever he wants, and they become his slaves.

## Chapter 49

<sup>1</sup> But I, the angel of repentance, tell you: have no fear of the devil. I was sent,” he said, “to be at your side, you who repent with your whole heart, and to make you strong in faith.

<sup>2</sup> Trust God, then, you who have written off your lives because of your sins, piling sin upon sin and loading your lives down. If you turn to the Lord with all your heart, do righteousness for the remaining days of your life, and serve him correctly in line with his will, he will cure your former sins and you will gain the strength to overcome the devil’s works. As for the devil’s threats, do not fear them at all; they are as slack as the muscles of a dead man.

<sup>3</sup> So listen to me and fear the one who can do all things, to save and to destroy, and keep these commandments, and you will live for God.”

<sup>4</sup> I told him, “Sir, now that you are with me, I have been strengthened in all the Lord’s ordinances. I know you will break the devil’s whole power, and that we will rule him and overcome all his works. And I hope, sir, that, with the Lord’s strengthening, I will be capable of keeping these commandments you have laid on me.”

<sup>5</sup> “You will keep them,” he said, “if your heart becomes pure toward the Lord; and all will keep them who cleanse their hearts of the empty desires of this age, and they will live for God.”

## Chapter 50

<sup>1</sup> He said to me, “You know that you slaves of God are living in a foreign land[^50-1-1], for your city is far from this city. If you know your own city, where you are going to live, why are you preparing fields, extravagant displays, buildings, and worthless dwellings here?

<sup>2</sup> Anyone who prepares such things for this city cannot go back to his own city.

<sup>3</sup> Foolish, double-minded, wretched man, can you not see that all of this belongs to someone else and lies under someone else’s control? The lord of this city will say, ‘I will not have you living in my city; get out of this city, because you do not use my laws.’

<sup>4</sup> You, then, who have fields and houses and many other possessions—when you are thrown out by him, what will you do with your field, your house, and everything else you prepared for yourself? For the lord of this country rightly says to you, ‘Either follow my laws or leave my country.’

<sup>5</sup> So what will you do, since you have a law in your own city? To keep your fields and your other property, will you throw away your law entirely and conform to the law of this city? Be careful: it may do you harm to throw away your law. If you wish to return to your city, you will not be admitted, since you rejected your city’s law, and you will find yourself locked out.

<sup>6</sup> So be careful. Since you are living in a foreign land, prepare no more for yourself than the sufficiency you need, and be ready, so that when the master of this city wants to throw you out for resisting his law, you can depart from his city, go to your own, and follow your own law without insult, rejoicing.

<sup>7</sup> Watch yourselves, you who serve the Lord and carry him in your heart. Do the works of God, bearing in mind his commandments and the promises he made, and trust that he will carry them out, provided his commandments are kept.

<sup>8</sup> In place of fields, then, buy up souls in trouble, as each of you can; visit widows and orphans and do not pass them by; and put your wealth and all your displays into fields and houses of this sort, which you received from God.

<sup>9</sup> For this is why the Master made you rich: so that you could perform these services for him. It is far better to buy fields, property, and houses of this kind, which you will find waiting in your city when you go to live in it.

<sup>10</sup> This lavish spending is good and holy, with no grief or fear in it, but joy. So do not take part in the lavish spending of the gentiles, for it does not benefit you, slaves of God.

<sup>11</sup> Practice your own kind of lavish spending, in which you can rejoice; do not counterfeit, do not touch what belongs to another, and do not desire it, for it is wicked to desire what belongs to others. Do your own work, and you will be saved.”

## Chapter 51

<sup>1</sup> As I walked in the field, looking at an elm and a vine and reflecting on them and their fruit, the shepherd appeared to me and said, “What are you thinking over about the elm and the vine?” “I am thinking, sir,” I said, “that they suit each other very well.”

<sup>2</sup> “These two trees,” he said, “are set as a symbol for the slaves of God.” “I would like to know, sir,” I said, “the symbol of the trees you speak of.” “Do you see the elm and the vine?” he asked. “I see them, sir,” I said.

<sup>3</sup> “This vine produces fruit,” he said, “while the elm is a tree without any. Yet unless the vine climbs the elm, it cannot fruit much, sprawled on the ground; and what it does produce is rotten, since it is not hanging from the elm. When the vine is draped over the elm, it yields fruit both of its own and from the elm.

<sup>4</sup> You see, then, that the elm also yields much fruit, no less than the vine, even more.” “How can it be more, sir?” I asked. “Because,” he said, “the vine hanging on the elm yields much good fruit, but flung on the ground it bears little and rotten fruit. This parable, then, is set for the slaves of God, for the poor and the rich.”

<sup>5</sup> “How, sir? Explain it to me.” “Listen,” he said. “The rich man has money, but toward the Lord he is poor, distracted by his wealth. His prayer and confession to the Lord are very meager, and what he does offer is weak and small, without further power. When the rich man rests on the poor man and supplies what he needs, he believes that by doing good to the poor man he can find a reward with God; for the poor man is rich in prayer and confession, and his prayer has great power with God. So the rich man supplies the poor man with everything, without hesitating.

<sup>6</sup> And the poor man, supplied by the rich man, prays to God and thanks him for the one who gives to him. The rich man in turn takes still more trouble over the poor man, so that his supply will not fail in his lifetime. For he knows that the prayer of the poor man is acceptable and rich before the Lord.

<sup>7</sup> Both, then, complete the work: the poor man works through the prayer in which he is rich, which he received from the Lord; this he gives back to the Lord, who supplies him. And the rich man likewise provides the poor man, without hesitating, with the wealth he received from the Lord. This is a great work and acceptable to God, because he understood about his wealth and worked for the poor man out of the Lord’s gifts and carried out the service rightly.

<sup>8</sup> In the eyes of people, then, the elm seems to bear no fruit, and they do not know or understand that when there is a drought, the elm, having water, feeds the vine, and the vine, having an unfailing supply of water, yields double fruit, both for itself and for the elm. So too the poor, praying to the Lord for the rich, bring their wealth to fullness, and the rich in turn, supplying the poor with what they need, bring their prayers to fullness.

<sup>9</sup> Both of them share in the righteous work. Anyone who does this will not be abandoned by God, but will be recorded in the books of the living.

<sup>10</sup> Blessed are those who have wealth and understand that they are enriched by the Lord; for the one who understands this will also be able to render some good service.”

## Chapter 52

<sup>1</sup> He showed me a great many trees with no leaves; they looked to me dried up, since they were all the same. He asked me, “Do you see these trees?” “Yes, sir,” I said, “all the same, and dry.” He answered, “These trees you are looking at are the people who live in this age.”

<sup>2</sup> “Why, sir,” I asked, “are they as if dried up, and all alike?” “Because,” he said, “in this age neither the righteous nor sinners can be told apart, but they are alike. For this age is winter for the righteous, and they cannot be told apart as they live among the sinners.

<sup>3</sup> Just as in winter the trees that have lost their leaves are alike, and it is not clear which are dry and which are alive, so in this age neither the righteous nor sinners are clear; all are alike.”

## Chapter 53

<sup>1</sup> He showed me many trees again, some sprouting and some dry, and said to me, “Do you see these trees?” “I see them, sir,” I said, “some sprouting and some dry.”

<sup>2</sup> “The trees that are budding,” he said, “are the righteous who will live in the coming age; for the coming age is summer to the righteous, but winter to sinners. When the Lord’s mercy shines forth, those who serve God will be brought into view, and everyone will be brought into view.

<sup>3</sup> For just as in summer the fruit of each tree becomes visible and is recognized for what it is, so too the fruits of the righteous will be visible, and all of them will be known to be flourishing in that age.

<sup>4</sup> But the gentiles and sinners, the dry trees you saw, will turn out dry and fruitless in that age and will be burned like firewood, and it will be plain that their conduct in life was evil. Sinners will be burned because they sinned and never repented; gentiles will be burned because they did not know their Creator.

<sup>5</sup> So you, bear fruit, so that in that summer your fruit may be recognized. Keep away from many pursuits, and you will not go wrong at all. For those who do many things sin much, distracted by their pursuits and not serving their Lord at all.

<sup>6</sup> How, then, can such a person ask anything of the Lord and receive it, when he does not serve the Lord? Those who serve him will receive what they ask; those who do not serve the Lord will receive nothing.

<sup>7</sup> But if someone has just one pursuit, he can also serve the Lord; for his mind will not be corrupted away from the Lord, but he will serve him with a pure mind.

<sup>8</sup> If you do these things, then, you can bear fruit for the age to come; and whoever does these things will bear fruit.”

## Chapter 54

<sup>1</sup> I was fasting and sitting on a mountain, thanking the Lord for everything he had done for me, when I saw the shepherd sitting beside me, asking, “Why have you come here so early in the morning?” “Because, sir,” I said, “I am keeping a station[^54-1-1].”

<sup>2</sup> “What is a station?” he asked. “A fast, sir.” “And what is this fast that you are keeping?” “As I used to, sir, so I fast.”

<sup>3</sup> “You do not know how to fast for the Lord,” he said, “and this useless fast you keep for him is no fast at all.” “Why do you say this, sir?” “I tell you,” he said, “that what you think you are keeping is not a fast. But I will teach you what a fast is that is acceptable and complete to the Lord. Listen,” he said.

<sup>4</sup> “God does not want a pointless fast like this; for if you fast for God in this way, you will accomplish nothing for righteousness. But fast for God like this:

<sup>5</sup> Do no wickedness in your life, but serve the Lord with a pure heart; keep his commandments, walking in his precepts, and let no evil desire come up in your heart. Trust God that if you do these things, fear him, and hold back from every evil thing, you will live for God. If you do these things, you will keep a great fast, acceptable to God.”

## Chapter 55

<sup>1</sup> “Listen to the parable I am about to tell you, which belongs to fasting.

<sup>2</sup> A man had a field with a great many slaves, and part of the field he planted as a vineyard. He chose one slave, faithful, well-pleasing, and honored, called him in, and said, ‘Here is the vineyard I planted: drive stakes around it until I come back, and do nothing else with it. If you obey this command of mine, you will be free in my household.’ And the slave’s master went away on a journey.

<sup>3</sup> After he left, the slave took the vineyard and fenced it with stakes. When he had finished fencing the vineyard, he saw that it was full of weeds.

<sup>4</sup> He reasoned to himself, ‘I have carried out this commandment of the master. I will dig this vineyard now, and it will look better dug, and without weeds it will give more fruit, not choked by the weeds.’ He took and dug the vineyard, and pulled out all the weeds in it. And that vineyard became very beautiful and flourishing, with no weeds to choke it.

<sup>5</sup> After a time the master of the slave and of the field came and went into the vineyard. When he saw the vineyard neatly fenced, and moreover dug, with all the weeds pulled out and the vines flourishing, he was very glad at the slave’s work.

<sup>6</sup> He called his beloved son, whom he had as heir, and the friends he had as advisers, and told them what he had commanded his slave and what he had found done. They rejoiced with the slave at the testimony the master gave him.

<sup>7</sup> He said to them, ‘I promised this slave freedom if he kept the commandment I gave him. He kept my commandment and added a good work to the vineyard, and it pleased me greatly. In return for this work he has done, I want to make him a joint heir with my son, because, having a good idea, he did not ignore it but carried it out.’

<sup>8</sup> The master’s son agreed with this decision, that the slave should become joint heir with the son.

<sup>9</sup> A few days later the master gave a banquet and sent him many dishes from the dinner. The slave took the dishes sent to him by the master, kept what was enough for himself, and distributed the rest to his fellow slaves.

<sup>10</sup> His fellow slaves, when they received the dishes, rejoiced and began to pray for him, that he might find greater favor with the master for having treated them so.

<sup>11</sup> The master heard all that had happened and was again very glad at what he had done. Calling his friends and his son together again, the master told them what he had done with the dishes he had received; and they agreed even more strongly that the slave should become joint heir with his son.”

## Chapter 56

<sup>1</sup> I said, “Sir, I do not understand these parables and cannot grasp them unless you explain them to me.”

<sup>2</sup> “I will explain everything to you,” he said, “and whatever else I say to you.

<sup>3</sup> I will show you his commandments. If you do some good beyond God’s commandment, you gain greater glory for yourself and will be more glorious in God’s eyes than you were going to be. If, then, while keeping the commandments of God, you add these services, you will rejoice, provided you keep them according to my commandment.”

<sup>4</sup> “Whatever you order, sir, I will keep,” I told him, “for I know you are with me.” “I will be with you,” he said, “since you are so eager to do good, and I will be with everyone who shares this eagerness.

<sup>5</sup> This fast,” he said, “when the Lord’s commandments are kept, is very good. This, then, is how you will keep the fast you intend to keep.

<sup>6</sup> First of all, guard against every evil word and every evil desire, and cleanse your heart of all the vanities of this age. If you guard against these, this fast will be complete for you.

<sup>7</sup> This is what you will do: after completing what is written, on the day you fast taste nothing but bread and water. Then reckon up the amount you would have spent that day on the foods you were going to eat, and give it to a widow or an orphan or someone in need. In this way you will humble yourself, so that the one who receives it may fill his soul from your humility and pray for you to the Lord.

<sup>8</sup> If you complete the fast in this fashion, as I have instructed you, your sacrifice will be welcome to God, this fast will be written down, and service rendered in this way is good, joyful, and pleasing to the Lord.

<sup>9</sup> You will keep these rules together with your children and your whole household. If you keep them, you will be blessed; blessed too will be all who hear them and keep them, and they will receive whatever they ask from the Lord.”

## Chapter 57

<sup>1</sup> I begged him hard to explain the parable of the field, the master, the vineyard, the slave who put stakes around it, the stakes, the weeds pulled up from the vineyard, the son, and the friends who served as advisers; I understood that all of this was some sort of parable.

<sup>2</sup> He replied, “You are far too bold in your questioning. You ought not to ask anything at all,” he said, “for if something has to be made clear to you, it will be.” I said to him, “Sir, anything you show me without explaining, I will have seen for nothing, not knowing what it means. Likewise, if you tell me parables and leave them unexplained, I will have listened to you for nothing.”

<sup>3</sup> He answered me again, “Whoever is a slave of God and has his Lord in his heart asks him for understanding, and receives it, and solves every parable, and the words of the Lord spoken in parables become known to him. But those who are weak and lazy in prayer hesitate to ask the Lord.

<sup>4</sup> The Lord, however, is full of compassion and gives without ceasing to all who ask him. You have been strengthened by the holy angel and have received such a prayer from him, and you are not lazy. Why, then, do you not ask the Lord for understanding and receive it from him?”

<sup>5</sup> I said to him, “Sir, since I have you with me, I have to ask you and question you. For you show me everything and speak with me; but if I saw or heard these things without you, I would ask the Lord to have them explained.”

## Chapter 58

<sup>1</sup> “I told you just now,” he said, “that you are crafty and headstrong in asking for the solutions of parables. But since you are so persistent, I will explain to you the parable of the field and everything that goes with it, so that you can make it known to all. Listen now,” he said, “and understand.

<sup>2</sup> The field is this world. The lord of the field is the one who created all things, set them in order, and gave them strength. The slave is the Son of God. The vines are this people, whom he himself planted.

<sup>3</sup> The stakes represent the holy angels of the Lord, who keep his people together. The weeds uprooted from the vineyard stand for the lawless deeds of God’s slaves. The dishes he sent from the dinner are the commandments he gave his people by way of his Son. The friends and advisers are the holy angels created first. The master’s absence is the time left until he comes.”

<sup>4</sup> I said to him, “Sir, all this is great and marvelous, and everything is glorious. Could I have understood these things? Not even another human being could, however intelligent. Sir, explain to me one more thing, which I am about to ask you.”

<sup>5</sup> “Say what you want,” he said. “Why, sir, is the Son of God put in the position of a slave in the parable?”

## Chapter 59

<sup>1</sup> “Listen,” he said. “The Son of God is not put in the position of a slave; he is put in a position of great authority and lordship.” “How, sir? I do not understand.”

<sup>2</sup> “Because God planted the vineyard,” he said—“that is, created the people—and handed it over to his Son. And the Son appointed the angels over them to guard them. He himself cleansed their sins, laboring hard and enduring many hardships; for no vineyard can be dug without labor or toil.

<sup>3</sup> Having cleansed the sins of the people, then, he showed them the paths of life by giving them the law he received from his Father.

<sup>4</sup> As for why the Lord took his Son and the glorious angels as advisers about the slave’s inheritance, listen.

<sup>5</sup> The preexistent Holy Spirit, which created all creation, God made to dwell in flesh of his choosing. This flesh, then, in which the Holy Spirit lived, served the Spirit well, walking in dignity and purity, and did not defile the Spirit in any way.

<sup>6</sup> Because it had lived well and purely, had labored together with the Spirit and cooperated in every matter, and had conducted itself strongly and courageously, he chose it as a partner with the Holy Spirit. For the conduct of this flesh pleased him, because while it had the Holy Spirit on earth it was not defiled.

<sup>7</sup> So he made the Son and the glorious angels his advisers, in order that this flesh, having served the Spirit blamelessly, might have some dwelling place and not seem to have lost the reward for its service. For all flesh found undefiled and spotless, in which the Holy Spirit has lived, will receive a reward.

<sup>8</sup> You now have the explanation of this parable too.”

## Chapter 60

<sup>1</sup> “This explanation has made me glad, sir,” I said. “Then listen further,” he said. “Keep this flesh of yours clean and unstained, so that the spirit dwelling in it can testify for it and your flesh be pronounced righteous.

<sup>2</sup> Take care that it never comes up in your heart that this flesh of yours is perishable, and you misuse it in some defilement. If you defile your flesh, you will defile the Holy Spirit as well; and if you defile the flesh, you will not live.”

<sup>3</sup> “And if someone, sir, was ignorant earlier, before these words were heard, how can a person be saved who has defiled his flesh?” “For the earlier acts of ignorance,” he said, “God alone is able to give healing, for all authority is his,

<sup>4</sup> provided that from now on you do not defile your flesh or your spirit; for the two are joined, and one cannot be defiled without the other. Keep both pure, then, and you will live for God.”

## Chapter 61

<sup>1</sup> I was sitting in my house, glorifying the Lord for all I had seen and reflecting on the commandments, how beautiful, powerful, cheerful, and glorious they are, and able to save a person’s soul. I said to myself, “I will be blessed if I walk by these commandments, and whoever walks by them will be blessed.”

<sup>2</sup> As I was thinking this over, all at once I saw him sitting beside me. “Why are you wavering about the commandments I gave you?” he asked. “They are good. Do not waver at all, but put on the faith of the Lord, and you will walk by them; for I will give you strength in them.

<sup>3</sup> These commandments are beneficial for those who are going to repent; for if they do not walk in them, their repentance is in vain.

<sup>4</sup> You who repent, shed the evils of this age that wear you down. Once you are clothed in every virtue of righteousness, you will be able to keep these commandments and add no more to your sins. Walk by these commandments of mine, then, and you will live for God. I have told you all this.”

<sup>5</sup> After saying this he said, “Come, let us go out into the countryside, and I will show you the sheep’s shepherds.” “Let us go, sir,” I said. We reached a plain, and he pointed out a young shepherd wearing an outfit saffron in color.

<sup>6</sup> He was pasturing a great many sheep, and these sheep seemed to be living in luxury, quite pampered, and they were cheerful, skipping this way and that. The shepherd himself was very cheerful over his flock; the very look of the shepherd was cheerful, and he ran about among the sheep.

## Chapter 62

<sup>1</sup> He asked me, “Do you see that shepherd?” “Yes, sir,” I said. “He is the angel of luxury and deceit,” he said. “He wears down the souls of God’s slaves and perverts them away from the truth, tricking them with the wicked desires through which they perish.

<sup>2</sup> They forget the commandments of the living God and walk in deceit and empty luxuries, and are destroyed by this angel, some to death and some to ruin.”

<sup>3</sup> “I do not know, sir,” I said, “what counts as death and what as ruin.” “Listen,” he said. “The sheep you saw so cheerful and bounding are those who have been wrenched away from God for good, having given themselves over to the desires of this age. For such people there is no repentance leading to life, since they added to their sins and blasphemed God’s name. Death, then, is for people like these.

<sup>4</sup> The sheep you saw that were not skipping but were grazing in one place are those who handed themselves over to luxuries and deceits, but spoke no blasphemy against the Lord. These have been ruined as regards the truth. In them there is a hope of repentance, by which they can live. Ruin, then, holds the hope of some renewal, but death means eternal destruction.”

<sup>5</sup> I went on a little farther, and he showed me a large shepherd, wild in appearance, wearing a white goatskin, with a bag on his shoulders, a very hard staff with knots, and a big whip. His look was so bitter that I was afraid of him; that was the look he had.

<sup>6</sup> This shepherd took over the sheep from the young shepherd, those pampered, luxurious ones that did not skip, and threw them into a steep place full of thorns and thistles, so that the sheep could not untangle themselves from the thorns and thistles but were caught in them.

<sup>7</sup> Entangled like this, they grazed among the thorns and thistles and were in great misery, beaten by him. He drove them this way and that and gave them no rest, and those sheep were not at all settled.

## Chapter 63

<sup>1</sup> Seeing them whipped and miserable like this, I grieved for them, because they were tormented so and had no relief at all.

<sup>2</sup> I asked the shepherd who was talking to me, “Sir, who is this shepherd who is so harsh and bitter, with no compassion at all for these sheep?” “This is the angel of punishment,” he said. “He is one of the righteous angels, but assigned to punishment.

<sup>3</sup> He takes those who wander away from God and walk in the desires and deceits of this age, and punishes them as they deserve with terrible and varied punishments.”

<sup>4</sup> “I would like to know, sir,” I said, “what these varied punishments are.” “Listen,” he said, “to the varied torments and punishments. They are the torments of everyday life: some are punished with losses, some with want, some with various illnesses, some with every kind of instability, some by being insulted by unworthy people, and some by suffering many other things.

<sup>5</sup> For many, unsteady in their plans, take on many projects, and nothing at all goes forward for them. They say they are not prospering in their undertakings, and it never comes up in their hearts that they have done evil deeds, but they blame the Lord.

<sup>6</sup> When every kind of affliction has pressed on them, they are handed over to me for sound training; they are strengthened in the Lord’s faith and spend the remaining days of their lives serving the Lord with a clean heart. And if they repent, the wicked things they did come to mind, and they give glory to God, admitting that he is a just judge and that each suffered fairly for his own conduct. From then on they serve the Lord with an unstained heart, succeed in all they undertake, and get from the Lord whatever they request. Then they give the Lord glory that they were delivered to me, and no more evil touches them.”

## Chapter 64

<sup>1</sup> “Explain one more thing to me, sir,” I said. “What are you after?” he asked. “Are those who live in luxury and are deceived tormented as long as they enjoy luxury and are deceived?” “They are tormented for the same length of time,” he answered.

<sup>2</sup> “They are tormented for very little time, sir,” I said. “Those who live in such luxury and forget God ought to be tormented seven times over.”

<sup>3</sup> He said to me, “You are foolish, and you do not understand the power of torment.” “If I understood, sir,” I said, “I would not be asking you to explain it to me.” “Listen,” he said, “to the power of both.

<sup>4</sup> An hour is all the time luxury and deceit last, whereas an hour of torment weighs as much as thirty days. If someone lives it up and is deceived for one day, then suffers torment for one day, that day of torment counts as a whole year. As many days as a person spends in luxury, that many years he is tormented. You can see how short the time of luxury and deceit is, and how long that of punishment and torment.”

## Chapter 65

<sup>1</sup> “Sir, I still do not understand at all about the time of deceit, luxury, and torment; explain it to me more clearly.”

<sup>2</sup> He replied, “Your foolishness has no end, and you refuse to clean your heart and serve God. See to it,” he said, “that the time does not expire and you are found a fool. Listen, then, as you wish, to understand these matters.

<sup>3</sup> The person who lives in luxury and is deceived for one day, and does what he wants, is clothed in great foolishness and does not understand what he is doing; for the next day he forgets what he did the day before. Luxury and deceit have no memory, because of the foolishness they are clothed in; but punishment and torment, when they cling to a person for one day, punish and torment him for a year; for punishment and torment have long memories.

<sup>4</sup> So, after a whole year of torment and punishment, he finally remembers the luxury and the deceit and understands that he suffers these evils on their account. Everyone who lives in luxury and is deceived is tormented like this, for though they had life, they gave themselves up to death.”

<sup>5</sup> “What luxuries, sir, are harmful?” “Every action is a luxury for a person if he does it with pleasure,” he said. “For the quick-tempered person who gives full satisfaction to his passion is living in luxury; so are the adulterer, the drunkard, the slanderer, the liar, the greedy person, the defrauder, and anyone who does things like these: each satisfies his own sickness. He lives in luxury in his action.

<sup>6</sup> All these luxuries are harmful to God’s slaves. It is because of these deceits that those who are punished and tormented suffer.

<sup>7</sup> There are also luxuries that save people; many who do good enjoy themselves, swept along by their own pleasure. This kind of luxury benefits God’s slaves and secures life for the person who has it. The harmful luxuries mentioned before, though, win them torments and punishments; and if they persist and do not repent, they win themselves death.”

## Chapter 66

<sup>1</sup> Some days afterward I saw him again on the plain where I had earlier seen the shepherds. “What do you want?” he asked. “I have come, sir, to ask you to command the punishing shepherd to leave my house, since he is giving me terrible trouble.” “You have to be afflicted,” he said, “because that is what the glorious angel decreed about you; he wants to put you to the test.” “But what wrong have I done, sir, to be given over to this angel?”

<sup>2</sup> “Listen,” he said. “Your sins are numerous, though not enough to hand you over to this angel. It is your household that committed serious lawless acts and sins, and the glorious angel was bitter over what they had done; so he ordered you to be afflicted for a while, to bring them to repentance and to cleanse themselves from every desire of this age. As soon as they repent and are clean, the angel of punishment will withdraw from you.”

<sup>3</sup> I said to him, “Sir, if they did such things as to embitter the glorious angel, what did I do?” “They cannot be afflicted otherwise,” he said, “unless you, the head of the household, are afflicted. For when you are afflicted, they will necessarily be afflicted too; but when you are doing well, they cannot have any affliction.”

<sup>4</sup> “But see, sir, they have repented with their whole heart.” “I know,” he said, “that they have repented with their whole heart. Do you think the sins of those who repent are forgiven immediately? Not entirely. The one who repents must torment his own soul and humble himself thoroughly in everything he does and be afflicted with all kinds of afflictions; and if he endures the afflictions that come upon him, the one who created and strengthened all things will surely have compassion and give him some healing,

<sup>5</sup> and certainly so, if he sees that the penitent’s heart is free of every evil thing. For you and your household it is good to be afflicted now. Why say more? You must be afflicted, as the Lord’s angel who handed you over to me ordered. Give the Lord thanks for this, that he judged you fit to be told of the affliction in advance, so that, forewarned, you could bear it bravely.”

<sup>6</sup> “Be with me, sir,” I said, “and I will be able to bear any affliction.” “I will be with you,” he said, “and I will ask the angel of punishment to deal with you more lightly. You will be afflicted only a short while, and then you will be put back in your house. Just go on humbling yourself and serving the Lord with an entirely pure heart—you, your children, and your household. Walk by the commandments I am giving you, and your repentance can be strong and pure,

<sup>7</sup> and if you keep these with your household, all affliction will leave you; and affliction will leave all who walk in these commandments of mine.”

## Chapter 67

<sup>1</sup> He showed me a great willow that overshadowed plains and mountains, and under the shelter of the willow came all who are called by the name of the Lord.

<sup>2</sup> Beside the willow stood a very tall, glorious angel of the Lord with a large sickle, cutting branches from the willow and handing them to the people sheltered by the willow. He gave them little rods, about a cubit long.

<sup>3</sup> After everyone had received rods, the angel put down the sickle, and that tree was whole, just as I had seen it.

<sup>4</sup> I marveled and said to myself, “How is the tree whole when so many branches have been cut?” The shepherd said to me, “Do not marvel that this tree remained whole when so many branches were cut off. When you have seen everything, it will be explained to you what it is.”

<sup>5</sup> The angel who had issued the rods to the people began to call them in again; they were summoned to him in the order in which they had received them, and each one handed his rods back. The Lord’s angel took them and looked them over.

<sup>6</sup> From some he received the rods dry and eaten as if by moths. The angel ordered those who handed back such rods to stand apart.

<sup>7</sup> Others handed back dry ones, but not eaten by moths. He ordered these too to stand apart.

<sup>8</sup> Others handed back half-dry ones. These stood apart.

<sup>9</sup> Others handed back their rods half-dry and with cracks. These stood apart.

<sup>10</sup> Others handed back their rods green and with cracks. These stood apart.

<sup>11</sup> Others handed back rods half dry and half green. These stood apart.

<sup>12</sup> Others brought rods two-thirds green and one-third dry. These stood apart.

<sup>13</sup> Others handed back rods two-thirds dry and one-third green. These stood apart.

<sup>14</sup> Others handed back their rods almost entirely green, with only the very tip of the rods dry; but they had cracks in them. These stood apart.

<sup>15</sup> Others had only a little green, and the rest of the rods dry. These stood apart.

<sup>16</sup> Others came carrying their rods green, as they had received them from the angel. The greater part of the crowd handed back rods like this. The angel was very glad over these. These stood apart.

<sup>17</sup> Others handed back their rods green and with shoots. These stood apart, and over these the angel was very glad.

<sup>18</sup> Others handed back their rods green and with shoots, and the shoots had a kind of fruit. Those people whose rods were found like this were very cheerful. The angel exulted over them, and the shepherd was very cheerful over them.

## Chapter 68

<sup>1</sup> The angel of the Lord ordered crowns to be brought. Crowns were brought, made as if of palm, and he crowned the men who had handed back the rods with the shoots and some fruit, and sent them off into the tower.

<sup>2</sup> The others he also sent into the tower, those who had handed back green rods with shoots but no fruit on the shoots, giving them seals.

<sup>3</sup> All who went into the tower had the same clothing, white as snow.

<sup>4</sup> Those who had handed back their rods green, as they received them, he dismissed, giving them clothing and seals.

<sup>5</sup> When the angel had done this, he said to the shepherd, “I am leaving; you are to dismiss these to the walls, each according to where he deserves to live. Check their rods carefully and release them accordingly; check carefully. See that no one slips past you,” he said. “If any does slip past you, I will test them at the altar.” With these words to the shepherd, he departed.

<sup>6</sup> When the angel had gone, the shepherd said to me, “Let us collect everyone’s rods and plant them, to find out whether any can live.” “Sir,” I said, “how could these dried-out ones live?”

<sup>7</sup> “This tree is a willow,” he answered, “a kind that loves life. If the rods are planted and take up a little moisture, many of them will live. Then we can try pouring water on them as well. If any can live, I will celebrate with them; if one does not, no one will be able to call me negligent.”

<sup>8</sup> The shepherd told me to call them, as each group had stood. They came company by company and handed the rods to the shepherd. The shepherd took the rods and planted them company by company, and after planting them poured so much water on them that the rods could not be seen for the water.

<sup>9</sup> After watering the rods he said to me, “Let us go, and after a few days come back and inspect all the rods; for the one who created this tree wants all who received branches from this tree to live. And I hope that these little rods, having received moisture and being watered, will live, most of them.”

## Chapter 69

<sup>1</sup> I said to him, “Sir, tell me what this tree is. I am puzzled about it, because the tree is whole even though so many branches were cut, and nothing seems to have been cut from it. That is what puzzles me.”

<sup>2</sup> “Listen,” he said. “That huge tree, which covers plains, mountains, and the whole earth, is the law of God that was given to the entire world; and this law is the Son of God, preached to the farthest parts of the earth. The people sheltered beneath it are those who heard the preaching and believed in him.

<sup>3</sup> Michael, the great and glorious angel, has authority over this people and steers them; it is he who sets the law in the hearts of those who believe. So he checks whether those he gave it to have kept it.

<sup>4</sup> You see each person’s rods; for the rods are the law. You see many rods that have become useless, and you will know all those who did not keep the law; and you will see each one’s dwelling.”

<sup>5</sup> “Sir,” I said, “why did he send some into the tower and leave others with you?” “Everyone who violated the law received from him,” he said, “he left under my authority for repentance; but those who have already pleased God by the law and kept it, he keeps under his own authority.”

<sup>6</sup> “Who then, sir,” I said, “are those who are crowned and go into the tower?” “All who wrestled against the devil and conquered him are crowned,” he said. “These are the ones who suffered for the law.

<sup>7</sup> The others, who also handed in green rods with shoots but no fruit, are those who were afflicted for the law but did not suffer or deny their law.

<sup>8</sup> Those who handed back green rods, just as they received them, are dignified and righteous people who walked in a pure heart and kept the Lord’s commandments. The rest you will know when I examine these rods that have been planted and watered.”

## Chapter 70

<sup>1</sup> A few days later we came to the place, and the shepherd sat down in the angel’s seat while I stood beside him. He said to me, “Put on a linen cloth[^70-1-1] and wait on me.” I put on a clean linen cloth made of sackcloth.

<sup>2</sup> When he saw me with the cloth tied on and ready to serve him, he said, “Call the men whose rods have been planted, company by company, in the order in which each handed in his rods.” I went out to the plain and called them all, and they all stood, company by company.

<sup>3</sup> He said to them, “Let each one pull up his own rods and bring them to me.”

<sup>4</sup> First to hand them in were those who had dry, cut rods, and since these were found dry and cut, he ordered them to stand apart.

<sup>5</sup> Next came those whose rods were dry but not cut. Some of them handed in green rods, and some dry rods, cut as if by a moth. Those who handed in green rods he ordered to stand apart, and those who handed in dry, cut ones he ordered to stand with the first group.

<sup>6</sup> Then came those with half-dry rods that had cracks. Many of them handed in green rods without cracks. Some handed in green rods with shoots, and fruit on the shoots, like the fruit of those who had gone into the tower crowned. Some handed in dry, eaten rods, some dry ones not eaten, and some as they had been, half-dry with cracks. He ordered each of them to stand apart, some by their own companies and some by themselves.

## Chapter 71

<sup>1</sup> Then came those whose rods were green but had cracks. All of these handed in green rods, and they stood with their own company. The shepherd was glad over them, because they had all changed and laid aside their cracks.

<sup>2</sup> Those who had rods half green and half dry also handed them in. Of these, some rods were found fully green, some half-dry, some dry and eaten, and some green with shoots. All these were dismissed, each to his own company.

<sup>3</sup> Then came those with rods two-thirds green and one-third dry. Many of them handed in green rods, many half-dry, and others dry and eaten. All of these stood with their own company.

<sup>4</sup> Then came those with rods two-thirds dry and one-third green. Many of them handed in half-dry rods, some dry and eaten, others half-dry and cracked, and a few green. All of these stood with their own company.

<sup>5</sup> Those whose rods had been green, with just a little dry and some cracks, handed them in. Of these, some handed in green rods, some green with shoots. These too went to their own company.

<sup>6</sup> Then came those who had only a little green, the rest of the rods being dry. Most of their rods were found green and had shoots, with fruit on the shoots, and others were wholly green. The shepherd was exceedingly glad over these rods, that they were found this way. These went off, each to his own company.

## Chapter 72

<sup>1</sup> When the shepherd had gone through all the rods, he said to me, “As I told you, this tree loves life. Can you see how many have repented and been saved?” “I can, sir,” I said. “You are meant to see,” he said, “how abundant, great, and glorious the Lord’s compassion is; he has given the spirit to those who deserve repentance.”

<sup>2</sup> “Why, sir,” I asked, “did not all repent?” “Those whose hearts he saw were going to become pure and serve him with their whole heart, to these he gave repentance,” he said. “But those in whom he saw deceit and wickedness, who would repent only in hypocrisy, to those he did not give repentance, lest they should again profane his name.”

<sup>3</sup> I said to him, “Sir, please tell me now what sort of people each of those who handed in rods is, and where each will live. Then those who believed and received the seal but broke it and did not keep it intact may hear it, recognize what they have done, repent, receive a seal from you, and glorify the Lord for taking pity on them and sending you to renew their spirits.”

<sup>4</sup> “Listen,” he said. “The people whose rods were found dry and moth-eaten are the apostates and betrayers of the church, who blasphemed the Lord with their sins and were ashamed besides of the Lord’s name called over them. They have perished to God for good. You can see that none of them repented, even though they listened to the words you spoke to them at my command. Life has withdrawn from people like this.

<sup>5</sup> Those who handed in dry rods not eaten away are near them. They were hypocrites who brought in strange teachings and turned God’s slaves aside, especially those who had sinned, not letting them repent but persuading them with their foolish teachings. These have hope of repenting.

<sup>6</sup> Many of them, as you can see, have repented since I gave them my commandments, and more will repent. The ones who do not repent have lost their life. But those among them who repented became good, and their dwelling is in the first walls; some have even gone up into the tower. You see, then,” he said, “that repentance from sins brings life, and failing to repent brings death.

## Chapter 73

<sup>1</sup> As for those who handed in half-dry rods with cracks in them, hear about them also. Those whose rods were half-dry are the double-minded: they are neither alive nor dead.

<sup>2</sup> Those who had half-dry rods with cracks in them are double-minded, slanderers, never at peace among themselves but always quarreling. Yet repentance is open to these as well,” he said. “You see that some of them have repented. And there is still hope of repentance among them.

<sup>3</sup> All of them who have repented will have their dwelling in the tower, and those of them who repented rather late will live in the walls. But those who do not repent, but persist in their ways, will die the death.

<sup>4</sup> Those who handed in their rods green but with cracks have always been faithful and good, though they are jealous of one another over first place and over some kind of honor. But all these people are foolish, being jealous of one another over first place.

<sup>5</sup> Even these, though, once they heard my commandments, being good people, cleansed themselves and soon repented. So their dwelling came to be in the tower; but if any of them goes back to dissension, he will be cast out of the tower and lose his life.

<sup>6</sup> Life belongs to all who keep the Lord’s commandments. In the commandments there is nothing about first place or about any honor, but about the patience and humility of a person. In such people, then, is the life of the Lord, but among quarrelers and lawbreakers there is death.

## Chapter 74

<sup>1</sup> Those who handed in rods half green and half dry are the ones tangled up in business affairs and not attached to the saints. That is why half of them is alive and half is dead.

<sup>2</sup> Many of them, when they heard my commandments, repented. All who repented have their dwelling in the tower. But some of them departed for good. These have no repentance, for because of their business affairs they blasphemed the Lord and denied him. They lost their life through the wickedness they practiced.

<sup>3</sup> Many of them doubted. These still have repentance, if they repent quickly, and their dwelling will be in the tower; but if they repent rather slowly, they will live in the walls; and if they do not repent, they too have lost their life.

<sup>4</sup> Those who handed in rods two-thirds green and one-third dry are the ones who denied him in various ways.

<sup>5</sup> Many of them repented and went to live in the tower. Many departed from God for good; these lost life for good. Some of them doubted and were divided. For these there is repentance, if they repent quickly and do not persist in their pleasures; but if they persist in their ways, these too work death for themselves.

## Chapter 75

<sup>1</sup> Those who handed in rods two-thirds dry and one-third green are people who became believers but then grew rich and became honored among the gentiles. They put on great pride and became haughty, abandoned the truth, and did not stay close to the righteous, but lived with the gentiles, and this way became sweeter to them. Yet they did not depart from God, but stayed in the faith, though not doing the works of faith.

<sup>2</sup> Many of them, then, repented, and their dwelling became the tower.

<sup>3</sup> Others lived with the gentiles for good, and, corrupted by the vanities of the gentiles, departed from God and did the deeds of the gentiles. These were counted with the gentiles.

<sup>4</sup> Some of them wavered, doubting that they could be saved after what they had done; others wavered and split into factions among themselves. For these waverers, because of what they did, repentance is still possible, but it must be prompt, so that they may have their dwelling in the tower. For those who refuse to repent and stay with their pleasures, death is at the door.

## Chapter 76

<sup>1</sup> Those who handed in green rods with only the tips dry and cracked have always been good, faithful, and glorious before God, but they sinned a very little because of small desires and small grievances against one another. But when they heard my words, most of them quickly repented, and their dwelling became the tower.

<sup>2</sup> Some of them doubted, and some who doubted created a greater division. In them, then, there is hope of repentance, because they have always been good; hardly any of them will die.

<sup>3</sup> Those who handed in their rods dry, with only a little green, are the ones who merely believed but did the works of lawlessness. They never departed from God, gladly bore the name, and gladly welcomed God’s slaves into their houses. When they heard of this repentance, then, they repented without hesitation, and they work every virtue and righteousness.

<sup>4</sup> Some of them are even afraid, knowing the deeds they did. All these, then, will have their dwelling in the tower.

## Chapter 77

<sup>1</sup> When he had finished explaining all the rods, he told me, “Go, and tell everyone to repent so that they may live for God. The Lord, moved by compassion, sent me to give repentance to all, although some are unworthy because of what they have done. But the Lord is patient, and wants the calling made through his Son to bring salvation.”

<sup>2</sup> I said to him, “Sir, I hope that all who hear this will repent; for I am convinced that each one, recognizing his own deeds and fearing God, will repent.”

<sup>3</sup> He answered me, “Everyone who repents with all their heart, cleanses themselves of the wicked acts listed above, and stops adding to their sins will be healed by the Lord of their earlier sins, so long as they do not doubt these commandments; and they will live for God. But everyone who adds to their sins and follows the cravings of this age will sentence themselves to death.

<sup>4</sup> You, however, walk in my commandments and you will live for God; and all who walk in them and act rightly will live for God.”

<sup>5</sup> When he had shown me all this and spoken to me, he said, “I will show you the rest in a few days.”

## Chapter 78

<sup>1</sup> When I had finished writing out the commandments and parables of the shepherd, the angel of repentance, he approached me and told me, “I want to show you everything that the Holy Spirit showed you, when he addressed you in the likeness of the church—for that Spirit is the Son of God.

<sup>2</sup> You were rather weak in the flesh, and so it was not made clear to you by an angel. But once the Spirit had strengthened you and you had grown strong enough even to see an angel, the building of the tower was shown to you by way of the church. You saw it all, fittingly and with dignity, as through a virgin. Now you see by means of an angel, but through that same Spirit.

<sup>3</sup> But you must learn everything more precisely from me. For this reason I was assigned by the glorious angel to live in your house, so that you may see everything with strength, no longer frightened as before.”

<sup>4</sup> He took me to Arcadia, to a breast-shaped mountain, and sat me down on its crest and showed me a huge plain, with twelve mountains around the plain, each mountain with a different look.

<sup>5</sup> The first was black as soot. The second was bare, without plants. The third was full of thorns and thistles.

<sup>6</sup> The fourth had plants that were half dry, the tops of the plants green but the parts near the roots dry; some of the plants dried up when the sun scorched them.

<sup>7</sup> The fifth mountain had green plants and was rugged. The sixth mountain was completely full of cracks, some small, some large. The cracks had plants in them, but the plants were not very luxuriant; they looked rather withered.

<sup>8</sup> The seventh mountain had cheerful plants, and the whole mountain was flourishing, and every kind of cattle and bird fed on that mountain; the more the cattle and birds grazed, the more the plants of that mountain thrived. The eighth mountain was full of springs, and every kind of the Lord’s creatures was watered from the springs of that mountain.

<sup>9</sup> The ninth mountain had no water at all and was entirely desert. It had on it wild animals and deadly reptiles that destroy people. The tenth mountain had enormous trees and was entirely shaded, and under the shelter of the trees sheep lay resting and chewing the cud.

<sup>10</sup> The eleventh mountain was thick with trees, and those trees were loaded with fruit, each adorned with a different fruit, so that anyone who saw them would long to eat their fruit. The twelfth mountain was entirely white, and its appearance was cheerful; the mountain was most beautiful in itself.

## Chapter 79

<sup>1</sup> In the middle of the plain he pointed out to me a large white rock that rose up out of the plain. The rock was taller than the mountains, and four-sided, so that it could have held the entire world.

<sup>2</sup> That rock was old, with a gate cut into it; but the cutting of the gate seemed to me recent. The gate shone brighter than the sun, so that I marveled at the brilliance of the gate.

<sup>3</sup> Twelve virgins stood around the gate. The four standing at the corners seemed to me more glorious, though the others were glorious too. They stood at the four sides of the gate, two virgins between each pair of corner ones.

<sup>4</sup> They were dressed in linen tunics and belted becomingly, with their right shoulders bare as though about to carry a load. That is how ready they were; for they were very cheerful and eager.

<sup>5</sup> After I saw this I marveled to myself that I was seeing great and glorious things. And I was puzzled again about the virgins, that being so delicate they stood so bravely, as if about to carry the whole sky.

<sup>6</sup> The shepherd said to me, “Why do you reason and puzzle in yourself and bring grief on yourself? Do not try, as an understanding man, to grasp what you cannot grasp, but ask the Lord, so that you may receive understanding and grasp it.

<sup>7</sup> You cannot see what lies behind you, but you can see what lies ahead. Let go of what is beyond your sight and do not agonize over it. Take charge of what you do see, and do not fuss about the rest. Everything I show you I will explain to you. Look, then, at what remains.”

## Chapter 80

<sup>1</sup> I saw six men who had come, tall, glorious, and alike in appearance, and they called a crowd of men. Those who came were also tall, handsome, and strong men. The six men ordered them to build a tower on the rock. There was a great uproar from those men who had come to build the tower, running here and there around the gate.

<sup>2</sup> The virgins standing around the gate told the men to hurry to build the tower; the virgins had stretched out their hands as though about to receive something from the men.

<sup>3</sup> The six men ordered stones to come up from some deep place and go into the building of the tower. Ten square stones came up, shining, not cut.

<sup>4</sup> The six men called the virgins and ordered them to carry all the stones that were to go into the building of the tower and pass through the gate and hand them to the men who were to build the tower.

<sup>5</sup> The virgins laid the first ten stones, those that came up from the deep, one on another, and carried them together, one stone at a time.

## Chapter 81

<sup>1</sup> As they stood together around the gate, the ones who looked strong carried them, getting under the corners of the stone, while the others got under the stone’s sides, and so they carried all the stones. They took them through the gate as ordered and passed them to the men at the tower, who took the stones and built.

<sup>2</sup> The building of the tower was set on the great rock and above the gate. So those ten stones were fitted together and filled the whole rock, and they became the foundation of the building of the tower. The rock and the gate were bearing the whole tower.

<sup>3</sup> After the ten, another twenty stones[^81-3-1] rose from the deep. These too were fitted into the tower’s structure, carried by the virgins like the first. After them thirty-five came up, and these were likewise set in the tower; and after these another forty stones were brought up, and all of them were thrown into the building of the tower. So there were four rows in the tower’s foundations.

<sup>4</sup> The stones stopped coming up from the deep, and the builders stopped for a little while. Again the six men commanded the crowd of people to bring stones from the mountains for the building of the tower.

<sup>5</sup> So stones were brought from all the mountains, of various colors, cut by the men, and handed to the virgins; the virgins carried them through the gate and handed them to the building of the tower. When the variously colored stones were laid in the building, they became alike, white, and changed their various colors.

<sup>6</sup> Some stones were handed in by the men for the building but did not become bright; they were found exactly as they had been put there, for the virgins had not handed them in, nor had they been carried through the gate. These stones looked out of place in the tower’s structure.

<sup>7</sup> When the six men saw the unsightly stones in the building, they ordered them to be removed and taken down to their own place, from where they had been brought.

<sup>8</sup> And they told the men who were bringing the stones, “You are not to hand any stones into the building. Lay them next to the tower, so that the virgins can carry them in through the gate and pass them into the building. Unless they are brought through the gate by these virgins’ hands, they cannot change their colors. So do not wear yourselves out for nothing.”

## Chapter 82

<sup>1</sup> On that day the building work came to an end, though the tower was not finished; it was to be built up higher still. The work stopped. The six men told all the builders to stand back for a bit and rest, but told the virgins not to leave the tower. I took it that the virgins had been left behind to guard the tower.

<sup>2</sup> When everyone had gone off and rested, I asked the shepherd, “Sir, why has the building of the tower not been finished?” “The tower cannot be finished yet,” he said, “until its lord comes and examines the construction, to replace any stones that prove to be rotten; for the tower is being built to his will.”

<sup>3</sup> “Sir,” I said, “I wanted to know what this tower, this building, is, and about the rock, the gate, the mountains, the virgins, and the stones that came up from the deep and were not cut but went into the building just as they were.

<sup>4</sup> And why were ten stones laid in the foundations first, then twenty, then thirty-five, then forty; and about the stones that went into the building and were taken out again and put in their own place. Set my soul at rest about all these things, sir, and explain them to me.”

<sup>5</sup> “If you are not found idly curious,” he said, “you will learn everything. For in a few days we will come here, and you will see the rest of what happens to this tower and learn all the parables exactly.”

<sup>6</sup> A few days afterward we came to the place where we had sat, and he said to me, “Let us go to the tower, for the tower’s lord is on his way to look it over.” We arrived at the tower, and nobody at all was there but the virgins.

<sup>7</sup> The shepherd asked the virgins whether the master of the tower had come. They said he was about to come to inspect the building.

## Chapter 83

<sup>1</sup> And look, after a little while I saw a long procession of many men coming, and in the middle a man so tall that he towered over the tower.

<sup>2</sup> The six men who had been in charge of the building walked with him, on his right and left, and all who had worked on the building were with him, and many other glorious people around him. The virgins who guarded the tower ran to him, kissed him, and began to walk near him around the tower.

<sup>3</sup> That man inspected the building so carefully that he felt every stone one by one. Holding a rod in his hand, he struck each of the stones that had been built in.

<sup>4</sup> When he struck, some of them turned black as soot, some scabby, some cracked, some chipped, some neither white nor black, some rough and not matching the other stones, and some with many spots. These were the varieties of the stones found rotten in the building.

<sup>5</sup> He ordered all these to be removed from the tower and set beside the tower, and other stones to be brought and put in their place.

<sup>6</sup> The builders asked him from which mountain he wanted stones brought to put in their place. He ordered that none be brought from the mountains, but from a plain nearby.

<sup>7</sup> The plain was dug, and shining square stones were found, and some round ones as well. All the stones that were in that plain were brought and carried through the gate by the virgins.

<sup>8</sup> The square stones were cut and put in the place of those removed; the round ones were not put into the building, because they were too hard to cut and slow to work. They were put beside the tower, as if they were to be cut and put into the building later; for they were very bright.

## Chapter 84

<sup>1</sup> When he had finished this, the glorious man who was lord of the whole tower called the shepherd and handed over to him all the stones lying beside the tower, those rejected from the building, and said to him:

<sup>2</sup> “Clean these stones carefully, and put into the building of the tower those that can fit with the rest; but throw far away from the tower those that do not fit.”

<sup>3</sup> After giving this order to the shepherd, he left the tower with all those he had come with. The virgins stayed standing around the tower, guarding it.

<sup>4</sup> I said to the shepherd, “How can these stones, having been rejected, go into the building of the tower?” He answered, “Do you see these stones?” “I do, sir,” I said. “Most of these stones I will cut and put into the building, and they will fit with the other stones.”

<sup>5</sup> “How, sir,” I asked, “can they fill the same place after they are cut down?” He answered, “Those found small will be put into the middle of the building, and the larger ones will be put farther out and hold them together.”

<sup>6</sup> After saying this he said, “Let us go, and in two days let us come back and clean these stones and put them in the building. For everything around the tower must be cleaned, lest the master suddenly come and find the area around the tower dirty and be offended, and these stones not go into the building of the tower, and I seem negligent in the master’s eyes.”

<sup>7</sup> Two days later we came to the tower, and he said to me, “Let us examine all the stones and see which can go into the building.” “Let us examine them, sir,” I said.

## Chapter 85

<sup>1</sup> Starting with the black stones, we examined them first. They were found just as they had been when they were removed from the building. The shepherd ordered them to be carried away from the tower and set apart.

<sup>2</sup> Then he examined the scabby ones, and took many of them and cut them down, and ordered the virgins to lift them and put them into the building. The virgins lifted them and put them into the middle of the building of the tower. The rest he ordered to be placed with the black ones, for they too were found to be black.

<sup>3</sup> Then he examined those with cracks. Many of these he cut down and ordered the virgins to carry into the building; they were set farther out, because they were found to be sounder. The rest could not be cut because of the number of their cracks, and for this reason they were rejected from the building of the tower.

<sup>4</sup> Then he examined the chipped ones. Many of them turned out to be black, and some had made large cracks. He ordered these too to be set with the rejected. The remainder he cleaned and cut and ordered to be put into the building. The virgins lifted them and fitted them into the middle of the building of the tower, for they were weaker.

<sup>5</sup> Then he examined those half white and half black. Many of them turned out to be black. He ordered these too to be removed with the rejected. All the rest were lifted by the virgins; being white, they were fitted into the building by the virgins themselves. They were set farther out, because they were found sound, so that they could hold the ones set in the middle; for none of them was chipped at all.

<sup>6</sup> Then he examined the rough, hard ones. A few were rejected because they could not be cut—they proved extremely hard. The others were cut, taken up by the virgins, and fitted into the center of the tower’s structure, for they were weaker.

<sup>7</sup> Then he examined those with spots, and of these very few had turned black and were thrown out with the others. The rest were found bright and sound; these were fitted by the virgins into the building and set farther out, because of their strength.

## Chapter 86

<sup>1</sup> Then he came to examine the white and round stones, and said to me, “What should we do about these stones?” “What do I know, sir?” I said. “Do you have no idea about them?”

<sup>2</sup> “I do not have this skill, sir,” I said. “I am no stonecutter, and I cannot figure it out.” “Can you not see,” he said, “how very round they are? To make them square, a great deal would have to be chipped away; yet some of them have to be placed in the building.”

<sup>3</sup> “If it has to be done, sir,” I said, “why do you wear yourself out instead of picking the ones you like for the building and setting them in?” He selected the larger, shining ones from among them and cut them to size; the virgins picked them up and set them in the outer parts of the building.

<sup>4</sup> The remaining ones were lifted and returned to the plain where they had been brought from. They were not discarded, because, he said, “a little of the tower still has to be built. The tower’s lord wants every one of these stones fitted into the structure, since they are so very bright.”

<sup>5</sup> Twelve women were called, most beautiful in appearance, dressed in black, belted, with their shoulders bare and their hair loose. These women seemed wild to me. The shepherd ordered them to lift the stones rejected from the building and carry them off to the mountains from where they had been brought.

<sup>6</sup> They carried them away gladly, taking every one of the stones and putting them back where they had been taken from. When all the stones were gone and none lay around the tower any longer, the shepherd said to me, “Let us walk around the tower and check for any flaw in it.” And I walked around with him.

<sup>7</sup> When the shepherd saw the tower beautiful in its construction, he was very cheerful. For the tower was built so that I longed, on seeing it, to have built it myself: it was built as if from a single stone, without one joint in it. The stone looked as though it had been carved from the rock; it seemed to me to be a single piece.

## Chapter 87

<sup>1</sup> Walking with him, I was glad to see such good things. The shepherd said to me, “Go and bring lime and fine potsherds, so that I can fill in the traces of the stones that were taken out and put into the building. For everything around the tower must be made smooth.”

<sup>2</sup> I did as he said and brought them to him. “Lend a hand,” he said, “and the job will soon be done.” He filled in the marks left by the stones that had gone into the building, and ordered the ground around the tower to be swept and cleaned.

<sup>3</sup> The virgins took brooms and swept, removed all the rubbish from the tower, and sprinkled water, and the place around the tower became cheerful and very beautiful.

<sup>4</sup> The shepherd said to me, “Everything has been cleaned. If the lord comes to inspect the tower, he has nothing to blame us for.” After saying this he wanted to leave.

<sup>5</sup> I took hold of his bag and began to beg him, by the Lord, to explain to me what he had shown me. He said, “I have a little business to attend to, and then I will explain everything to you. Wait for me here until I come.”

<sup>6</sup> “But sir, what am I to do here by myself?” I asked. “You are not by yourself,” he said, “since these virgins are here with you.” “Then place me in their care,” I said. The shepherd called them and said, “I am leaving this man in your care until I return,” and he went away.

<sup>7</sup> I was alone with the virgins, and they were rather cheerful and kind to me, especially the four more glorious ones among them.

## Chapter 88

<sup>1</sup> “The shepherd will not come here today,” the virgins told me. “Then what am I to do?” I asked. “Wait for him till evening,” they said. “If he comes, he will speak with you; if he does not, you will stay here with us till he comes.”

<sup>2</sup> “I will wait for him until evening,” I told them, “but if he does not come, I will go home and be back in the morning.” “You were entrusted to us,” they answered, “and you cannot leave us.”

<sup>3</sup> “Where am I to stay, then?” I asked. “You will sleep among us,” they said, “as a brother and not as a husband; you are our brother, and we mean to live with you from now on, because we are very fond of you.” I was embarrassed to stay with them.

<sup>4</sup> The one who seemed the first of them began to kiss and embrace me. When the others saw her embracing me, they too began to kiss me, and led me around the tower, and played with me.

<sup>5</sup> I became like a younger man and began to play with them myself: some danced in a circle, some danced, some sang, while I walked silently with them around the tower and was cheerful with them.

<sup>6</sup> When evening came, I wanted to go home, but they would not let me; they held me back. I stayed with them through the night and slept beside the tower.

<sup>7</sup> The virgins laid their linen tunics out on the ground and had me lie down in the middle of them, and they did nothing but pray. I prayed along with them, ceaselessly, as much as they did. The virgins were glad to see me praying like this. And I stayed there with the virgins till the next morning, till the second hour.

<sup>8</sup> Then the shepherd arrived and asked the virgins, “You have not mistreated him in any way, have you?” “Ask him,” they said. “Sir,” I told him, “I was glad to stay with them.” “And what did you eat for supper?” “Sir, I feasted on the Lord’s words all night.” “Did they receive you well?” “Yes, sir,” I said.

<sup>9</sup> “Now, which do you want to hear first?” he asked. “The order in which you showed me things from the start, sir. I ask that as I question you, you explain to me in that order.” “As you like,” he said. “I will explain it to you in that way, and I will hide nothing at all from you.”

## Chapter 89

<sup>1</sup> “First of all, sir,” I said, “explain this to me: what are the rock and the gate?” “This rock and the gate,” he said, “are the Son of God.” “How, sir, is the rock old and the gate new?” “Listen,” he said, “and understand, you dull man.

<sup>2</sup> The Son of God is older than all his creation, so that he became the Father’s adviser in his creation. That is why the rock is old.” “But why is the gate new, sir?” I asked.

<sup>3</sup> “Because,” he said, “he appeared in the last days of the age. That is why the gate became new, so that those who are to be saved may enter through it into the kingdom of God.

<sup>4</sup> Did you notice,” he said, “that the stones that came through the gate were placed in the building of the tower, while those that did not come in were sent back to their own place?” “I did, sir,” I said. “Likewise,” he said, “nobody will enter God’s kingdom without taking his holy name.

<sup>5</sup> Imagine you wanted to go into a certain city, and that city was surrounded by a wall and had a single gate. Could you get into the city any way but by the gate it has?” “How else could it be done, sir?” I said. “Well, if you can enter the city only by its own gate,” he said, “then in the same way no one can go into the kingdom of God by any other route than the name of his beloved Son.

<sup>6</sup> Did you see the crowd building the tower?” “I saw, sir.” “They are all glorious angels,” he said; “the Lord is walled around by them. The gate is the Son of God. This is the one entrance to the Lord. No one, then, will enter to him in any other way except through his Son.

<sup>7</sup> Did you see the six men and the glorious, great man in their midst, who walked around the tower and rejected stones from the building?” “I saw, sir,” I said.

<sup>8</sup> “The glorious man,” he said, “is the Son of God, and the six who hold him up on his right and left are the glorious angels. Not one of these glorious angels will enter God’s presence without him. Whoever has not received his name will not enter the kingdom of God.”

## Chapter 90

<sup>1</sup> “And the tower, sir, what is it?” I asked. “This tower,” he said, “is the church.”

<sup>2</sup> “And who are these virgins?” “Holy spirits,” he said. “Nobody can be found in God’s kingdom any other way unless these virgins clothe that person with their garment. If you take only the name and do not also receive the garment from them, it will do you no good. These virgins are the powers of the Son of God. If you carry the name but not his power, you carry his name in vain.

<sup>3</sup> The stones you saw rejected,” he said, “wore the name but did not put on the clothing of the virgins.” “What is their clothing, sir?” “Their names themselves are their clothing,” he said. “Whoever wears the name of the Son of God must also wear the names of these virgins; for the Son himself wears the names of these virgins.

<sup>4</sup> All the stones you saw go into the building of the tower, handed over by their hands and remaining in the building, are clothed with the power of these virgins.

<sup>5</sup> This is why you see the tower as one stone together with the rock. So it is with those who have believed in the Lord through his Son and have been dressed in these spirits: they will be one spirit, one body, and their garments one color. The home of those who wear the virgins’ names is in the tower.”

<sup>6</sup> “Then why, sir, were the stones rejected? They passed through the gate and were put into the building of the tower by the hands of the virgins.” “Since you care about everything,” he said, “and examine it exactly, listen about the rejected stones.

<sup>7</sup> All of them took the name of the Son of God and also received the power of these virgins. With these spirits they grew strong and joined God’s slaves, and the whole group had one spirit, one body, and one garment: they thought alike and did what is right.

<sup>8</sup> After some time, however, they were persuaded by the women you saw dressed in black clothes, with bare shoulders and loose hair, and beautiful. When they saw these, they desired them and put on their power, and took off the garment and power of the virgins.

<sup>9</sup> So they were thrown out of the house of God and handed over to those women. But those who were not deceived by the beauty of these women stayed in the house of God. You now have the explanation of the rejected stones.”

## Chapter 91

<sup>1</sup> “And sir,” I asked, “if people like this repent and throw off their desires for those women, and return to the virgins and live by their power and their works, will they not enter the house of God?”

<sup>2</sup> “They will,” he said, “provided they discard the deeds of those women, take up the virgins’ power again, and live by their deeds. That, in fact, is why the building was halted: so that these people, if they repent, may be added to the tower. If they do not repent, others will go in, and these will be expelled for good.”

<sup>3</sup> For all this I thanked the Lord, who had compassion on everyone who calls on his name and sent the angel of repentance to us who had sinned against him; he renewed our spirit, and when we were already ruined with no hope of living, he gave our life a new start.

<sup>4</sup> “Now, sir,” I said, “tell me why the tower is not built on the ground but on the rock and on the gate.” “Are you still foolish and senseless?” he said. “I am obliged to ask you everything, sir,” I said, “because I cannot understand anything at all; for everything is great and glorious and hard for people to grasp.”

<sup>5</sup> “Hear this,” he said. “The name of the Son of God is great and cannot be contained, and it upholds the whole world. If the entire creation is upheld by the Son of God, what do you suppose he does for those he has called, who bear the Son of God’s name and walk by his commandments?

<sup>6</sup> You see, then, whom he supports: those who wear his name with their whole heart. He himself became their foundation, and he gladly supports them, because they are not ashamed to wear his name.”

## Chapter 92

<sup>1</sup> “Tell me, sir, the names of the virgins and of the women dressed in black clothes.” “Listen,” he said, “to the names of the stronger virgins, those standing at the corners.

<sup>2</sup> The first is Faith, the second Self-Control, the third Power, the fourth Patience. The ones between these are named Sincerity, Innocence, Purity, Cheerfulness, Truth, Understanding, Harmony, and Love. A person who bears these names together with the name of the Son of God can enter God’s kingdom.

<sup>3</sup> Listen also to the names of the women with black clothes. Four of these are stronger: the first is Unbelief, the second Lack of Self-Control, the third Disobedience, the fourth Deceit. Their followers are called Grief, Wickedness, Licentiousness, Short Temper, Falsehood, Foolishness, Slander, Hatred. A slave of God who wears these names will see the kingdom of God but will not enter it.”

<sup>4</sup> “And the stones, sir, that were fitted from the deep into the building—who are they?” “The first ten, which were laid in the foundations,” he said, “are the first generation; the twenty-five are a second generation of righteous men; the thirty-five are the prophets of God and his ministers; and the forty are the apostles and teachers who proclaimed the Son of God.”

<sup>5</sup> “Why, then, sir, did the virgins hand these stones into the building of the tower too, after carrying them through the gate?”

<sup>6</sup> “It is because they were the first to wear these spirits,” he said, “and they never parted from each other at all: the spirits did not leave the people, nor the people the spirits, but the spirits stayed with them until they died. Without these spirits with them, they would have been of no use for the construction of this tower.”

## Chapter 93

<sup>1</sup> “Tell me more, sir.” “What are you asking?” he said. “Why, sir, did the stones come up from the deep and were put into the building of the tower, though they had worn these spirits?”

<sup>2</sup> “They had no choice but to come up through water,” he said, “to be made alive; there was no other way for them to enter God’s kingdom unless they laid aside the deadness of their former life.

<sup>3</sup> So these who had fallen asleep also received the seal of the Son of God and entered the kingdom of God. For before a person wears the name of the Son of God, he is dead; but when he receives the seal, he puts away deadness and takes up life.

<sup>4</sup> The seal, then, is the water[^93-4-1]. Into the water people go down dead, and they come up alive. To those, too, this seal was proclaimed, and they used it, in order to enter the kingdom of God.”

<sup>5</sup> “And the forty stones, sir—why did they also come up from the deep with them, when they already had the seal?” “It was because these apostles and teachers, who announced the name of the Son of God, had died in his power and faith, and they preached to those who had died before them, and handed them the seal of the preaching themselves.

<sup>6</sup> So they went down into the water with them and came up again. But these went down alive and came up alive, while those who had fallen asleep earlier went down dead and came up alive.

<sup>7</sup> By means of these, then, they were brought to life and recognized the name of the Son of God. This is also why they came up together with them and were joined to the tower’s structure, built in without being cut; they had died in righteousness and great purity. The one thing they lacked was this seal. You now have the explanation about them too.” “I do, sir,” I said.

## Chapter 94

<sup>1</sup> “Now, sir, tell me about the mountains: why are their appearances different and varied?” “Listen,” he said. “These twelve mountains are tribes that inhabit the whole world. The Son of God was proclaimed to them through the apostles.”

<sup>2</sup> “But why are the mountains varied, each with a different appearance? Tell me, sir.” “Listen,” he said. “These twelve tribes that inhabit the whole world are twelve nations. They differ in outlook and in mind. As you saw the mountains varied, so are the varieties of mind and outlook among the nations. I will explain to you the conduct of each one.”

<sup>3</sup> “First, sir, tell me this: why, though the mountains are so varied, when their stones were put into the building they all became bright with a single color, like the stones that came up from the deep?”

<sup>4</sup> “Because all the nations under heaven,” he said, “on hearing and believing, were called by the name of the Son of God. Once they received the seal, they shared a single outlook and mind; one faith and one love became theirs, and together with the name they put on the virgins’ spirits. Hence the structure of the tower took on one color, shining like the sun.

<sup>5</sup> But after they came together and became one body, some of them defiled themselves and were thrown out of the race of the righteous and became again what they had been before, or even worse.”

## Chapter 95

<sup>1</sup> “How, sir, did they become worse, when they had come to know God?” “A person who does not know God and does wrong,” he said, “gets some punishment for his wrongdoing; but one who has come to know God ought no longer to do wrong but to do good.

<sup>2</sup> If a person who is obliged to do good does evil instead, does he not seem to do more evil than one who does not know God? That is why people who never knew God and do evil are sentenced to death, while those who have known God, have seen his mighty acts, and still do evil will be punished twice over and die forever. In this way the church of God will be purified.

<sup>3</sup> You saw stones lifted out of the tower, delivered to the evil spirits, and expelled from it. ‘There will be one body of the cleansed, just as the tower, once cleaned, came to look as though made from one stone’: that is how the church of God will be once it is purified and the wicked, the hypocrites, the blasphemers, the double-minded, and those who commit various kinds of evil have been thrown out.

<sup>4</sup> When these have been expelled, the church of God will be a single body, with one mind, one outlook, one faith, one love. Then the Son of God will rejoice and be delighted in them, having got his people back clean.” “Sir,” I said, “it is all so great and glorious.

<sup>5</sup> Tell me more, sir, about the strength and the doings of each mountain, so that every soul that trusts in the Lord may hear and glorify his great, marvelous, and glorious name.” “Listen,” he said, “to the variety of the mountains, that is, of the twelve nations.

## Chapter 96

<sup>1</sup> From the first mountain, the black one, those who believed are like this: deserters, those who blaspheme the Lord, and those who betray God’s slaves. For these there is no repentance, but only death; and that is why they are black, for their kind too is lawless.

<sup>2</sup> From the second mountain, the barren one, those who believed are like this: hypocrites and teachers of wickedness. They resemble the first group, having no fruit of righteousness. Just as their mountain bears nothing, so such people have a name but no faith inside, and the fruit of truth is absent from them. For these repentance is available, if they act quickly; if they dawdle, they will share the death of the first.”

<sup>3</sup> “Why, sir,” I asked, “do these have a chance of repentance and not the first group? Their deeds are almost the same.” “The reason repentance is set aside for these,” he said, “is that they never blasphemed their Lord or turned traitor to God’s slaves. They played the hypocrite for the sake of profit, and each one taught what fit the cravings of sinful people. They will pay some penalty, but repentance is kept for them because they did not become blasphemers or traitors.

## Chapter 97

<sup>1</sup> From the third mountain, the one covered with thorns and thistles, those who believed are like this: some are rich, others are entangled in many business concerns. The thistles are the rich, and the thorns are those caught up in all manner of business.

<sup>2</sup> These, then, who are entangled in many and varied business affairs, do not attach themselves to God’s slaves, but wander off, choked by their activities. The rich attach themselves only with difficulty to God’s slaves, afraid that they will be asked for something. People like this will enter the kingdom of God only with difficulty.

<sup>3</sup> For just as it is hard to walk barefoot among thistles, so it is hard for such people to enter the kingdom of God.

<sup>4</sup> Even for all these there is repentance, but it must be quick, so that the good they did not do in earlier times they may now catch up on in the days remaining, by doing something good. If they repent and do good, they will live for God; but if they hold to their conduct, they will be given over to those women, who will kill them.

## Chapter 98

<sup>1</sup> From the fourth mountain, the one with many plants, green at the top but dry near the roots, some withered by the sun, those who believed are of this kind: some are double-minded, and some have the Lord on their lips but not in their hearts.

<sup>2</sup> That is why their foundations are dry and powerless: only their words live, while their deeds are dead. People like this are neither alive nor dead. They resemble the double-minded, who are likewise neither green nor dry, because they are neither alive nor dead.

<sup>3</sup> As these plants withered when they met the sun, so the double-minded, as soon as they hear of tribulation, worship idols out of cowardice and are ashamed of their Lord’s name.

<sup>4</sup> Such people are neither alive nor dead. But they too can live, if they repent promptly; if they do not repent, they have already been given over to the women who carry off their life.

## Chapter 99

<sup>1</sup> From the fifth mountain, the one with green plants that is rugged, those who believed are of this kind: they are faithful, but slow to learn, headstrong, pleased with themselves, wanting to know everything and knowing nothing at all.

<sup>2</sup> Because of this headstrong attitude, understanding departed from them, and a stupid folly entered them. They praise themselves as people of understanding and want to be self-appointed teachers, although they are fools.

<sup>3</sup> Because of this arrogance many have been emptied out, exalting themselves. For self-will and empty confidence are a great demon. Many of them were thrown out, but some repented, believed, and submitted themselves to those with understanding, recognizing their own folly.

<sup>4</sup> And for the rest of such people repentance is set: they did not become wicked, but foolish and senseless. If they repent, they will live for God; but if they do not repent, they will live with the women who work evil against them.

## Chapter 100

<sup>1</sup> Those from the sixth mountain, the one with large and small cracks and withered plants in the cracks, who believed, are of this kind.

<sup>2</sup> Those with the small cracks are people who hold things against one another, and from their slanders they have withered in the faith; but many of them have repented. The rest will repent when they hear my commandments, for their slanders are small, and they will repent quickly.

<sup>3</sup> Those with the large cracks persist in their slanders and become resentful, holding grudges against one another. These were thrown away from the tower and rejected from its building. People like this will hardly live.

<sup>4</sup> If God, our Lord, who is lord of all and has authority over all his creation, bears no grudge against those who confess their sins but is merciful, can a mortal man full of sins hold a grudge against another man, as though he could destroy or save him?

<sup>5</sup> I say to you, the angel of repentance: all of you who hold this attitude, put it away and repent, and the Lord will heal your earlier sins, if you cleanse yourselves of this demon; otherwise you will be handed over to it for death.

## Chapter 101

<sup>1</sup> From the seventh mountain, on which there were green and cheerful plants, the whole mountain flourishing, and every kind of cattle and the birds of the sky fed on the plants on this mountain, and the plants they fed on grew more luxuriant, those who believed are of this kind.

<sup>2</sup> They were always sincere, guileless, and blessed, with nothing against one another, but constantly delighting in God’s slaves, dressed in the holy spirit of these virgins, always compassionate toward everyone, and out of their own labor they gave to every person with no reproach and no hesitation.

<sup>3</sup> The Lord, then, seeing their sincerity and complete childlikeness, increased them in the labors of their hands and showed them favor in all they did.

<sup>4</sup> I say to you who are like this, I the angel of repentance: remain like this, and your offspring will never be wiped out. For the Lord has tested you and written you into our number, and all your offspring will live with the Son of God; for you received from his Spirit.

## Chapter 102

<sup>1</sup> From the eighth mountain, where the many springs were and all the Lord’s creation was watered from the springs, those who believed are of this kind:

<sup>2</sup> apostles and teachers who proclaimed the message to the entire world and taught the Lord’s word with dignity and purity, who never misappropriated anything for evil desire, but always lived in righteousness and truth, exactly as they received the Holy Spirit. The passing of such people is with the angels.

## Chapter 103

<sup>1</sup> From the ninth mountain, the desert one, which had in it the reptiles and wild animals that destroy people, those who believed are of this kind:

<sup>2</sup> The ones with spots are ministers who served badly, who robbed widows and orphans of their livelihood and enriched themselves from the ministry they were given to perform. If they stick to the same craving, they are dead and have no hope of life; but if they turn around and complete their ministry in purity, they can live.

<sup>3</sup> The scabby ones are those who denied and did not return to their Lord, but became barren and desolate. Not attaching themselves to God’s slaves, but living apart, they destroy their own souls.

<sup>4</sup> For as a vine left in a fence, if it is neglected, is ruined and laid waste by weeds and in time becomes wild, and is no longer useful to its owner, so such people have given up on themselves and become useless to their Lord, having turned wild.

<sup>5</sup> For these, repentance is possible, unless it turns out they denied from the heart; but if someone is found to have denied from the heart, I am unsure whether he can live.

<sup>6</sup> I am not saying this about the present time, as though someone who denies might receive repentance; for it is impossible for a person who is now about to deny his Lord to be saved. But repentance appears to be set aside for those who denied him long ago. If anyone intends to repent, then, he should hurry, before the tower is finished; otherwise those women will ruin him, to his death.

<sup>7</sup> And the chipped ones are deceitful slanderers, and they are the wild animals you saw on the mountain. For just as wild animals destroy and kill a person with their poison, so the words of such people destroy and kill a person.

<sup>8</sup> So they are crippled in their faith by the way they live inwardly. Some have repented and been saved. The others of this sort can be saved too, if they repent; if they do not, they will die by the hands of those women whose strength they carry.

## Chapter 104

<sup>1</sup> From the tenth mountain, where there were trees sheltering some sheep, those who believed are of this kind:

<sup>2</sup> overseers and hospitable people[^104-2-1], who always gladly welcomed God’s slaves into their homes without hypocrisy. The overseers, for their part, always shielded those in need and the widows, unceasingly, by their service, and always lived in purity.

<sup>3</sup> The Lord will shelter all of these forever. Those who have acted this way stand in glory before God, and their place is with the angels already, provided they keep serving the Lord to the very end.

## Chapter 105

<sup>1</sup> From the eleventh mountain, where there were trees full of fruit, each adorned with a different fruit, those who believed are of this kind:

<sup>2</sup> those who suffered for the name of the Son of God, who suffered willingly with their whole heart and gave up their lives.

<sup>3</sup> “But sir,” I asked, “why do all the trees bear fruit, and yet some trees’ fruits are finer?” “Listen,” he said. “Everyone who has ever suffered for the name stands in glory with God, and the sins of every one of them were removed, because they suffered for the name of the Son of God. Now hear why their fruits differ, and why some excel.

<sup>4</sup> All who were brought before the authorities, were questioned, and did not deny, but suffered willingly, are more glorious before the Lord; theirs is the surpassing fruit. But those who were cowardly and hesitant, and weighed in their hearts whether they would deny or confess, and suffered—their fruits are smaller, because this thought came up in their hearts. For it is an evil thought, that a slave should deny his own lord.

<sup>5</sup> Watch out, then, you who entertain such thoughts, lest this thought remain in your hearts and you die to God. You who suffer for the name ought to glorify God, because God considered you worthy to bear this name and to have all your sins healed.

<sup>6</sup> So you should consider yourselves blessed; yet you suppose that suffering for God is some great achievement. The Lord is giving you life as a gift, and you fail to see it. Your sins weighed heavily on you, and if you had not suffered for the Lord’s name, you would be dead to God because of your sins.

<sup>7</sup> I say this to you who hesitate between denying and confessing: confess that you have a Lord, lest by denying him you be handed over to prison.

<sup>8</sup> If the gentiles punish their slaves whenever one denies his master, what do you think the Lord will do to you, who has authority over all? Remove these thoughts from your hearts, so that you may live for God forever.”

## Chapter 106

<sup>1</sup> From the twelfth mountain, the white one, those who believed are of this kind: they are like newborn infants, in whose hearts no evil arises, who do not even know what wickedness is, but have always remained as children.

<sup>2</sup> Such people will certainly live in the kingdom of God, because in no matter did they defile the commandments of God, but remained childlike all the days of their lives in the same frame of mind.

<sup>3</sup> All of you, then, who remain,” he said, “and are like infants[^106-3-1], with no malice, will be more glorious than all those mentioned before. For all infants are glorious before God and first with him. Blessed are you, then, whoever removes wickedness from yourselves and puts on innocence: you will be the first of all to live for God.”

<sup>4</sup> When he had finished the parables about the mountains, I said to him, “Sir, now explain what happened with the stones taken from the plain and put into the building to stand in for the ones taken from the tower, and with the round stones that went into the building, and those that are still round.”

## Chapter 107

<sup>1</sup> “Listen,” he said, “about all these as well. The stones taken from the plain and put into the building of the tower in place of the rejected ones are the roots of the white mountain.

<sup>2</sup> Because those who had believed on the white mountain were all found innocent, the lord of the tower commanded that these, taken from the roots of that mountain, be placed in the tower’s structure. He knew that once these stones were built into the tower, they would remain bright and not one of them would go black.

<sup>3</sup> But if he had added[^107-3-1] stones from the other mountains, he would have had to visit that tower again and purify it. All of these were found bright, those who believed and those who are going to believe[^107-3-2]; for they are of the same race. Blessed is this race, because it is innocent.

<sup>4</sup> Now listen about the round and bright stones. They too are all from the white mountain. Hear why they were found round[^107-4-1]: their riches darkened them a little and clouded them away from the truth; yet they never departed from the true God, and no evil word came from their mouths, but only all fairness and the virtue of truth.

<sup>5</sup> When the Lord observed their disposition—that they were able to side with the truth and also stay good—he commanded that their riches be pared down, but not removed completely, so that they could do some good with what remained to them and live for God, since they come from a good stock. That is why they were trimmed a little and set in the structure of this tower.

## Chapter 108

<sup>1</sup> The rest, who remained round and were not fitted into that structure, because they had not yet received the seal[^108-1-1], were put back in their place; for they were found very round.

<sup>2</sup> This age and the emptiness of their riches must be trimmed away from them, and then they will fit into the kingdom of God. They must enter the kingdom of God; the Lord has blessed this innocent race, and no one of this race will perish. For even if one of them is tempted by the wicked devil and commits some fault, he will run back quickly to his Lord.

<sup>3</sup> I count you all happy, I the angel of repentance, whoever of you are innocent as infants, for your portion is good and honored before God.

<sup>4</sup> And I say to all of you who have received this seal: keep sincerity, do not be mindful of offenses or persist in your malice or in the remembrance of the bitterness of offenses; become of one spirit, and heal and remove from yourselves these evil divisions, so that the lord of the sheep may rejoice over them.

<sup>5</sup> He will rejoice[^108-5-1] if everything is found sound and none of them has fallen away. But if some of them are found fallen away, woe to the shepherds.

<sup>6</sup> And if the shepherds themselves turn out to have fallen, what will they tell the owner of the flock? That the sheep made them fall? No one will believe them; it is incredible that a shepherd should be harmed by sheep. They will be punished instead for lying. And I am the shepherd[^108-6-1], and I will have to answer most strictly for you.

## Chapter 109

<sup>1</sup> Mend yourselves, then, while the tower is still being built.

<sup>2</sup> The Lord lives among people who love peace; for peace is dear to him, but from the quarrelsome and those lost to malice he is far away. Give him back, then, the spirit whole, just as you received it.

<sup>3</sup> Suppose you handed a launderer a new, undamaged garment and expected to get it back undamaged, and the launderer returned it to you torn. Would you take it? Would you not lose your temper at once and chase him with insults—‘I gave you an undamaged garment; why did you rip it and ruin it? Because of the rip you made, it is no longer usable’? Would you not say all this to a launderer about the rip he made in your clothing?

<sup>4</sup> If you grieve over your garment like this and complain that you do not receive it back whole, what do you think the Lord will do to you, who gave you a whole spirit, and you made it completely useless, so that it can be of no use to its master? For its usefulness began to be nil, since it was ruined by you. Will not the Lord of that spirit, then, put you to death for this deed of yours?

<sup>5</sup> “Of course,” I said, “he will strike down everyone he finds dwelling on offenses.” “Do not tread on his mercy,” he said, “but give him honor instead, since he is so patient with your wrongs and is nothing like you. Repent in a way that helps you.

## Chapter 110

<sup>1</sup> All these things written above, I the shepherd, the angel of repentance, have shown and told to God’s slaves. If you believe, then, and listen to my words and walk in them and correct your ways, you will be able to live. But if you persist in malice and in remembering offenses, no one of that kind will live for God. All these things I had to say have been said to you.”

<sup>2</sup> The shepherd himself said to me, “Have you asked me everything?” I said, “Yes, sir.” “Why then did you not ask me about the shape of the stones placed in the structure, how we filled in the shapes?” I said, “I forgot, sir.”

<sup>3</sup> “Hear now,” he said, “about them. They are those who have now heard my commandments and repented with their whole heart. When the Lord saw that their repentance was good and pure, and that they could remain in it, he ordered their earlier sins to be wiped out. These shapes were their sins, and they were smoothed over, so that they would not show.”

## Chapter 111

<sup>1</sup> After I had finished writing this book[^111-1-1], the angel who had handed me over to this shepherd came to the house where I was and sat down on the couch, while this shepherd stood at his right. Then he called me and said these things to me:

<sup>2</sup> “I handed you and your household over to this shepherd, so that he might look after you.” “Yes, sir,” I said. “If you wish to be shielded from all harassment and all cruelty,” he said, “and to prosper in every good work and word and every virtuous act of fairness, then follow the commandments I gave you through him, and you will be able to overcome all wickedness.

<sup>3</sup> If you keep his commandments, every desire and the sweetness of this age will fall under your control, and success in every worthy undertaking will follow you. Take on his gravity and modesty, and tell everyone that he is highly honored and respected by the Lord, a ruler of great authority, powerful in his task. He alone, in the whole world, has been given authority over repentance. Does he strike you as powerful? And yet you look down on the gravity and restraint he has toward you.”

## Chapter 112

<sup>1</sup> I said to him, “Ask him yourself, sir, whether, since he has been in my house, I have done anything out of order by which I have offended him.”

<sup>2</sup> “I too am aware,” he said, “that you have done nothing out of order, and you never will. I tell you this so that you will persevere. For he thinks well of you when he talks with me. Now you must pass these words on to others, so that those who have repented or are about to repent may think as you do, and he may speak well of them to me, and I to the Lord.”

<sup>3</sup> “And I, sir,” I said, “tell everyone of the Lord’s great deeds. I hope that all who sinned before, if they hear these things, will gladly repent and regain life.”

<sup>4</sup> “Keep at this ministry, then, and bring it to completion,” he said. “All who carry out his commands will have life and high honor with the Lord. But all who do not observe his commands run away from their own life and set themselves against him; they do not follow his commands but give themselves to death, and each one becomes liable for his own blood. As for you, I say: serve these commands, and you will find a cure for your sins.

## Chapter 113

<sup>1</sup> I have sent you these virgins to stay with you, because I saw that they are friendly to you. So you have them as helpers, to make it easier for you to keep his commandments; for these commandments cannot be kept without these virgins. I can see that they like being with you, but I will tell them never to leave your house.

<sup>2</sup> Only keep your house clean, for they gladly live in a clean house. They are pure, chaste, hardworking, and all of them are in favor with the Lord. If they find your house spotless, they will stay with you; but at the slightest hint of defilement they will quit your house immediately, for these virgins have no love at all for pollution.”

<sup>3</sup> I said to him, “I hope, sir, to please them, so that they will always gladly live in my house. And just as this man[^113-3-1], to whom you handed me over, does not blame me, neither will they blame me.”

<sup>4</sup> He said to the shepherd, “I know that the slave of God wants to live and will keep these commandments and will keep the virgins in purity.”

<sup>5</sup> Having said this, he handed me over to the shepherd again and called the virgins. “You obviously like living[^113-5-1] in this man’s house,” he told them, “so I put him and his household in your charge: never leave his house.” They accepted these words gladly.

## Chapter 114

<sup>1</sup> Then he said to me, “Conduct yourself bravely in this ministry; make known to every person the Lord’s great deeds, and you will have favor in this ministry. Whoever walks in these commandments will live and be happy in his life; but whoever neglects them will not live and will be unhappy in his life.

<sup>2</sup> Tell everyone not to cease, whoever can do right; it is beneficial for them to practice good works. And I say that every person ought to be rescued from hardships. For the one who is in need and suffers hardships in daily life is in great torment and distress.

<sup>3</sup> Whoever, then, rescues a soul like this from distress gains great joy for himself. For the one who is troubled by such hardship is tortured with a torment equal to that of someone in chains. Many, because of such calamities, when they cannot bear them, bring death on themselves. Whoever knows the calamity of such a person and does not rescue him commits a great sin and becomes guilty of his blood.

<sup>4</sup> Do good works, then, you who have received gifts from the Lord, so that the building of the tower is not completed while you delay. The work of building it has been held up because of you. Unless you hurry to do what is right, the tower will be finished, and you will be shut out.”

<sup>5</sup> After he had spoken with me, he rose from the couch, took the shepherd and the virgins, and left, telling me that he would send that shepherd and the virgins back to my house.
