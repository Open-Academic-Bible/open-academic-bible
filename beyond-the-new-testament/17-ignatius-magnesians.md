# Ignatius to the Magnesians

<sup>1</sup> From Ignatius, who is also Theophorus[^0-1-1], to her who has been blessed by the favor of God the Father in our Savior Christ Jesus. In him I salute the church seated in Magnesia by the Meander, and I pray that she will have a rich measure of joy in God the Father and in Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Having seen how well ordered your love toward God is, I rejoiced and decided to talk to you in the faith of Jesus Christ.

<sup>2</sup> I have been honored with a name most fitting for God, and in the chains I carry I sing the praise of the churches. I pray that in them there will be a union of the flesh and spirit of Jesus Christ, our life for all time, and a union of faith and love, which nothing surpasses; and, more important still, a union of Jesus and the Father. If we endure in him all the abuse of this age’s ruler and escape it, we will reach God.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> I have had the honor of seeing you[^2-1-1] through Damas, your bishop (a man worthy of God), through the worthy presbyters Bassus and Apollonius, and through my fellow slave Zotion, a deacon. May I benefit from him! He is subject to the bishop as to God’s grace, and to the presbytery as to the law of Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> You also ought not to treat the bishop’s age lightly[^3-1-1]. Give him complete respect, in line with the power of God the Father. I know that the holy presbyters too have not accepted the seeming youthfulness of his rank but, as people with wisdom in God, give way to him: not to him, really, but to the Father of Jesus Christ, the bishop of everyone.

<sup>2</sup> In honor of the one who wanted us, then, it is fitting to obey without any pretense. For no one deceives this visible bishop; rather, a person cheats the invisible one. In such a case the reckoning is not with flesh but with God, who knows what is hidden.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> It is fitting not only to be called Christians but to be Christians, as some call a man bishop and then do everything apart from him. People like that do not seem to me to have a clear conscience, because they do not meet on a secure footing[^4-1-1], in line with the commandment.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> Because affairs are heading toward an end, and two things lie before us at once, death and life, and each person will proceed to the place that is their own,

<sup>2</sup> there are two kinds of currency, God’s and the world’s, and each carries its own imprint. The unbelieving carry this world’s imprint, but the faithful carry, in love, the imprint of God the Father through Jesus Christ. Unless we are willing, of our own choice, to die into his passion[^5-2-1] through him, his life is not in us.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> Having observed the entire community in faith in the people named above, and having come to love them, I urge you to strive to do all things in godly harmony. The bishop should preside in God’s place, the presbyters in the place of the apostolic council, and the deacons, who are my dearest friends, have been trusted with the service of Jesus Christ, who was beside the Father before the ages and was shown at the end.

<sup>2</sup> All of you, then, having taken on God’s character, show respect to one another, and let no one look at a neighbor in a merely human way. Instead, love one another always in Jesus Christ. Let nothing exist among you that could divide you; unite with the bishop and with those who preside, as a model and a lesson in imperishability.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> As the Lord, who is one with the Father, did nothing apart from him, neither by himself nor through the apostles, so you should do nothing without the bishop and the presbyters. And do not attempt to let anything look sensible when you act alone. Come together instead: one prayer, one request, one mind, one hope, in love and in the flawless joy that is Jesus Christ; nothing is better than him.

<sup>2</sup> Hurry, every one of you, as to a single temple of God, a single altar, one Jesus Christ, who came from one Father, remained with one, and went back to one.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> Do not be misled by teachings of a different sort, or by ancient myths that are good for nothing. If we still live in line with Judaism, we are confessing that we have not received grace.

<sup>2</sup> The godliest prophets lived according to Christ Jesus; that is why they were also hunted down. Breathing in his grace, they set out to persuade the disobedient that God is one, who has made himself known by Jesus Christ his Son. This Son is his Word, issuing from silence[^8-2-1], and in all respects he satisfied the one who sent him.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> So if people who had lived among old customs arrived at a fresh hope, having stopped keeping sabbath and now living by the Lord’s day[^9-1-1], the day on which our life rose up through him and through his death, which some deny; by that mystery we gained faith, and it is why we hold out, so as to be discovered as disciples of Jesus Christ, our one teacher,

<sup>2</sup> how could we possibly live without him? Even the prophets were his disciples in the Spirit, and they looked for him as their teacher. And so the one they were rightly waiting for raised them from the dead when he came.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> We must not be dull to his goodness. If he treated us the way we act, we would be finished. So, as his disciples, let us learn to live by the standard of Christianity; anyone called by a name beyond this one does not belong to God.

<sup>2</sup> Set aside, then, the bad yeast[^10-2-1] that has grown old and sour, and change to new yeast, which is Jesus Christ. Be salted[^10-2-2] in him, so that none among you spoils, for you will be tested by your smell.

<sup>3</sup> Talking about Jesus Christ while living as a Jew[^10-3-1] is out of place. Christianity did not put its trust in Judaism; Judaism put its trust in Christianity, into which every tongue that believed in God was gathered.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> I am not saying these things, dear friends, because I have found that any of you are this way. Being less than you, I simply want to keep you from stumbling onto the hooks of vain glory, and to assure you fully about the birth, the suffering, and the resurrection that occurred when Pontius Pilate was governing. These events truly and certainly happened through Jesus Christ, our hope; may none of you ever swerve from it.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> May I take pleasure in you in all respects, provided I am worthy. Though I am bound, I cannot compare with any one of you who are set free. I know you are not conceited; Jesus Christ is in you. And I know that you are the more embarrassed whenever I praise you, as scripture says: “The just person is his own accuser[^12-1-1].”

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> So take care to be made secure in the teachings of the Lord and the apostles, so that all you undertake may go well[^13-1-1], in body and spirit, faith and love, Son and Father and Spirit, from start to finish, along with your highly respected bishop, your presbytery (a beautifully braided spiritual wreath), and your deacons who live for God.

<sup>2</sup> Be subject to the bishop and to each other, as Jesus Christ was to the Father, and the apostles to Christ and the Father, so that there may be unity of body and of spirit.

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> I have kept my appeal short because I know you are filled with God. Keep me in mind when you pray, so that I may attain God, and the church in Syria too, whose name I am not fit to bear. I rely on your joint prayer and love, so that the church in Syria may deserve to be watered with dew by your church.

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> The Ephesians send greetings from Smyrna, where I am writing; they are here to give glory to God, as you are, and together with Polycarp, the bishop of the Smyrnaeans, they have given me rest in every way. Greetings also from the rest of the churches, in honor of Jesus Christ. Be well in God’s harmony, possessing the spirit that cannot be divided, who is Jesus Christ.
