# Epistle of Barnabas

An anonymous treatise later ascribed to Barnabas, Paul's companion, probably written in Alexandria around 130. It reads the Jewish scriptures allegorically and argues that their true meaning belongs to Christians; it ends with its own version of the "two ways." It is copied immediately after Revelation in Codex Sinaiticus (fourth century) and appears in the list of New Testament books in Codex Claromontanus. Clement of Alexandria quotes it as the work of "the apostle Barnabas"; Eusebius counts it among the disputed books.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Greetings to you, sons and daughters, in the name of the Lord who loved us—in peace.

<sup>2</sup> God’s ordinances[^1-2-1] toward you are so great and so rich that I rejoice beyond measure, over and above all bounds, over your blessed and glorious spirits. Such a deeply rooted gift of spiritual grace you have received!

<sup>3</sup> For this reason I take even greater joy in myself, as I hope to be saved, because I truly see the Spirit poured out on you from the rich fountain of the Lord. That is how much the sight of you, so longed for by me, took me by surprise.

<sup>4</sup> I am persuaded of this and aware in myself that, having spoken among you, I understand many things,[^1-4-1] since the Lord has journeyed with me on the road of righteousness. So I too am wholly compelled to love you more than my own life, because great faith and love dwell in you, in the hope of his life.[^1-4-2]

<sup>5</sup> Having reasoned, then, that if I take the trouble to share with you some part of what I received, it will bring me a reward for serving such spirits, I have hurried to send you a little at a time,[^1-5-1] so that together with your faith you may have perfect knowledge.

<sup>6</sup> There are, then, three teachings[^1-6-1] of the Lord: the hope of life, which is the beginning and the end of our faith; righteousness, which is the beginning and the end of judgment; and love shown in joy and gladness, the witness of deeds done in righteousness.

<sup>7</sup> For the Master has made known to us through the prophets the things that are past and the things that are now, and has given us a first taste of what is to come. As we see each of these things take effect one by one, just as he said, we ought to bring a fuller and loftier gift[^1-7-1] in the fear of him.

<sup>8</sup> I myself will point out a few things to you, not as a teacher but as one of you, by which you will be cheered in the present circumstances.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> Since the days are evil and the one who is at work[^2-1-1] holds the power, we ought to give heed to ourselves and search out the ordinances of the Lord.

<sup>2</sup> Fear and endurance, then, are the helpers of our faith, and patience and self-control are our allies.

<sup>3</sup> While these remain pure in what concerns the Lord, wisdom, understanding, insight, and knowledge rejoice together with them.

<sup>4</sup> For he has made clear to us through all the prophets that he has no need of sacrifices or whole burnt offerings or offerings. At one point he says:

<sup>5</sup> “Your many sacrifices—what are they to me?” says the Lord. “I have had my fill of whole burnt offerings; lambs’ fat and the blood of bulls and goats are not what I desire—not even when you come to show yourselves to me. Who asked you for these, from your own hands? You will never again set foot in my courtyard. Bring me fine flour, and it is worthless; incense is hateful to me; your new moons and your sabbaths I will not put up with.”[^2-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> These things, then, he has abolished, so that the new law of our Lord Jesus Christ, which is free from the yoke of compulsion, might have an offering that human beings did not make.

<sup>7</sup> And again he says to them, “Did I command your ancestors, when they were leaving the land of Egypt, to bring me whole burnt offerings and sacrifices?

<sup>8</sup> No, this is what I commanded them: let none of you hold a grudge in your heart against your neighbor, and do not love a false oath.”[^2-8-1]

<sup>9</sup> We ought therefore to grasp, if we are not senseless, the purpose behind the goodness of our Father. He speaks to us because he wants us not to go astray as they did, but to seek how we may draw near to him.

<sup>10</sup> So to us he says: “A sacrifice to the Lord is a broken heart;[^2-10-1] a fragrant aroma to the Lord is a heart that glorifies the one who made it.”[^2-10-2] We ought, then, brothers and sisters, to be most careful about our salvation, so that the evil one, having slipped error in among us, may not fling us away from our life.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> He speaks to them again about these matters: “Why are you fasting for me,” says the Lord, “so that your voice is heard crying today? That is not the fast I selected,” says the Lord—“a person afflicting their own soul,

<sup>2</sup> not even if you bend your neck like a hoop, put on sackcloth, and spread ashes beneath you. Not even so will you call it an acceptable fast.”[^3-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> But to us he says: “Look, here is the fast I chose,” the Lord says: “Release every bond of wrongdoing, untie the knots of oppressive contracts, set the crushed free, and rip up every dishonest record. Divide your bread with the starving; if you spot someone naked, give them clothing; welcome the homeless into your home, and when you notice someone lowly, do not look down on them, nor turn away from your own family.

<sup>4</sup> Then your light will burst out early, your garments will quickly spring up,[^3-4-1] your righteousness will go ahead of you, and God’s glory will surround you.

<sup>5</sup> Then you will call, and God will answer you; before you have finished speaking he will say, ‘Look, here I am’—if you remove from yourself the chain, the pointing finger,[^3-5-1] and the grumbling word, and give your bread to the hungry from your own soul, and show pity to a soul brought low.”[^3-5-2]

<sup>6</sup> For this reason, brothers and sisters, the one who is patient, foreseeing that the people he prepared in his beloved would believe in sincerity, showed us everything in advance, so that we might not be dashed to pieces as newcomers to their law.[^3-6-1]

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> We must therefore dig deeply into present circumstances and search for whatever can save us. Let us flee entirely from all works of lawlessness, so that those works do not catch up with us, and let us despise the error of the present time, that we may be loved in the time to come.

<sup>2</sup> Let us not give our souls such slack that they have the freedom to run along with sinners and evil people, lest we become like them.

<sup>3</sup> The ultimate stumbling block[^4-3-1] has come near, of which it is written, as Enoch says:[^4-3-2] “For to this end the Master has cut short the times and the days, so that his beloved may hurry and come into his inheritance.”

<sup>4</sup> The prophet also speaks in this way: “Ten kingdoms will reign on the earth, and behind them a little king will arise, who will humble three of the kings at once.”[^4-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> Daniel likewise says about the same matter: “And I looked at the fourth beast, evil and powerful and harsher than every beast of the sea; ten horns grew out of it, and out of these a little horn as a side shoot, and it brought low three of the great horns at once.”[^4-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> You ought to understand this. And I ask you also, as one of you, and especially because I love you all more than my own soul: pay attention to yourselves now, and do not be made like certain people by piling up your sins and saying that the covenant is both theirs and ours.

<sup>7</sup> Ours, indeed—but they lost it for good, and in this way, even after Moses had received it. The scripture says: “Moses stayed on the mountain, fasting for forty days and forty nights, and he took the covenant from the Lord: stone tablets inscribed by the finger of the Lord’s hand.”[^4-7-1]

<sup>8</sup> But they went back to the idols and lost it. For the Lord says it this way: “Moses, Moses, go down quickly; the people you brought from the land of Egypt have broken the law.”[^4-8-1] Moses realized it and threw the two tablets from his hands; their covenant was shattered, so that the covenant of the beloved Jesus could be sealed within our hearts, on the hope of faith in him.

<sup>9</sup> I want to write many things to you, not as a teacher but as befits a person who loves you, so as not to fall short of what we have; so I have hurried to write, as your humble servant.[^4-9-1] Let us therefore keep watch in the final days. For the entire span of our faith will do us no good unless now, in this lawless season and amid the coming stumbling blocks, we hold our ground, as befits children of God, so that the Black One[^4-9-2] gets no opening.

<sup>10</sup> Let us flee from all vanity and thoroughly hate the works of the evil way. Do not slip away on your own and live apart as though you were already justified; instead, come together and discuss what benefits everyone.

<sup>11</sup> For the scripture says: “Alas for those who consider themselves wise and are experts in their own estimation.”[^4-11-1] Let us become people of the Spirit, a flawless temple for God. As far as we are able, let us practice reverence for God and fight to guard his commandments, so that we may find joy in his ordinances.

<sup>12</sup> The Lord will judge the world without partiality. Each will be paid back according to what they have done. If someone is good, their righteousness will go ahead of them; if wicked, the wage of wickedness will lie before them.

<sup>13</sup> Otherwise, taking our ease as if we were the called, we might doze off in our sins, and the evil ruler, seizing his power over us, might thrust us away from the kingdom of the Lord.

<sup>14</sup> And here is something further for you to grasp, my brothers and sisters: when you see how, after such great signs and wonders in Israel, they were abandoned all the same, let us be careful that we are not found—as it is written—among “the many called but the few chosen.”[^4-14-1]

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> It was for this that the Lord endured handing over his flesh to destruction: so that we might be made clean by the forgiveness of sins, that is, by the blood of his sprinkling.

<sup>2</sup> It is written about him—some of it for Israel, some for us—and it reads: “He was wounded because of our transgressions and weakened because of our sins; through his stripes we were healed. He was led like a sheep to slaughter, and like a lamb that makes no sound before the one who shears it.”[^5-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> We ought therefore to give the Lord the very greatest thanks, because he has made known to us what is past, has made us wise in the present, and as for the future we are not without understanding.

<sup>4</sup> The scripture says: “Nets are not unjustly spread for winged creatures.”[^5-4-1] He means that a person deserves to perish who, knowing the way of righteousness, locks themselves into the way of darkness.

<sup>5</sup> And here is something else, my brothers and sisters: if the Lord endured suffering for our souls—he who is master of the entire world, and to whom God said from the world’s foundation, “Let us make a human being in our image and likeness”[^5-5-1]—how did he come to endure suffering at human hands? Learn:

<sup>6</sup> the prophets, who had their grace from him, prophesied about him; and he himself endured, in order to put an end to death and to show the resurrection from the dead—because it was necessary for him to be revealed in the flesh—

<sup>7</sup> so that he might fulfill the promise to the ancestors and, while preparing the new people for himself, show on earth that he will himself bring about the resurrection and then judge.

<sup>8</sup> And so, as he taught Israel and performed such great wonders and signs, he proclaimed his message, and he loved them beyond measure.

<sup>9</sup> When he picked his own apostles, who were to preach his gospel, men lawless beyond every kind of sin, in order to show that he “came not to call the righteous but sinners,”[^5-9-1] he made himself known as the Son of God.

<sup>10</sup> For if he had not come in flesh, humanity could not possibly have been saved by seeing him,[^5-10-1] since people who stare at the sun—which will one day cease to be, though it is the work of his hands—cannot bear to look straight into its beams.

<sup>11</sup> So the Son of God came in the flesh for this: to sum up the full total of the sins of those who pursued his prophets to death.

<sup>12</sup> For this, then, he endured. God says that the blow to his flesh came from them: “When they strike their own shepherd, then the sheep of the flock will be destroyed.”[^5-12-1]

<sup>13</sup> He himself was willing to suffer this way, since it was necessary that he suffer upon a tree.[^5-13-1] For the one who prophesies about him says: “Spare my soul from the sword,” and “Fasten my flesh with nails, for companies of evildoers have stood up against me.”[^5-13-2]

<sup>14</sup> And again he says: “Look, I have set my back to the lashes and my cheeks to the blows, and I have set my face like solid rock.”[^5-14-1]

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> So when he gave the command, what does he say? “Who is the one who contends with me? Let him stand against me. Or who is the one who claims to be justified against me? Let him approach the servant of the Lord.[^6-1-1]

<sup>2</sup> Woe to you, because all of you will wear out like a garment, and a moth will devour you.”[^6-2-1] Again the prophet says—since he was put in place as a mighty stone to crush—“Look, I will set in the foundations of Zion a stone, costly, chosen, a cornerstone, highly honored.”[^6-2-2]

<sup>3</sup> Then what does it say? “And whoever hopes in him[^6-3-1] will live forever.” Is our hope, then, in a stone? Of course not! It is because the Lord has put his flesh in a position of strength. For he says, “And he set me like a solid rock.”[^6-3-2]

<sup>4</sup> Again the prophet says: “A stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.”[^6-4-1] And again he says: “This day is great and marvelous, the one that the Lord made.”[^6-4-2]

<sup>5</sup> I am writing to you quite plainly, so that you may understand. I am the lowliest servant of your love.[^6-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> What does the prophet say again? “A gathering of evildoers surrounded me; they encircled me like bees around a honeycomb,”[^6-6-1] and, “For my clothing they cast lots.”[^6-6-2]

<sup>7</sup> Because he was to be revealed in the flesh and suffer, his suffering was disclosed beforehand. For the prophet speaks against Israel: “Woe to their soul, for they have devised wicked counsel against themselves, saying: ‘Let us tie up the righteous one, since he is a burden to us.’”[^6-7-1]

<sup>8</sup> What does the other prophet, Moses, tell them? “See, the Lord God says this: Go into the good land that the Lord promised on oath to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and inherit it—a land that flows with milk and honey.”[^6-8-1]

<sup>9</sup> And what does knowledge say? Learn: “Place your hope,” it says,[^6-9-1] “in Jesus, who will soon appear to you in the flesh.” For humankind is suffering earth; the shaping of Adam was out of the surface of the ground.[^6-9-2]

<sup>10</sup> What, then, does it mean to say “to the good land, a land flowing with milk and honey”? Our Lord is to be praised, brothers and sisters: he has put into us wisdom and a mind for his secrets. For the prophet tells a parable of the Lord. Who will grasp it, if not someone wise and learned who loves their Lord?

<sup>11</sup> Since, then, he has renewed us in the forgiveness of sins, he has made us into a different type,[^6-11-1] so that we have souls like those of children, as though he were forming us anew.

<sup>12</sup> Scripture says something about us when it addresses the Son: “Let us create humankind in our image, in our likeness, and let them rule the wild creatures of the land, the birds of the sky, and the fish of the sea.”[^6-12-1] Then the Lord, looking at the fine thing he had molded, said: “Grow and multiply and fill the earth.”[^6-12-2] That was said to the Son.

<sup>13</sup> I will show you again how he speaks to us. In the last days he made a second creation. The Lord says: “See, I am making the last things like the first.”[^6-13-1] This is what the prophet announced: “Go into a land flowing with milk and honey, and master it.”[^6-13-2]

<sup>14</sup> Notice, then, that we have been re-formed, just as he says again through another prophet: “Look,” says the Lord, “I will remove from these people”—that is, the ones the Spirit of the Lord saw ahead of time—“the hearts of stone, and I will put hearts of flesh in them.”[^6-14-1] He said this because he himself was going to appear in the flesh and make his home in us.

<sup>15</sup> For the home of our heart, my brothers and sisters, is a holy temple for the Lord.

<sup>16</sup> The Lord says yet again: “And by what means will I appear before the Lord my God and be glorified?” He answers: “I will give thanks to you in the gathering of my brothers and sisters, and I will sing hymns to you among the assembled saints.”[^6-16-1] So we are the ones he has brought into the good land.

<sup>17</sup> So why the milk and honey? Because a baby is first given life with honey and then with milk. Likewise we, too, who are given life through trust in the promise and through the word, will live and have mastery over the earth.

<sup>18</sup> Now we said above: “And let them grow and multiply and rule over the fish.”[^6-18-1] Who, then, is able now to rule wild animals or fish or birds of the sky? We must realize that to rule is a matter of authority, so that someone gives orders and is master.

<sup>19</sup> If this is not happening now, then he has told us when: when we ourselves have been perfected and become heirs of the Lord’s covenant.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> So understand, children of gladness, that the good Lord revealed everything to us beforehand, so that we might know the one whom we should praise, giving thanks in all things.

<sup>2</sup> If the Son of God—Lord, and destined to judge the living and the dead—endured suffering so that his wound might make us alive, then let us trust that he, the Son of God, could not have suffered except for us.

<sup>3</sup> Yet even while he was being crucified, he was given vinegar and gall to drink. Listen to how the temple priests have shown this beforehand. A command was written down: “Whoever does not observe the fast shall be put to death.”[^7-3-1] The Lord gave that command because he himself, for the sake of our sins, was going to offer the vessel of the Spirit[^7-3-2] as a sacrifice—so that the type that was given in Isaac, who was brought to the altar, would also be completed.

<sup>4</sup> What, then, does it say in the prophet? “And let them eat from the goat that is offered at the fast for all sins.” Pay close attention: “And let the priests alone, all of them, eat the unwashed entrails with vinegar.”[^7-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> “Why? Since you intend to give me gall with vinegar to drink when I am going to offer my flesh for the sins of my new people, you alone shall eat, while the people fast and mourn in sackcloth and ashes.” He did this to demonstrate that he had to suffer at their hands.

<sup>6</sup> Mark what he commanded: “Take two goats, handsome and alike, and bring them forward; and let the priest take the one as a whole burnt offering for sins.”[^7-6-1]

<sup>7</sup> And what are they to do with the other? “Accursed,” he says, “is the one.”[^7-7-1] Notice how the type of Jesus is revealed:

<sup>8</sup> “And all of you, spit on him, stab him, and wind the scarlet wool around his head, and thus let him be driven out into the wilderness.”[^7-8-1] When that is done, the man who carries the goat takes it to the wilderness, removes the wool, and lays it on a bush they call rachēl,[^7-8-2] whose shoots we usually eat when we come across them in the country. Only the rachēl has sweet fruit.

<sup>9</sup> What does this mean? Pay attention: “One on the altar, and one accursed”—and why is the accursed one crowned? Because they will see him then, on that day, wearing the scarlet robe down to the feet around his body, and they will say, ‘Is this not the one whom we once crucified, rejecting him, piercing him and spitting on him? Truly this was the one who then said that he was the Son of God.’

<sup>10</sup> For how is he like that one? For this reason the goats are alike, fine and equal: so that when they see him coming then, they will be struck with amazement at how much he resembles the goat. See, then, the type of Jesus, who was about to suffer.

<sup>11</sup> And why do they put the wool among the thorns? This is a type of Jesus, set forth for the church: whoever wants to lift off the scarlet wool has to suffer a great deal, because the thorn is dreadful, and only by enduring pain will that person get the better of it. In the same way, he says, those who wish to see me and lay hold of my kingdom must seize me through affliction and suffering.[^7-11-1]

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> What do you suppose this is a type of: that Israel was ordered to have the men whose sins are complete offer a heifer, slay it and burn it, and that then boys gather[^8-1-1] the ashes into containers, put scarlet wool on a stick (note, again, the type of the cross and the scarlet wool) with hyssop, and then sprinkle the people one at a time so that they may be cleansed of their sins?[^8-1-2]

<sup>2</sup> Understand how plainly he speaks to you. The calf is Jesus; the sinful men who offer it are the ones who brought him to the slaughter. After that there are no longer men, no longer the glory of sinners.[^8-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> The boys who do the sprinkling are those who announced to us the pardon of sins and the cleansing of the heart, those to whom he gave authority over the gospel—twelve in number, to bear witness to the tribes, since Israel has twelve tribes—to preach it.

<sup>4</sup> And why three boys who sprinkle? As a witness to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, because these are great before God.

<sup>5</sup> And why is the wool placed on the wood? Because the kingdom of Jesus rests on the tree,[^8-5-1] and because those who hope in him will live forever.

<sup>6</sup> And why the wool and the hyssop together? Because in his kingdom there will be evil and filthy days, in which we shall be saved—because someone with a pain in the flesh is healed by the grime of the hyssop.

<sup>7</sup> Because these things were done in this way, they are clear to us but obscure to them, since they did not hear the voice of the Lord.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> He speaks again about ears: how he circumcised our heart. The Lord declares through the prophet: “At the report heard by the ear they listened to me.”[^9-1-1] And again: “People from far away will hear with their ears; they will learn what I did.”[^9-1-2] And this: “Be circumcised in heart,” the Lord says.[^9-1-3]

<sup>2</sup> And again: “Hear, Israel; thus says the Lord your God.”[^9-2-1] And again the Spirit of the Lord prophesies: “Who is the person who wants to live forever? Let them truly listen to the voice of my servant.”[^9-2-2]

<sup>3</sup> And he says again: “Listen, heaven; give ear, earth: the Lord has spoken these words as testimony.”[^9-3-1] And again: “Hear the word of the Lord, you leaders of this people.”[^9-3-2] And again: “Listen, children, to the sound of someone shouting in the wilderness.”[^9-3-3] So he circumcised our hearing so that, once we had heard the word, we would believe.

<sup>4</sup> But the circumcision in which they trust has also been abolished, for he declared that circumcision was to be not of the flesh. But they went astray, because an evil angel had tricked them with cleverness.

<sup>5</sup> He tells them: “This is what the Lord your God says”—here I find a commandment—“Do not sow among thorns; be circumcised for your Lord.”[^9-5-1] And what does he say? “Circumcise your hard heart, and do not stiffen your neck.”[^9-5-2] Take another saying: “Look,” the Lord says, “all the nations are uncircumcised in their foreskin, but this people is uncircumcised in heart.”[^9-5-3]

<sup>6</sup> But you will say, “Yet the people have been circumcised as a seal.”[^9-6-1] So has every Syrian and Arab, and all the priests of the idols. Do they too, then, belong to their covenant? Even the Egyptians are among the circumcised.

<sup>7</sup> So learn fully about all this, children of love: Abraham, who first gave circumcision, circumcised in the Spirit, looking ahead to Jesus, having received the teachings of three letters.[^9-7-1]

<sup>8</sup> For it says: “And Abraham circumcised eighteen and three hundred men from his household.”[^9-8-1] What knowledge, then, was given to him? Notice that he names the eighteen first, and then, after a pause, the three hundred. The eighteen: I is ten and H is eight—that is Jesus.[^9-8-2] And since the cross was going to have its grace in the T, he mentions the three hundred too. So he reveals Jesus in the two letters and the cross in the one.

<sup>9</sup> He knows who put the innate gift of his teaching[^9-9-1] in us. No one has learned a more sincere word from me; but I know that you are worthy.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> As for what Moses said—“You shall not eat pork, or eagle, or hawk,[^10-1-1] or raven, or any fish that has no scale on it”[^10-1-2]—he took three teachings in his understanding.

<sup>2</sup> Furthermore, he says to them in Deuteronomy: “And I will establish my ordinances with this people.”[^10-2-1] So it is no commandment of God that they not eat; rather, Moses spoke in the Spirit.

<sup>3</sup> As for the pig, he said it with this in mind: “You shall not be joined,” he says, “to people of that sort”—those who resemble pigs. That is to say, when they are well off they forget the Lord, but when they are in want they recognize the Lord, just as the pig does not know its owner while it is eating, but squeals when it is hungry and, once it has been fed, falls silent again.

<sup>4</sup> “Nor shall you eat eagle, hawk, kite,[^10-4-1] or raven.” “You will never,” he says, “be joined to, or be made like, people of that kind”—those who do not know how to earn their food by labor and sweat, but seize others’ possessions in their lawlessness, and keep watch as if walking in innocence, looking around for someone to strip out of greed. These birds are just the same: they alone do not obtain their own food, but sit idle, searching for a way to devour other creatures’ flesh, and are pests because of their wickedness.

<sup>5</sup> “And you shall not eat,” he says, “the moray eel, or the octopus, or the cuttlefish.”[^10-5-1] “You shall not,” he says, “be made like people of that kind, by joining yourself to them”—people who are utterly ungodly and already condemned to death. For these fish alone swim in the deep, accursed, not floating on the surface like the others, but living on the ground below the depths.

<sup>6</sup> “But neither shall you eat the hare.”[^10-6-2] Why? “You shall not,” he says, “become a corrupter of boys,[^10-6-1] or be made like such people,” because every year the hare gains another opening for its excretion: it has as many holes as it has lived years.

<sup>7</sup> “And you shall not eat the hyena either.”[^10-7-2] “You will never,” he says, “be an adulterer or a corrupter,[^10-7-1] or be made like them.” Why? Because this creature switches its nature from one year to the next, being male at one time and female at another.

<sup>8</sup> And the weasel too he rightly hated. “You shall not,” he says, “become like those we hear of who commit lawlessness with the mouth through uncleanness,[^10-8-1] and you shall not be joined to the unclean women who commit lawlessness with the mouth.” For this animal conceives through the mouth.

<sup>9</sup> Having received three teachings about foods, Moses spoke in this way in the Spirit; but they, following the desire of the flesh, took them to be about eating.

<sup>10</sup> David also receives knowledge of these same three teachings, and says: “Blessed is the one who did not walk in the counsel of the ungodly”—as the fish go into the dark depths; “and did not stand in the way of sinners”—as those who seem to fear the Lord sin like the pig; “and did not sit in the seat of pests”[^10-10-1]—as the birds sit waiting to seize. You now have the complete account of food as well.

<sup>11</sup> Moses says again: “You may eat any animal that has a split hoof and brings up its cud.”[^10-11-1] What does he mean? That someone receiving food knows who feeds them and, resting in that person, seems to be glad. He spoke well, with the commandment in view. What does he say, then? “Hold fast to people who revere the Lord, who ponder in their heart the exact sense of the word[^10-11-2] they received, who speak of the Lord’s ordinances and observe them, who know that reflection is a work of joy, and who chew over the Lord’s word.” And the split hoof? It means that the righteous person lives in this world and still looks ahead to the holy age. See how well Moses gave the law.

<sup>12</sup> But how would they ever come to see or understand any of this? We, on the other hand, having rightly grasped the commandments, speak of them as the Lord wanted. It was for this purpose that he circumcised our ears and our hearts: that we might take in these things.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> Let us look into whether the Lord took any care to reveal beforehand something about the water and the cross. About the water it is written, with respect to Israel, that they will not accept the baptism that brings forgiveness of sins, but will build for themselves.[^11-1-1]

<sup>2</sup> For the prophet says: “Be amazed, heaven, and let the earth bristle with horror even more at this: this people has committed two evils. They abandoned me, the spring of life, and dug themselves a pit of death.”[^11-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> “Is my holy mountain Sinai a deserted rock? For you will be like the young of a bird, fluttering up, taken from the nest.”[^11-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> And once more the prophet says: “I will walk ahead of you and flatten mountains, smash bronze doors and snap iron bolts. I will hand you treasures hidden in darkness, concealed and unseen, so that they will know that I, the Lord, am God.”[^11-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> And: “You will dwell in the high cave of a strong rock.” And: “His water is faithful; you will see a king in glory, and your soul will meditate on the fear of the Lord.”[^11-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> In yet another prophet he says: “Whoever does these things will be like a tree planted at the passageways of the waters, giving its fruit when its time comes, with leaves that never drop off, and whatever it does will prosper.”[^11-6-1]

<sup>7</sup> “The ungodly are not like that—no, not so! They are like the dust that the wind blows off the surface of the ground. That is why the ungodly will not stand up at the judgment, nor sinners in the assembly of the just. For the Lord is acquainted with the path of the just, but the path of the ungodly will come to nothing.”[^11-7-1]

<sup>8</sup> See how he linked water and cross as one. This is what he is saying: Blessed are those who, hoping in the cross, descended into the water. For he places the reward at its own time—“Then,” he says, “I will repay.” But when he says right now, “the leaves will not fall,” he means that any word coming out of your mouth in faith and love will lead many to conversion and to hope.

<sup>9</sup> And another prophet says again: “And the land of Jacob was praised above all the earth.”[^11-9-1] This means that he glorifies the vessel of his spirit.[^11-9-2]

<sup>10</sup> Then what does he say? “And there was a river flowing from the right, and beautiful trees came up from it; and whoever eats from them will live forever.”[^11-10-1]

<sup>11</sup> This means that we go down into the water full of sins and filth, and come up bearing fruit in our heart, holding the fear and the hope in Jesus in the Spirit. “And whoever eats from these will live forever”: this means, he says, that whoever hears these things being spoken and believes will live forever.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> In the same way, he again defines something about the cross in another prophet, who says: “And when will these things be accomplished?” says the Lord. “When a tree has been bent down and stands up again, and when blood drips from a tree.”[^12-1-1] Here again you have something about the cross and about the one who was going to be crucified.

<sup>2</sup> He speaks to Moses again when Israel was being fought by foreign peoples—to remind them, while they were being attacked, that it was because of their sins that they had been delivered to death. The Spirit speaks to Moses’s heart that he should make a representation of the cross and of the one who would suffer, to show that unless they place their hope in him, they will be attacked forever. So Moses lays one piece of armor over another in the middle of the battle line, and, standing higher than all, he spread out his arms; and then Israel again prevailed. But whenever he let them down, the Israelites were dying.[^12-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Why? So that they would know that they cannot be saved unless they hope in him.

<sup>4</sup> And again in another prophet he says: “All day long I spread out my hands to a disobedient people who contradict my righteous way.”[^12-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> Moses again fashions a type of Jesus—making clear that he has to suffer, and that the very person they will imagine they have destroyed will himself bring life—in a sign shown while Israel was falling. (The Lord had caused serpents of every kind to bite them, so that they died, because the transgression had come about through the serpent, by way of Eve.)[^12-5-1] His purpose was to expose them: because of their transgression they would be delivered up to the anguish of death.

<sup>6</sup> Moreover, the same Moses who commanded, “You shall have no molten or carved image for a god,”[^12-6-1] does this very thing himself in order to show a type of Jesus. So Moses makes a bronze serpent and sets it up in a place of honor, and calls the people together by proclamation.

<sup>7</sup> Once assembled, they begged Moses to pray on their behalf that they might be healed. Moses told them: “Whenever any one of you is bitten, let that person go to the serpent lying on the pole[^12-7-1], and set their hope, in faith, that this serpent, though dead, can give life; and they will be saved right away.” And that is what they did. In this too, once more, you see the glory of Jesus: everything is in him and directed to him.[^12-7-2]

<sup>8</sup> Again, what does Moses say to Jesus the son of Nun,[^12-8-1] when he gave him this name, since he was a prophet, so that all the people would listen to him alone?[^12-8-2] That the Father reveals everything about the Son Jesus.

<sup>9</sup> Moses speaks to Jesus the son of Nun, having given him this name when he sent him ahead to scout the land: “Take a scroll into your hands and write down what the Lord says: that the Son of God will uproot the entire house of Amalek in the last days.”[^12-9-1]

<sup>10</sup> Look yet again: Jesus is no son of a human being but the Son of God, made visible in the flesh through a type. And because people are going to say that the Messiah is David’s son, David himself prophesies, fearful and perceiving the sinners’ error: “The Lord said to my Lord, Take your seat at my right until I place your enemies as a footrest under your feet.”[^12-10-1]

<sup>11</sup> And again Isaiah speaks in this way: “The Lord said to my Messiah, my Lord,[^12-11-1] whose right hand I grasped, that nations should listen before him, and I will break apart the strength of kings.” See how David calls him Lord, and does not call him son.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> Let us consider whether this people inherits, or the first, and whether the covenant belongs to us or to them.

<sup>2</sup> Listen, then, to what scripture says concerning the people: “Isaac pleaded on behalf of Rebecca, his wife, since she was childless, and she became pregnant. Then Rebecca went to ask the Lord, and the Lord told her: ‘There are two nations in your belly, two peoples in your womb; one people will have the advantage over the other, and the greater will serve the lesser.’”[^13-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> You should recognize who Isaac is and who Rebecca is, and about whom he has shown that this people is greater than that one.

<sup>4</sup> And in another prophecy Jacob says more plainly to his son Joseph: “Look, the Lord has not deprived me of seeing your face; bring me your sons, so that I may bless them.”[^13-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> Joseph brought forward Ephraim and Manasseh, wanting Manasseh, as the elder, to receive the blessing; he had set him by the right hand of his father Jacob. But Jacob saw by the Spirit an image of the people who would come later.[^13-5-1] And what does the scripture say? “Jacob crossed his arms and put the right one upon Ephraim’s head, the younger, second-born son, and blessed him. Joseph said to Jacob, ‘Move your right hand onto Manasseh’s head, because he is my firstborn.’ But Jacob replied, ‘I know it, child, I know it. Still, the greater will be subject to the lesser—yet this one will be blessed too.’”[^13-5-2]

<sup>6</sup> See, then, in regard to whom he has laid it down that this people is first and the heir of the covenant.

<sup>7</sup> If, moreover, he also made mention of it through Abraham, we have the perfection of our knowledge. What, then, does he say to Abraham, when he alone believed and it was counted as righteousness? “Look, I have made you, Abraham, the father of nations who believe in God while uncircumcised.”[^13-7-1]

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> Yes. But let us look at whether he really did give the covenant which he swore to the ancestors that he would give the people. He did; but through their sins they proved unfit to receive it.

<sup>2</sup> For the prophet says: “For forty days and forty nights Moses fasted on Mount Sinai, so as to take the Lord’s covenant to the people. And from the Lord Moses took the two tablets, written in the Spirit by the finger of the Lord’s hand; he picked them up and carried them down to deliver them to the people.”[^14-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Then the Lord told Moses: “Moses, Moses, go down quickly, for the people you brought out of the land of Egypt have acted lawlessly.”[^14-3-1] Moses grasped that they had cast themselves idols again, so he hurled the tablets away, and the tablets of the Lord’s covenant broke into pieces.

<sup>4</sup> Moses received it, but they did not prove worthy. Learn how we received it. Moses received it as a servant, but the Lord himself gave it to us, to be a people of inheritance, having endured suffering for our sake.

<sup>5</sup> And he appeared so that those people might reach full measure in their sins, while we receive the covenant of the Lord Jesus through the one who inherits.[^14-5-1] For this he was made ready: that by appearing he might rescue from darkness our hearts, already worn out by death and surrendered to the lawlessness of error, and set up a covenant within us through his word.

<sup>6</sup> For it is written how the Father instructs him, once he has redeemed us from darkness, to prepare a holy people for himself.

<sup>7</sup> So the prophet says: “I am the Lord your God. I called you in righteousness, I will grasp your hand and make you strong; I have appointed you a covenant for the race,[^14-7-1] a light for the nations, to open blind eyes, to lead out of fetters those who are bound, and from the prison house those who live in darkness.” We know, then, what we have been ransomed from.

<sup>8</sup> Again the prophet says: “Look, I have made you a light of the nations, so that you may be for salvation to the end of the earth. So says the Lord God who redeemed you.”[^14-8-1]

<sup>9</sup> And the prophet says once more: “The Spirit of the Lord is on me, which is why he anointed me to announce good news of grace to the lowly.[^14-9-1] He sent me to heal those with broken hearts, to proclaim release to captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to announce a year of the Lord’s favor and a day of repayment, to console everyone who mourns.”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> Further, about the sabbath it is written in the Ten Words,[^15-1-1] which he spoke to Moses face to face on Mount Sinai: “And hallow the Lord’s sabbath with pure hands and a pure heart.”[^15-1-2]

<sup>2</sup> And in another place he says: “If my children keep the sabbath, then I will lay my mercy upon them.”[^15-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> He speaks of the sabbath at the start of creation: “And God made the works of his hands in six days, completed them on the seventh day, rested on it, and made it holy.”[^15-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> Consider, children, the meaning of “he completed in six days.” It says that in six thousand years the Lord will bring all things to completion—for with him a single day stands for a thousand years. He himself testifies to this when he says: “Look, the Lord’s day will be like a thousand years.”[^15-4-1] So, children, in six days, that is, within the six thousand years, everything will be completed.

<sup>5</sup> “And he rested on the seventh day.” He means this: when his Son comes and ends the era of the lawless one,[^15-5-1] judges the ungodly, and alters the sun, moon, and stars, then he will indeed rest on the seventh day.

<sup>6</sup> Furthermore, he says: “You shall sanctify it with clean hands and a clean heart.” So if anyone today can sanctify the day that God sanctified by being pure in heart, we are completely mistaken.

<sup>7</sup> Notice, then, that we will rightly sanctify it only then, when we are at rest[^15-7-1]—when we ourselves are able, having been made righteous and having received the promise, with lawlessness no more and all things made new by the Lord. Then we shall be able to sanctify it, having first been sanctified ourselves.

<sup>8</sup> Then he tells them: “Your new moons and your sabbaths I find intolerable.”[^15-8-1] Do you see how he puts it? Not the sabbaths of today are acceptable to me, but the one that I have made, on which, after I have given everything rest, I will begin an eighth day, which is the start of another world.

<sup>9</sup> That is why we keep the eighth day with rejoicing, the day on which Jesus also rose from the dead and, having appeared, went up into the heavens.

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> I will tell you also about the temple, and how those wretched people, led astray, trusted in the structure instead of in their God who created them, as if it were God’s house.

<sup>2</sup> For they consecrated him in the temple almost as the gentiles do. But learn what the Lord says in doing away with it: “Who has measured the sky with a hand-span, or the earth with a fistful? Was it not I?” says the Lord. “Heaven is my throne and the earth is a footstool under my feet. What sort of house could you build for me, and where is my resting place?”[^16-2-1] You have seen that their hope is empty.

<sup>3</sup> Moreover, he says again: “Look, those who tore down this temple will themselves build it.”[^16-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> And it is happening. Because they waged war, it was torn down by their enemies, and now the enemies’ own servants will rebuild it.

<sup>5</sup> Again, it was made known how the city, the temple, and the people of Israel were to be given up. For the scripture says: “In the last days it will come about that the Lord will give over the sheep of the pasture, along with their sheepfold and their tower, to ruin.”[^16-5-1] And it happened as the Lord had said.

<sup>6</sup> Let us ask, however, whether a temple of God exists. It does—in the place where he himself says that he is building and finishing it. For it is written: “And it will come about that, when the week is finished, a temple of God will be built in glory, in the name of the Lord.”[^16-6-1]

<sup>7</sup> So I conclude that there is a temple. Now learn how it will be built in the name of the Lord. Before we trusted in God, the dwelling of our heart was perishable and frail, truly a temple made with hands, since it was full of idol worship and a home for demons, because we did everything opposed to God—

<sup>8</sup> but it will be built in the Lord’s name. Make sure the Lord’s temple is built in glory. How? Having obtained forgiveness of sins and put our trust in the name, we have become new, remade as at the beginning. And so God really lives in our dwelling place, in us.

<sup>9</sup> How? Through the word of his faith, the call of his promise, the wisdom of his ordinances, the commands of his teaching; he himself prophesying within us and dwelling within us; he opens to us, who were slaves to death, the temple’s door—the mouth—and by granting repentance leads us into the temple that cannot decay.

<sup>10</sup> Whoever yearns to be saved looks past the human speaker to the one dwelling and talking within that person, struck with wonder that such words have never come from the speaker’s mouth before and that they themselves never even longed to hear anything like them. Here is the spiritual temple that is being built for the Lord.

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> To the extent that it was possible to make these things clear to you simply, my soul hopes, in line with my desire, that I have not left out anything that concerns salvation.

<sup>2</sup> For if I were to write to you about things present or things to come, you would not understand, because they are set in parables. So much for these things.[^17-2-1]

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> Let us move on to a different kind of knowledge and teaching.[^18-1-1] There are two ways of teaching and power: the way of light and the way of darkness. The difference between the two ways is great. Over one stand light-bearing angels of God as guides; over the other, angels of Satan.

<sup>2</sup> The one is Lord from everlasting to everlasting; the other is ruler of the present age of lawlessness.

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> The way of light, then, is this: if anyone wants to travel the road to the appointed place, let them hurry by their works. The knowledge given to us for walking in it is as follows.

<sup>2</sup> You shall love your Maker; you shall revere your Creator; you shall give glory to the one who ransomed you from death. You shall be single-hearted and wealthy in spirit. You shall not attach yourself to travelers on the road of death. You shall despise anything that does not please God, and despise every pretense. You shall never forsake the Lord’s commandments.

<sup>3</sup> You shall not exalt yourself, but shall be humble-minded in every way. You shall not take glory upon yourself. You shall not form an evil plan against your neighbor. You shall not let your soul grow bold.

<sup>4</sup> You shall not practice sexual immorality, nor commit adultery,[^19-4-1] nor corrupt boys.[^19-4-2] The word of God must not leave your mouth in the midst of anyone’s uncleanness.[^19-4-3] You shall not play favorites in correcting a fault. You shall be gentle and quiet, trembling at the words you have heard. You shall not keep a grudge against your brother.[^19-4-4]

<sup>5</sup> You shall not hesitate over whether it will come about or not.[^19-5-1] You shall not misuse the name of the Lord.[^19-5-2] You shall love your neighbor above your own soul. You shall not kill a child by abortion, or again put to death one already born. You shall not withdraw your hand from[^19-5-3] your son or your daughter, but from their youth you shall teach them reverence for God.

<sup>6</sup> You shall not desire what belongs to your neighbor, nor be grasping. Nor shall you attach yourself wholeheartedly to the proud, but you shall keep company with the humble and the righteous. Whatever experiences come upon you, accept them as good, knowing that nothing happens without God.

<sup>7</sup> You shall not be double-minded or talkative.[^19-7-1] You shall be subject to your masters as to a type of God,[^19-7-2] with respect and fear. You shall not command your slave or servant girl in bitterness—they hope in the same God—otherwise they may stop fearing the God who is over both of you. For he did not come to call people by outward appearance, but those for whom the Spirit has made preparation.

<sup>8</sup> You shall share all things with your neighbor, and not call anything your own;[^19-8-1] for if you are partners in the imperishable, how much more in the perishable! You shall not be quick with your tongue, because the mouth is a snare that kills. As far as you can manage, you shall keep yourself pure on behalf of your soul.

<sup>9</sup> Do not be someone whose hands reach out for getting but are gripped shut for giving. You shall love like the apple of your eye all who speak the Lord’s word to you.

<sup>10</sup> You shall remember the day of judgment night and day, and every day you shall seek out the faces of the saints,[^19-10-1] either toiling by speech and going out to encourage them and working by the word to save a soul, or laboring with your hands for the ransom of your sins.

<sup>11</sup> You shall not hesitate to give or complain as you give, and you will know who the good repayer of the reward is. You shall guard what you received, neither adding to it nor taking from it.[^19-11-1] You shall hate evil[^19-11-2] to the very end. You shall judge with justice.

<sup>12</sup> You shall not cause a split, but shall bring peace to those who are fighting by uniting them. You shall confess your sins. You shall not approach prayer with a bad conscience. This is the way of light.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> The way of the Black One[^20-1-1], on the other hand, is twisted and thick with curses. It is a path of everlasting death along with punishment, and on it lie the things that ruin souls: idol worship, audacity, overweening power, hypocrisy, double-heartedness, adultery, murder, plunder, pride, wrongdoing, fraud, spite, willfulness, drug-mixing, magic, greed, and no fear of God.

<sup>2</sup> These are people who hound the good, hate the truth, and love a lie; they have no idea what righteousness earns, they hold on neither to the good nor to a just verdict, they give no heed to a widow or an orphan, and they stay alert not out of the fear of God but to do wrong. Gentleness and endurance are far from them. They are lovers of futility, hunters for a reward, merciless to the poor, unwilling to labor for the overworked, glib at slander, ignorant of their own Maker, killers of children, spoilers of what God has shaped;[^20-2-1] they turn away from the needy, grind down the afflicted, speak up for the wealthy,[^20-2-2] and as judges wrong the poor—sinners through and through.

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> It is a fine thing, then, to learn the Lord’s ordinances, every one of them written here, and to live by them. The person who does so will be honored in God’s kingdom; the one who picks the opposite will be destroyed along with their deeds. That is the reason for the resurrection, and for the recompense.

<sup>2</sup> I appeal to those of you who hold high positions, if you will take any advice from me given with good will: you have among you people toward whom you can do good; do not fail them.

<sup>3</sup> The day is near on which everything will perish along with the evil one. The Lord is near, and his reward.[^21-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> Again and again I ask you: be good lawgivers to one another, remain faithful advisers to one another, remove all hypocrisy from among you.

<sup>5</sup> And may God, who rules the whole world, grant you wisdom, understanding, insight, knowledge of his ordinances, and endurance.

<sup>6</sup> Be taught by God, seeking out what the Lord seeks from you, and do it, so that you may be found[^21-6-1] on the day of judgment.

<sup>7</sup> If there is any remembrance of what is good, remember me as you think on these things, so that my longing and my wakefulness may lead to some good. I make this request of you, asking it as a favor.

<sup>8</sup> As long as the good vessel[^21-8-1] is with you, do not neglect any of these things, but keep seeking them out and fulfilling every commandment; for they are worthy.

<sup>9</sup> That is why I have been all the more eager to write what I was able, to cheer you. Farewell, children of love and peace. May the Lord of glory and of all grace be with your spirit.[^21-9-1]
