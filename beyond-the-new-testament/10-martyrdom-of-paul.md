# Martyrdom of Paul

Chapters: [1](#chapter-1) · [2](#chapter-2) · [3](#chapter-3) · [4](#chapter-4) · [5](#chapter-5) · [6](#chapter-6) · [7](#chapter-7)

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Luke arrived from Gaul[^1-1-1] and Titus from Dalmatia, and they were waiting for Paul in Rome. Paul was delighted to see them, and he rented a barn[^1-1-2] outside the city, where he taught the word of truth together with the brothers and sisters[^1-1-3]. His name was soon known everywhere, and many souls were being joined to the Lord, so that all Rome echoed with it. A great crowd of believers from Caesar’s household came to him, and there was great joy.

<sup>2</sup> Late in the day a cupbearer of Caesar named Patroclus went to the barn. The crowd kept him from reaching Paul, so he sat in a high window and listened to him teaching God’s word. The wicked devil, envious of the love among the brothers and sisters, made Patroclus fall from the window; he died, and word was quickly sent to Nero. Paul sensed it in the Spirit and said, “Brothers and sisters[^1-2-1], the evil one has found a place to put you to the test. Go outside, and you will see a boy lying where he has fallen from a height, almost dead. Lift him up and carry him here to me.” They went and brought him, and when the crowds saw him they were alarmed. Paul said to them, “Now, brothers and sisters, let your faith be seen. Come, all of you, let us weep before our Lord Jesus Christ, that this boy may live and we may be left in peace.” Everyone groaned, and the boy’s breath came back. They sat him on a mule and sent him off alive, together with others from Caesar’s house.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> When Nero learned that Patroclus was dead, he was very upset. As he came back from the bath he told another man to take over the serving of the wine. But his attendants reported to him, “Caesar, Patroclus lives! He is standing at the table.” Hearing that Patroclus was alive, Caesar took fright and did not want to go in. When he did enter and saw Patroclus, he lost his composure and said, “Patroclus, you live?” “I live, Caesar,” he answered. “Who brought you to life?” Nero asked. The boy, swept along by the confidence of his faith, said, “Christ Jesus, king of the ages[^2-1-1].” Shaken, Caesar said, “So he will rule over the ages and put an end to every kingdom?” “Yes,” Patroclus told him, “every kingdom under heaven he brings down, and he alone will last through the ages; no kingdom will escape him.” Nero slapped him in the face and said, “Patroclus, have you enlisted with that king as well?” “Yes, my lord Caesar,” he said, “for when I was dead he raised me.”

<sup>2</sup> Barsabas Justus the flat-footed[^2-2-1], Urion the Cappadocian, and Festus the Galatian, who were Nero’s chief men, said, “We too are soldiers of that king of the ages.” Nero shut them up in prison after cruelly torturing those whom he had loved very much, and he gave orders to search for the soldiers of the great king, and he issued a decree that all those found to be Christians and soldiers of Christ be put to death.

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> Among the many, Paul too was led in, bound. All his fellow prisoners kept their eyes on him, so that Caesar realized that he was the commander of the troops. He said to him, “Man of the great king, who are now my prisoner, what made you decide to enter the Roman empire in secret and to recruit soldiers from my province?” But Paul, filled with the Holy Spirit, said in front of everyone, “Caesar, we recruit soldiers not only from your province but from the whole inhabited world. For this has been ordered of us: that no one who wishes to serve my king be turned away. If it is your wish as well to serve him, it is not wealth or the splendors of this present life that will save you, but if you submit and make your plea to him, you will be saved. For in one day he is going to make war on the world with fire.”

<sup>2</sup> When Caesar heard this, he ordered all the prisoners to be burned with fire, but Paul to be beheaded according to the law of the Romans. But Paul did not keep the word to himself; he shared it with the prefect[^3-2-1] Longus and with Cestus the centurion. Meanwhile Nero was in Rome, driven by the power of the evil one, with many Christians being put to death without trial, until the Romans stood in front of the palace and shouted, “Enough, Caesar! These men are ours; you are destroying the strength of the Romans!” Persuaded by this, he stopped, and ordered that no one touch any Christian until he had learned about their case.

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> Then, after the decree, Paul was brought before him, and Nero kept insisting that he be beheaded. Paul said, “Caesar, it is not for a short time that I live for my king; and if you behead me, I will do this: I will be raised and appear to you, because I did not die, but live to my Lord Christ Jesus, who is coming to judge the world.”

<sup>2</sup> Longus and Cestus asked Paul, “Where did you get this king of yours? You believe in him and refuse to change your mind, even if it costs you your life.” Paul shared the message with them: “People caught in this ignorance and delusion, change your minds and be rescued from the fire coming upon the whole inhabited world. The king I serve as a soldier is not one who comes from the earth, as you suppose, but one from heaven, the God who lives; because of the lawless acts committed in this world he is coming as judge. Whoever believes in him is blessed and will live for all time, once he arrives and burns the world until it is clean.” Then they begged him, “We implore you, help us, and we will release you.” He answered, “I am no runaway from Christ; I am a lawful soldier of the living God. Had I known I was going to die, Longus and Cestus, I would have done it[^4-2-1]. But since I live to God and love myself[^4-2-2], I am leaving for the Lord, to return with him in his Father’s glory.” They said to him, “But if you are beheaded, how will we live?”

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> While they were still talking, Nero sent a man named Parthenius, with Pheretas[^5-1-1], to see whether Paul’s head had been cut off yet. They found him alive. Paul called them over and said, “Put your trust in the God who lives and who raises from the dead me and everyone who trusts in him.” They replied, “We are off to Nero now. But when you have died and risen again, we will put our trust in your God.”

<sup>2</sup> Longus and Cestus kept begging him for salvation, and he told them, “Hurry to my tomb at daybreak. Two men will be there at prayer, Titus and Luke, and from them you will receive the seal in the Lord[^5-2-1].”

<sup>3</sup> Then Paul stood facing east and lifted his hands to heaven and prayed for a long time. During the prayer he spoke with the fathers in Hebrew, and then stretched out his neck, saying nothing more. When the executioner[^5-3-1] struck off his head, milk spurted onto the soldier’s tunic. The soldier and all who were standing by saw it and were amazed, and they glorified God, who had given such glory to Paul. They left and reported what had happened to Caesar.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> When Nero heard it, he was amazed for a long time and could not decide what to think. Then, about the ninth hour[^6-1-1], Paul came while many philosophers and the centurion were standing with Caesar. He stood in front of them all and said, “Caesar, look: it is Paul, the soldier of God. I did not die, but live in my God. But for you, wretched man, there will be many evils and a great punishment, because you have unjustly shed the blood of the righteous, and within not many days.” After saying this, Paul went away from him. Nero, hearing it and thoroughly shaken, ordered the prisoners to be released, along with Patroclus and Barsabas and those with him.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> As Paul had arranged, Longus and the centurion Cestus went at dawn in fear to Paul’s tomb. When they came up, they saw two men praying, with Paul between them, so that they were stunned at the sight of the extraordinary marvel. But Titus and Luke, gripped by ordinary human fear when they saw Longus and Cestus coming toward them, turned to run away.

<sup>2</sup> The pursuers answered, “We are not chasing you to put you to death, as you imagine, you blessed people of God, but toward life, so that you may give us what Paul promised us. We saw him a moment ago standing among you at prayer.” Hearing this from them, Titus and Luke gladly gave them the seal in the Lord[^7-2-1], glorifying God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom belongs the glory through the ages of ages. Amen.
