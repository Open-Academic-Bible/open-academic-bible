# 1 Clement

A letter from the church in Rome to the church in Corinth, written about 95–97 and traditionally ascribed to Clement, a leader of the Roman church. It urges the Corinthians to end a dispute in which older leaders had been removed. It is copied after Revelation in Codex Alexandrinus (fifth century), and in a twelfth-century Syriac New Testament it stands among the New Testament letters. Around 170 a bishop of Corinth wrote that it was still read aloud in his church, and Eusebius says it was read publicly in many churches.

<sup>1</sup> God’s church staying as exiles in Rome[^0-1-1] writes to God’s church staying as exiles in Corinth—to the called, those sanctified by God’s will through our Lord Jesus Christ. Grace to you and peace from almighty God be multiplied, through Jesus Christ.

## Chapter 1

<sup>1</sup> Dear friends, the sudden and repeated calamities and reverses that have come upon us explain why we have been slow, we believe, to give our attention to the issues under discussion among you: the foreign and alien, foul and unholy uprising[^1-1-1], so unfit for God’s chosen, which a handful of impetuous and willful people have fanned into such a blaze of madness that your revered and celebrated name, which everyone ought to love, has been badly slandered.

<sup>2</sup> For who has stayed with you and not tested your thoroughly virtuous and firm faith? Who has not admired your sensible and gentle devotion in Christ? Who has not proclaimed the magnificent character of your hospitality? Who has not called your perfect and secure knowledge blessed?

<sup>3</sup> You did everything without favoritism and lived by the laws of God, submitting to your leaders and giving the proper honor to the elders[^1-3-1] among you. You directed the young to think moderate and dignified thoughts; you told the women to carry out everything with a blameless, dignified, and pure conscience, loving their husbands as they should; and you taught them to keep to the rule of submission and to manage their households with dignity, in complete good sense.

## Chapter 2

<sup>1</sup> You were all lowly in mind, with no showing off, obeying instead of demanding obedience, happier to give than to get[^2-1-1]. Satisfied with Christ’s provisions and attending closely to his words, you kept them stored in your hearts, and his sufferings were before your eyes.

<sup>2</sup> So a deep and abundant peace was granted to all, together with an insatiable eagerness to do good, and the Holy Spirit was poured out in fullness on everybody.

<sup>3</sup> Full of holy purpose, with eager good will and devout confidence, you stretched out your hands to almighty God and begged him to be merciful if you had sinned in anything unintentionally.

<sup>4</sup> You struggled day and night on behalf of the whole community of believers[^2-4-1], so that the number of his chosen ones would be saved with mercy and a clear conscience.

<sup>5</sup> You were genuine and guileless, harboring no resentment toward each other.

<sup>6</sup> Every uprising and every division was hateful to you. You grieved over your neighbors’ failures and counted their shortcomings as your own.

<sup>7</sup> You never regretted doing good, and you were ready for every good work[^2-7-1].

<sup>8</sup> Adorned with a thoroughly virtuous and reverent way of life, you did everything in the fear of him. The Lord’s commands and requirements were written on the breadth of your heart[^2-8-1].

## Chapter 3

<sup>1</sup> All honor and growth were given to you, and what is written came true: “The beloved one ate and drank and grew broad and fat, and kicked.”[^3-1-1]

<sup>2</sup> From this came jealousy and envy, strife and uprising, persecution and disorder, war and captivity.

<sup>3</sup> So the dishonored rose up against the honored, the obscure against the famous, the foolish against the wise, the young against the elders[^3-3-1].

<sup>4</sup> This is why righteousness and peace stand far off: each person has dropped the fear of God, grown nearsighted in faith toward him, and given up walking in the laws of his commands or conducting themselves as Christ requires; instead each follows the cravings of an evil heart, having taken up the unjust and impious jealousy through which death came into the world[^3-4-1].

## Chapter 4

<sup>1</sup> For so it stands written: “After some days it happened that Cain presented to God an offering of the earth’s fruits, and Abel also presented one, from the firstborn of the sheep and from their fat.”[^4-1-1]

<sup>2</sup> “And God looked favorably on Abel and his gifts, but did not pay attention to Cain and his sacrifices.”

<sup>3</sup> “And Cain was deeply grieved, and his face fell.”

<sup>4</sup> “And God said to Cain, ‘Why are you so sad, and why has your face fallen? If you offer rightly but do not divide rightly[^4-4-1], have you not sinned?

<sup>5</sup> Be still: his turning back is to you, and you will rule over him.’”

<sup>6</sup> “Then Cain told his brother Abel, ‘Let us walk out to the open country.’ While the two were out there, Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him.”

<sup>7</sup> You see, brothers and sisters, how jealousy and envy brought about the murder of a brother.

<sup>8</sup> Because of jealousy our father Jacob[^4-8-1] ran away from the face of his brother Esau.

<sup>9</sup> Jealousy led to Joseph[^4-9-1] being persecuted to the point of death and sold into slavery.

<sup>10</sup> Jealousy compelled Moses to run away from Pharaoh, king of Egypt, when a fellow countryman said to him, “Who appointed you ruler and judge over us? Are you planning to kill me as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?”[^4-10-1]

<sup>11</sup> Because of jealousy Aaron and Miriam[^4-11-1] had to lodge outside the camp.

<sup>12</sup> Jealousy sent Dathan and Abiram[^4-12-1] down alive into Hades, because they rose up against Moses, the servant of God.

<sup>13</sup> Because of jealousy David was envied not only by foreigners[^4-13-1] but was even persecuted by Saul, king of Israel.

## Chapter 5

<sup>1</sup> But to set aside the examples of old, let us come to the athletes[^5-1-1] who lived closest to our own time. Let us take the noble examples of our own generation.

<sup>2</sup> It was jealousy and envy that brought persecution on the greatest and most righteous pillars of the church[^5-2-1], who struggled even to the point of death.

<sup>3</sup> Let us place before our eyes the good apostles:

<sup>4</sup> Peter: because of unjust jealousy he endured not one or two but many labors, and after this testimony[^5-4-1] he went to the place of glory that was due him.

<sup>5</sup> Through jealousy and strife Paul demonstrated what the prize for perseverance is,

<sup>6</sup> seven times he wore chains[^5-6-1], was driven into exile, was stoned; he became a herald in the east and in the west, and won the noble renown that his faith deserved.

<sup>7</sup> Having taught the whole world righteousness, having come to the far edge of the west[^5-7-1], and having borne witness before the rulers, he was set free from the world and taken up to the holy place, the greatest model of endurance.

## Chapter 6

<sup>1</sup> To these men who lived holy lives a great crowd of the chosen was added, who suffered many insults and tortures out of jealousy and became a most noble example among us.

<sup>2</sup> Women too were persecuted because of jealousy, as Danaids and Dircae[^6-2-1], suffering terrible and profane abuse; they reached the firm goal in the race of faith and, though weak in body, received a noble prize.

<sup>3</sup> Jealousy has driven wives away from their husbands and changed what was said by our father Adam: “This at last is a bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh.”[^6-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> Jealousy and strife have overturned great cities and uprooted great nations.

## Chapter 7

<sup>1</sup> Dear friends, we are writing this not only to warn you but to remind ourselves as well, for we are in the same arena[^7-1-1], and the same contest awaits us.

<sup>2</sup> So let us give up empty and futile worries, and come to the glorious and revered rule[^7-2-1] of our tradition.

<sup>3</sup> Let us see what is good, what is delightful, and what is welcome before the one who made us.

<sup>4</sup> Let us look hard at Christ’s blood and see how valuable it is to his Father; poured out for our salvation, it brought the grace of repentance to the whole world.

<sup>5</sup> Let us go through all the generations and learn that in every generation the Master has given a place for repentance to those willing to turn to him.

<sup>6</sup> Noah preached repentance[^7-6-1], and those who listened were saved.

<sup>7</sup> Jonah[^7-7-1] preached destruction to the people of Nineveh; but when they repented of their sins, they won God’s favor by pleading with him and received salvation, though they were strangers to God.

## Chapter 8

<sup>1</sup> The ministers of God’s grace spoke about repentance through the Holy Spirit.

<sup>2</sup> The Master of all himself also spoke about repentance, and he swore an oath: “As I live, says the Lord, I take no pleasure in the death of the sinner, but in his repentance,”[^8-2-1] and he added a kind assurance:

<sup>3</sup> “Turn back, house of Israel, from your lawless ways.[^8-3-1] Tell my people’s children: even if your sins stretch from the earth up to the sky, even if they are redder than scarlet and darker than sackcloth, if you come back to me with your whole heart and say, ‘Father,’ I will listen to you as I would a holy people.”

<sup>4</sup> Elsewhere he says: “Wash, and be pure. Take the evil of your souls away from before my eyes. Quit your wicked ways, learn to do right, pursue justice, rescue the wronged, give the orphan a fair verdict, and vindicate the widow. Come, let us have this out, says the Lord. If your sins are as crimson, I will bleach them like snow; if they are like scarlet, I will bleach them like wool. Should you be ready to obey me, you will eat the land’s best; but should you refuse and turn deaf to me, the sword will devour you, for the Lord’s mouth has said so.”[^8-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> Since he wants all those he loves to share in repentance, he has confirmed it by his almighty will.

## Chapter 9

<sup>1</sup> So let us obey his splendid and glorious will; becoming suppliants for his mercy and goodness, let us fall down before him and turn to his compassion, abandoning pointless toil, strife, and the jealousy that ends in death.

<sup>2</sup> Let us fix our gaze on those who served his magnificent glory perfectly.

<sup>3</sup> Let us take Enoch, who was found righteous through obedience and was taken up, and his death was not found[^9-3-1].

<sup>4</sup> Noah, found faithful, announced a rebirth[^9-4-1] for the world by his ministry, and through him the Master preserved the animals that entered the ark in harmony.

## Chapter 10

<sup>1</sup> Abraham, who was called the friend[^10-1-1], was found faithful in that he obeyed the words of God.

<sup>2</sup> Through obedience he went out from his country, his kin, and his father’s house, so that by leaving a small country, a weak family, and a humble house he would inherit God’s promises. For God tells him:

<sup>3</sup> “Leave your homeland, your kin, and your father’s household, and head for the land I am going to show you. I will make of you a great nation. I will bless you, and I will make your name great, and you will be blessed. I will bless those who bless you, and I will curse those who curse you; and all the tribes of the earth will be blessed through you.”[^10-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> And again, after he parted from Lot, God told him: “Raise your eyes and look from the spot where you stand, northward and southward, eastward and seaward, for all the land within your sight I will give to you and your descendants for all time.[^10-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> I will make your descendants as numerous as the sand of the earth[^10-5-1]; if a person is able to number the sand of the earth, your descendants too will be numbered.”

<sup>6</sup> And again it says: “God led Abraham outside and told him, ‘Gaze up at the sky and count the stars, if you can number them. That is how many your descendants will be.’ Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness.”[^10-6-1]

<sup>7</sup> A son[^10-7-1] was given him in old age as the result of his faith and hospitality; and in obedience he offered the boy up as a sacrifice to God on the mountain God pointed out.

## Chapter 11

<sup>1</sup> Because of hospitality and piety Lot got out of Sodom safely when the whole region around was condemned to fire and sulfur. In this the Master made plain that he does not abandon those who place their hope in him, but sends to punishment and torture those who are inclined the other way[^11-1-1].

<sup>2</sup> For when his wife went out with him but was of a different mind and out of harmony with him, she was made a sign: she became a pillar of salt[^11-2-1], and remains so to this day, so that everyone may know that the double-minded and those who doubt God’s power become a judgment and a warning for all generations.

## Chapter 12

<sup>1</sup> Because of faith and hospitality Rahab the prostitute[^12-1-1] was saved.

<sup>2</sup> Joshua son of Nun[^12-2-1] sent spies to Jericho; the king of the land learned that they had arrived to spy on the country, and he sent out men to catch them, intending that once caught they would be put to death.

<sup>3</sup> The hospitable Rahab took them in and hid them in the upper room, under stalks of flax.

<sup>4</sup> The king’s men arrived and said, “The spies from our country have come into your house; hand them over, for the king commands it.” But she answered, “The men you are looking for did come to me, but they left at once and are on their way down the road,” pointing in the wrong direction.

<sup>5</sup> “I know with certainty,” she told the men, “that the Lord God is giving this land to you, because the dread and terror of you have descended on its people. So when you are about to take it, preserve me and my father’s household.”

<sup>6</sup> They said to her, “It will happen as you have said to us. When you realize we are coming, gather everyone belonging to you into your house, and they will be rescued; but anyone found out of doors will be killed.”

<sup>7</sup> They went on to give her a token: she was to hang a scarlet thread[^12-7-1] from her house, making clear in advance that there will be redemption through the Lord’s blood for all who believe and hope in God.

<sup>8</sup> You see, dear friends, that in this woman there was not only faith but prophecy.

## Chapter 13

<sup>1</sup> Let us be humble, then, brothers and sisters: put aside all arrogance, pride, stupidity, and anger, and do what is written. For the Holy Spirit says: “Let not the wise person boast[^13-1-1] in wisdom, nor the strong in strength, nor the rich in wealth; let the one who boasts boast in the Lord, in searching for him and doing justice and righteousness.” Above all, let us bear in mind the words of the Lord Jesus, which he spoke as he taught considerateness and patience.

<sup>2</sup> This is how he put it: “Be merciful, and you will receive mercy[^13-2-1]; forgive, and you will be forgiven. What you do will be done to you; what you give will be given to you; the way you judge is the way you will be judged; the kindness you show will be shown to you; the measure you use will be the measure you receive.”

<sup>3</sup> Let us strengthen ourselves in this command and these instructions, so that we may walk obediently to his holy words, in humility. For the holy word says:

<sup>4</sup> “On whom will I look, if not on the gentle, the quiet, the one who trembles at my words?”[^13-4-1]

## Chapter 14

<sup>1</sup> It is right and holy, then, brothers and sisters, that we obey God rather than follow the leaders of a loathsome jealousy, who act in arrogance and disorder.

<sup>2</sup> For we will suffer no ordinary harm but great danger if we recklessly give ourselves over to the wishes of people who launch themselves into strife and uprisings, to estrange us from what is good.

<sup>3</sup> Let us be kind to one another according to the compassion and sweetness of the one who made us.

<sup>4</sup> For it is written: “The kind will live in the land, the innocent will be left in it; but the lawless will be destroyed from it.”[^14-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> And again he says: “I saw the ungodly exalted and lifted up like the cedars of Lebanon.[^14-5-1] I passed by, and there he was no more; I searched for his place and did not find it. Guard innocence and look at uprightness, for there is a future for the peaceful person.”

## Chapter 15

<sup>1</sup> Let us then join those who live in peace with devotion, and not those who pretend to want peace.

<sup>2</sup> Somewhere it says: “This people pays me honor with their lips, but their heart is a long way from me.”[^15-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> And again: “With their mouth they bless, but in their heart they curse.”[^15-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> And it says again: “They loved him with their mouth and lied to him with their tongue; their heart was not straight with him, and they were not true to his covenant.”[^15-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> “Therefore may the lying lips fall silent[^15-5-1], those that speak lawless things against the righteous one.” And again: “Let the Lord destroy every deceitful lip and every proud tongue, those who say, ‘Our tongue we will magnify; our lips are our own; who is our master?’[^15-5-2]

<sup>6</sup> ‘Because the poor are oppressed and the needy groan, now I will rise up, says the Lord; I will place him in safety,

<sup>7</sup> I will deal openly with him.’”[^15-7-1]

## Chapter 16

<sup>1</sup> For Christ belongs to the humble, not to those who exalt themselves over his flock.

<sup>2</sup> The scepter of God’s majesty[^16-2-1], the Lord Jesus Christ, did not arrive with a showy parade of pride or arrogance, though he could have; he came in humility, as the Holy Spirit said of him. For it says:

<sup>3</sup> “Lord, who has trusted what we heard?[^16-3-1] To whom has the Lord’s arm been uncovered? We proclaimed in his presence: he is like a child[^16-3-2], like a root in dry ground. He has no appearance or splendor; we looked at him, and he had no appearance or attractiveness, but his appearance was dishonored, falling short of the appearance of human beings. He is a man struck with wounds and pain, familiar with sickness, because his face was turned away; he was dishonored and counted as nothing.

<sup>4</sup> He bears our sins and is pained for our sake, and we considered him to be in distress, in suffering, and in affliction.

<sup>5</sup> But he was wounded for our sins[^16-5-1] and made weak for our lawless acts. The discipline that brings our peace was on him; by his bruise we were healed.

<sup>6</sup> All of us like sheep went astray; each person went astray in their own way.

<sup>7</sup> The Lord delivered him up[^16-7-1] for the sake of our sins, and he, though ill-treated, does not open his mouth. As a sheep to the slaughter he was led, as a lamb silent before its shearer, thus he opens not his mouth. In his humiliation his judgment was taken away.

<sup>8</sup> Who will tell of his generation? For his life is taken away from the earth.

<sup>9</sup> Because of the lawless acts of my people he has come to death.

<sup>10</sup> And I will hand over the wicked[^16-10-1] in exchange for his burial and the rich in exchange for his death, since he did no lawless thing and no deceit was found in his mouth. And the Lord wishes to cleanse him from the wound.

<sup>11</sup> If you make an offering for sin, your soul will see long-lived offspring.

<sup>12</sup> And the Lord wishes to relieve[^16-12-1] the suffering of his soul, to show him light and mold him with understanding, to vindicate the righteous one who serves many well; and he himself will carry their sins.

<sup>13</sup> Because of this he will have many as his inheritance[^16-13-1] and will share out the spoils of the strong, since his life was given over to death and he was reckoned with the lawless.

<sup>14</sup> He himself carried the sins of many and was delivered over because of their sins.”

<sup>15</sup> And again he himself says: “I am a worm[^16-15-1] and no human being, scorned by people and despised by the nation.

<sup>16</sup> All who look at me ridicule me; they speak with their lips and shake their heads: ‘He trusted in the Lord; let him rescue him, let him save him, since he delights in him.’”

<sup>17</sup> Look, dear friends[^16-17-1], at the pattern we have been given. If the Lord was so lowly, what are we to do, who through him have come under the yoke of his grace?

## Chapter 17

<sup>1</sup> Let us also imitate those who walked around in goatskins and sheepskins[^17-1-1], announcing the coming of Christ. We mean the prophets Elijah and Elisha, and Ezekiel too; and besides these, others who have a good report.

<sup>2</sup> Abraham received a great testimony and was called the friend of God, and as he gazes at the glory of God in humility he says: “I am earth and ashes.”[^17-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Again, it is written about Job: “Job was righteous and blameless, truthful, God-fearing, avoiding every evil.”[^17-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> But he accuses himself, saying: “Nobody is free of stain, even if his life is only one day long.”[^17-4-1]

<sup>5</sup> Moses was called faithful in all his house[^17-5-1], and through his service God brought judgment on Egypt by their plagues and torments. Yet even he, highly honored as he was, spoke no proud words; from the bush, as a divine message was given to him, he said: “Who am I, that you are sending me? I am faint of voice and slow of speech.”[^17-5-2]

<sup>6</sup> And again he says: “I am steam from a pot.”[^17-6-1]

## Chapter 18

<sup>1</sup> And what shall we say of David, who has such a good record? Of him God said: “I have found a man after my own heart, David the son of Jesse; I anointed him with everlasting mercy.”[^18-1-1]

<sup>2</sup> But David himself also says to God: “Have mercy on me, O God[^18-2-1], in your great mercy; in the multitude of your compassions wipe away my lawless act.

<sup>3</sup> Cleanse me thoroughly of my lawlessness, and from my sin purify me, for I know my lawlessness, and my sin is always before me.

<sup>4</sup> Against you alone have I sinned, and what is evil in your sight I have done, so that you may be justified in your words and win when you are judged.

<sup>5</sup> For look, I was conceived in lawlessness, and in sins my mother bore me.

<sup>6</sup> For look, you have loved truth; the unseen and hidden things of your wisdom you have shown me.

<sup>7</sup> You will sprinkle me with hyssop and I will be clean; you will bathe me, and I will turn whiter than snow.

<sup>8</sup> You will make me hear joy and gladness; the humbled bones will rejoice.

<sup>9</sup> Turn your face away from my sins, and wipe out all my lawless acts.

<sup>10</sup> Create a clean heart in me, O God, and renew an upright spirit within me.

<sup>11</sup> Do not cast me away from your face, and do not take your Holy Spirit from me.

<sup>12</sup> Give me back the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a guiding spirit.

<sup>13</sup> I will teach the lawless your ways, and the ungodly will turn back to you.

<sup>14</sup> Deliver me from bloodshed, O God, the God of my salvation.

<sup>15</sup> My tongue will exult in your righteousness. Lord, you will open my mouth, and my lips will tell of your praise.

<sup>16</sup> For if you had wanted sacrifice, I would have given it; you take no pleasure in burnt offerings.

<sup>17</sup> A broken spirit is a sacrifice to God; a broken and humbled heart God will not scorn.”

## Chapter 19

<sup>1</sup> The humility and submission of so many and such great people, so well attested, have through obedience made better not only us but the generations before us, those who received his words in fear and truth.

<sup>2</sup> Having shared in many deeds great and glorious, let us return to the goal of peace that has been handed down to us from the start. Let us look to the Father and Maker of the whole cosmos, and cling to the splendid, surpassing gifts and benefactions of peace that he gives.

<sup>3</sup> Let us picture him in our minds and look with the eyes of the soul at his patient purpose; let us think how free of wrath he is toward his whole creation.

## Chapter 20

<sup>1</sup> The heavens, moved by his governance, submit to him in peace.

<sup>2</sup> Day and night run the course he has appointed, without hindering one another.

<sup>3</sup> The sun and moon and the dancing choirs of stars, at his command, in harmony and without any deviation, travel the paths he has laid down for them.

<sup>4</sup> The earth, bearing fruit at his will in its own seasons, brings forth abundant food for people, animals, and every living creature on it, without wavering or changing anything he has decreed.

<sup>5</sup> The unsearchable depths of the abysses and the indescribable regions of the world below are held together by the same commands.

<sup>6</sup> The vast basin of the boundless sea, gathered into its reservoirs by his creative act, does not cross the barriers set around it, but does exactly as he commanded it.

<sup>7</sup> For he said: “You will come this far, and your waves will break within you.”[^20-7-1]

<sup>8</sup> The ocean that humans cannot cross, and the worlds beyond it, are steered by the same directions from the Master.

<sup>9</sup> The seasons of spring, summer, autumn, and winter give way to one another in peace.

<sup>10</sup> The stations of the winds perform their service at the proper time without hindrance; ever-flowing springs, made for enjoyment and health, give their breasts without fail to sustain human life; and even the smallest of living creatures come together in harmony and peace.

<sup>11</sup> The great Creator and Master of all commanded all these things to be in peace and harmony, doing good to everything, but far beyond measure to us who have run for shelter to his compassion through our Lord Jesus Christ,

<sup>12</sup> to whom be glory and majesty forever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 21

<sup>1</sup> Take care, dear friends, that his many benefits do not turn into a judgment against us, if we do not live worthily of him and do what is good and pleasing in his sight, in harmony.

<sup>2</sup> Somewhere it says: “The Lord’s spirit is a lamp that searches the storerooms of the belly.”[^21-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Let us see how near he is, and that nothing escapes him, neither our thoughts nor the reasonings we make.

<sup>4</sup> It is right, then, that we should not desert our post and abandon his will.

<sup>5</sup> Let us offend[^21-5-1] foolish and senseless people, who exalt themselves and boast in the arrogance of their speech, before we offend God.

<sup>6</sup> Let us show reverence for the Lord Jesus Christ, whose blood was given for us; let us show respect for our leaders, honor our elders, train the young in the discipline of the fear of God, and guide our wives toward what is good.

<sup>7</sup> Let them show the lovable character of purity; let them demonstrate the sincere intention of their gentleness; let them make the moderation of their tongue plain through their silence; and let them show their love, not according to favoritism, but impartially and in holiness to all who fear God.

<sup>8</sup> Let our children share in the discipline of Christ. Let them learn what humility is worth before God, what pure love can do before God, how good and great the fear of him is, saving all who live in it in holiness with a clean mind.

<sup>9</sup> He searches thoughts and intentions; his breath is within us[^21-9-1], and he will take it away whenever he wishes.

## Chapter 22

<sup>1</sup> Faith in Christ confirms all of this, for through the Holy Spirit he himself invites us: “Come, children, hear me[^22-1-1]; I will teach you the fear of the Lord.

<sup>2</sup> Who is the person who wants life, who loves to see good days?

<sup>3</sup> Hold your tongue back from evil, and your lips from speaking deceit.

<sup>4</sup> Turn away from evil and do good.

<sup>5</sup> Look for peace and go after it.

<sup>6</sup> The eyes of the Lord look on the righteous, and his ears hear their plea; but the Lord’s face confronts those who do evil, to wipe their memory from the earth.

<sup>7</sup> The righteous one cried out, and the Lord heard him and snatched him from all his afflictions.”

<sup>8</sup> “The sinner’s afflictions are many[^22-8-1], but those who hope in the Lord will be surrounded by mercy.”

## Chapter 23

<sup>1</sup> The Father, who is compassionate in everything and generous, has a tender heart for those who fear him, and kindly and gently gives his gifts to those who come to him with a single mind.

<sup>2</sup> So let us not be double-minded, and let our soul not entertain doubts about his surpassing and glorious gifts.

<sup>3</sup> Let this writing[^23-3-1] be far from us, which says: “Wretched are the double-minded, those who doubt in their souls, who say, ‘We have heard all this since the days of our ancestors, and look, we have grown old, and nothing of the kind has happened to us.’

<sup>4</sup> You fools! Compare yourselves to a tree: take a vine. First it loses its leaves, then a shoot develops, then a leaf, then a blossom, after that an unripe grape, then a full ripe bunch. See how within a short time the tree’s fruit comes to ripeness.”

<sup>5</sup> In truth his purpose will be accomplished swiftly and suddenly, as scripture also confirms: “He will come soon and not delay, and the Lord will suddenly come to his temple, the Holy One you are waiting for.”[^23-5-1]

## Chapter 24

<sup>1</sup> Think about how the Master constantly shows us the resurrection that is to come, dear friends; he made the Lord Jesus Christ its first fruits[^24-1-1] by raising him from the dead.

<sup>2</sup> Let us look, dear friends, at the resurrection that takes place in its season.

<sup>3</sup> Day and night show us a resurrection: night goes to sleep, day rises; day departs, night comes on.

<sup>4</sup> Let us take the crops: how and in what way does sowing happen?

<sup>5</sup> The sower went out[^24-5-1] and cast every seed on the ground; they fall dry and bare on the earth and rot. Then out of that decay the greatness of the Master’s providence raises them, and from one seed grows many, and bears fruit.

## Chapter 25

<sup>1</sup> Let us look at the astonishing sign that happens in the eastern lands, that is, around Arabia.

<sup>2</sup> There is a bird called the phoenix. It is unique of its kind and lives five hundred years; when the moment of its death arrives, it builds itself a coffin[^25-2-1] of frankincense, myrrh, and other spices, and when the time has run out it enters the coffin and dies.

<sup>3</sup> As the flesh decays, a kind of worm is born, which feeds on the moisture of the dead creature and grows feathers. Then, when it has grown strong, it picks up that nest where the bones of its predecessor are, carries them from the land of Arabia to Egypt, to the place called Heliopolis,

<sup>4</sup> and in the daytime, in full view of everyone, flies up to the altar of the sun, lays them there, and then heads back.

<sup>5</sup> The priests then look up the records of the dates and find that it has come when the five hundredth year was complete.

## Chapter 26

<sup>1</sup> Do we really think it great and wonderful, then, that the Creator of all will bring about a resurrection of those who have served him in holiness with confidence in good faith, when even by means of a bird he shows us the greatness of his promise?

<sup>2</sup> Somewhere it says: “You will raise me up, and I will confess you,”[^26-2-1] and: “I lay down and slept; I woke up, for you are with me.”

<sup>3</sup> And Job says again: “You will raise up this flesh of mine, which has endured all this.”[^26-3-1]

## Chapter 27

<sup>1</sup> With this hope, then, let our souls be bound to the one who is faithful in his promises and just in his judgments.

<sup>2</sup> The one who ordered us not to lie will certainly not lie himself, since nothing is impossible with God except lying[^27-2-1].

<sup>3</sup> Let faith in him be rekindled in us, and let us understand that all things are near to him.

<sup>4</sup> By a word of his majesty he established all things, and by a word he can destroy them.

<sup>5</sup> “Who will say to him, ‘What have you done?’ Or who will withstand his mighty power?”[^27-5-1] He will do everything when he wishes and as he wishes, and not one of the things he has determined will pass by.

<sup>6</sup> All things are before him, and nothing escapes his plan,

<sup>7</sup> for “the heavens recount the glory of God[^27-7-1], and the firmament announces the work of his hands. Day after day pours out speech, night after night announces knowledge; there are no speeches or words whose voices go unheard.”

## Chapter 28

<sup>1</sup> Since all things are seen and heard by him, let us fear him and give up the foul desires behind wicked deeds, so that by his mercy we may be sheltered from the judgments to come.

<sup>2</sup> For where can any of us flee from his mighty hand? What world will take in any who desert him?

<sup>3</sup> Somewhere the scripture says: “Where shall I go, and where hide from your face?[^28-3-1] Should I climb to heaven, you are there; should I go off to the farthest part of the earth, your right hand is there; should I spread out my bed in the abysses, your spirit is there.”

<sup>4</sup> Where, then, could anyone go, or where could anyone run, to escape the one who contains all things?

## Chapter 29

<sup>1</sup> Let us approach him, then, with holiness of soul, lifting pure and undefiled hands[^29-1-1] to him, loving our gentle and compassionate Father, who made us a portion of his own choosing.

<sup>2</sup> For so it is written: “When the Most High gave out the nations their portions[^29-2-1], when he scattered Adam’s children[^29-2-2], he established the limits of the peoples to match the number of God’s angels. The Lord’s own portion became his people Jacob, and Israel the cord-measured share of his inheritance.”

<sup>3</sup> And in another place he says: “Look, the Lord takes for himself a nation[^29-3-1] out of the nations, the way a person takes the first fruits of the threshing floor, and out of that nation will come the holy of holies.”

## Chapter 30

<sup>1</sup> Since we are the portion of the Holy One[^30-1-1], let us do everything that belongs to holiness, avoiding slander, foul and impure embraces, drunkenness, rebellious novelties, loathsome desires, vile adultery, and detestable pride.

<sup>2</sup> “For God,” it says, “opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”[^30-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> Let us cling, then, to those to whom grace has been given from God. Let us clothe ourselves in harmony, with humility and self-control, keeping ourselves far from all whispering and slander, and being justified by deeds and not by words.

<sup>4</sup> For it says: “Someone who says many things will also be answered in turn.[^30-4-1] Or does the eloquent person think himself righteous?

<sup>5</sup> Blessed is one born of woman who lives a short time.[^30-5-1] Do not be abundant in words.”

<sup>6</sup> Let our praise be in God and not from ourselves, for God hates those who praise themselves.

<sup>7</sup> Let others give testimony to our good conduct, as it was given to our righteous forebears.

<sup>8</sup> Boldness, self-will, and daring belong to those cursed by God; gentleness, humility, and meekness belong to those blessed by God.

## Chapter 31

<sup>1</sup> Let us cling, then, to his blessing and see what the ways of blessing are. Let us unroll the events of the beginning.

<sup>2</sup> Why was our father Abraham blessed? Was it not because through faith he practiced righteousness and truth?

<sup>3</sup> Isaac, knowing with confidence what was to come, gladly let himself be led as a sacrifice.

<sup>4</sup> Jacob humbly left his homeland on account of his brother, went to Laban and worked as his slave, and received the twelve-tribe scepter[^31-4-1] of Israel.

## Chapter 32

<sup>1</sup> If anyone considers each of these cases one by one with sincerity, they will recognize the greatness of the gifts he has given.

<sup>2</sup> From him are the priests and all the Levites serving at God’s altar; from him is the Lord Jesus, as to his human descent; from him are kings, rulers, and leaders in Judah’s line; and the remaining tribes of his are in no small glory, because God promised: “Your offspring will be like the stars of heaven.”[^32-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> All of them, then, were glorified and made great, not through themselves or their works or the righteous deeds they did, but through his will.

<sup>4</sup> We too, then, being called by his will in Christ Jesus, are justified not by ourselves, nor by our own wisdom, insight, devotion, or deeds done in purity of heart, but by faith, the means by which almighty God has justified every person since the beginning of time[^32-4-1]. To him be the glory for ever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 33

<sup>1</sup> What then are we to do, brothers and sisters? Should we stop doing good and abandon love? May the Master never allow this to happen among us! Rather, let us hurry to complete every good work, with intensity and eagerness.

<sup>2</sup> For the Creator and Master of all himself rejoices in his works.

<sup>3</sup> By his enormous power he fixed the heavens, and with understanding beyond grasping he put them in order. He separated the land from the water that surrounded it and settled it on the secure foundation of his own purpose; he ordered the animals that roam on it to exist by his decree. The sea and its creatures he prepared in advance and enclosed within the limits of his power.

<sup>4</sup> Above all, his most excellent and supremely great creation by thought, the human being[^33-4-1], he formed with his holy and flawless hands as the imprint of his own image.

<sup>5</sup> For God says this: “Let us make humankind in our image and likeness.”[^33-5-1] And God made humankind; male and female he made them.

<sup>6</sup> When he had finished all this, he approved it, blessed it, and said, “Increase and multiply.”[^33-6-1]

<sup>7</sup> Note that every one of the righteous was decked out in good works, and that the Lord himself took delight in having decked himself with good works.

<sup>8</sup> Since we have this example, let us come to his will without hesitation; with all our strength let us do the work of righteousness.

## Chapter 34

<sup>1</sup> The good worker receives the bread of their labor with confidence; the lazy and slack one cannot look the employer in the eye.

<sup>2</sup> We must therefore be eager to do good, for everything comes from him.

<sup>3</sup> For he tells us in advance: “Look, the Lord is coming, and his reward is before his face, to give to each according to their work.”[^34-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> He urges us, then, who believe in him with all our heart, not to be idle or slack in any good work.

<sup>5</sup> Let our boast and confidence rest in him; let us be subject to his will. Let us consider the whole multitude of his angels and how they stand ready to serve his will.

<sup>6</sup> The scripture says: “Myriads of myriads stood beside him[^34-6-1], and thousands of thousands waited on him, and they called out: ‘Holy, holy, holy is the Lord Sabaoth[^34-6-2]; all creation is full of his glory.’”

<sup>7</sup> And let us too, gathered together in harmony and of one conscience, cry out to him as with one mouth, earnestly, so that we may share in his great and glorious promises.

<sup>8</sup> For it says: “What eye has not seen and ear has not heard[^34-8-1], and what has not come up in the human heart—all that the Lord has prepared for those who endure for him.”

## Chapter 35

<sup>1</sup> How blessed and wonderful are God’s gifts, dear friends!

<sup>2</sup> Life in immortality, brightness in righteousness, truth in frankness, faith in trust, self-control in holiness—all these already lie within our grasp.

<sup>3</sup> What are the things reserved for those who endure? The Creator and Father of the ages, the all-holy one, is the one who knows how great and beautiful they are.

<sup>4</sup> Let us therefore strive to be found among the number of those who wait, so that we may share in the promised gifts.

<sup>5</sup> How can this happen, dear friends? If our understanding is firmly rooted in faith in God; if we pursue what pleases and delights him; if we carry out what befits his blameless will and keep to the way of truth, throwing off all injustice, wickedness, greed, quarrels, ill will, and deceitfulness, whispering and slander, hatred of God, haughtiness and boasting, vanity and inhospitality.

<sup>6</sup> Those who practice these things are detestable to God—and not only the practitioners but also those who give them their approval.

<sup>7</sup> For the scripture says: “God said to the sinner[^35-7-1], ‘Why do you tell about my statutes and take my covenant into your mouth?

<sup>8</sup> You hated instruction and tossed my words aside. If you saw a thief you joined in with him, and you placed your share with adulterers. Your mouth was crowded with evil, and your tongue was knitting together fraud. You sat and spoke against your brother; you set a trap for your own mother’s son.

<sup>9</sup> You did these things, and I kept silent; you supposed, lawless one, that I would be like you.

<sup>10</sup> I will rebuke you and set you face to face with yourself.

<sup>11</sup> Understand this, you who forget God, lest he seize you like a lion, with no one to rescue you.

<sup>12</sup> A sacrifice of praise will honor me, and that is the way by which I will show him God’s salvation.’”

## Chapter 36

<sup>1</sup> This is the path, dear friends, on which we found our salvation, Jesus Christ, the high priest[^36-1-1] of our offerings, the champion and helper of our weakness.

<sup>2</sup> By him we look up to the heights of heaven; by him we gaze at the spotless and exalted face of God as in a mirror; by him the eyes of our hearts were opened; by him our foolish, darkened understanding is made to flourish toward light; by him the Master has willed that we taste immortal knowledge. He, being the radiance of God’s majesty[^36-2-1], is as much above the angels as the name he has inherited is superior.

<sup>3</sup> For it is written: “He makes his angels spirits and his servants a flame of fire.”[^36-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> But of his Son the Master said: “You are my Son, and today I have fathered you.[^36-4-1] Request it from me and I will hand over the nations as your inheritance, the ends of the earth as your possession.”

<sup>5</sup> And he says to him again: “Take your seat at my right until I place your enemies as a footstool under your feet.”[^36-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> Who are the enemies? The wicked, who oppose his will.

## Chapter 37

<sup>1</sup> Let us serve as soldiers, then, brothers and sisters[^37-1-1], with all earnestness, under his blameless commands.

<sup>2</sup> Let us consider those who serve as soldiers under our rulers[^37-2-1], how orderly, how readily, how obediently they carry out their orders.

<sup>3</sup> Not all are prefects or commanders of a thousand or of a hundred or of fifty, or the like; rather, each one in their own rank carries out the orders of the emperor[^37-3-1] and the officers.

<sup>4</sup> The great cannot exist without the small, nor the small without the great; there is a kind of blending in everything, and in this there is usefulness.

<sup>5</sup> Consider our body[^37-5-1]: the head is worth nothing without the feet, and likewise the feet without the head; the tiniest parts of the body are indispensable and useful to the whole. They all breathe together and obey one rule, to keep the whole body safe.

## Chapter 38

<sup>1</sup> Let our whole body be kept safe in Christ Jesus, and let each person submit to their neighbor, in keeping with the spiritual gift each has been given.

<sup>2</sup> The strong should look after the weak, and the weak should show respect to the strong. The rich should give to the poor, and the poor should thank God for providing someone to meet their need. The wise should reveal their wisdom, not through speeches but through good deeds. The humble should not bear witness to themselves but let another do so. Those who are pure in body[^38-2-1] should not boast, knowing that it is someone else who gives them self-control.

<sup>3</sup> Let us consider, brothers and sisters, what material we were made from, what kind of beings we were when we entered the world, from what tomb and darkness the one who formed and created us brought us into his world, having prepared his benefits for us before we were born.

<sup>4</sup> Since we have all this from him, we ought to thank him for everything. To him be glory forever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 39

<sup>1</sup> Foolish, senseless, stupid, and uneducated people mock us and ridicule us, wanting to exalt themselves in their own minds.

<sup>2</sup> For what can a mortal do? What strength does a creature of earth have?

<sup>3</sup> For it is written: “No shape stood before my eyes[^39-3-1]; I only heard a breath and a voice:

<sup>4</sup> ‘Surely no mortal can be clean before the Lord? Or can a man be faultless in what he does, when he does not trust his servants[^39-4-1] and has found some crookedness in his angels?

<sup>5</sup> Not even heaven is clean in his sight; and as for those who live in clay houses—and we are made of the same clay—he crushed them as a moth is crushed; from morning to evening they are gone; since they cannot help themselves, they perish.

<sup>6</sup> He breathed on them, and they died, because they had no wisdom.

<sup>7</sup> Call out, to see whether anyone will answer you, or whether you will see any of the holy angels; for anger destroys the foolish, and jealousy kills the one who has gone astray.

<sup>8</sup> I have seen fools taking root, but their dwelling was suddenly devoured.

<sup>9</sup> May their children be far from salvation; may they be cheated[^39-9-1] at the gates of lesser people, with no one to rescue them. For what has been prepared for them the righteous will eat, but they themselves will not be delivered from evils.’”

## Chapter 40

<sup>1</sup> Since these things are clear to us, and we have looked into the depths of divine knowledge, we ought to do everything in order, whatever the Master commanded us to carry out at the appointed times.

<sup>2</sup> He commanded that offerings and services be performed, and not carelessly or in disorder, but at fixed times and hours.

<sup>3</sup> Where and by whom he wants them carried out, he himself fixed by his supreme purpose, so that everything done in holiness and with his approval may be welcome to his will.

<sup>4</sup> Those who bring their offerings at the times set are welcome and blessed, for by following the Master’s regulations they do not go wrong.

<sup>5</sup> For the high priest has been given his own services, the priests have their own place appointed, and the Levites have their own ministries to perform; the lay person[^40-5-1] is bound by the rules for lay people.

## Chapter 41

<sup>1</sup> Each of us, brothers and sisters, in our own rank, should be pleasing to God, keeping a good conscience and not overstepping the fixed rule of our ministry, with dignity.

<sup>2</sup> Brothers and sisters, the perpetual sacrifices[^41-2-1], and those for vows, for sin, and for guilt, are not offered everywhere, but only at Jerusalem; even there they are not offered in every spot, but in front of the temple at the altar, once the offering has been examined for blemishes by the high priest and the ministers mentioned above.

<sup>3</sup> Those who do anything contrary to what he wills receive death as the penalty.

<sup>4</sup> You see, brothers and sisters: the greater the knowledge we have been counted worthy of, the greater the danger we are exposed to.

## Chapter 42

<sup>1</sup> The apostles received the good news for us from the Lord Jesus Christ; Jesus the Christ was sent out by God.

<sup>2</sup> Christ, then, came from God, and the apostles from Christ; both things came about in good order from the will of God.

<sup>3</sup> So, once they had received their orders, been fully convinced through the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, and been made certain by the word of God, they set out with the Holy Spirit’s full conviction, proclaiming that God’s kingdom was about to come.

<sup>4</sup> Preaching in country and city, they appointed their first converts, after testing them by the Spirit, as overseers[^42-4-1] and deacons[^42-4-2] for those who would believe.

<sup>5</sup> And this was nothing new, for long ago it had been written about overseers and deacons. For somewhere the scripture says this: “I will appoint their overseers in righteousness and their deacons in faith.”[^42-5-1]

## Chapter 43

<sup>1</sup> Is it so surprising that those entrusted by God in Christ with this task appointed the persons we have mentioned? Even the blessed Moses, “a faithful servant in all his house,”[^43-1-1] noted down in the sacred books all the instructions given to him, and the other prophets followed him, adding their testimony to the laws he had given.

<sup>2</sup> Jealousy arose about the priesthood, and the tribes were at odds over which of them was to be distinguished with that glorious title. So he ordered the twelve tribal leaders to bring him staffs inscribed with each tribe’s name. He took them, tied them up, sealed them with the leaders’ rings, and laid them in the tent of testimony[^43-2-1] on God’s table.

<sup>3</sup> Then he shut the tent and sealed the keys, and the rods as well,

<sup>4</sup> and said to them, “Brothers[^43-4-1], God has chosen the tribe whose staff blossoms to serve him as priests and ministers.”

<sup>5</sup> At daybreak he summoned all Israel, the six hundred thousand men[^43-5-1], displayed the seals to the tribal leaders, opened the tent of testimony, and brought out the staffs. And Aaron’s staff was found not only budding but loaded with fruit.

<sup>6</sup> How does this strike you, dear friends? Did Moses not know in advance that this would come about? He knew perfectly well. But so that no disorder would arise in Israel, he did it this way, for the glorifying of the name of the true and only God. To him be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 44

<sup>1</sup> Our apostles also knew, through our Lord Jesus Christ, that there would be strife over the office of overseer.

<sup>2</sup> For this reason, having received perfect foreknowledge, they appointed the persons just mentioned, and afterward gave instructions[^44-2-1] that, when they died, other approved men should succeed to their ministry.

<sup>3</sup> Those who were appointed by them, or afterward by other eminent men with the agreement of the entire church, and who have ministered to Christ’s flock blamelessly, in humility, peacefully and without servility, having had a good name with everyone for a long time—we consider it unjust to remove such people from their ministry.

<sup>4</sup> For it will be no small sin on our part if we remove from the office of overseer those who have offered its gifts blamelessly and in holiness.

<sup>5</sup> Blessed are the elders who went on before, who had a fruitful and complete departure; they need not fear that anyone will remove them from the place established for them.

<sup>6</sup> For we see that you have removed some from the ministry that they carried out blamelessly, though they lived honorably.

## Chapter 45

<sup>1</sup> You are contentious, brothers and sisters, and zealous about matters that have no bearing on salvation.

<sup>2</sup> You have studied the holy scriptures, which are true and given through the Holy Spirit.

<sup>3</sup> You know that nothing unjust or counterfeit is written in them. You will not find righteous people thrown out by holy people.

<sup>4</sup> Righteous people were persecuted, but only by the lawless; they were thrown in prison, but only by the unholy; they were stoned by lawbreakers and killed by those who had adopted a foul and unrighteous jealousy.

<sup>5</sup> They endured all this gloriously.

<sup>6</sup> What shall we say, brothers and sisters? Was Daniel[^45-6-1] thrown into the lion pit by people who feared God?

<sup>7</sup> Or were Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael[^45-7-1] imprisoned in a fiery furnace by people who practiced the grand and glorious religion of the Most High? Certainly not! Who, then, did such things? Detestable people, full of every evil, who flared up into such fury that they brought torture on those who served God with holy and unblemished intent, not knowing that the Most High is the champion and shield of those who serve his all-virtuous name with a pure conscience. To him be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

<sup>8</sup> But those who endured with confidence inherited glory and honor; they were lifted up, and their names were written by God in his memorial forever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 46

<sup>1</sup> We must hold fast to such examples, brothers and sisters.

<sup>2</sup> For it is written: “Hold fast to the holy ones, for those who hold fast to them will be made holy.”[^46-2-1]

<sup>3</sup> And again in another place he says: “With the innocent you will be innocent[^46-3-1], with the chosen you will be chosen, and with the crooked you will be twisted.”

<sup>4</sup> Let us then hold fast to the innocent and righteous, for they are God’s chosen.

<sup>5</sup> Why are there quarrels, angry outbursts, factions, divisions, and war among you?

<sup>6</sup> Do we not have one God, one Christ, and one Spirit of grace poured out on us? And one calling in Christ[^46-6-1]?

<sup>7</sup> Why do we drag apart and tear up the limbs of Christ, and make war on our own body, reaching such a pitch of folly that we forget we are limbs of one another[^46-7-1]? Remember the words of the Lord Jesus.

<sup>8</sup> For he said: “Woe to that man![^46-8-1] It would be better for him if he had never been born than to trip up one of my chosen ones. It would be better for him to have a millstone tied around his neck and be thrown into the sea than to pervert one of my chosen ones.”

<sup>9</sup> Your division has led many astray, driven many to despair, caused many to doubt, and brought grief to us all; and your rebellion goes on.

## Chapter 47

<sup>1</sup> Take up the letter[^47-1-1] of the blessed apostle Paul.

<sup>2</sup> What did he first write to you at the beginning of the gospel?

<sup>3</sup> In truth he wrote to you by the Spirit about himself, Cephas[^47-3-1], and Apollos, because even then you had formed factions.

<sup>4</sup> But that factionalism brought less sin on you, for you were taking sides with apostles of high repute and with a man approved by them[^47-4-1].

<sup>5</sup> But now consider who has led you astray and diminished the honor of your famous brotherly love.

<sup>6</sup> It is shameful, dear friends, utterly shameful, and unworthy of our conduct in Christ, to hear that the very firm and ancient church of the Corinthians is rebelling against its elders because of one or two persons.

<sup>7</sup> This report has reached not only us but also those who stand apart from us[^47-7-1], so that blasphemies are heaped on the Lord’s name because of your folly, and you create danger for yourselves.

## Chapter 48

<sup>1</sup> Let us remove this quickly, throw ourselves before the Master, and weep as we plead with him to be kind, to be reconciled with us, and to bring us back to the dignified and pure way of our brotherly love.

<sup>2</sup> For this is a gate of righteousness standing open to life, as it is written: “Open the gates of righteousness for me[^48-2-1], so that I may enter by them and praise the Lord.

<sup>3</sup> This is the gate of the Lord; the righteous will enter through it.”

<sup>4</sup> Though many gates stand open, the one in righteousness is the one in Christ; blessed are all who enter it and direct their way in holiness and righteousness, doing everything without disturbance.

<sup>5</sup> Let someone be faithful, let someone be able to explain knowledge, let someone be wise in judging words, let someone be pure in deeds.

<sup>6</sup> The greater that person seems to be, the more humble they ought to be, and the more they should seek what benefits everyone, and not their own advantage[^48-6-1].

## Chapter 49

<sup>1</sup> Let the one who has love in Christ keep the commands of Christ.

<sup>2</sup> Who can explain the bond of God’s love?

<sup>3</sup> Who is capable of describing the greatness of its beauty?

<sup>4</sup> The height to which love lifts us is beyond description.

<sup>5</sup> Love unites us with God; love conceals a multitude of sins[^49-5-1]; love puts up with everything[^49-5-2] and is patient through everything. Nothing in love is vulgar, nothing arrogant. Love has no schism, love does not revolt, love does everything in harmony. In love all God’s chosen were brought to completion; without love nothing is pleasing to God.

<sup>6</sup> In love the Master took us to himself; because of the love he had for us, Jesus Christ our Lord, by God’s will, gave his blood for us, his body for our bodies, and his life for our lives.

## Chapter 50

<sup>1</sup> You see, dear friends, how great and wonderful love is, and there is no describing its perfection.

<sup>2</sup> Who can be found in it? Only those God makes worthy. Let us beg and ask of his mercy that we be found in love, free of human partiality, flawless.

<sup>3</sup> All the generations from Adam until today have passed on, but those made perfect in love by God’s grace occupy the place of the devout, and they will be revealed when Christ’s kingdom comes to visit[^50-3-1].

<sup>4</sup> For it is written: “Enter your chambers for a very little while[^50-4-1], until my anger and wrath pass, and I will remember a good day and raise you from your graves.”

<sup>5</sup> We are blessed, dear friends, if we do God’s commands in the harmony of love, so that our sins are forgiven through love.

<sup>6</sup> For it is written: “Blessed are those whose lawless acts have been forgiven[^50-6-1], whose sins have been covered over. Blessed is the one against whom the Lord will not reckon sin, and in whose mouth there is no deceit.”

<sup>7</sup> This blessing came upon those chosen by God through Jesus Christ our Lord, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 51

<sup>1</sup> For all the ways in which we have slipped and done wrong through some assault of the adversary, let us ask to be forgiven. And those who became the leaders of the uprising and the division ought to look to what we hope for in common.

<sup>2</sup> For those who live in fear and love prefer to suffer abuse themselves rather than have their neighbors suffer it; they would rather bear condemnation themselves than that of the harmony that was so rightly and justly handed down to us.

<sup>3</sup> For it is better for a person to acknowledge their failings than to harden their heart, as the hearts of those who revolted against Moses, God’s servant, were hardened—their judgment became obvious,

<sup>4</sup> for they went down alive into Hades, and death will shepherd them[^51-4-1].

<sup>5</sup> Pharaoh, his army, and all Egypt’s rulers, with their chariots and horsemen, were engulfed in the Red Sea and died for no other reason than that their foolish hearts grew hard, after the signs and wonders done in Egypt by God’s servant Moses.

## Chapter 52

<sup>1</sup> The Master, brothers and sisters, has need of nothing; he asks nothing from anyone, except that we confess to him[^52-1-1].

<sup>2</sup> David, the chosen one, says: “I will praise the Lord[^52-2-1], and it will please him better than a young bull with growing horns and hooves. Let the poor see it and be glad.”

<sup>3</sup> And he says again: “Sacrifice a thank offering to God[^52-3-1] and give the Most High what you have vowed; and call on me on the day of your distress, and I will free you, and you will glorify me.”

<sup>4</sup> For the sacrifice to God is a broken spirit[^52-4-1].

## Chapter 53

<sup>1</sup> For you know the sacred scriptures, dear friends, and know them well, and you have looked deeply into the oracles of God. We write these things, then, as a reminder.

<sup>2</sup> Moses went up the mountain and spent forty days and forty nights[^53-2-1] in fasting and abasement. Then God told him: “Hurry down from here, for the people you brought out of Egypt have broken the law. They have swiftly strayed from the path I commanded them and made themselves cast idols.”

<sup>3</sup> And the Lord said to him: “I have told you once and twice[^53-3-1]: I have looked at this people, and they are stubborn. Let me wipe them out and blot their name from beneath the sky, and I will make you a nation great and marvelous, much more than they are.”

<sup>4</sup> Moses said: “Not so, Lord! Forgive the sin of this people, or else wipe me out of the book of the living[^53-4-1].”

<sup>5</sup> What great love! What unsurpassable perfection! A servant speaks boldly to the Lord, asks forgiveness for the multitude, or else begs to be blotted out together with them.

## Chapter 54

<sup>1</sup> Who among you, then, is noble, who is compassionate, who is filled with love?

<sup>2</sup> Let such a person say: “If the uprising, strife, and divisions are because of me, I will leave; I will go wherever you wish, and do what the majority commands; only let the flock of Christ live in peace with the elders appointed over it.”

<sup>3</sup> Whoever does this will win great renown in Christ, and every place will welcome them, for “the earth and its fullness belong to the Lord.”[^54-3-1]

<sup>4</sup> Those who live as citizens of God’s commonwealth, a life never to be regretted, have done this and will do it.

## Chapter 55

<sup>1</sup> Let us bring examples from the nations too. Many kings and leaders, when a time of plague came, were told by oracles to give themselves up to death, so that they might save their citizens with their own blood. Many left their own cities so that there would be no more rebellion.

<sup>2</sup> We know of many among us who have given themselves up to chains in order to free others. Many have handed themselves over to slavery and with the price paid for them have fed others.

<sup>3</sup> Many women, strengthened by the grace of God, have done many deeds of courage.

<sup>4</sup> The blessed Judith[^55-4-1], when the city was under siege, asked the elders to let her go out into the camp of the foreigners.

<sup>5</sup> She gave herself up to danger and went out for love of her country and her people who were under siege, and the Lord handed Holofernes over to the hand of a woman.

<sup>6</sup> Esther[^55-6-1], perfect in faith, was no less ready to risk her life to rescue the nation of Israel when it was about to perish: by her fasting and self-humbling she entreated the all-seeing Master of the ages, who saw the humility of her soul and delivered the people for whose sake she had run the risk.

## Chapter 56

<sup>1</sup> Let us also, then, intercede for those who find themselves in some fault, so that gentleness and humility may be granted them to yield not to us but to the will of God. In this way their remembrance in God’s presence and that of the saints, with mercy, will be fruitful and complete.

<sup>2</sup> Let us accept discipline, dear friends, which no one ought to resent. The warning we give one another is good and extremely beneficial, for it joins us to the will of God.

<sup>3</sup> For the holy word says this: “The Lord disciplined me severely[^56-3-1], but he did not hand me over to death.”

<sup>4</sup> “For the Lord disciplines the one he loves[^56-4-1], and scourges every child[^56-4-2] he receives.”

<sup>5</sup> “The righteous one,” it says, “will discipline me in mercy and rebuke me, but let not the oil of sinners anoint my head.”[^56-5-1]

<sup>6</sup> And it says again: “Blessed is the one whom the Lord has corrected[^56-6-1]; do not turn away from the Almighty’s warning, for he himself causes pain and then restores;

<sup>7</sup> he strikes, and his hands heal.

<sup>8</sup> Six times he will rescue you from distress, and in the seventh no evil will touch you.

<sup>9</sup> In famine he will rescue you from death, and in war he will redeem you from the sword’s power;

<sup>10</sup> he will conceal you from the lash of the tongue, and you will not be afraid when evils come.

<sup>11</sup> You will laugh at the unjust and lawless, and you will not fear wild beasts;

<sup>12</sup> the savage beasts will keep peace with you.

<sup>13</sup> Then you will know that your house is at peace, and the dwelling of your tent will not fail.

<sup>14</sup> You will know that your offspring will be many, and your children like all the herbage of the field.

<sup>15</sup> You will come to the grave like ripe grain harvested in its season, or like a stack of the threshing floor gathered in at the proper time.”

<sup>16</sup> Think, dear friends, how well protected those are whom the Master disciplines: being a good Father, he disciplines us so that we may obtain mercy through his holy training.

## Chapter 57

<sup>1</sup> You, then, who laid the foundation of the uprising, submit to the elders and be disciplined toward repentance, bending the knees of your heart.

<sup>2</sup> Learn to be subject, putting aside the swaggering, arrogant stubbornness of your tongue. It is better for you to be found among the small but respected in Christ’s flock than to appear superior and be thrown out of his hope.

<sup>3</sup> For this is what all-virtuous Wisdom[^57-3-1] says: “Look, I will pour out to you a word from my breath, and I will teach you my speech.

<sup>4</sup> Because I called and you did not listen, because I stretched out words and you paid no attention, but made my counsels useless and disobeyed my rebukes, therefore I in turn will laugh at your destruction and will rejoice when ruin comes upon you, when sudden confusion arrives, when disaster comes like a storm, or when affliction and siege come upon you.

<sup>5</sup> It will happen that when you call on me, I will not hear you; the wicked will look for me and will not find me. For they hated wisdom, did not select the fear of the Lord, did not want to give heed to my counsels, and scoffed at my reproofs.

<sup>6</sup> Therefore they will eat the fruit of their own way and be filled with their own ungodliness.

<sup>7</sup> Because they wronged the simple[^57-7-1] they will be killed, and an inquiry will destroy the ungodly. But whoever listens to me will dwell in safety, trusting in hope, and will be at rest without fear of any evil.”

## Chapter 58

<sup>1</sup> Let us obey his all-holy and glorious name, then, escaping the threats that Wisdom spoke beforehand against the disobedient, so that we may dwell in confidence on the most holy name of his majesty.

<sup>2</sup> Accept our advice, and you will have nothing to regret. For as God lives, and the Lord Jesus Christ lives, and the Holy Spirit lives, the faith and hope of the chosen, the person who with humility and untiring forbearance does, without regret, the ordinances and commands given by God will be placed in the ranks and honored in the number of those who are saved through Jesus Christ, through whom he has the glory for ever and ever. Amen.

## Chapter 59

<sup>1</sup> But if some should disobey what he has said through us, they should know that they are ensnaring themselves in transgression and considerable danger.

<sup>2</sup> We, however, will be clear of this sin, and we will make our request earnestly, offering petition and prayer that the Maker of all keep the counted number of his chosen ones throughout the world intact, through his beloved servant[^59-2-1] Jesus Christ, through whom he called us from darkness to light, from ignorance to full knowledge of the glory of his name,

<sup>3</sup> so that we hope[^59-3-1] in your name, the origin of all creation. You opened the eyes of our hearts to know you, the only Most High in the highest places, the Holy One at rest among the holy. You bring low the insolence of the haughty, scatter the schemes of nations, raise the lowly to the heights and humble the lofty, make people rich and make them poor, put to death and bring to life; you alone discover spirits and are God of all flesh. You look down into the abysses, observe human works, help those in danger, rescue those without hope; you create every spirit and watch over it[^59-3-2]. You multiply the nations on earth and out of all of them chose those who love you, through Jesus Christ, your beloved servant, through whom you instructed us, made us holy, and honored us.

<sup>4</sup> Master, we ask you to be our helper and ally. Rescue those of us in trouble, take pity on the lowly, set the fallen on their feet, appear to those in need, heal the sick, turn back the straying members of your people. Feed the hungry, ransom our prisoners, strengthen the weak, encourage the fainthearted. Let all the nations know you—that you alone are God, that Jesus Christ is your servant, and that we are your people and the flock you graze[^59-4-1].

## Chapter 60

<sup>1</sup> You brought to light the permanent order of the world by what you have made work in it. You, Lord, founded the inhabited earth; you are faithful through every generation, just in your judgments, marvelous in power and splendor, wise in creating and prudent in making creation stand firm, good in the things we see and kind to those who trust in you; merciful and compassionate, forgive us our lawless deeds, our injustices, our offenses, and our failures.

<sup>2</sup> Do not hold against us every sin of your male and female slaves[^60-2-1], but purify us with the purification of your truth and guide our steps so that we walk with a holy heart and do what is good and pleasing in your sight and in the sight of our rulers.

<sup>3</sup> Yes, Master, let your face shine on us in peace for our good, so that your mighty hand may shelter us and your high arm free us from all sin; and rescue us from those who hate us without reason.

<sup>4</sup> Grant harmony and peace to us and to all dwelling on the earth, as you gave to our ancestors who called on you in holiness, in faith and truth, becoming obedient to your almighty and glorious name, and to our rulers and leaders on earth.

## Chapter 61

<sup>1</sup> You, Master, gave them the authority of sovereignty through your majestic and unspeakable might, so that we, recognizing the glory and honor you have granted them, may submit to them[^61-1-1] without opposing your will. Grant them, Lord, health, peace, harmony, and stability, that they may exercise without stumbling the rule you entrusted to them[^61-1-2].

<sup>2</sup> For you, heavenly Master, King of the ages, give human beings glory, honor, and authority over everything on earth. Lord, guide their purpose toward what is good and pleasing in your sight, so that, administering in peace, gentleness, and devotion the authority you gave them, they may find you gracious.

<sup>3</sup> You alone are able to do these things and even better things for us, and we acknowledge[^61-3-1] you through the high priest and guardian of our souls, Jesus Christ. Through him let glory and majesty be yours, now and for every generation, for the ages of ages. Amen.

## Chapter 62

<sup>1</sup> Concerning the matters that belong to our religion and that are most helpful for a virtuous life, for those who wish to direct it in devotion and righteousness, we have written to you at sufficient length, brothers and sisters[^62-1-1].

<sup>2</sup> For we have searched every corner on faith, repentance, genuine love, self-control, sobriety, and perseverance, reminding you that you must be well-pleasing to almighty God, in holiness, with righteousness, truth, and long-suffering, being of one mind and holding no grudges, in love and peace with sincere forbearance—just as the ancestors we mentioned earlier pleased him, by their humble conduct toward God the Father and Maker and toward all people.

<sup>3</sup> We reminded you of these things with all the greater pleasure because we knew for certain that we were writing to faithful and highly respected people who have studied the oracles of God’s instruction.

## Chapter 63

<sup>1</sup> It is fitting, then, that we who have come to such and so many examples should bow our necks and take the place of obedience, so that, ceasing from the futile rebellion, we may reach without any blemish the goal set before us in truth.

<sup>2</sup> You will give us joy and gladness if, obeying what we wrote through the Holy Spirit, you cut out the unlawful anger of your jealousy, in line with the entreaty[^63-2-1] we made in this letter about peace and harmony.

<sup>3</sup> We have sent faithful, sensible men who have lived blamelessly among us from youth to old age; they will be witnesses between you and us.

<sup>4</sup> We have done this so that you may know that our whole concern has been, and is, that you may quickly be at peace.

## Chapter 64

<sup>1</sup> In conclusion: may the all-seeing God, Master of spirits and Lord of all flesh, who chose the Lord Jesus Christ and us through him as a people set apart[^64-1-1], give every soul that is called by his majestic and holy name faith, reverence, peace, steadfastness, patience, self-control, purity, and moderation, to please his name, through our high priest and protector, Jesus Christ. Through him let glory and majesty, power and honor be his, now and for all the ages of ages. Amen.

## Chapter 65

<sup>1</sup> Send back quickly to us, in peace and with joy, the men we have sent from us—Claudius Ephebus and Valerius Bito, together with Fortunatus—so that they can sooner report to us the peace and harmony we hope for and long for, and that we may the sooner rejoice at your stability.

<sup>2</sup> Jesus Christ our Lord’s grace be with you, and with everyone, everywhere, who has been summoned by God through him. Through him may glory, honor, power, and greatness be his, with an everlasting throne, from age to age and for the ages of ages. Amen.[^65-2-1]
