# The Open Academic Bible

**A free, academic, non-confessional translation of the Bible, made in the open.**

What a passage seems to mean often depends on the translation in your hands. Every translation makes choices, and some of
those choices are shaped less by the Hebrew and Greek than by the theological preferences of one tradition or another.
When scholars quote the Bible, they reach for translations that read the text in its historical and literary setting
and follow the evidence of the manuscripts, even when the result is uncomfortable for a particular doctrine.

The Open Academic Bible aims to give that kind of translation to everyone, for free. A student should not have to buy a
Bible to have a reliable, scholarly text.

## Our mission

- **Follow the evidence, not a doctrine.** Translate what the Hebrew and Greek say in their own time and place, and say
  plainly where the meaning is uncertain.
- **Use the best texts there are.** The New Testament follows the newest critical Greek text (UBS6, shared with the
  coming Nestle-Aland 29), and Matthew already follows the working text of the Editio Critica Maior. The Old Testament
  follows the traditional Hebrew (Masoretic) text, with the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Greek Septuagint and other ancient
  witnesses where scholars agree they preserve a better text.
- **Show our work.** Footnotes give the main alternatives ("Or ..."), the literal wording ("Heb ...", "Gk ..."), and the
  places where the ancient manuscripts differ. Every change from our base text is listed with its reason.
- **Free for everyone.** Read it, share it, quote it, teach from it, print it for your own study.
- **Keep improving.** The translation will be updated as new scholarship and new editions of the Hebrew and Greek appear.

Read the full [Preface](PREFACE.md) for how and why the translation was made.

|---|---|---|

| [Apocrypha](apocrypha/) | 18 | as in academic study Bibles, between the Testaments |
| [New Testament](new-testament/) | 27 | the order of the new critical Greek editions: Gospels, Acts, Catholic Letters, Paul (with Hebrews after 2 Thessalonians), Revelation |
| [Beyond the New Testament](beyond-the-new-testament/) | 25 | early Christian writings that did not become part of the New Testament, included for study, not as scripture |

## Where the translation stands

This is a **draft**, released early so that people can use it and help improve it.

| Part | Status |
|---|---|
| New Testament | Revised verse by verse from the critical Greek text, with alternatives and manuscript differences in the footnotes. A final pass checking disputed words against the major critical commentaries is under way, book by book (Matthew is finished). |
| Old Testament | A full first revision is done. A deeper pass, division by division (Torah, Prophets, Writings), is planned. |
| Beyond the New Testament | Translated for this edition from the Greek and Latin. Two books (the Apocalypse of Peter and 3 Corinthians) are provisionally rendered from earlier English translations, because the original-language texts were not available to us. |
| Apocrypha | Printed for now in the World English Bible (public domain) while our own revision is completed. |

The translation is a revision of the Berean Standard Bible, which its publishers have dedicated to the public domain.

## Feedback is welcome

We especially welcome feedback from an academic standpoint: the languages, the manuscripts, the history.
Please [open an issue](../../issues/new/choose) and pick the kind of feedback. Point to the verse, say what you suggest,
and give your evidence (the Hebrew or Greek, a lexicon, a critical commentary, a manuscript). See
[CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md) for what helps most.

Every comment will be read and considered, though we cannot promise to debate every point. The standpoint of this
project is academic and non-confessional, so arguments for one theological position are better served by the many fine
translations made for that purpose.

## Support the project

The Open Academic Bible is free and will stay free. Its quality depends on the scholarship behind it: every disputed
word is checked against the critical commentaries, lexicons and editions of the Hebrew and Greek, and many of the most
current of these works are expensive.

If you would like to help, contributions go toward:

- **Critical commentaries and reference works**, such as recent volumes of the International Critical Commentary and
  other current scholarly commentaries.
- **New critical editions** of the Hebrew and Greek texts as they are published.
- **The ongoing work** of translating, checking and maintaining the text.

**[Support the project on Buy Me a Coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/camiloquin)**

Scholarly books are as welcome as funds. If you can contribute a recent critical commentary or reference work,
please get in touch through the same page.
